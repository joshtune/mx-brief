You actually can burn out from a job you love

Burnout isn't from work volume — it's from unwanted 'shoulds.'

If you're stacking a side hustle on top of a job, this reframes burnout risk as a filtering problem, not a time-management one.

What it is: A short mindset clip on burnout, framed as a filtering problem rather than a workload problem.

How it works:

  • Core claim: burnout doesn't come from doing too much — it comes from doing too much of what you don't want to do (the "shoulds")
  • More "wants," fewer "shoulds" = you can sustain more total work with less rest
  • Cites borrowing the idea of "strategic hibernation" from someone she calls Mechan (name unclear/garbled in transcript — verify before republishing)
  • Her practice: once a year, sometimes quarterly, a full week of zero meetings and zero new projects to get clarity on what actually energizes her vs. what she's just grinding through out of obligation

Why it matters for you: For a side hustler, this is a permission slip to audit your task list ruthlessly — cut the "shoulds" in your day job and your side project alike, because volume isn't what burns you out, resentment is.
