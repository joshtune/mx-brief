Nobody Really Experiences You

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-03 · 13,287 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Speed beats analysis: move fast, fail forward.

If your side hustle is stuck in a planning doc, this is the argument that shipping three bad versions beats perfecting one you never launch.

What it is: A sub-60-second clip built on one idea — adaptability and speed of iteration, not raw strength or intelligence, is what actually wins.

How it works:

  • Opens with the misattributed-but-sticky Darwin line: it's not the strongest that survives, it's the one most adaptable to change.
  • Codie ties it to advice from "my richest mentor": *"I win more for one reason. I move fast."*
  • The punchline is the whole method: "By the time most people are already done analyzing, I've already made three mistakes and found a better way."
  • Implied loop: ship → break → learn → re-ship. Three cheap failures generate more real signal than one expensive analysis.
  • The framing is deliberately anti-research. Analysis paralysis is treated as the default failure mode of smart people, not as diligence.

The catch (say it out loud):

  • "Move fast" only compounds when mistakes are *cheap and reversible*. Three bad landing pages = free tuition. Three bad LLC filings or lease signings = real money gone.
  • This is a hook clip, not a framework. There's no cost-of-failure test, no rollback plan, no "which decisions deserve analysis." You have to supply that.

Tools & links:

  • No tools, repos, books, or URLs mentioned in this clip. It's pure motivational short-form.

Why it matters for you: For a side hustle, your real constraint is cycles-per-month, not IQ — pick the version of your idea you can put in front of a paying human this week, and let the three mistakes teach you what the research never would.
