You Don't Burn Out, You're Just Not Doing The Right Thing

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-20 · 13,172 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Burnout is misdiagnosed — it's task mismatch, not volume.

If your side hustle is draining you after hours, the fix probably isn't sleeping more — it's auditing which specific tasks in the hustle you actually hate and killing or offloading them.

What it is: A 90-second reframe of burnout — you're not exhausted from doing too much, you're exhausted from doing too much of the work you don't want to do.

The instructional secret:

  • Burnout isn't a volume problem, it's a composition problem. Same hours, different mix, different outcome.
  • It's not "love your job vs. hate your job" either — it's the pile of *shoulds* buried inside a job you otherwise like.
  • The tell: obligations that feel inescapable. Not the workload, the trapped feeling.
  • Codie's claim: when you're obsessed with the right work, you need less rest, not more. Rest isn't the lever — task selection is.
  • Cost of ignoring it: slowed execution and compromised decision-making. For an operator, that's the expensive part — bad calls, not bad vibes.
  • Your nervous system has a finite tolerance for hated work before it snaps. Treat it as a budget you're spending, not a mood.

How to run it:

  • Audit the *inside* of the work, not the work itself. List tasks, mark each want vs. should.
  • Shift the ratio: more wants, fewer shoulds. Delete, delegate, or automate the shoulds.
  • Strategic hibernation (Codie credits Nietzsche): once a year, or every other quarter, take 1 week of zero meetings, zero new projects.
  • The hibernation isn't vacation — it's a diagnostic. The output is clarity on which work lights you up vs. which work is poisoning work you're actually meant to do.
