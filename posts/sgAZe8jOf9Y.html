Specialist vs Generalist in the AI Era: The Real Answer

A Life Engineered · 2026-06-19 · 2,260 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Go deep on a specialty, but never exclusionary.

Direct guidance on how to position your skills and frame past mistakes when interviewing in an AI-shifted market.

What it is: A short clip pairing career-positioning advice (specialist vs generalist) with a mental model for handling the dreaded "tell me about a mistake" interview question.

The core message:

  • Have a specialty where you go *really* deep — don't be a shallow generalist.
  • But never niche *exclusionary*: avoid saying "I only do X." That shrinks your surface area.
  • When interviewing, maximize your surface area — show range alongside depth.
  • This holds independent of AI hype; it's durable positioning advice.

How to handle the "mistake" question (the interview hack):

  • Reframe: work is poker, not chess — you operate with imperfect information, not perfect visibility.
  • Reject outcome-ism: judge decisions by what you knew at the time, not by how they turned out. A good decision can yield a bad outcome and vice versa.
  • Mistakes aren't moral judgments — with imperfect information, they're inevitable.
  • Frame your answer as: "I should have done X, but I acted on what I believed reality was — and reality turned out different." Separates decision quality from outcome.
