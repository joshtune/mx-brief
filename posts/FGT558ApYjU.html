The $10M business built on Facebook Groups

My First Million · 2026-11-09 · 4,643 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Watch-trading Facebook group became a $5-10M business

It's a clean case study of a niche Facebook group turned into a revenue machine through dealer vetting and annual fees, though the transcript we have is only the intro.

What it is: A South Dakota founder's watch-trading Facebook group grew into a multi-community business doing roughly $5-10M a year (the transcript calls it "Moda communities", which may be a transcription error).

How it works (what they did to make money):

  • Origin (2018): The founder, based in Sioux Falls, SD, wanted to buy a watch and found no good place to do it, so he started a Facebook group for watch buyers and sellers.
  • Scale: 600,000+ members and over a dozen full-time employees.
  • Gatekeeping: Entry is trust-based. Dealers must do a vetting interview with the founder before they can sell.
  • Primary revenue, dealer fees: Approved dealers pay about $1,000-$10,000 per year (the transcript reads "$1 to5 to $10,000") for the right to list watches for sale.
  • Expansion: He cloned the model into other collectible verticals, with separate groups for collectible cars, gold, and more.
  • Distribution: Facebook supplies the audience and discovery, so there is no app to build and no ad spend to acquire members.

What the transcript doesn't cover (it cuts off after the intro):

  • The founder's name and the exact business name
  • Take-rate on transactions, if any
  • Any other revenue streams, such as ads, escrow, authentication or events
  • How dealer demand was grown and how the vetting runs day to day

Tools & links:

  • No tools or URLs are mentioned in the available transcript. The platform is Facebook Groups.

Why it matters for you: The pattern is to own a trusted marketplace layer on top of a platform you don't have to build, and charge the supply side (dealers) a high annual fee for access. Watch the full episode for the missing money details.
