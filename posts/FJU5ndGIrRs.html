The Side Channel Strategy That Gets You the Offer

A Life Engineered · 2026-08-14 · 3,113 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Text your hiring manager between interview rounds to win offers.

If you keep losing offers to a marginally-better candidate, the gap probably isn't your technical skill — it's that nobody on the hiring side feels like they'd lose something by passing on you.

What it is: A tactic for building a direct, informal communication line with the hiring manager during an interview loop, so that by decision time they feel like they're already working with you.

The problem it solves:

  • "Silver medal syndrome" — consistently finishing second in interview loops
  • The cause usually isn't skill; it's that the other candidate had *some* edge, often relational
  • Hiring decisions between close candidates get settled on gut feel, not scorecards

How it works:

  • Open a side channel with the hiring manager: LinkedIn DM, personal email, or SMS
  • After every single interview step, message them — don't wait for the loop to end
  • Format the message as signal, not flattery: "Great call with [person]. Had a follow-up question on [thing they mentioned]."
  • Keep it substantive — a follow-up question is a reason to write; "thanks!" is not
  • Let it compound across rounds into an actual thread, not a one-off note

The psychology behind it:

  • Loss aversion: people care more about what they'll lose than what they'll gain
  • A hiring manager without a relationship is deciding whether to *gain* a team member — low stakes
  • A hiring manager with a text chain is deciding whether to *lose* a colleague they already feel invested in — high stakes
  • That asymmetry is what flips a close call in your favor

Where the real payoff lands:

  • Not just the offer decision — negotiation
  • A hiring manager who wants you specifically will go to bat internally for a higher band, level, or comp package
  • Their advocacy is worth more than anything you can argue directly with a recruiter

How to actually run it:

  • Ask for the hiring manager's preferred contact during the recruiter screen or the HM call itself
  • If you only have LinkedIn, connect early in the process — before you need it
  • Use the "due diligence / alignment" conversation (asking about the team, the roadmap, their challenges) as your natural on-ramp
  • Cadence over volume — one thoughtful message per step, not a barrage
