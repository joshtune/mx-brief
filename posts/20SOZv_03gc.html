Mohnish Pabrai's $10K to $1M investing strategy.

Pabrai: dollar-cost average into Berkshire, wait 50 years

Mohnish Pabrai — the investor who paid $650K to lunch with Buffett and compounds capital for a living — gives his exact mechanical answer for turning $10K into seven figures, and it takes about 90 seconds to fully understand.

Who he is: Mohnish Pabrai — founder of Pabrai Investment Funds, self-described Buffett/Munger "cloner," famous for winning the 2007 charity lunch auction with Warren Buffett for ~$650K alongside Guy Spier. His whole public brand is that he invented nothing and copied everything that worked.

The question posed: "How would I take $10K and turn it into $1M?" — constrained to 2025 conditions, with the S&P 500 explicitly ruled out as an answer.

His answer, step by step:

  • Treat Berkshire Hathaway as your index — one holding, not a portfolio. It is already a diversified operating business plus a stock portfolio, so it does the diversification work an index fund would.
  • Buy the Class B shares (BRK.B), not the ~$700K+ Class A shares. B shares are the retail-accessible tranche.
  • Dollar-cost average — buy on a fixed schedule regardless of price. "Day in, day out."
  • Do nothing else. No timing, no rotating, no stock picking, no genius required. He says this twice, deliberately.

The math he runs on air:

  • Assume a conservative 10% annual return — notably below Berkshire's historical average, so the model has slack in it.
  • At 10%, money doubles roughly every 7 years (the Rule of 72: 72 ÷ 10 ≈ 7.2).
  • A 20-something investing over a 49-year horizon gets 7 doubles (49 ÷ 7).
  • 7 doubles = 2^7 = 128x. $10,000 → $1.28M.
  • "Life is all about doubles" is the whole thesis: the returns are unremarkable, the *duration* is the entire trick.

The uncomfortable part nobody says out loud: the plan requires a 49-year hold. It is not a $10K-to-$1M strategy so much as a $10K-to-$1M-*by-retirement* strategy. There's no leverage, no acceleration, no exit before then — and it assumes Berkshire post-Buffett keeps compounding at 10%, which is the one live risk in an otherwise inert plan.
