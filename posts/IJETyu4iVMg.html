Cooking robots: 1,000 shipped. 8 million in funding.

My First Million · 2026-09-03 · 5,348 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Posha's countertop cooking robot: 1,000 units, $8M raised.

A rare robotics company that actually ships hardware to real customers — and the founder lays out the exact pricing, funding, and 8-year timeline that got them there.

Featured company: Posha — a countertop cooking robot that makes fresh meals at home, positioned as "a private chef in every home."

The numbers:

  • 1,000 real-world deployments — actual robots in actual home kitchens, not demo videos
  • $8M raised, backed by Accel
  • $1,500 retail price — pitched as "less than a month of takeout"
  • 8 years old as a project (started in the founder's parents' ground floor in India), formalized as a company ~5 years ago
  • Target customer: family of four

The thesis (how they justify the product):

  • If Waymo, Tesla, and Zoox can use computer vision + AI to move you from point A to point B, the same stack can cook a fresh meal
  • Attacks a real three-way tradeoff: takeout is ~$25/serving, home cooking eats 60 minutes, and the alternative is Sam's "man kibble" — three pounds of ground beef, rice, and soy sauce stretched over four days
  • The wedge isn't novelty, it's cost + time: fresh food, no labor, cheaper than the takeout habit it replaces

What it actually does (live demo):

  • Cooked spaghetti alfredo with mushrooms on set, start to finish, unattended
  • Real ingredients, not pods or pre-packaged trays: fresh olives, garlic, milk, butter, pasta, parmesan
  • The machine handled the entire cook — the founder's framing is that this is the whole differentiator vs. every meal-kit and "smart appliance" that still needs a human at the stove

Why 1,000 units is the headline: Shaan's framing — "you're in the 1% of non-bullshitting robotics companies." Robotics is drowning in demo-reel startups; shipping a thousand physical units into consumer homes is the moat and the credibility.
