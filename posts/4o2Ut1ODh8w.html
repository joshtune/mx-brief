Sometimes People Turn You Into The Villian

Codie Sanchez · 2026-07-03 · 18,372 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Why people cast you as the villain—and how to respond.

Every deal, partnership, or side hustle that sours produces two stories, and the loudest one usually isn't yours—so learn to stop defending the record.

What it is: A short mindset piece on reputation, boundaries, and refusing to litigate your character in public.

How it works:

  • People cast you as the villain because it's easier than reflecting—if you're the problem, they don't have to feel guilty.
  • Your signals get reframed: a boundary becomes arrogance, quiet becomes hostility, distance becomes betrayal. None of it has to be true; it just keeps their conscience comfortable.
  • Codie's own example: posted a mild contrarian take on boundaries, was labeled a villain by morning by people who'd never met her.
  • The business parallel: every deal that goes sideways has two versions, and "the person who behaved worst usually tells theirs the loudest."
  • The move: don't write the defense thread. Be the person who knows their own character and doesn't need to correct the record—the people who actually know you respect you more for the silence.

Why it matters for you: Running a side hustle means partners, customers, and ex-collaborators will spin their version when things break; your defense is a known character and a closed mouth, not a rebuttal.
