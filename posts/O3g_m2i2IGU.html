Every AI Business Ranked from Worst to Best

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-11-05 · 82,229 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
13 AI businesses ranked by difficulty, demand, durability

It's a ranking, not a build-along: no workflows or repos, just Nate's opinionated take on which AI business to start (and a pitch for his community), so skim it for the framework and skip it if you want tutorials.

What it is: Nate ranks 13 AI business models from worst to best, scoring each out of 5 on Technical, Market and Durability. Only #13 to #11 were in the transcript I got, because it cuts off mid-sentence.

How it works:

  • Scoring: Technical (lower score = more skill needed), Market (more buyers than sellers right now?), Durability (can it become a lasting, growing business?)
  • Scores are subjective, based on his own experience, friends' businesses and his community. Prices quoted are what businesses charge, not what you'll earn.
  • #13 AI SaaS (Technical 4, Market 1, Durability 3):
  • Build with Claude Code and charge a monthly subscription.
  • Most hyped and most scalable, but the hardest to start. Building takes an afternoon; distribution, payment and churn are the real business.
  • Security and infrastructure get serious at hundreds or thousands of users.
  • Market stat: ~560K new App Store apps in H1 this year, almost as many as all of 2025, with downloads barely moving.
  • A platform update could kill a thin wrapper.
  • Works best if you already have an audience, a service business or money.
  • #12 Faceless AI channels (Technical 2, Durability 1):
  • AI-generated content on YouTube, TikTok or Instagram with no face on camera.
  • Views alone don't pay, and YouTube won't monetize mass-produced AI slop.
  • You need an audience someone will pay to reach, via sponsorships or a paid community. His examples: a news page with sponsors, or a sports-picks account upselling a paid Discord.
  • Brain-rot content can go viral but has no buyers.
  • Nate says he's building a few faceless channels for AI Automation Society for fun. The only cost is something like a Claude Code subscription.
  • Platform rules can change overnight.
  • #11 AI websites:
  • Build business sites with Claude Code, Codex or Lovable, in an afternoon instead of weeks.
  • He'd point a total beginner here: it's visual, and you can build a demo site in ~20 minutes before pitching.
  • Dev agencies still charge $5K to $10K, so you can undercut them.
  • He says there's a ceiling on the model, but the transcript cuts off before the explanation.
  • #10 to #1 are not in the provided transcript.
