The pattern she usually runs here:

  • Free money is the most expensive money — investor cash, a partner's "I'll help for equity," a 0% intro card all reprice later, usually at the worst moment.
  • Free advice is priced at the advisor's incentives, not your outcome.
  • Free time is the one you never invoice — the side hustle that "costs nothing" costs the only non-renewable input you have.
  • The contrarian move is to pay cash and stay unencumbered: keep ownership, keep control, keep the ability to walk.

How to apply it to a side hustle:

  • Before accepting anything free, name the currency it's actually charging: equity, obligation, data, attention, or optionality.
  • Price your own hours at a real number. If the hustle doesn't clear it, it's a hobby — fine, just label it correctly.
  • Prefer boring paid tools over free ones that own your data or your distribution.
  • Say no to "partners" who want a percentage instead of an invoice. Cash is the cheapest way to pay for help.
