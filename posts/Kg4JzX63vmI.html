CEO's job: be your team's confidence when theirs breaks

If you're running a side hustle with even one person under you, this is the 60-second script for talking someone off the ledge without going soft.

What it is: A short clip where Codie answers "what do you tell someone who wants to give up?" with a single line and a leadership frame.

The instructional secret:

  • The line: "Until death, all defeat is psychological." She doesn't explain it — she makes the person sit with it. Silence does the work.
  • Reframe the failure as *selection*, not performance: "I chose you out of a sea of thousands because you're capable." You're not praising the work, you're reminding them of the judgment that hired them.
  • Name the failure point precisely: it stays in your head *until you say it out of your mouth*. Verbalizing quitting is what makes it real. So don't.
  • The job description she's asserting: a CEO's role is to believe in their people harder than those people believe in themselves. Confidence is a resource you loan out when someone's is empty.

How it works in practice:

  • Don't debug their work when they're spiraling — debug their belief first. Tactical feedback on top of a collapsed ego just confirms the collapse.
  • Borrowed authority beats generic encouragement. "You got this" is noise. "I picked you, and I don't pick wrong" transfers your credibility to them.
  • Keep it short. The whole intervention is under a minute. Long pep talks signal the situation is as bad as they fear.
