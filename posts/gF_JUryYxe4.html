3 Steps to Turn Your Hard Work Into a Promotion

A Life Engineered · 2026-11-04 · 25,073 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Stop polishing code nobody asked for; solve real pain.

If your best work keeps earning a polite 'nice work' instead of a promotion, this gives you a repeatable 3-step loop for making your impact visible and wanted.

What it is: A career-framework pep talk built around the 'value loop': solve one person's problem, generalize it, announce it, so effort turns into reputation and promotion.

The message to you:

  • Hard work and technical quality don't get promoted. Solving problems *other people* care about does.
  • His story: weeks of refactoring earned a polite 'nice work'. A 2-hour Python script that automated a tedious task for the program management team made him a hero.
  • Stop perfecting your product in a vacuum. Find your market: ask about pain, not performance.
  • One-time fixes make you helpful. A repeatable system makes you essential.

How it works (the value loop, instructional):

  • Step 1, Solve for one: Find one person with a nagging problem and solve it completely. Don't boil the ocean.
  • Examples: automate a report your manager builds by hand, fix a flaky test annoying the team.
  • Aim for a clear, undeniable win, not a 'pretty good' one.
  • Step 2, Generalize the solution: Turn the one-off into a reusable tool, template, or documented process.
  • His script became an internal website, configurable, documented, in a shared repo.
  • It went from 'Steve's script' to 'the support automation tool'.
  • Step 3, Announce the product: The step most people skip.
  • Post it in a team channel, mention it in a meeting, write a quick wiki page.
  • Frame it as: here's a common pain, here's the tool that solves it, here's how to use it.
  • This signals you're the person who solves a *class* of problems.
  • The payoff: More people come to you, you build a reputation, opportunities follow, then promotion. His tool reached production and other teams forked it.

What to prepare for:

  • Your career depends on being visible to the people who feel the pain, not just on code quality.
  • AI won't change these, per him: high performers get protected and paid well, high-judgment people can't be automated, the best projects go to the best performers, and companies fight to keep people they can't replace.
  • His claim: AI widens the gap between people with these skills (finding the right work, building influence, showing judgment in messy situations, telling your impact story, surviving reorgs) and those without.
  • Skip chasing this month's hot AI tool; invest in durable skills.

Do this week:

  • Ask 3 teammates or adjacent teams, 'What's the most annoying recurring task you do?' Ask about pain, not about how you're performing.
  • Pick one small, fixable pain and ship a complete fix.
  • Package it (config, README, shared repo), then post a short announcement in a team channel.

Why it matters for you: Refactors and clean code are for you; automations that remove other people's pain are what get noticed, so aim your skills at problems others complain about, then tell people you fixed them.
