He Got Inspired by My Videos, Quit His Job, Now Makes $8K/Month

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-11-25 · 15,033 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Student builds $8K/month AI resale-pricing app for $20

It's a concrete, low-cost side hustle blueprint: a non-coder turned a personal pain point into a photo-to-price app that out-earned his day job in under two months.

What it is: A case study of Gibb, a full-time college student with a full-time job and no coding background, who built an AI app that tells you what an item is worth from a photo. It now brings in about $7,500 to $8,000 a month in profit.

How the idea happened:

  • He listened to a Koerner Office episode on government surplus auctions. While it played, he made a GovDeals account and bid on two industrial refrigerators from a school district 10 minutes away.
  • He won them for about $75 each. New, they cost about $4,000. Both worked fine.
  • Then he couldn't price them. They sat on Facebook Marketplace for ages, and he had no idea what comparable fridges sold for.
  • A later episode with John Cheney covered building apps with Replit and AI, even if you can't code. Gibb combined the two: the pain of pricing stuff to resell became an app that does the pricing.

How it's carried out:

  • Build: About $20 and one hour in Replit gave him a working app. You upload a photo and it estimates the item's resale value.
  • Startup cost: $20, with no prior business or coding experience.
  • Timeline: He started roughly 2 months before recording, when he made about $1,600/month at his job. He's now at about $7,500/month profit, enough to quit.
  • Ops: He hired a buddy to do the day-to-day work, so it's now low-friction for him.
  • Marketing (teased, not in the available transcript): He convinced an influencer to make a video that got hundreds of thousands of views but only three paying customers. A local news segment grew the business 500% in 2 weeks. He pays a creator with about 7,000 followers $200/month to bring in about 25% of his customers.

Tools & links:

  • Replit — AI app builder he used to ship the app without coding
  • GovDeals — government surplus auctions where he found the fridges and the idea
  • Facebook Marketplace — where the unsold fridges sat, which exposed the pricing pain

Caveat: The provided transcript cuts off early. The marketing tactics (influencer, local news, the $200/month creator deal) are only teased in the intro, and the pricing, app store details and unit economics aren't covered. Watch the full video for those.

Why it matters for you: Start from a problem you personally hit while hustling, build the thinnest AI version for about $20, then pay small creators for distribution. That's a repeatable side-hustle pattern.
