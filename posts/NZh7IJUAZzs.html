He dropped out of school to start a company

Codie Sanchez · 2026-06-19 · 33,410 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Richest plumber built, sold, then regretted selling.

It's a gut-check on why the exit you're chasing for your side hustle might not be the win you think it is.

What it is: A 90-second case study on Charlie Mullins, the school dropout who built Pimlico Plumbers into a $130M exit — and regretted it.

How it works:

  • Charlie Mullins dropped out at 16, started Pimlico Plumbers in 1979 with a second-hand van, a bag of tools, and zero formal qualifications.
  • Grew it into London's biggest independent plumbing company: 400 employees, $70M annual revenue.
  • In 2021 he sold 90% for $130M, cashed out of his $12M London penthouse, and retired to Marbella.
  • The twist: he regretted it. Once he stepped away, the hands-on, personal culture that made the company special evaporated.
  • His move: he came back and launched a new venture, WeFix — choosing to stay in the game over staying retired.

The instructional secret: The exit isn't the prize. Mullins optimized for the paycheck and lost the thing he actually valued — ownership, culture, and being in the work. The real reward is what you build and your ongoing role in it, not the lump sum that ends it.
