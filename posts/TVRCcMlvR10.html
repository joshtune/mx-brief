Weekly AI news: Claude Duet, PHP exploit chaos

Skip the Claude Duet hype, but if any of your web apps run PHP (or sit behind PHP-based tooling like WordPress), the AI-found 20-year-old vulnerability being mass-exploited within hours is worth your attention.

What it is: A weekly AI news roundup; the transcript only covers the first two stories (Claude Duet and a PHP vulnerability), so the other ~22 items are unverified.

How it works:

  • Claude Duet is Anthropic's proactive personal assistant, in the vein of ChatGPT's Dots, Meta's Muse and xAI's Grockbot. It surfaces reminders unprompted (e.g. a morning briefing), drafts emails and messages, and renders an inline dashboard in the chat.
  • It pulls from point-of-sale, online store, CRM, support, payroll and bank data.
  • Connectors named: Gmail, Slack, QuickBooks, Stripe, Gusto, LinkedIn, DocuSign, HubSpot, Canva, Shopify.
  • Access is waitlist-only for Pro and Max plans. Matt hasn't tried it, so everything is from Anthropic's demo.
  • Anthropic says a dedicated classifier and safety system prompt check each message to guard against leaks of sensitive data like card numbers and government IDs.
  • PHP vulnerability: found by OpenAI, which used GPT 5.6 Soul to identify the exploit before attackers did. The bug has been in PHP for about 20 years.
  • Exploiting it used to need an exceptionally complex chain of steps at the edge of what human researchers could manage. AI models now make that kind of obscure bug much easier to weaponize.
  • OpenAI shared a tool that lets developers check whether they run a vulnerable PHP version that needs patching. Some sites went down briefly to patch.
  • PHP reportedly powers up to 70% of websites, including Wikipedia, WordPress, Drupal, DeviantArt and 4chan.
  • Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince reported thousands of exploit attempts in the 10 hours before the public announcement. That rose to tens of thousands in the 45 minutes after, hundreds of thousands within 2 hours, and millions since.
  • Matt says there were other big cybersecurity issues this week, but the transcript cuts off before he covers them.
