Matt Wolfe · 2026-07-31 · 39,262 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Opus 5 lands: cheaper, divisive, weirdly great at 3D games

The only actionable signal here for a web app builder is that Opus 5 trades some knowledge-work quality for much lower cost — and that a viral 'utterly perfect' prompt pattern is producing near-AAA Three.js output.

What it is: Matt Wolfe's weekly AI news roundup, dominated this week by the Claude Opus 5 release and the community backlash/enthusiasm split around it.

Opus 5 — the benchmark story:

  • Beats or matches Fable 5 on terminal coding, knowledge work, novel problem solving, agentic search, and computer use
  • Fable 5 still leads agentic coding by ~5.6 points (Matt notes Anthropic greyed out the bar so it reads as a tie)
  • The real differentiator is cost, not capability — near-Fable performance at materially lower price
  • Practical upshot: you hit rate limits far slower on Opus 5 than Fable 5

Opus 5 — the vibes story (the part benchmarks miss):

  • Widespread dev complaints: verbose, scattered reasoning, "ADHD" personality, exhausting to read
  • Theo: treats every comment as a P0 requiring thousands of lines of fix; "never answers the actual question you ask it"
  • Multiple people rolling back to Opus 4.8 for knowledge work
  • Being called one of the first genuine regressions in modern LLM releases
  • Caveat: this is Twitter sentiment, not measurement — treat as a prompt to A/B it yourself, not gospel

The one thing it's genuinely elite at — 3D game generation:

  • Side-by-side comparisons (Chris, Chubby) show a massive Three.js graphics-quality jump vs. Opus 4
  • Matt Schumer one-shotted a Call of Duty clone with sound effects from a single prompt, running for hours unattended
  • Others (James Altucher, etc.) reproduced near-identical quality using the same shared prompt — so it's the prompt + model combo, not cherry-picking
  • The prompt's actual trick: aggressive superlative anchoring — "utterly perfect," "don't stop until you're utterly wowed," repeated throughout. Sounds like a joke; it measurably shifts output quality
