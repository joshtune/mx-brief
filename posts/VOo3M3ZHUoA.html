Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-14 · 31,524 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
White-label GoHighLevel as niche software for local businesses

There's a public leaderboard that tells you exactly which white-label SaaS businesses have 100, 500, or 1,000+ paying customers — cross-reference it with their public pricing pages and you have a validated side hustle list with the revenue already reverse-engineered for you.

What it is: GoHighLevel — an all-in-one marketing/CRM/booking/phone/AI platform out of Dallas — lets anyone white-label the entire product, slap their own name on it, and resell it as their own SaaS. Chris's angle: HighLevel publishes an awards page that leaks how many customers each reseller has.

The cheat code (the actual insight):

  • HighLevel's SaaSpreneur awards page is public and ranks resellers by customer count
  • Gold = 100+ paying subscribers · Platinum = 500+ · Diamond = 1,000+
  • Customer count is public. Pricing is on the reseller's own website. Multiply the two → their revenue
  • Example math: 101 customers × $297/mo = ~$30K/mo revenue against a $497/mo flat platform cost = ~$29.5K/mo gross profit
  • Cost doesn't scale: $497/mo whether you have 10 customers or 10,000. This is the whole reason margins get absurd
  • HighLevel's floor: you must resell for at least $97/mo per customer. Most resellers charge $200–$500; some over $1,000

How you'd actually run it:

  • Pick one boring local vertical and build the whole product identity around it — bounce house rentals, med spas, pest control, dentists, roofers
  • Chris's stated thesis: the durable businesses off this model are always the ones serving unglamorous local service companies, not the generic "marketing agency in a box" crowd
  • Configure a niche-specific snapshot (pipelines, automations, booking flows, templates for that trade), rebrand the UI, point your own domain at it
  • Sell as "software for [trade]" not "a HighLevel reseller" — customers never learn HighLevel exists (the Great Value spaghetti analogy: same factory, different label)
  • Platinum and Diamond counts are near-equal on the page, which Chris reads as: escape velocity is the hard part; growth past ~500 customers gets easier

Format note: He walks five businesses pulled off the leaderboard, then opens the software and builds a starter version of the fifth one live on screen — mistakes included. This is the part to watch if you want the build mechanics, not just the thesis.

Reality check before you quit your job: the $497 is your *software* cost, not your business cost. Nothing on that page tells you the ad spend, support headcount, churn, or onboarding labor behind those customer counts — and local-service SaaS churn is the thing that quietly eats this model. The leaderboard is a demand-validation tool, not a P&L.

Why it matters for you: If you want a side hustle with real recurring revenue and no engineering lift, this is a rare case where the market research is already done and published — you're picking a niche and doing distribution, not building software.
