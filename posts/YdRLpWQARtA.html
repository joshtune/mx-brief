How to find your arbitrage (from the guy who’s done it 3x)

My First Million · 2026-10-26 · 52,093 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Nick Kokonas on spotting arbitrage across wildly different games

Kokonas went from options trader to top restaurateur to software exit, and this episode digs into the repeatable mispricing mindset behind all three.

What it is: Episode 876. Shaan Puri interviews Nick Kokonas, a serial founder who won in trading, restaurants and software without prior experience in the latter two.

Heads up: The transcript supplied covers only the cold open and first few minutes, so the detailed money-making breakdown is mostly not in the source. What follows is what the excerpt supports, and the gaps are marked.

Who he is (per the intro):

  • Started as an options trader in the 90s
  • Started one of the best restaurants in the world, with no restaurant experience
  • Founded a software company and sold it for hundreds of millions
  • Shaan's pitch: "one of the most interesting founders you have never heard of"

What he did to make money (from the excerpt):

  • Trading: Began with about $60K, which he calls badly undercapitalized for a trading floor. He and partner Keith Goggan ran a large short position on Plug Power in the 90s.
  • Arbitrage mindset: Shaan frames his edge as finding mispricings. Kokonas's own take is that "every arb is just about time scale."
  • Restaurants: Built what the cold open calls the best restaurant in America. His answer on how is only teased: knowing what great is.
  • Software: Pitches "geoence" (his term, as transcribed; spelling unverified): localized, dynamic, variable pricing for time-slotted businesses. He believes it could make someone hundreds of billions.
  • Early trade story: He showed his dad what he was doing. The reaction was "Is what you're doing legal?" The money made is teased but not stated in the excerpt.

Mindset rules he shares:

  • Risk calibration: If you wake up feeling great, you're not taking enough risk. If you wake up throwing up, you're taking too much. You want a tingle of discomfort (origin: Keith Goggan's line, originally "nausea").
  • Dream-setting: He and his wife visited one place every year from about 1995, treated it as a north star, and bought it years later. He says goals can be unrealistic and still useful.
  • ATNA ("all talk, no action"): He and his wife use it as an accountability slur. It pushed him to bid on the property, and the seller accepted his lowball offer within 15 minutes.
