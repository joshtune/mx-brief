Before Your Next Interview, I’m Begging You: Lie a Little

A Life Engineered · 2026-10-21 · 35,358 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Ethical interview 'lying': curate, don't fabricate

A former Amazon principal engineer and bar raiser, with about 1,000 interviews behind him, tells you what to say and what to cut so you stop sinking yourself with honest-but-dumb answers.

What it is: Steve Huynh (ex-Amazon principal engineer and bar raiser) argues that interviews are an artificial game. Strategic omission and framing, never fabrication, is how you play it ethically. He lists five scenarios; the transcript captured three.

The message to engineers:

  • The interview isn't fair, but it's the game, so learn the rules.
  • 'Lying' here means curating what you present. Everything you say should still be true.
  • Don't break the frame. You're the candidate and they're the hiring team, so play your part.
  • Oversharing and over-honesty carry only downside.

Scenario playbook (instructional):

  • 'How are you doing today?'
  • It's a pleasantry, not a diagnostic report.
  • Say 'I'm doing great' with a slight smile and a slightly upbeat tone, even if you slept badly or are nervous.
  • Volunteering your rough week makes you look like you don't get it.
  • Act like a competent person who's ready to work.
  • 'Tell me about yourself'
  • You can't answer it 100% truthfully, so you're omitting things no matter what. Choose what to omit on purpose.
  • Pick three items that paint a picture of you and match the traits the company selects for.
  • Don't restate your resume, and don't overshare.
  • Everything you say should be true, just flattering and relevant to the role.
  • 'What was your specific contribution to that project?'
  • Contributions are messy, there's no time for a full breakdown, and the interviewer can't verify anything. That tempts people to exaggerate.
  • He gives the example of a Microsoft candidate who claimed he was 'responsible for the entire Azure launch'. The claim was ridiculous, and the truth turned out to be much smaller.
  • Don't inflate. Be specific about your real piece.
  • The transcript cuts off before he gives his recommended answer structure.
  • Scenarios 4 and 5: not in the available transcript, so watch the video for them.

What to prepare:

  • A canned 'doing great' opener.
  • A 3-item 'about me' pitch mapped to the target company's values (at Amazon, think Leadership Principles).
  • A tight, honest, specific statement of your contribution for each project on your resume.

Why it matters for you: Treat interviews as scripted performances. Prep three flattering-but-true talking points, rehearse your project contributions, and don't hand interviewers reasons to reject you.
