One Family Controls 70% of All Motels

How Patel families came to own most US motels

It's a clean case study of a zero-labor-cost, family-funded roll-up: the exact playbook that got one community to roughly 70% of a whole industry.

What it is: A case study of how Patel-family owners (Gujarati Indian immigrants) came to own about 70% of US motels, from just ~1% of the population.

How it works (the money playbook):

  • Origin: A few thousand refugees arrived in the US in the early '70s and bought motels.
  • Fire the staff: Motels are labor-intensive, and labor is the biggest cost, so they cut all hired help on day one.
  • Family does the work: Family members cleaned, ran the front desk and did maintenance themselves.
  • Live on-site: The family moved into two of the rooms, so there was no separate housing cost.
  • Price below the neighbors: Costs were close to zero, so they charged nightly rates under every nearby motel and won on price.
  • Sponsor the next owner: When a nephew came of age, the family helped him buy his own motel.
  • Repeat: The nephew got his motel running, then helped the next relative, and so on.
  • Compounding: After ~50 years of this loop, Patel ownership reached ~70% of US motels.
