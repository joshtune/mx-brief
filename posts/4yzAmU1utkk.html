The Interview Question That Strikes Fear: "Tell Me About a Big Mistake"

A Life Engineered · 2026-06-20 · 4,453 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
How to nail the 'biggest mistake' interview question.

Reframes a dreaded behavioral question so you can answer it confidently in your next interview without sounding incompetent.

What it is: A playbook for answering the "tell me about a time you really messed up" behavioral interview question.

How it works:

  • The question is really probing one thing: a time you lost trust with someone.
  • Anchor on Ethan Evans' (former Amazon VP) line: "Delivery is the currency of trust." Losing trust = you didn't deliver what you said you would.
  • That makes a story easy to find — pick a time you didn't deliver, then explain *why*.
  • Frame the why around ambiguity and risk, not personal failure: work is poker (uncertainty everywhere), not chess (perfect information).
  • Systems are too big to hold in your head; risk is everywhere — so mistakes are expected, not disqualifying.
  • Implicit structure: state what you committed to → what went wrong → the real (uncertainty-driven) reason → no need to grovel.

Tools & links:

  • Ethan Evans (ex-Amazon VP) — source of the "delivery is the currency of trust" framing; search his LinkedIn/newsletter for more.

Why it matters for you: The message: interviewers expect mistakes — prep one delivery-failure story framed around uncertainty, and this question stops being scary.
