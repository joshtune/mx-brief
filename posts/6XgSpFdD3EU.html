BMAD's Founder on the Future of AI Coding (And the Slop Apocalypse)

Cole Medin · 2026-08-25 · 4,590 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
BMAD's creator on slop debt and human-on-the-loop agents.

Brian's core thesis — that agent decay shows up as token burn and slowing cycles rather than broken builds, and that the fix is human-*on*-the-loop scaffolding — is a direct design spec for how your mx-workflow commands should gate and re-verify work.

What it is: Podcast-style session (not a tutorial) with Brian, founder of BMAD, hosted by Cole Medin and Rasmus inside Cole's Dynamous community. Recorded as a follow-up to an earlier BMAD vision workshop, this one is Q&A-driven: BMAD's origin, where it's heading, how enterprises are adopting it, and the "slop apocalypse."

The "slop apocalypse" thesis (the actual meat):

  • It will not manifest as broken code or agents that can't implement. That's the wrong thing to watch for.
  • It manifests as more token spend and more cycles — things just get slower.
  • Mechanism (from the description): comments drift out of sync with what the code actually does; nobody refactors what the agent wrote; the accumulated drift is what degrades the agent over time.
  • Implication: your codebase's *readability to an agent* is a performance metric, not a style preference.

Human-on-the-loop, not human-in-the-loop:

  • Brian: "I really do believe human on the loop is the pinnacle of what to try to get to."
  • The distinction is control without babysitting — you supervise the loop rather than approving every step, but you never hand over the wheel.
  • BMAD is explicitly built around this: "you are on control. The agent is guiding you through it using it as a partner."

Origin story (why the framework exists):

  • Brian managed multiple engineering teams; started BMAD ~a year+ ago trying to get his own senior engineers productive with the agentic loop in Cursor.
  • Experienced engineers loved it as "spicy autocomplete" — multi-edits, quick completions, ask-mode.
  • They rejected the *agent loop* outright: "this is trash, it's getting stuff wrong, I can do it quicker myself."
  • BMAD is the answer to that gap: structured skills + strategy across every stage of the SDLC, so the agent loop survives contact with senior engineers.

Why it matters for you: mx-workflow already has the review layer BMAD is reaching for (`/mx:review`, `/mx:ratchet`, `mx-comment-analyzer`) — the gap is that yours are *invoked*, while BMAD's are *structural*; the slop thesis argues comment-drift detection and ratcheting should run automatically on every agent-authored diff, not when you remember to ask.
