What it is: A sub-two-minute MFM clip where an unnamed billionaire guest answers what he actually buys — and mostly, what he doesn't.

The guest's spending philosophy:

  • Core rule: *"Don't buy what you don't need, or spend less than you make"* — his stated framing of why most people never accumulate wealth
  • Claims he "instinctively can't waste" — frugality as reflex, not discipline
  • Reluctant to fly private, despite affording it
  • No expensive watches. Suits are Banana Republic-tier (per Shaan/Sam's ribbing)
  • Explicitly anti-status-symbol: "I hate status symbols"

What he does buy:

  • A house on the water
  • Boats — but not a yacht. "I cannot do a normal yacht."
  • An ocean exploration ship — the one flex he's genuinely excited about
  • Funds scientists to use the ship for research, and tags along on expeditions

The actual pattern:

  • Spend routes toward things that produce experience or output (expeditions, science), not signaling (watches, yachts, private jets)
  • The distinction isn't cheap-vs-expensive — an exploration vessel costs more than most yachts. It's *legible-status* vs *personally-meaningful*
  • Money-making mechanics: none disclosed in this clip. No company, no exit, no revenue detail
