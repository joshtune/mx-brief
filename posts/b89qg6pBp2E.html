How this tiny golf game makes $500K a year

My First Million · 2026-10-20 · 124,992 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
A hole-in-one golf stand that profits on pure odds

It's a one-guy, one-iPad business that makes money from probability math, though the transcript excerpt only covers the pitch.

What it is: A hole-in-one challenge stand with a floating green and one attendant with an iPad. Shaan and Sam estimate it nets about $300-500K a year, and say it's the only one in the world they know of.

How the money works (from the excerpt):

  • The core model: "The business is math." Players pay to hit balls at a par 3 and try for a hole-in-one.
  • The odds: The average amateur has about a 1 in 25,000 chance of a hole-in-one on a par 3.
  • Payouts: The operator has said publicly that it pays out about once every two weeks. That payout rate is itself marketing.
  • The profit estimate: The hosts work from the ball price and the odds. They put net profit at roughly $300-500K a year, which is their own back-of-envelope figure, not audited numbers.
  • The operation: It's a little stand with a floating green and one guy with an iPad, so overhead is tiny.
  • The open question: The hosts ask why this isn't all over the place. That implies room to replicate it, but the excerpt doesn't give their answer.

What's missing: The excerpt doesn't give the price per ball, the prize size, the location or business name, the insurance setup, or startup costs. Don't treat the $500K as verified. Watch the full episode for those details.
