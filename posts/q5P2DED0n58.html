Should You Raise Prices?

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-31 · 10,494 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Price off value delivered, not competitor rates.

If your side hustle is underpriced because you benchmarked against competitors instead of the money you make clients, this hands you a one-line formula to fix it tonight.

What it is: A 60-second pricing reframe — stop anchoring to competitors, start anchoring to the dollar value you create for the client.

The core claim:

  • "Your price is almost never too high. Your perceived value is too low."
  • The gap between what you charge and what you're worth is what Codie calls the fear tax — the most expensive line item in the business, and one that never appears in the books.
  • Fear tax is invisible by definition: you don't see the revenue you didn't ask for.

How it works — the "value split":

  • Calculate how much your work makes or saves a client over one year. Be concrete: hours reclaimed × their loaded rate, revenue lifted, churn avoided, headcount not hired.
  • Multiply that annual figure by 10–20%.
  • That number is your price.
  • Example math: save a client $120k/year → your price is $12k–$24k. Not "what the other freelancer charges."

Why competitor pricing is the trap:

  • "A price isn't good or bad except by comparison." Choose the comparison deliberately — compare to the client's ROI, not to the cheapest person in your category.
  • Benchmarking sideways caps you at the market's floor and hides your actual leverage.

The customer-loss objection, answered:

  • You won't lose customers who understand the value — you'll filter into customers who actually value the thing you deliver.
  • Attrition here is a feature: the price-shoppers who leave were the ones eating your margin and your calendar.

Tools & links:

  • No tools, repos, or links mentioned in the video — it's a pure short-form framework.

How to apply it this week:

  • Pick your current highest-paying client. Write down the annual dollar impact of your work in one sentence.
  • Run the 10–20% math. Compare to what you actually charge — the delta is your fear tax.
  • Quote the new number to the *next* prospect, not the existing one. Test on new deal flow where rejection costs you nothing.
  • Lead with the value calculation in the pitch, not the price. Perceived value has to move before the number does.

Why it matters for you: For a side hustle, raising price is the only growth lever that costs zero extra hours — same delivery, more revenue, fewer clients to manage around a day job.
