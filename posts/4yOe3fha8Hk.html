Backup generator sales and service scaled to $25M

A founder breaks down exactly how a generator sales-and-service business went from $12M ARR to a contracted $25M by owning the unglamorous B2B niche nobody wants — including who pays and why.

What it is: A backup generator wholesale, sales, and service business doing ~$12M ARR today with contracted growth to ~$25M.

The numbers (straight from the transcript):

  • ~$12M ARR currently
  • Line of sight to $14M this year
  • Contracted out to ~$25M

How they make money:

  • Wholesale generators — moving units at volume, not one-off retail
  • Service generators — the recurring layer; backup power is compliance-critical, so service contracts renew rather than churn
  • High-end estate properties — residential whales who want guaranteed uptime and pay for it
  • Regulated facilities — this is the real engine: telecom towers, nursing homes, police stations, fire stations, data centers. These customers are *legally required* to have backup power, so demand is non-discretionary
  • Government/institutional contracts — big counties, big state contracts, "marquee names"

Who they beat and how:

  • Up-market: goes head-to-head against Caterpillar, Cummins, and Generac on direct deals — wins by being nimbler and more service-attentive than industrial giants
  • Down-market: goes head-to-head against small mom-and-pop operators — wins on scale, contract credibility, and ability to service a state-sized account
  • The wedge is the squeeze in the middle: too big for the local guy, too responsive for the corporate OEM

Why the model works:

  • Regulation manufactures the demand — nursing homes and 911 infrastructure can't opt out of backup power
  • Sales *plus* service means every unit sold seeds an annuity
  • Data centers as a customer segment ties the business directly to the AI/compute buildout without any AI risk
  • "Boring" keeps competition thin — nobody's raising a seed round to service generators
