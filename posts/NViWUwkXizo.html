What it is: Codie breaks down her "6 M" framework by reverse-engineering her company's three best-performing ads line by line, explaining the psychology behind each one.

How it works — Ad 1: Exclusivity

  • Opens with "I'll let you in on a little secret" instead of "here are 5 tips" — exclusive framing beats generic info because people value what they can't easily get
  • Don't slap "secret" on everything — weave it in subtly: "I'm opening this to only 100 people," "I haven't shared this publicly before"
  • Aside: ads featuring her husband Chris outperform solo ads — a human/relationship element boosts conversion

How it works — Ad 2: Self-referencing

  • Opens with "If you need to hire more but your margins won't cover it, this video is for you"
  • Template: "If you are X / if you have X problem, this is for you" — forces the reader to ask "is that me?" and get their first micro-yes
  • Same trick pharma commercials use ("if you're living with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis...")

How it works — Ad 3: Reciprocity

  • Give something of real, nameable value *before* asking for anything — "free training" doesn't count, a named asset (e.g. "the exact spreadsheet my team uses to rank our top 100 ads") does
  • Rule: give it before they've done anything for you — gating a "free" gift behind a sales call isn't reciprocity, it's a transaction
  • Her example: giving away $1M in cash/prizes at a launch event, plus a free personality-test lead magnet at ownerscore.com

How it works — Message (the 2nd M): Position the customer as the hero (Luke Skywalker), not your brand — your company is Yoda, the guide with the map. Amateurs make their brand the star; pros make the customer the star.
