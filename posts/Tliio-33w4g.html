Cole Medin · 2026-08-17 · 9,373 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Workshop recording: making your whole SDLC AI-native.

It's a 2-hour system-level blueprint for turning coding agents into a repeatable team standard across the SDLC — which is exactly the layer mx-workflow sits at, and the framing Cole now uses when training orgs.

What it is: A repost of Cole's 2-hour "AI Native Engineering Org" workshop — the full path from traditional SDLC to an AI-centric one, covering PM, dev, and QA from ideation through deployment and production validation.

The hook he opens with:

  • Cites a randomized study where devs finished tasks 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster (this is the METR RCT, widely circulated in 2025).
  • His diagnosis: the model isn't the bottleneck. The missing pieces are a system and a team standard. Orgs adopting coding assistants without either get slower.
  • That's the thesis the whole workshop hangs on — standardization over tool-chasing.

What's different about this one:

  • Not a build-along. This is Cole in trainer mode, not builder mode — he explicitly says he's shifted his business from "done for you" agent contracts to training orgs on how to make their SDLC AI native. Expect process and org design, not Archon internals.
  • He's packaging the workshop around diagrams + a primary template he walks through — the template is the artifact worth extracting, since it's the reusable standard he hands to client orgs.
  • Scope is deliberately end-to-end and shallow-per-stage: 2 hours means the *whole* process at usable depth, not any one stage deeply. Treat it as an architecture map for your own workflow repo.
  • Coverage spans all three roles — PMs (ideation/specs), developers (implementation), QA (validation) — rather than the usual dev-only framing.

Why it matters for mx-workflow: Your repo already *is* a team standard — commands, agents, gates, ratchets — so the value here isn't new tactics, it's the SDLC coverage audit: map your `/mx:*` commands against his ideation → PRD → build → QA → deploy → production-validation spine and find which stages you've left to ad-hoc prompting. Your PM-side (`/mx:prd`) and QA-side (`/mx:qa`, `/mx:review`, `/mx:ratchet`) are strong; the gap he'd point at is post-deploy validation in production and the adoption/standardization layer — how a team ratifies one standard rather than each dev running their own harness.
