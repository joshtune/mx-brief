I Let Strangers Pitch Me Their Business Live

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-04 · 6,837 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Live business pitches; veterans massage side hustle stands out

The first pitch, a $68K/month veterans massage business built on free VA-paid sessions, is a concrete, copyable playbook for a side hustle.

What it is: Chris and two co-investors take 10 live pitches (2 min pitch, 10 min teardown) from strangers in front of 400 people. They invest in three, and the audience votes for a winner. The transcript only covers the first pitch, Utah Veterans Massage.

How it works (Utah Veterans Massage):

  • Origin: Founder Aean Harden was a burned-out construction remodeler. He did a 4-month accelerated program and became a licensed massage therapist.
  • The niche: He first worked at a medical massage company serving veterans. Veterans get 12 free sessions, so they pay nothing. The payer pays about $90 per session, which is big money for a new therapist.
  • The hard part: It took about 8 months to figure out how to get into the veterans' network. That is the real moat.
  • Start small: 18 months ago he rented a room one day a week and grew from there.
  • Growth: 70 sessions a month a year ago, now about 750. Seven locations plus three independent contractor therapists who take referrals.
  • Numbers: $68K monthly revenue, about $20K profit (roughly 30% margin), up about 10x year over year.
  • Locations: Small 1-2 room spaces on leases of a year or less, at about $500-650 a month.
  • Ops: Clients come to the office, not their homes. Therapists only show up and rebook the client for the same time next week in the scheduling software. The founder and his wife handle all billing, insurance and scheduling.
  • The ask: $100K for 5% equity (a $2M valuation), plus a partner with multi-state healthcare experience. The money would fund expansion to 40 other states and an operations manager hire.
  • Judges' reaction: Enthusiastic, though the panel doesn't know healthcare. The transcript cuts off before the real teardown, so we don't see their critique.

Why it matters for you: The pattern to steal is a government-funded or insurance-funded customer who pays nothing out of pocket. Pair it with a cheap, licensed skill and a 1-room rental, and you can start one day a week. The catch is that the payer network took months to crack, and he isn't fully a side hustle anymore.
