Elon's rocket road trip beats 'impossible' with brute force

A two-minute case study in Elon Musk's actual problem-solving algorithm — reject the constraint, ask what physics actually forbids, then throw absurd manual labor at whatever's left.

What it is: A short Shaan/Sam riff on the story of SpaceX moving a rocket from Texas to Florida by road instead of by barge — used as a template for how Musk dismantles "impossible."

The story, step by step:

  • Default method: Everyone ships rockets to Florida on a barge. Slow, but it's how it's done.
  • Musk's call: "No, we're going to drive it instead."
  • Team's first response: "It's impossible."
  • Musk's frame: *"Physics are the only laws. Everything else is a suggestion."* Regulations, convention, and logistics norms are negotiable; the laws of physics are not.
  • Objection 1 — overpasses: Rocket won't clear them. Reframed as a question: "What's the shortest route to Florida without encountering a single overpass?" Answer: an extremely circuitous back-roads path.
  • Objection 2 — power lines and telephone wires: Still too low even on back roads.
  • The fix: Some of the smartest engineers on Earth ride in a van ahead of the convoy with long poles, physically lifting each wire so the rocket passes underneath. Repeat at the next wire. And the next.
  • The takeaway: "You just have to approach it like a caveman almost, to beat the impossible."

The transferable algorithm:

  • Separate hard constraints (physics) from soft ones (convention, process, "how it's done").
  • Convert a blocker into a search query — not "can we?" but "what's the shortest path with zero instances of X?"
  • Accept an ugly, high-effort, unscalable manual workaround if it unlocks the outcome. Elegance is optional; shipping isn't.
  • Expect the first answer from smart people to be "impossible." That's a starting position, not a verdict.

Money angle: No revenue breakdown here — this is a mindset clip, not a business teardown. The commercial subtext is SpaceX's core edge: refusing incumbent-aerospace defaults (barges, contractors, cost-plus norms) is exactly how the cost curve got broken.
