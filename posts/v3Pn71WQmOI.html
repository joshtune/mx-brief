Live coaching a mid-level Amazon engineer to interview senior.

Watch a bar raiser dissect exactly why a strong resume still sounds mid-level in interviews — and how to fix it.

What it is: A live interview-coaching session where ex-Amazon principal engineer & bar raiser Steve Quinn reworks a 6-year AWS engineer's self-presentation to sound senior.

How it works:

  • The subject: Aaron, 6 years at Amazon (Alexa NLU building wake-word-free device directedness, then AWS Bedrock eval frameworks — internal, then external managed service, then agent evaluation). Great resume, struggles to interview senior.
  • The coach: Steve Quinn — ~20 years at Amazon, made principal engineer, conducted nearly 1,000 interviews as a bar raiser.
  • The core diagnosis: He knows how to do the work but can't *tell the story* in a way that reads as senior.

Instructional takeaways (the intro fixes):

  • Rehearse your intro until it's automatic — practice it so many times you're not thinking about individual words. But don't over-script.
  • Talk about YOU, not a timeline — Aaron's first attempt was a chronological tour of teams/orgs/projects. The intro should be about Aaron, not the org chart.
  • Cut the context bloat — too much detail about which org/team/project drowns the person.
  • Kill the "we" statements — "we the team did X" hides your contribution. Employers hire *you*, not the team. Be crisp about what YOU did and achieved.
  • Frame your lane clearly — Steve's rewrite positions Aaron fast: "engineering side of science/NLU at Alexa and AWS Bedrock" instantly signals he's an engineer, not an applied scientist.
  • Name your gaps honestly — the coaching surfaces Aaron's thin experience scaling systems out, which senior interviews probe.
