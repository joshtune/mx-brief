In five years, your salary will depend on how many AI agents you manage.

My First Million · 2026-09-08 · 7,209 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Zapier CEO: your pay will track your AI agent fleet.

Wade Foster, who runs a 900-person no-code automation company, says the corporate ladder is being rewritten from 'how many people you manage' to 'how many agents you manage' — with one deliberate carve-out for sales.

What it is: A 2-minute clip from Shaan Puri's interview with Wade Foster, co-founder and CEO of Zapier, on how career value gets measured once AI agents do the individual-contributor work.

Who is Wade Foster / Zapier:

  • Co-founded Zapier in 2011 (Columbia, MO) with Bryan Helmig and Mike Knoop; Y Combinator S12.
  • Zapier connects 7,000+ SaaS apps via no-code workflows ('Zaps'); bootstrapped after a ~$1.3M seed, profitable since 2014, fully remote from day one.
  • Revenue: ~$300M+ ARR, reported ~$5B valuation on secondary sales. Foster has stayed CEO the entire run.
  • How Zapier makes money: usage-tiered SaaS subscriptions (free → Starter → Professional → Team → Enterprise), priced on task volume and premium app access; now layering AI agents, tables, and interfaces on top of the core automation product.

The thesis:

  • Old ladder: IC → manager → your comp scales with how well you run a fleet of humans.
  • New ladder: your comp scales with how big and how effective an AI agent fleet you can run.
  • Foster: 'I feel pretty confident that's the direction we're heading.'

The exception — sales:

  • For some purchase categories, buyers still want to talk to and buy from a human.
  • Foster does not want AEs waking up to babysit 20 AI reps; he wants them building relationships and closing trust.
  • Sales should use AI as leverage, not be replaced by a managed agent fleet.

Tools & links:

  • Zapier — the no-code automation platform Foster runs; now shipping agents alongside Zaps.
  • Zapier Agents — Zapier's own AI agent product, the concrete version of the 'fleet' he's describing.
  • My First Million — full episode lives on the main feed.

Why it matters for you: If the manager-of-agents framing is right, the engineers who get paid most in 2030 are the ones who build, orchestrate, and QA agent fleets today — and the clip is a short, credible signal from someone whose company's revenue depends on that shift being real.
