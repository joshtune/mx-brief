How To Use Debt Like The 1%

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-04 · 102,242 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Borrow against assets instead of selling them.

If your side hustle needs capital, this shows you how to fund it without liquidating the thing that's compounding — and without the tax hit.

What it is: A breakdown of the securities-backed line of credit (SBLOC) — the "buy, borrow, die" playbook Elon Musk and Larry Ellison use — scaled down to a normal person's brokerage account.

The instructional secret: Never sell the golden goose. Sell an asset and you eat capital gains tax *and* kill the compounding. Borrow against it and you keep 100% of the upside while the loan funds your next move. The whole strategy works on one condition: asset growth rate > interest rate.

How it works:

  • The billionaire version: Musk needed $44B for Twitter. Instead of dumping Tesla stock, he pledged $62.5B in shares as collateral for a $12.5B margin loan. Kept the shares, kept the voting rights, kept every dollar of upside.
  • The normal-person version: You have $100K in a brokerage account and need $20K to buy a business, a rental, or crypto.
  • Option 1 — sell $20K: You're left with $80K compounding at 10% → ~$128K over 5 years. Plus you owe capital gains (not even in her math).
  • Option 2 — SBLOC $20K: Full $100K keeps compounding at 10% → $161K. Subtract ~$6K/yr interest at 6%, pay back the $20K principal → ~$135K. $7K better even assuming the borrowed $20K earned literally nothing.
  • Paying the interest: Cover it from salary or business cash flow, same as a mortgage payment. It's a line item, not a crisis.
  • The tax kicker: Interest on the loan may be tax-deductible if the proceeds are used for investment — ask your CPA. You can never deduct cash you simply withdrew.
  • Collateral that qualifies: stocks, real estate, business equity, crypto, equipment.
  • "Buy, borrow, die": Buy the asset → borrow against it → keep borrowing against the growing base → never repay. At death the estate settles the loan, and heirs get a stepped-up basis. You never trigger a taxable sale in your lifetime.

The part she glosses over:

  • SBLOCs are margin loans with margin-call risk. Asset drops hard, bank demands cash or force-sells your position at the bottom. Musk's $62.5B collateral for $12.5B is a ~5x cushion — that ratio is the actual lesson, not the loan itself.
  • The 6% vs 10% spread is an assumption, not a guarantee. Rates are variable; a 10% annualized return is not.
  • Deductibility hinges on the money going into investments, not a vacation.
