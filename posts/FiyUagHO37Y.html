Old Phones Are Becoming Robots?!?

Matt Wolfe · 2026-10-24 · 52,165 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
UCLA's PhoneBot turns cheap old smartphones into walking robots

It's a robotics story with zero web-app content, so skip it unless you want a 60-second look at on-device AI running on commodity phone hardware.

What it is: PhoneBot, a UCLA (Dennis Hong's robotics lab) walking robot where a cheap used smartphone is the entire brain and sensor suite.

How it works:

  • The phone *is* the robot, not a remote: camera = eyes, motion sensors = balance.
  • The walking policy runs on-device on the phone itself.
  • The AI was trained in simulation first; early versions walked with a limp before being loaded onto real hardware.
  • Self-righting: lay it flat on its face and it gets back up on its own.
  • Current limits: tiny, no arms yet, but it can already follow and film you.

Cost:

  • Phones: roughly $50-80 used.
  • Body: about $400.
  • Comparable research humanoids: $3,500 up to $14,000.

Tools & links:

  • No URLs given in the transcript. The parts list and 3D print files are said to be open-sourced, but no repo is named. Search 'PhoneBot UCLA' for the release.

Why it matters for you: Nothing here helps you build web apps directly. The only loose thread is that on-device inference on phone-class hardware is getting good enough for real-time control, which is a hint of what browser and mobile edge AI can handle. Otherwise skip it.
