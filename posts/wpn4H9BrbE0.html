The Hidden Pattern Match in Interviews: Problem Size

A Life Engineered · 2026-07-07 · 6,733 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Interviews match the size of problems you solve.

Reframes what interviewers actually score you on — the scale of problems you've owned, not whether you nail the perfect solution.

What it is: A short reframe of how interviewers evaluate you — as a pattern-match on problem *size*, not solution correctness.

How it works:

  • Interviewers carry a mental template of their open role: it has junior-, mid-, and senior-sized problems attached to it.
  • As you describe your work, they're bucketing each story: is this a junior problem? Mid? Senior?
  • They line your problems up against theirs and check for a match at the target level.
  • The solution itself matters less than the *scale* you signal — "I work on these size problems" is the actual message being received.
  • They want to hear you've handled problems similar to or larger than the role requires.

How to apply it:

  • Before interviewing for a level, define what a junior/mid/senior problem looks like *at that company* and pick stories that clear the bar.
  • Lead your examples with the size and scope of the problem (blast radius, ambiguity, cross-team impact), not just the technical fix.
  • If you're targeting a level-up, deliberately surface problems one notch *above* your current title.
  • Don't bury senior-scale work inside a tidy solution narrative — name the scale explicitly.
