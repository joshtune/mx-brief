Why Employees Quit

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-01 · 29,183 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Oversold job interviews cause fast turnover; anti-sell instead.

If your side hustle ever needs a first hire, the interview is where you either buy yourself a loyal operator or a 2x-salary replacement bill.

What it is: A 90-second argument that employees quit because of a mismatch created in the interview, not because of pay — plus a hiring tactic Codie calls the "anti-sell."

How it works:

  • The diagnosis: You sold them a 5K, they showed up to an ultramarathon. Quiet quitting starts week one, not month twelve.
  • The two failure points: (1) overselling the role in the interview, (2) skipping the first 90 days of onboarding.
  • The cost: Replacing an employee runs up to 2x their salary — that's the invoice for being "too nice" and hiding the truth.
  • The anti-sell: In the interview, deliberately lead with the hard parts. Literal script from the transcript: "Sometimes there's going to be long hours. I think this quarter will be really rough. This unglamorous part of the job you should know about."
  • The filter: Anyone still excited *after* hearing the worst is the person who stays. The fast-quitters wanted a different job entirely and would have self-selected out if you'd been honest up front.
  • The reframe: Stop selling the 5K. Hire ultra runners who live for the distance.

What's missing: She names the first-90-days onboarding gap as half the problem and then never addresses it. This video only solves the interview side.

Why it matters for you: A side hustle's first hire is a single point of failure — you can't absorb a 2x-salary mis-hire the way a company can, so front-loading the ugly truth is the cheapest insurance you have.
