My First Million · 2026-08-23 · 68,332 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Tradwife influencer turns 20M followers into farm-goods revenue

A clean case study in converting an audience into a physical-product business — 20M followers routed into sourdough starter, electrolytes, and meat at a reported $70–80M/year run rate.

Featured individual: Hannah Neeleman — creator behind Ballerina Farm, a Utah-based farm/lifestyle brand. Former ballerina turned farm-content creator, and the most visible face of the "tradwife" influencer category.

The audience:

  • ~20 million followers aggregated across platforms (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube)
  • Content formula: her, on the farm, making sourdough from scratch, cooking recipes, running daily farm life — no ad-read energy, just process footage
  • The content itself *is* the product demo — every sourdough video is a soft ad for the mix she sells

How they make money (per the transcript):

  • Sourdough mix — the flagship SKU, directly downstream of the most-viewed content
  • Electrolyte mix — high-margin powder, the classic creator-brand supplement play
  • Meat — beef/protein boxes sourced from the farm; heavy DTC logistics, but authenticity is unfakeable
  • Milk — sold at one point, since discontinued (raw-milk regulatory friction is the obvious suspect)
  • Reported revenue: ~$70–80M/year

The mechanic worth stealing:

  • Own the means of production, then film it. The farm is both the content set and the supply chain — no sponsor dependency, no dropship arbitrage.
  • Sell the input, not the outcome. She doesn't sell bread; she sells the mix so you can make the bread you watched her make.
  • Category creation as a moat: "tradwife" is a content genre she anchors, which means organic reach compounds instead of being bought.

Tools & links:

  • Ballerina Farm — the DTC storefront (sourdough starter/mix, electrolytes, meat boxes)
  • @ballerinafarm on Instagram — the top of the funnel

Caveat: the segment is short and the revenue figure is Shaan/Sam recalling from memory ("like 80 or 70 million") — treat it as directional, not audited.

Why it matters for you: The playbook is a distribution-first product company — build the audience around an authentic process, then sell the exact inputs that process requires; the revenue math works because CAC is effectively zero.
