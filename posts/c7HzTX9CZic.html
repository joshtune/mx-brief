This guy beat the S&P500 for 11 years doing (almost) nothing

My First Million · 2026-10-19 · 43,713 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Anonymous investor beats S&P with 2.9 trades a year

It's a concrete, numbers-backed case of an unnamed ex-founder turning a company exit into roughly 22% annual returns by barely trading, so you get the exact playbook and no guru fluff.

What it is: Sam Parr profiles an unnamed Hampton acquaintance, an eight-figure exit founder turned public-stock investor, who beat the S&P 500 in 11 of 12 years while making about 2.9 buy/sell decisions a year. The name is withheld, and the transcript cuts off before the rest of the episode.

Who he is:

  • Founded and sold a company, with an exit of "something like eight figures"
  • Now invests full-time in public stocks
  • Sam checked the brokerage account himself ("pull up your laptop"), then got the guy's permission to share the numbers

The results (exported brokerage account, analyzed in Claude):

  • Return: about 21.8% annualized vs 13.5% for the S&P 500, starting in 2014 (12-13 years)
  • Win rate: beat the market 11 of 12 years; the only miss was 2022
  • Compounding: roughly $10M at the start becomes about $60M (Sam's estimate, depends on start timing)
  • Activity: 2.9 decisions per year, where a decision is one stock bought or sold
  • Range: zero to six decisions a year, with zero in about 3 of the last 10 years
  • Longest idle stretch: 3 years (2022-2025) with no activity
  • Portfolio size: about 15 stocks owned over the decade (give or take 5)
  • Holdings: not obscure. They include a very large travel stock (think a popular airline) and a popular apparel brand (a Lululemon type, but not Lululemon)

What he did to make money (the playbook):

  • Narrow circle of competence: studies only a few businesses he can truly understand
  • Deep research: reads annual reports and goes deep on each company
  • Written notes: keeps very detailed notes and documents his research daily
  • Waits on price: a company can be a great business and still too expensive, so he doesn't buy yet
  • Buys on a cheap moment: acts the same day the price gets cheap enough
  • Worked example: studied one company for about 6 months before COVID, liked it but thought it was expensive, then bought the day COVID made it cheap (company not named)
  • Then holds: does nothing for years. Sam's framing is that this is 4 months of thinking per decision and zero action, and that movement is not progress

Caveats:

  • The returns are self-reported from one account, and the 2.9-decisions stat is a count of buys and sells only
  • The transcript is truncated, so the remaining episode topics are not covered here: Zuck's victory lap and Sam's own story
  • The 21.8% vs 13.5% figures are the hosts' reading of the chart

Why it matters for you: The whole money-making method is deep research on a few understandable companies, a written thesis, a price discipline, and then inaction, which is copyable if you can resist trading.
