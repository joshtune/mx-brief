The Hidden GTA VI Cash Cow

Codie Sanchez · 2026-11-19 · 70,037 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Profit from behavior shifts, not the product launch

The side-hustle lesson is to stop chasing the hyped product and sell to the habits it creates, which is a cheap way to find demand before the crowd does.

What it is: A ~1-minute thesis that GTA 6's launch is a demand shock for businesses serving stay-at-home gamers, not just for Rockstar.

The instructional secret:

  • When something massive happens, don't ask who's selling the product. Ask how it changes what millions of people *do*.
  • The money sits in the businesses that serve the new habit. Rockstar's $1.65B first-day take is the crowded trade.

How it works (her examples):

  • Food delivery: millions of people decide not to leave the house, so they need food brought to them. Analysts predict 50M additional visits to delivery and fast-food apps in the month after launch (her figure; the source isn't named).
  • Internet infrastructure: the game is ~212 GB, so downloads are already slowing networks. Comcast is offering speed boosts to get people downloading faster.
  • At-home entertainment: anything that keeps people occupied indoors benefits.
  • Who captures it: DoorDash and Uber Eats drivers, pop-up snack shops, ISPs.

Tools & links:

  • None named in the video. The outro teases a free list of businesses to buy, but no URL appears in the transcript.

The catch (reading between the lines):

  • It's a pattern, not a playbook. There are no numbers on margins, startup cost or how to get in.
  • Delivery is a commodity gig, and the surge is temporary. The real edge is being set up before the spike, and she doesn't cover that.
  • The claims are unverified: the $1.65B figure and the 50M visit forecast are taken at face value.

Why it matters for you: Side hustles win by serving a wave the day it starts, so every big launch, event or outage is a prompt to ask what it makes people need, then offer the small, local version of that.
