What it is: Matt Wolfe's weekly AI news dump. Headlines promise OpenAI pausing model development, an AI-assisted skin cancer vaccine, and a new local open-source model (Qwen) — but the available transcript is dominated by a hands-on demo of Tripo 2.0, an image-to-3D generator.

How it works (Tripo 2.0):

  • Feed it a single image → it generates a 3D mesh, then optionally a texture/skin pass as a second step
  • Exports to Blender, Unreal Engine, and 3D printing — Matt calls the output "borderline game-ready right out of the box"
  • Mesh quality is solid; texturing is where it breaks down on real people. Body, clothing, and skin tone were near-perfect on a photo of himself; the face came out mangled (distorted lips, warped nose, texture bleeding into sunglasses)
  • Works noticeably better on AI-generated fantasy/stylized source images than on real photos — a wolf character from a Nano Banana image textured cleanly
  • Style presets: unlit, cartoon (adds black outline), sketch, hologram
  • Quirk worth knowing: the unlit variant silently drops geometry — the wolf's wings vanished, leaving only the bone structure. Don't assume style variants are the same mesh
  • Free tier gives 2 generations; the whole demo ran on it

Practical pipeline this implies:

  • Text prompt → image generator (Nano Banana) → Tripo → GLB/mesh → your renderer
  • For a web app, that's a route to first-party 3D assets without a modeler or asset-store licensing

Tools & links:
