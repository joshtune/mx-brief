AI-steered cyborg cockroaches for search and rescue

Skip it: it's a fun 60-second science curiosity with nothing you can use to build better web apps.

What it is: A short news clip on Osaka University's Madagascar hissing cockroaches. Each carries a tiny AI backpack that steers it through rubble.

How it works:

  • Wires in the antenna and rear: a pulse to the antenna makes it turn, and a pulse to the rear makes it run.
  • Older systems steered the roach around every obstacle, even ones it could climb.
  • The backpack now classifies terrain as flat ground, a climb, a drop, or a hole. It's right about 92% of the time.
  • When a climb starts, the AI stops steering and lets the roach handle it.
  • No new robot legs or hardware were designed. The roach's own body does the hard part.
  • Singapore sent 10 cyborg roaches into the Myanmar earthquake rubble last year and found no one. Osaka's version is still lab-only.

Tools & links:
