Every Claude 2.0 Concept Explained for Non-Coders

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-10-30 · 33,649 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
25 Claude concepts, plain English, no code required

You get Nate's actual test numbers on model choice and effort levels (Sonnet vs Opus, low vs max), which tell you where to stop overpaying for Claude, even though this is a concept explainer and not a build-along.

What it is: A 25-concept glossary of the post-"Claude 2.0" ecosystem (a nickname, not a model), split into five parts. The transcript I got cuts off during concept 4, so everything below covers Part 1 ("the brain") only.

How it works:

  • Context: In about 6 weeks Anthropic shipped 3 new models, docs/slides/designs inside chats, dashboards, animated explainers and a new agent app called Duet. The $200 plan reportedly has no 5-hour limit anymore.
  • Models (5.5 family):
  • Haiku: cheapest and fastest.
  • Sonnet: everyday workhorse.
  • Opus: long, messy jobs.
  • Free plan gets Sonnet and Haiku, not Opus.
  • Nate's Sonnet vs Opus test (7 real workflows):
  • Sonnet wins on clear instructions. His example was a resource guide, done in about half the time and usually cheaper.
  • Opus wins on vague goals, like a 90-day plan.
  • His team analogy: don't put your most senior person on every quick email.
  • Fable 5.1: The top-tier model, built for demanding reasoning and long autonomous runs.
  • Pay-per-use costs about 2.5x Opus.
  • Anthropic's own notes say Opus 5.5 performs near Fable level on most work.
  • On Max/Ultra plans, Fable can use up to half your weekly limit. On Pro you pay with usage credits.
  • Nate says it was pulled by the government when Fable 5 first dropped because it was too powerful. I'm only relaying his claim.
  • /advisor command: Lets Claude consult a stronger model (e.g. Fable) at key moments, so you get some of the brain power without running the whole job on it.
  • Tokens & context window:
  • Tokens set both your cost and how fast you hit session and weekly limits.
  • Every current model in Claude Code has a 1M-token window, roughly 555k words.
  • Across 7 of his own tasks, Claude read about 160M tokens and wrote about 800k, a ratio of roughly 200:1. So the files and instructions you load matter more than prompt length.
  • Effort levels: Low, medium, high, extra high, max. Default on 5.5 is medium. Change it with `/effort` or the desktop app's bottom-right picker. Nate's same-prompt test on Opus 5.5:
  • Low: about 17 min, under $4, glitchy.
  • Extra high: about 1.5 hrs, about $26, the winner.
  • Max: about 2.5 hrs, about $50, still buggy. Anthropic's docs say max is prone to overthinking.
  • His setup: medium daily, extra high for hard work like video editing.
