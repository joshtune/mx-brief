The surprising origin of Amazon Prime

Amazon Prime started as an earnings-call stunt

Prime, now Amazon's moat, began as a rushed move to give investors good news after the dot-com crash, which is a useful reminder that big revenue engines often start as scrappy fixes.

What it is: A short clip on how Amazon Prime came about. Jeff Bezos needed good news for Wall Street in a bad quarter.

How it works (what Amazon did):

  • The trigger: The clip places this right after the internet crash. Bezos could see the quarter wouldn't be what Wall Street wanted, and he told the team, "We need something."
  • The codename: Prime was first called Project 100. The team had 100 days to figure out how to announce it and start running it.
  • The product: A fast-shipping subscription. The clip stresses it was a 2-day delivery program, not a 2-day shipping program, so the promise was about when the package arrives.
  • The original scope: No perks. It was purely a fast-shipping program, and the video-streaming and other bundled benefits came later.
  • The real origin: In the speakers' telling, it was Bezos trying to have good news for investors in a rough time. It wasn't a grand strategy.

How it made money (only what the clip supports):

  • Customers paid a recurring fee for faster delivery.
