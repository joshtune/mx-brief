My AI Dark Factory Dream Is Dead (Here's What Actually Works)

Cole Medin · 2026-10-16 · 31,743 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Why fully autonomous dark factories fail, and what replaces them

Cole explains why he moved from a hands-off dark factory to a human-anchored AI software factory, which bears directly on how mx-workflow should split autonomous work from human checkpoints.

What it is: Cole's postmortem on a year-long public experiment building a 'dark factory' (agents take an epic and ship to prod unattended), and the 'AI software factory' he built instead.

Caveat: The transcript is cut off after the intro. The factory architecture diagram and the concrete lessons are not in it, so everything below is the thesis only.

How it works:

  • The thesis: Better LLMs get better at *building* software, but not at *knowing what you want*. Intent is the bottleneck, not capability. 'LLMs can't read our minds.'
  • Why it fails: Humans never specify features or codebase-maintenance preferences perfectly. Even with AGI, a no-steering-wheel setup ships the wrong thing, confidently.
  • Dark factory (what he dropped): Dan Shapiro's Level 5 autonomy. You supply the destination, never the directions. A high-level epic or release doc goes in and prod comes out.
  • Software factory (what he built): Still highly autonomous. He says he is 'not losing that much autonomy'. The harness defines the exact process the agent follows, and the human stays in the loop at the points where intent matters.
  • Not vibe coding: The engineering effort goes up front into the harness. Agents are trusted at runtime only because the process is tightly defined.
  • Prediction: Every company converges on a similar system within a couple of years. He says teams he works with are independently arriving at the same shape.
  • Portable lessons: You can adopt pieces of it in a normal agentic workflow without building the whole factory.
