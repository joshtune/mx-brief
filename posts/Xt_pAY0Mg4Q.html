What Anthropic's $900 Billion Bet Means for You

Anthropic's IPO, its $900B compute bill, and circular AI financing

Not a build video: there are no workflows, repos or tools, only a market explainer on the company whose API your automations run on, so skim it for context and skip it if you want instruction.

What it is: A finance-news explainer, not a tutorial. Nate walks through Anthropic's IPO, the money behind it, and what it means for Claude subscribers. The transcript is cut off partway through, so the ending is missing.

How it works (the claims, as Nate states them):

  • IPO numbers: Priced at $83/share, raised about $81B (the largest IPO ever), opened Thursday at $111.50, and was worth about $1.8T after day one.
  • Day two: The whole AI trade sold off. Shares closed around $88, and anyone who bought at Thursday's open is down about 20%.
  • Revenue mix (from the prospectus): About 11,900 large customers make up 75% of revenue. The other 25% comes from about 40 million customers spending under $100K/year, which includes Pro, Max, Ultra and API users like you.
  • Control: Founders keep control through one special share with 50.1% of the votes, plus a mission-focused trust that elects most of the board. Nate's read is that Wall Street can't easily squeeze users. Nothing about plans or models changed this week.
  • The $900B math: Actual debt is only about $6B, with over $130B in cash and investments before the IPO. The scary number is about $900B in commitments, mostly compute, with $70.7B due in the next 12 months. Nate compares it to signing a 20-year lease on a building you plan to grow into.
  • Circular financing: Amazon and Google own big stakes in Anthropic, sell it compute, and buy Claude. Broadcom leases chips to Anthropic and agreed to lend up to $42B to cover the rent. Seven chip and cloud companies, Nvidia among them, bought another $26B of IPO stock. He cites the same pattern with Nvidia and OpenAI.
  • Macro angle: In H1 last year, computer equipment and software spending was about 4% of the US economy but about 92% of its growth. In other words, AI capex was carrying GDP.
  • Risk signal: Nvidia is down about 46% in 7 weeks, and Jensen Huang resigned as CEO. Anthropic fell right alongside it. If AI demand slows, the loop runs in reverse for everyone at once.
