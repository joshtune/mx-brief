The $15M business built around sharing P&Ls

My First Million · 2026-11-07 · 7,277 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Peer groups where members share P&Ls, worth ~$15M

It's a business whose entire product is members laying their profit and loss statements open, and the hosts guess it grosses $13–15M a year.

What it is: A case study of "NADA 20 groups" (the transcript spells it "NATA 20", for the National Automotive Dealer Association). Auto dealers join non-competitive peer groups and review each other's P&Ls line by line. The hosts guess the business does $13–15M a year in revenue.

How it works:

  • Auto dealers who want to grow apply to join, so there's a gate.
  • Members are placed in groups of 24.
  • Groups are non-competitive, so members can be open with each other.
  • Each member brings the P&L for their business and walks through it line by line.
  • The group reacts in two ways: "you're killing it, I'm going to copy that" or "you suck, have you thought about doing this?"
  • Scope has widened. It now admits founders of HVAC companies, marina owners and boat repair shops.
  • The hosts call it "the most simple peer group ever."

How it makes money (from what's in the transcript):

  • Revenue guess: ~$13–15M a year, and that's Shaan/Sam's estimate, not a reported figure.
  • The product is access to a curated group of non-competing peers, not content.
  • The mechanism is application-gated membership. The transcript gives no pricing, fee structure or membership count.
  • The expansion into HVAC and marine businesses suggests the model works outside auto dealers.

What's missing: The transcript cuts off at "for a hundred years they've been...", so there's no fee detail, founder story, unit economics or history. Watch the full episode for those.
