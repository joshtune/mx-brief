Google Is BACK (Gemini 4 Neon Tested)

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-11-22 · 91,816 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Gemini 4 Neon roundup: viral demos, real bug-fixing, limits

Nate mostly curates other people's Neon builds and benchmarks rather than building himself, so watch it to decide whether Neon is worth a switch from Opus, not to copy a workflow.

What it is: A roundup of community demos and benchmarks for Google's Gemini 4 Neon, which Nate says beats Claude Opus on the Artificial Analysis leaderboard at about 25% lower cost. It's mostly other people's work, not Nate building.

How it works (what people actually did):

  • Robot dog in 3D (the "bugged dev"): One image of a robot dog with a rail gun, prompted only with "build this in 3D." Neon added 240 Hz physics, leg forces and inverse kinematics for foot planting, plus walk, trot and gallop gaits. The post got about 150K views in a day.
  • Still image to animated scene (Ashtosh): One still image, prompted "animate this in Three.js." Neon downloaded a depth model from Hugging Face and ran it locally on his Mac. It sliced the image into depth layers, then built live rain, splashable puddles, brushable curtains and generated rain sound. Nobody told it how.
  • 17 demos in one day (Chubby, in Antigravity):
  • *Terrorreaker:* a destructible voxel arena at 60 fps. He asked only for destruction and a reset button. Neon invented three tools that behave differently, with a cannon that rips out big chunks and a chisel that chips a few blocks.
  • *Bug fixing:* three broken programs went from failing to 100% passing. A booking checker went 1/5 → 100%, a task scheduler 8/14 → 100%, and an async loader 10/16 → 100%.
  • *Other demos:* a one-prompt historical sailing ship with clean water and hole rendering.
  • Eclipse Village (Emily): A moon shadow sweeps a voxel town and house lights switch on as it passes. Caveat from Nate: the prompt was very long. It specified the house count, the camera position and what happens every few seconds of the loop. The best results come from good specs, not magic.
  • Google's own demo: A first-person ski simulator on real Mont Blanc terrain with 10,000+ lines of custom shaders and avalanches. Nate treats it as launch-day marketing.
  • Real-work benchmark (Powell): Two real repos with 105 bugs planted back in, and each model told to find and fix what it could.
  • Neon on its highest setting scored 63, just above "6.2 Astra" at 62.7 and above Fable 5.2 at 58.
  • Nate notes people have mixed feelings about Astra 6.2.
  • Hacker News anecdote: A dev was stuck for 2 weeks on low-level graphics work with an OpenAI model on one machine and Fable 5.2 on another. Neon unblocked them in about 20 minutes.
  • Nate's four strengths for Neon:

1. Visual builds: 3D scenes, games, landing pages and dashboards, anything you can see in a browser. This is where almost all the viral content comes from.

2. Fixing and debugging, backed by the bug test, Chubby's programs and the graphics story.

3. The other two strengths and his own head-to-head against Opus are cut off in the transcript I was given. I can't summarize them.
