AI Didn’t Hack the Banks (Here’s What Did)

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-11-17 · 23,949 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Bank and airline outages: bugs and legacy tech, not AI hackers

This is a news-commentary take with no build or tutorial, so skip it if you're here for Nate's workflows and tools, but it's a decent 5-minute read on why AI-driven patching could make legacy systems wobble.

What it is: A short opinion video arguing that last week's Microsoft, FAA, and Chase outages were mundane failures, not AI or foreign hacks, though AI-driven vulnerability patching may cause more of them.

How it works:

  • Microsoft (Thursday): down most of the day. Microsoft blamed a network upgrade gone wrong.
  • FAA (Friday): nationwide ground stop of 4+ hours, traced to one new router a contractor plugged in at a radio site in Arkansas. It flooded the network. An AI air traffic tool had launched 3 days earlier, but its maker says it's unrelated.
  • Chase (Monday): payments failed. Chase called it a software bug in its own hardware, not a cyber attack.
  • The X theory: posts with 26K-200K likes claim China, AI agents, or a coordinated attack. Nate doesn't buy it.
  • Why not a hack:
  • A competent spy stays hidden, and crashing Chase for an afternoon is the fastest way to get caught.
  • The head of Homeland Security says he is 98% sure it wasn't a cyber attack.
  • Chase is public, so a real attack would have to be reported to the SEC within 4 business days. Calling a hack a bug would be a legal risk.
  • Where AI does fit (his thesis):
  • Anthropic's Mythos model, released in April to a small group of companies including JP Morgan Chase because it was too good at finding security holes. It reportedly found 10,000+ serious flaws in internet software within a couple of months.
  • Banks now have long lists of holes to patch. Bank of America reportedly hired about 1,000 extra contractors for patching.
  • Pushing heavy change into decades-old legacy systems makes them break.
  • Caveat he states himself: nobody has confirmed a Mythos patch caused the Chase outage.
  • Takeaways:
  • Treat a banking app outage as a bug first, not a robbery.
  • Keep some cash in an account at a second bank.
  • Reuters reports banks may take systems offline more often while they patch.
  • Bank of America's CEO says patching tapers off once the backlog is cleared, so expect a few bad Mondays.
