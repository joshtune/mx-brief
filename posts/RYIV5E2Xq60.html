I REALLY Want Open Models to Win, But They Just Don't Cut It (Yet)

Cole Medin · 2026-11-05 · 6,953 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Open models ace benchmarks, then flunk long agentic runs

Cole runs a plan → implement → review → play-test skill harness (his 'AI software factory') on open models and shows where it breaks, which is a ready-made stress test for your mx-workflow skills.

What it is: Cole explains why open-weight models (Kimi K3.1, GLM, Qwen, DeepSeek) match Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra on benchmarks but fall apart on long-running agentic coding, and what he does about it.

How it works:

  • The benchmark gap: Kimi K3.1 Pro Max matches or slightly beats GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 on Terminal Bench 4.0. Real builds tell a different story.
  • Short tasks are a tie: On shorter tasks, even ones needing deep reasoning, open and frontier outputs were roughly equivalent. He judged them with a separate reviewer agent plus his own read. Open models cost far less.
  • Long tasks are the failure mode: When an open model drives a large multi-step harness, it stops following instructions and degrades over time. That's the thesis of the video.
  • His test harness: The 'AI software factory' is a skills-and-rules workflow. He and the agent plan together, implement from the plan, then he and a second agent review and play-test before merge. He runs it on every bug fix and feature, not a bare prompt in Claude Code or Kimi Code.
  • Where open models already win: Routine steps inside business automations. Cole cites claims that open-weight models will run ~90% of enterprise agentic workflow steps within 2–5 years. He says they're at par for about a quarter of the price of Haiku 5.5, and they can run locally for sensitive data.
  • Why he cares: He doesn't want to depend on Claude Code and Codex subscriptions. Rate limits are getting worse, and paying API prices for Opus 5.5 or GPT-6 Astra isn't realistic at his token volume.
  • Caveat on this summary: The transcript cuts off just as he starts the harness breakdown. The detailed test results and his proposed solution (mixing models) aren't in what I received, so I haven't guessed at them.
