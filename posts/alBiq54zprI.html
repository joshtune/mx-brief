Poppi founder on going kitchen-experiment to $2B exit

A four-and-a-half-year, $0-to-$550M revenue run told in actual numbers by the founder, with the specific levers — Shark Tank rebrand, COVID timing, founder-led TikTok — that made each jump.

Who: Allison Ellsworth, co-founder (with her husband) of Poppi, the prebiotic soda brand sold for $2 billion to PepsiCo.

The business, start to finish:

  • Origin: She had gut/"tummy" problems, was drinking apple cider vinegar for it, and hated the taste. Built a better-tasting ACV soda in her kitchen. Personal problem → product.
  • Phase 1 — Farmers markets (~2 years): Sold under the name Mother Beverage. Slow, unglamorous, direct-to-human selling. Her words: if she'd known how brutal the beverage industry is, she wouldn't have started.
  • Phase 2 — Shark Tank: Got a deal on air. The deal came bundled with a partner who pushed the packaging/positioning rework.
  • Phase 3 — The rebrand: Mother Beverage → Poppi. She calls it "brand glow-up of the century," and the old Shark Tank clip vs. today is the before/after. This is the single most-cited domino in the episode.
  • Phase 4 — Launch timing: Relaunched as Poppi March 3, 2020 — first week of COVID. Accidental perfect timing: everyone suddenly reading labels, thinking about immune systems and gut health.

The revenue ramp (her numbers, as stated):

  • Year 1: ~$3M
  • Year 2: ~$25M (transcript garbled as "2250")
  • Then: $50M → $200M
  • Then: $500M+
  • ~4.5 years from launch to ~$550M revenue → $2B sale

What they actually did to make money:

  • Founder-led TikTok, early. One of the first beverage brands to go all-in. Dances, recipe videos, transition videos — deliberately making a fool of herself to chase virality. She worked it nights and weekends as a personal obsession.
  • Ignored the expert. Rohan (prior MFM guest) told her TikTok was "for young kids and dancers, don't try." She did it anyway. Nobody understood the platform yet — that was the arbitrage.
  • Rode a wild-west channel before CAC caught up. Organic reach was effectively free in 2020.
  • Super Bowl, bought 5 days out. Their first Super Bowl ad was purchased five days before the game — late-inventory buy, not a year-planned media campaign.
  • Product-market timing over product genius. She's blunt: "lightning in a bottle," "not normal." Growth this fast isn't a repeatable playbook and she doesn't pretend it is.

Founder notes worth keeping:

  • On imposter syndrome: no — she was confident in what they were building. The disorientation came *after* the sale, not during.
  • She never stopped to celebrate. Four and a half years of "too soon to celebrate," then it was over.
  • "Not knowing is such a beautiful thing" — the ignorance about how hard beverage is was load-bearing to ever starting.
