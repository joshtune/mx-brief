Ray Dalio: The one rule that cuts investment risk by 80%

My First Million · 2026-07-17 · 56,811 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Dalio's diversification math for maximizing returns per risk.

Dalio hands you the exact formula—15 uncorrelated bets—that took him from borrowing $4K to running the world's biggest hedge fund.

Featured guest: Ray Dalio — founder of Bridgewater Associates, the largest and most successful hedge fund in the world.

The origin story (how he built the wealth):

  • Started Bridgewater in 1975.
  • 1981–82: Predicted emerging-market debt crises. Mexico defaulted August 1982 — he was invited to testify to Congress.
  • Got it catastrophically wrong on the economy's direction. Lost his own money AND his clients' money.
  • At ~34–35, married with two kids, he laid off all five employees and borrowed $4,000 from his dad to survive.
  • That rock-bottom moment became the turning point — everything after went up.

The two lessons that built the empire:

  • Humility to balance audacity — he actively wanted people to attack his ideas and prove him wrong before he acted.
  • Diversification math — reduce downside risk without cutting upside returns.

The "Holy Grail" rule (the 80% claim):

  • Find 15 good, uncorrelated return streams.
  • The number 15 comes from the marginal benefit of diversification at varying correlation levels (he keeps a chart on it).
  • Result: cut ~80% of your risk without reducing return → improves return-to-risk ratio by roughly 5x. That's how you get upside without the downside.

His decision-making system (how he actually invests):

  • For every decision, back-test it: "If I made this call in these circumstances, how would it have performed historically?"
  • Turn each validated call into a decision rule, then program it into the computer.
  • Scan globally: dump every instance where that rule triggers, worldwide — not just the one you happened to spot.
  • Collect many such rules and make them uncorrelated → automated, diversified game plan.

Most common mistake smart investors make:

  • They don't have a game plan.

On money & happiness:

  • No correlation between happiness and how much money you make. You need a purpose — know what the money is *for*.
  • He built personality tests and gave them to Elon Musk, Bill Gates, and Reed Hastings.
