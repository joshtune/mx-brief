Claude sold everything he owned

Guy used Claude to liquidate his house.

A 60-second clip showing an agent handling pricing research, listing, buyer negotiation, and scheduling end-to-end — the clearest consumer proof that agentic AI now closes loops, not just drafts text.

What it is: A short MFM clip where Shaan describes a TikTok of someone who sold every piece of furniture in his house on Facebook Marketplace in 48 hours by handing the whole job to Claude.

The play, step by step:

  • Took photos of all his furniture (he was moving and needed the house empty)
  • Uploaded the photos to Claude with a single blanket instruction set
  • Prompt #1 — research: "Here's my stuff. Research what it costs." → Claude priced each item against comps
  • Prompt #2 — list: Claude generated and posted the Facebook Marketplace listings
  • Prompt #3 — negotiate: "Talk to people on my behalf" → Claude handled inbound buyer messages
  • Prompt #4 — schedule: He gave Claude his calendar availability with the constraint "I don't care when, as long as it's open"
  • Prompt #5 — logistics: Claude booked buyers to come to the house and pick items up
  • Result: entire house liquidated in 48 hours, owner did approximately zero of the coordination work

Why this clip got traction:

  • The tedious middle — pricing research, message ping-pong with lowballers, scheduling pickups — is exactly the friction that stops people from selling stuff at all
  • Shaan's takeaway: "even the tiniest part is now fixable" — the unit of automation has dropped below "job" to "annoying errand"
  • No custom code, no API, no integration. Photos plus plain-English instructions.

Tools & links:

  • Claude — the agent that did the pricing, listing, messaging, and scheduling
  • Claude "Co-work" mode is what the original TikTok credited (the agentic/computer-use style workflow, not plain chat)
  • Facebook Marketplace — the sales channel; no API used, the agent operated it like a person would

The business angle (what MFM is actually pointing at):

  • Any workflow that is "research price → create listing → answer buyers → book a time" is now a one-prompt job: resale, rentals, ticket flipping, estate clearouts, storage-unit arbitrage
  • The moat isn't the AI, it's the inventory and the trust to let an agent speak as you
  • Obvious wedge product: an "agent liquidator" service — flat fee or 10-20% of sale price to empty someone's house before a move

Caveats worth naming:

  • Single anecdote from a TikTok, unverified, no revenue figure given
  • Letting an agent negotiate and schedule strangers into your home is a real trust/safety surface

Why it matters for you: This is the cleanest short demo of an agent closing a full commercial loop — research, publish, negotiate, schedule — which is the same loop shape you'd wire up for any side-business automation.
