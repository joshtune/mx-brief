"Politics" Isn't Dirty - It's Just Human Interaction

A Life Engineered · 2026-07-31 · 2,346 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Reframing office politics as goal reconciliation, not backstabbing

If you've been dodging "politics" as beneath you, this reframes it as the actual mechanism by which your project gets prioritized over someone else's — a skill you need whether you like the word or not.

What it is: A short reframe — politics isn't manipulation, it's the process businesses and people use to reconcile competing goals against limited resources.

The core argument:

  • Most developers hear "politics" and think nasty, dirty, zero-sum. That reaction is understandable but expensive.
  • Working definition offered: *how businesses and the people inside them reconcile different goals and limited resources.*
  • The concrete case: you have a product to ship, I have a product to ship, we have dependencies on each other or we step on each other's toes. You want your schedule. I want mine. Something has to break the tie.
  • That tie-breaking process is politics. It *can* get nasty — it doesn't have to.
  • Implication: if you refuse to participate, you don't opt out of the outcome. You just lose the tie-break by default.

The one instruction given:

  • Be aware that relationships matter. That's the first lever. Framed as a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have — the video presents relationships as where the process starts, before any negotiation tactic.

What to prepare for:

  • Expect dependency conflicts and schedule collisions to be resolved socially, not by a scheduler or a ticket priority field.
  • Your technical argument is the input, not the decision. Someone has to want to hear it.
  • Build the relationships *before* the conflict, not during it — you can't negotiate from cold with someone whose roadmap you're about to disrupt.

Why it matters for you: The message to engineers is blunt — refusing to play politics isn't integrity, it's forfeiting; start by investing in relationships before you need them.
