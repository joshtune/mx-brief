A first-hand look at what an early Facebook check actually looked like — and the one metric that made it a no-brainer.

What it is: A short teaser clip where a guest recounts meeting Mark Zuckerberg in 2004 and writing an early $38,000 check into Facebook — later described as worth ~$6 billion.

How it works:

  • In '04, Sean Parker walked Zuckerberg into the guest's office at Tribe (an early social network) and said "you got to see this."
  • Zuckerberg showed up unapologetic — basketball shorts, Hawaiian flip-flops, feet on the table, business card reading "CEO."
  • The investment thesis was one metric: 60–80% of Facebook users logged on every day (or stayed logged in) — extraordinary daily engagement/retention.
  • Guest's framing: "Anyone would have made the same investment if they could have" — the edge was access (being in the room with Zuckerberg + Sean Parker), not insight.

Numbers:

  • Check size: $38,000
  • Claimed later value: ~$6 billion
  • Daily active usage cited: 60–80%

  • No repos, tools, or external URLs mentioned in the transcript.

Why it matters for you: The whole bet reduced to a single retention number — a clean reminder that for consumer product, daily-active engagement is the signal that overrides everything else (including how the founder dresses).
