Four leadership lies keeping you stuck on the ground floor

Sanchez argues leadership isn't a soft skill but the skill that caps your income, and the four lies she dismantles are exactly the traps a side-hustler falls into the moment they hire their first contractor.

What it is: The "lobby" level of Sanchez's "leadership elevator" framework: four beliefs about leading people that feel virtuous but quietly cap your growth.

The instructional secret: Every leadership lie is a form of self-protection disguised as care. Wanting to be liked, voting on decisions, rescuing failures, calling the team family. Each one protects the leader's comfort, not the team's performance. Strip the ego out and the hard calls get easy.

How it works:

  • Lie 1: You should be liked. Approval is a drug that turns leaders into mascots. The Jony Ive / Steve Jobs story: Ive asked Jobs to go easier on the team, Jobs called it vanity. Ive later admitted at Jobs's funeral that he was right. Your job is to be trusted, not liked.
  • Lie 2: Leadership is a democracy. Howard Schultz closed roughly 7,100 Starbucks stores for an evening in 2008 to retrain 135,000 baristas. He didn't put it to a vote. Committees sand the edges off every decision until it's safe and useless. Take input from everyone, orders from no one.
  • Lie 3: The leader saves everyone. The "office firefighter" looks heroic but trains a company of arsonists. Every fire you put out is one your people never learn to prevent. If everything needs you, you're not indispensable, you're the bottleneck.
  • Lie 4: Your team is family. Families don't do performance reviews or fire grandma for missing quota. Calling it family lets weak performers hide behind loyalty while strong ones carry them. Nobody should ever be surprised when they're fired. If they are, you failed first.
