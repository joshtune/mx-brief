How Whatnot figured out its early growth strategy

My First Million · 2026-09-23 · 3,165 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Whatnot's referral-loop Funko Pop giveaways sparked its growth to $20B.

A concrete, numbers-backed breakdown of the exact referral mechanic Whatnot used to bootstrap a marketplace from zero before it became a $20B company.

What it is: A short breakdown of the viral referral loop Whatnot (livestream collectibles marketplace) used in its earliest days to build an initial user base, told by insiders on My First Million.

Who/what: Whatnot — now valued at $20 billion — started this growth engine roughly six years before this conversation aired.

How it worked:

  • Ran a weekly giveaway of a $1,000 Funko Pop.
  • Entry required sharing — built for unlimited/compounding sharing, not a one-time post.
  • Referral links/codes: anyone who signed up via your link or used your code earned you an extra entry ticket.
  • First giveaway flopped: a $500 item, only 104 total entries, zero purchases generated.
  • Second giveaway scaled to 250 entries (~2.5x growth) — the loop started compounding, making the unit economics work.
  • Used the growing giveaway traction to "take over" Funko Pop-focused Reddit communities and Facebook groups by consistently offering the best prizes in that niche.

Tools & links: None mentioned in the transcript — no specific platforms, tools, or URLs referenced beyond Reddit and Facebook as distribution channels.

Why it matters for you: It's a rare specific, numbers-driven example (104 entries → 250 entries → exponential) of how a low-cost incentive loop (not paid ads) took a company from a flop to a $20B outcome — a directly copyable playbook for early-stage referral mechanics.
