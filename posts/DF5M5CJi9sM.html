How AI is reshaping junior and senior engineering careers

Demetri Spanos, a longtime AI/ML practitioner who mentors early-career engineers, says what to prepare for when no one can predict where AI coding goes.

What it is: Steve Huynh interviews Demetri Spanos (decades in AI/ML) on how AI is disrupting engineering careers. The transcript I have cuts off early, so this covers the junior-engineer half only, and the senior half is missing.

How it works:

  • The message: Nobody has stable career advice right now. Demetri could say "learn Go or Rust" in 2016 with confidence. He can't do that for AI tooling today.
  • Adoption arc: AI/ML went from a few specialists at Google or Facebook (~20 years ago), to 4-5 person ML teams (~2015), to touching almost every tech job now.
  • Two groups, two problems: Juniors and mid/late-career engineers are both being disrupted, but their incentives and problems differ.
  • Skeptic vs. booster spectrum:
  • Skeptics: AI will handle a substantial share of low-stakes coding within 3-4 years.
  • Boosters: we're already there for anything but the hardest problems. Use frontier models, have AI generate the test systems, ship, and don't read the code.
  • Even skeptics have moved fast. People who said "I'd never use this" six months ago now call heavy use inevitable within a couple of years.
  • Junior anxiety: New grads worry whether the skills they just learned will matter in 5 years.
  • His advice so far: He has informally mentored ~7 early-20s engineers. The ones he connects with want to keep the ability to write high-quality code by hand. He frames this as a personal preference, and the clip cuts off before the full reasoning.
  • Chaos is the baseline: In the teaser, he calls it a fool's errand to assume a stable AI workflow pattern exists. He also says it's an open question whether we'll still need to understand what the models do inside our systems.

What to prepare for:

  • Assume AI writing a big share of low-stakes code within ~3 years, even on the skeptical view.
  • Assume your workflow will keep changing. Don't build your identity around one toolchain.
  • Expect AI to touch your job whatever your specialty is.
  • Keep hand-coding skills sharp. This is his mentee advice, though the reasoning isn't in this excerpt.

Instructional steps: The excerpt has no step-by-step tutorial. The closest actionable items are:

  • Pick a stance on the skeptic-to-booster spectrum and revisit it every few months, since it's moving fast.
  • Deliberately practice writing quality code by hand alongside AI use.
  • Treat 'which tech to bet on' as less certain than it was in 2016.
