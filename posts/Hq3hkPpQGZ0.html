Stop Saying Yes

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-14 · 11,324 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Saying yes to every customer kills your margins.

If your side hustle is bleeding time on one-off custom requests, this is the argument for collapsing your work into three fixed offers so you can actually deliver on nights and weekends.

What it is: A short pitch for offer discipline — kill infinite customization, replace it with exactly three clear offers.

The core claim:

  • Saying yes to every customer request *feels* flexible, but each yes reduces margin, adds friction, and confuses buyers
  • Codie's book example: a client shipping 172 variations of one product — a business that had effectively been redesigned by its customers, one request at a time
  • The fix: collapse down to three clear offers

What changed after the cut (her reported outcomes):

  • Close rates went up — a specific offer is easier to say yes to than an open menu
  • Churn came down — customers got what they expected, because the offer was legible before purchase
  • Revenue finally became real profit, not gross activity

Why three offers works (the mechanism she names):

  • Customer side: specificity lets buyers understand what you do fast, which shortens the sales cycle
  • Delivery side: your team (or future you) gets a repeatable way to deliver — you stop "reinventing the business every time someone asks for something new"
  • The unstated engineering version: every custom request is a fork in the codebase. 172 variations is 172 branches nobody merged.

The honest caveat:

  • This is a book ad. The transcript states the *what* and the *outcome*, not the *how* — no method for picking which three offers, no pricing ladder, no migration path off legacy custom work. "I break down exactly how to do this in my new book" is the payoff.
  • No repos, no tools, no URLs mentioned in the video.

Tools & links:

  • None referenced in the transcript — the only pointer is to Codie Sanchez's new book via Contrarian Thinking

Why it matters for you: A side hustle dies from scope creep before it dies from lack of demand — pick three offers you can deliver on repeat and refuse everything else, because you don't have a team to absorb the custom work.
