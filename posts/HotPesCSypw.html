The Interview Hack: Choose a Problem They Can't Solve

A Life Engineered · 2026-07-06 · 15,173 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Frame interview stories around problems that stump the interviewer.

A concrete tactic for your next interview intro that makes senior interviewers instantly peg you as clever.

What it is: A framing trick for interview intros — lead with a hard problem you solved, not the how, so the interviewer's brain fails to solve it on the spot and defaults to being impressed.

How it works:

  • Interviewers are wired to auto-solve any problem they hear — decades of habit, they can't turn it off.
  • In your intro, name a genuinely hard subproblem and say "I solved this" — state the problem, withhold the solution.
  • When the interviewer can't immediately produce even a high-level answer, their gut read flips to "this person is sharp."
  • Own your specific contribution: use "I" for the subproblem you personally cracked; "we" is fine for the big multi-team initiative, but zoom in on your piece.
  • The leverage is the gap — a problem that's easy to describe but hard to solve does the selling for you.

Tools & links:

  • No external tools, repos, or links mentioned in this clip.

Why it matters for you: The prep is picking your stories deliberately — mine your past work for a genuinely hard subproblem you owned, and lead with the problem, not the walkthrough.

The message: Interviews are read on impression as much as substance; engineer the impression by choosing what stumps the room.
