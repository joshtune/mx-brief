The Simplest $10K+/M Business You've Never Heard Of

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-22 · 57,662 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Free whiteboards in restaurants, sold as ad space

A zero-capital, low-time side hustle where a single placed board reportedly nets $3,000/year in ad sales — and the real money is in the upsell behind it.

What it is: Rachel and her partner give restaurants a free dry-erase "specials" board for the host stand, then sell printed ad slots around the edges to local businesses for ~$3,600/year per board. Six months in, they're at $10K/month, peaking at $15K.

The idea in one sentence: Put ads where people are *already* looking — the specials board everyone scans while deciding what to order — instead of building new attention (table plexiglass, bar banners, TV screens with install + software).

How it works:

  • Spot the pattern: most restaurants have a chalkboard/sandwich board by the host stand for happy hour and appetizer specials. It has no ads on it.
  • Swap chalk for dry erase — liquid chalk markers on a whiteboard — so the restaurant can still update specials daily while the ads stay put.
  • Ads are printed directly onto the board, not stickers or overlays. Design the layout, send the file to a printer, printer produces it as-is.
  • Give the board to the restaurant free. That's the pitch — free signage, better-looking than what they have, zero cost.
  • Sell the surrounding slots to local businesses at $3,600/year each. One board placed ≈ $3,000/month in the aggregate math they describe.
  • Startup cost: $0, minimal ongoing time — "once you set it up, it's just doing its thing."
  • Neither side of the sale is hard: finding restaurants that'll take a free board is easy, and finding local businesses that want cheap local eyeballs is easy.
  • The actual genius (per Chris): the board is a foot-in-the-door lead-gen asset for their existing marketing agency. The upsell is to a far more profitable service that has nothing to do with restaurants or local advertising. The board is the wedge, not the business.
  • Downside risk is close to nil: "Worst case, you don't sell any. You just put it in a different restaurant."
