What it is: A positioning-and-offer video: how one person becomes an "AI consultant/partner" using Claude Code as the delivery engine, and how to pick projects clients will actually pay for.

The core reframe:

  • Don't sell yourself as an "AI builder" or "the automation guy" — those sell *features*
  • Sell as an AI partner — partners sell *outcomes*, and outcomes command higher prices
  • Nate's credibility claim: scaled an AI agency past $100k/mo, exited, now runs a 375+ member free community; clients spanned real estate, HVAC, coaching, marketing agencies

Why Claude Code specifically:

  • Natural language is the interface — no CS degree, no hand-written code required
  • The actual skill is *describing what you want clearly* + *spotting automation opportunities*
  • Build time collapse: what took him ~2 hours in n8n now takes 20–30 minutes in Claude Code, often less
  • Thesis: barrier to entry keeps dropping, single-operator leverage keeps rising

The three-bucket qualification framework (the actual meat):

Every business is trying to move one of three numbers. If a build doesn't clearly land in one, don't take it — you won't be able to prove value.

  • 1. Get more customers — new leads, booked appointments, conversions from a source. Most growth-focused; where owners' attention and budget already live
  • 2. Make each customer worth more — AOV, LTV, retention, time-between-repeat-purchase, upsell rate. Highest leverage in *mature* businesses, since the math beats new acquisition cost
  • 3. Cut costs — hours per task, error rate, ticket count, time to completion. Same outcome, less labor/rework/waste. Easiest to baseline and attribute ("I saved you 20 hours a week" is provable on a calendar)

Mapping projects to buckets:

  • Lead qualification systems, automated follow-up → *more customers*
  • CRM automation, onboarding flows → *worth more per customer*
  • Internal knowledge assistants, reporting dashboards, ticket sorting → *cut costs*
  • Overlap is fine — onboarding arguably hits buckets 2 and 3; pick the one you can measure
