Tony Hawk on video game royalties and skatepark philanthropy

Hawk lays out the exact deal structure that turned a skateboard brand into a $4.5M royalty check, plus how he's funded nearly 1,000 skateparks since 2002.

What it is: Sam Parr and Shaan Puri interview Tony Hawk about how he made his first million, the economics of skateparks, and why his foundation gives donors 'concrete proof' of where their money goes.

Who he is:

  • Pro skateboarder, best known for the 900 and the *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater* franchise
  • Runs the Tony Hawk Foundation (now The Skatepark Project), started 2002
  • Long-time Charity Water donor; sponsored a well in his daughter's name and visited it in Ethiopia (where Shaan met him ~10 years ago)

How he made money:

  • Video game royalties — took a royalty deal on *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater* instead of a flat fee. When the 4th game shipped and hit top 10, the first three were *still* top 10. Activision slid a $4.5M check across the table. That was his first million.
  • Sponsorships and licensing — his name is a global brand; he got recognized on a mountainside in Ethiopia where nobody knew the Spotify founder
  • Compounding IP — the lesson: back-catalog royalties stack. Four games selling simultaneously beats one big upfront payment.

Skatepark economics (0:03:42):

  • Foundation has contributed to nearly 1,000 skateparks since 2002
  • Pitch to donors: unlike research grants, a skatepark is 'literal concrete proof' of where your money went. You can go skate it.
  • Same playbook as Charity Water: GPS-tracked wells, flow meters, donor site visits. Transparency is the growth hack for nonprofits.

Business lessons:

  • Show the impact — Charity Water and Hawk's foundation both win because donors can see exactly what they funded
  • Take equity/royalties over cash — the Pro Skater deal is the canonical 'bet on yourself' story
  • Give now, not later — Sam admits he's been deferring philanthropy; Hawk started at 34

Tools & links:
