Nate stress-tests Jev, a decision-only AI model, on 12 real use cases.

Jev skips text generation entirely and just outputs decisions — if you're building classification, routing, or real-time filtering automations, this could replace slower/costlier LLM calls in your stack.

What it is:

  • Jev is a new type of AI model that makes decisions but never generates text — no chat, no reasoning tokens, just structured output.
  • Built via a training method called RLCD ("reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions"), announced by one of ChatGPT's co-inventors, detailed on the model creator's blog.

How it works:

  • Three output types only: a yes/no with a confidence score, a choice (pick one category), and a score (e.g. 0–10 scale).
  • Example from the video: a support ticket gets classified as urgent (99% confidence), routed to "technical" team, and scored 1/2 on frustration — all as structured JSON, no generated prose.
  • You configure it by defining the decision criteria/classification rules upfront; it doesn't infer intent the way a chat model does.
  • Claimed 20–200x faster and 40–400x cheaper than typical LLMs for these tasks, since output tokens are free (it's not generating token-by-token text).
  • Limitation: can't write, summarize, find themes, or do deep analysis — decisions only.
  • Limitation: small 64K token context window vs. ~1M for models like Claude/GPT.

Demo use cases shown:

  • A Chrome extension that labels X/Twitter posts in real time as "breaking," "golden nugget," or "AI slop" using Jev on the backend as content streams in.
  • A "Jev trader" bot that re-evaluates Bitcoin price direction every second and places trades in real time, leaning on Jev's speed for rapid-fire decisions.
  • A YouTube comment-processing test (feeding in ~5,000 comments) to test categorization at scale — cut off in the available transcript.
