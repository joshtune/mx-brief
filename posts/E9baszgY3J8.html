The Most Overlooked Side Hustle You Can Start With $0

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-09 · 34,677 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Estate sale company: zero startup cost, cash-positive week one.

It's the rare side hustle with no capital, no tech, no payment-chasing, and a demographic tailwind — you could sign your first client in under two weeks.

What it is: An estate sale liquidation business — you run the sale of a deceased or downsizing person's household contents and take a cut of gross revenue. Chris interviews Braden, who left a corporate territory sales job in Benton, Arkansas (~25k people) and hit six figures a year.

The economics:

  • You keep ~40%, the client gets ~60% of gross
  • You collect all the money at the sale — no invoicing, no chasing payments, no AR risk
  • Any incidental fees get passed straight through to the client
  • Braden's arc: tiny estate → 32,000 sq ft warehouse doing $25,000 per weekend
  • Chris's hook claim: $0 in, ~$6,000 profit in week one

How it works:

  • Form an LLC, post an ad online, book a sale — Braden claims first sale within 2–3 weeks of standing still
  • Startup kit: masking tape, a price gun (Amazon), a phone, and as many folding tables as you can borrow
  • Staff it with friends and family; no payroll on day one
  • POS system is "ideal" but your phone works — Square-style tap-to-pay is enough to start
  • Market size floor: works in any community of 5,000+ people. You do not need a metro

Why demand outstrips supply:

  • Baby boomer aging/retirement wave = structurally growing inventory of estates
  • Trigger events (death, divorce, downsizing, relocation) are exactly when people spend freely and decide fast
  • Almost no one is competing in small markets

The revenue stack (each estate is multiple line items):

  • Sale of the contents (the core cut)
  • The move itself
  • Listing the house for them
  • Braden's real play, teased at the end: the estate sale business is a front — a lead gen machine for a second, much more profitable business almost no one is running. (The transcript cuts off before the reveal; watch the back half.)

How Braden actually got here:

  • Read *The 4-Hour Workweek*, started a 2x10 ft booth in an Arkansas antique mall to offload childhood collectibles
  • Buddy dragged him yard-saling at 5am Saturdays — mapped routes, bought vintage tools, Star Wars, vintage clothing, flipped it in the booth for a few hundred a month
  • Winter killed yard sales, so he moved upstream to estate sales — then realized he should be *running* them, not shopping them

  • Bluevine small business AI study — the study referenced in the read
  • Price gun (generic, Amazon) + masking tape + borrowed tables — literally the full capex
  • Phone-based POS in place of dedicated hardware

Why it matters for you: This is the anti-SaaS side hustle — no build phase, no users to acquire, no runway, and you find out in one weekend whether it works; the interesting engineering angle is that nobody in these markets has software for scheduling, pricing, or inventory, which is exactly the lead-gen wedge Braden is hinting at.
