5 Types of AI Software Factories Clearly Explained

Cole Medin · 2026-11-16 · 16,475 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Five AI software factory types, with tradeoffs

Cole gives you a vocabulary for how much autonomy to hand your agents, which is the framing to steal for deciding what mx-workflow should automate and what should stay gated.

What it is: A taxonomy of five AI software factory setups (dark, fleet, PR, release, self-improving), with pros and cons for each, aimed at finding the right balance of control and autonomy.

How it works:

  • Factory defined: The next level of autonomy for coding agents. You stop guiding the agent ticket by ticket and let it handle a *batch* of issues.
  • Human stays in the loop: You plan up front with the agent, including dependency mapping. The agent then builds in parallel through build loops and deploys to a staging environment. You review the batch there.
  • Core thesis: Planning up front and validation at the end are the bottlenecks now. Batch both, and let the agent run autonomously in the middle.
  • Dark factory (covered in the excerpt): Send in a loosely structured request, and the system plans, builds, reviews and merges to prod with no human. Cole thinks we will probably never get there. You will never communicate intent perfectly every time, and a miscommunicated intent ships a bad implementation straight to prod. A smarter model doesn't fix this, because the failure is in the human-to-agent intent handoff. He says he tried it himself by running a public experiment, but the excerpt cuts off before the details.
  • Fleet factory: Named in the intro. He says it is the exception where you might not sign off before production. The excerpt doesn't cover details.
  • PR factory: Named only. Details not in the excerpt.
  • Release factory: This is the setup he runs for most of his own work. He says releases are scoped small, so it is *not* a return to waterfall (the common misconception).
  • Self-improving factory: Named only. Details not in the excerpt.
  • Caveat: The transcript provided is truncated after the dark factory section, so fleet, PR, release and self-improving specifics are not summarized here.

Why it matters for you:

  • Batch the work: Move mx-workflow from per-ticket commands (`/mx:plan`, `/mx:implement`) toward batch runs. That means one planning pass with dependency mapping, parallel builds, and a single staging review.
  • Closest existing pieces: `/mx:loop` (sequential Linear tickets) and `/mx:build-with-agent-team` are already near this model.
  • Keep the human gates: `/mx:qa`, `/mx:reject` and `/mx:ratchet` fit his "validation at the end" bottleneck. Treat them as the sign-off step and don't let anything auto-merge to prod.
  • Intent capture: The dark-factory failure mode argues for investing in `/mx:prd` and `/mx:plan` quality.
