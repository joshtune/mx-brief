Seven rules for outsourcing without torching your budget.

If your side hustle depends on contractors or an agency building the thing you can't build yourself, this is the checklist that decides whether you get a product or a $250k lesson.

What it is: A post-mortem on a failed outsourced dev project — Codie burned ~$250-270k over 3-4 months on an agency and walked away with a broken product — turned into seven operating rules for hiring outside teams.

The failure pattern:

  • She was building a tech product but wasn't a tech company
  • Took the standard advice: "outsource what you're not good at"
  • Result after 3-4 months: failed promises, terrible product, and "hiding the ball" (the agency obscuring real status)
  • Root cause isn't offshore vs. onshore — it's that nobody inside the company owned the outcome

The seven rules:

  • Never outsource the lead. Outsource the team, but the leader is your direct report. No internal owner = no skin in the game = no accountability when it breaks.
  • Be diabolical on readouts. Daily standup, daily ship list, weekly loom. "Receipts or it didn't happen."
  • Evaluate the true cost. Offshore or onshore doesn't matter — do the math before you sign the SOW.
  • Interrogate their clients first. Talk to a handful of the agency's actual clients and ask the uncomfortable ones: What shipped on time and on budget? How were they managed? What did they *actually* spend vs. the quote?
  • Milestone-based contracts only. Never a lump sum. You need an exit at every checkpoint so you can walk the moment benchmarks are missed.
  • Grade them weekly, 1-10. Tie fulfillment to hitting at least an 8. The agency does *not* grade itself — put someone inside your company on grading.
  • Assume you'll micromanage. Hyper-specific briefs, constant oversight. You're one of many projects on their board, not the only one.

The engineering read:

  • Rules 2, 5, and 6 are just CI/CD discipline applied to a vendor: short feedback loops, gated releases, objective pass criteria
  • "Hiding the ball" is what happens when the only status signal is self-reported — the daily ship list is your build log
  • Milestone contracts are the business equivalent of not merging to main until tests pass
