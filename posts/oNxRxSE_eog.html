I Coached a Ex-Microsoft Manager for His Netflix Interview

Live coaching of a manager's failing behavioral interview stories.

Watch a 19-year veteran get torn down and rebuilt on the exact behavioral-interview mistakes that keep senior engineers and managers from landing the offer.

What it is: A real-time mock-interview teardown where ex-Amazon bar raiser Steve Wynn (principal, ~1,000 interviews) coaches John — a laid-off 19-year Microsoft engineering manager — for an EM role at Netflix.

The core message: Your experience is rarely the problem. How you *tell* the story is. John has the tenure but his answers bury the leadership signal interviewers are actually scoring.

The three fixes Steve targets:

  • Cut the corporate backstory — John opens with a 3-part career history (on-prem → cloud → management). It eats airtime and crowds out the leadership story. Lead with the leadership, not the résumé timeline.
  • Own mistakes to build status, not shrink it — frame failures as decisions you led and corrected, so accountability raises your bar-rating instead of lowering it.
  • Show technical depth, don't talk around it — managers especially tend to abstract away the engineering; name the hard technical calls explicitly.

What to prepare (the instructional takeaways):

  • Have a single strongest story and make it a clean turnaround narrative: what was broken → conditions that caused it → what *you* did → measurable outcome.
  • Add context interviewers always probe: did you inherit the team or build it? Promoted in or brought in? John fumbled this — answer it crisply up front.
  • Don't lean on environmental noise (security mandates like Microsoft's SFI, AI competition, reorgs) as the story — that's backdrop. The story is your decisions inside that noise.
  • Deliver incremental wins, not just the 18-month grand plan — and say so in the interview, because it shows stakeholder judgment.
