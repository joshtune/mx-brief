Why Jevons Paradox matters today

My First Million · 2026-07-26 · 89,507 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Efficiency gains increase total consumption, not decrease it.

It's a 60-second mental model for why cheaper AI inference means more AI spend, not less — useful for sizing any market you're building into.

What it is: A short clip (no featured business, no guest, no revenue breakdown) where Shaan and Sam explain the Jevons paradox — the 1800s observation that making a resource cheaper to use increases total demand for it.

The story as told:

  • William Stanley Jevons, British economist, 1800s.
  • Watches the steam engine make coal use dramatically more efficient.
  • Conventional wisdom at the time: there's a fixed amount of work to do, so efficiency = we burn less coal.
  • Jevons' call: wrong — everyone will want a steam engine, so Britain will burn *way more* coal and face a shortage.
  • He was right. Efficiency lowered the cost per unit of work, which expanded the set of things worth doing with coal.

The mechanism (why it holds):

  • Efficiency ↓ cost per unit → ↑ number of economically viable use cases → ↑ total consumption.
  • Only fails when demand is genuinely capped (you can't eat two dinners). Demand for compute, energy, and "work done" is not capped.

Where this bites today:

  • Cheaper inference doesn't shrink AI spend — it unlocks workloads that were uneconomic at 10x the price. Hence data center and power buildout alongside falling per-token cost.
  • Same pattern for anything you're pricing: a 10x cost drop in your product usually grows the market rather than cannibalizing your revenue.
