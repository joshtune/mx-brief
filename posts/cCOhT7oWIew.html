Avoid These 7 Bad Habits If You Want To Own A Business

Codie Sanchez · 2026-10-11 · 48,168 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Seven owner habits that quietly drain your income

If your side hustle lives in stolen evening hours, protecting deep-work blocks and keeping the few people who help you is the difference between momentum and burnout.

What it is: A listicle of seven owner-sabotaging habits, each paired with a borrowed management framework. The transcript available covers habits 1 and 2 plus most of the 'three C's' fix. Habits 3–7 were cut off, so they're not covered here.

How it works:

  • Habit 1, Death by paper cuts:
  • Small requests ("can I pick your brain?") cost far more than their length. A UC Irvine study found a 23-minute average to refocus after an interruption.
  • The secret: Paul Graham's *manager schedule vs. maker schedule*. Managers work in ~1-hour slots. Makers need ~4-hour uninterrupted blocks.
  • The fix: Label your calendar blocks by mode (manager or maker) and don't mix them.
  • Habit 2, Underappreciating your most valuable asset (your people):
  • Wins become expected, extra effort becomes normal, and good people leave.
  • Herzberg's two-factor theory splits motivation into hygiene factors (salary, policies, job security) and motivators (achievement, responsibility, recognition). Fixing hygiene stops unhappiness but doesn't create engagement. Ping-pong tables and nap pods don't move the needle.
  • The 3 C's of culture:
  • Credit: Give specific, named, individual praise. Cited stat: people who get high-quality recognition are 45% less likely to leave within 2 years. "You're all doing great" in Slack counts for nothing.
  • Cause: A mission bigger than the task. Hers: create 100 million owners.
  • Calculated bets: Celebrate well-reasoned, well-executed experiments that fail. Punishing them kills initiative.
