Set up an AI chief of staff for email and calendar

The message to engineers is to hand your inbox, calendar and follow-up busywork to an AI agent so you can spend your time on the thinking only you can do, and this video walks through the setup.

What it is: A guided setup session where Peter Yang shows the host how to build an AI "chief of staff" that triages email and calendar twice a day. The transcript I have cuts off early, so this covers only the first part of the setup.

The message to engineers:

  • Busywork (reading email, drafting replies, scheduling) eats the time you need for thinking work.
  • Hand the busywork to AI and partner with it on the rest.
  • Cold open: "most of the people around you are waiting on something only you can give", and the work that slips is the work only you can do. Delegating the rest protects that work.
  • The host quit tech 2.5 years ago and stopped writing code. His approach is to learn from people already on the cutting edge instead of catching up the slow way.
  • Side note: one guest has an "engineering manager bot" that manages other engineer bots to build stuff. Agent orchestration is where this is heading.

What to prepare for:

  • Email and calendar are becoming agent-managed surfaces. Peter already runs his entirely through AI.
  • Voice-dictating instructions to the agent replaces typing.
  • Agents are moving from reactive to proactive. His bot was already prepping the host for the podcast unprompted.
  • The two universal AI uses Peter has found are a chief of staff (triage) and a long-term advisor (business and life advice). The advisor setup is not in the portion I have.

How it works (the chief-of-staff setup):

  • Install Claude Code and a chat-based agent (the transcript spells it "Grok Bot"; likely a transcription error, so I can't confirm the real name).
  • Connect the agent to your Gmail and calendar.
  • Schedule it to scan all inboxes at 7:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. and return a numbered list of items needing action.
  • For anything needing a reply, have it draft first. You edit, then tell it to send.
  • Also have it review your sent mail. If someone hasn't replied in about 5 days, it reminds you to follow up, or follows up for you.
  • Start with a 30-day backfill. Ask it to surface the most important emails and follow-ups (why each matters, when to respond, thread link) and to list subscriptions you should cancel.

Copy-paste prompt (from Peter's course, step 2):

  • "Review my email activity over the past 30 days and return most important emails or follow-ups. For each explain why it matters, when I should respond and link to the thread. Subscriptions or recurring emails I should consider canceling."

Caveats:

  • Giving an agent send access to your inbox is a real risk. Peter's draft-first, approve-then-send flow is the safeguard, so don't skip it.
