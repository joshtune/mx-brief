The $3 billion business built on nursery rhymes

My First Million · 2026-09-26 · 50,751 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
How Moonbug turned nursery-rhyme YouTube channels into a $3B exit.

A blueprint for buying undervalued content channels, bundling them, and flipping for a 10x return — with the exact dollar figures at every step.

What it is: The story of Moonbug Entertainment — founded by Rene Rechtman — which acquired three independent kids' YouTube channels and sold the combined business for roughly $3 billion.

How it works:

  • Rechtman noticed that of the top 100 most-viewed children's channels online, none were owned by major studios — a gap between attention and ownership.
  • Cocomelon is the single most-viewed YouTube channel in the world.
  • Moonbug raised capital (transcript cites both ~$150M and ~$400M in total equity + debt across the deals — treat as directional, not precise) to fund three acquisitions:
  • Cocomelon — bought for $103 million
  • Blippi — bought for $70 million
  • Little Baby Bum — bought for $65 million
  • Total spend on the three channels: ~$238 million in acquisitions, funded by ~$400M raised in equity and debt.
  • The combined entity sold for $3 billion — roughly a 10x return on invested capital.

Payouts on exit:

  • Rene Rechtman (founder): $300 million
  • Co-founder: $300 million
  • Head of M&A: $60 million
  • CFO: $20–30 million

Why it matters for you: A clean case study in arbitrage — find high-attention, low-institutional-ownership assets, roll them up fast, and exit before the market catches on.
