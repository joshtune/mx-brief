I Built a Monster PC Just to Run AI Locally

Alex Ziskind · 2026-08-28 · 30,429 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Dual RTX Pro 6000 build runs Qwen3 235B locally.

This is the x86 alternative to a Mac Studio for local inference — two RTX Pro 6000s hit 68 tok/s on a 142 GB model, but the price and power draw are in a different universe from any Mac mini.

What it is: A from-scratch workstation build with two NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 GPUs, purpose-built to run large LLMs locally.

The hardware (what's confirmed on camera):

  • 2× NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 — Blackwell-generation workstation cards, 96 GB VRAM each, ~192 GB pooled
  • Motherboard with no video out — Alex hits this mid-build; you need a GPU installed just to POST and see BIOS
  • A power supply he openly describes as "terrified" — two of these cards is a 1600W-class PSU conversation
  • Case chosen for airflow and GPU clearance; he calls the internal layout "really smartly designed"
  • Full build sequence on camera: CPU seating, motherboard mount, first GPU, then the second

The payoff benchmark:

  • Model: Qwen3 235B, 142 GB on disk
  • Generation speed: 68 tokens/second
  • That's a frontier-class open model running entirely in VRAM — no offload to system RAM, which is exactly why it stays fast

Cost reality vs. Apple silicon:

  • RTX Pro 6000 street price runs roughly $8–10K per card — the GPUs alone are ~$16–20K before CPU, board, RAM, PSU, or case
  • A maxed M3 Ultra Mac Studio (512 GB unified memory) lands around $9–10K and holds bigger models than 192 GB of VRAM, but generates noticeably slower on dense models
  • A Mac mini M4 Pro (64 GB) is ~$2K and tops out around 32B-class quantized models — a different weight class entirely, not a competitor to this box
  • Practical read: this build is ~5–10× a well-specced Mac mini and buys you speed, not just capacity

Power and noise — the part that matters for an office:

  • He warns viewers off the fans on camera; this is an audible machine under load
  • Two 600W-class cards plus CPU means real wall draw and real heat, unlike a Mac mini's ~150W ceiling
