Natural money-makers versus founders: how they think

It profiles how deal-brokers like Carl Icahn and trader Nick Kakonus actually made their money, and what separates them from ordinary founders.

What it is: Episode 872. Sam Parr pitches the "natural money maker", a different animal from a great founder or CEO, and walks through examples (Icahn, a trader named Nick Kakonus, and his friend Sulie). The transcript cuts off mid-story, so only trait #1 and the start of the trader story are covered. The other five traits aren't in the source.

The concept:

  • Natural money makers do deals, brokering, and inserting themselves into situations.
  • They tend to build privately, not publicly.
  • They've built multiple businesses worth $100M to billions, across unrelated fields.
  • Different from founders: they have all the vision and recruiting skill, plus they're colder, more logical, and more rational about money.
  • Sam says he's a decent entrepreneur, not a natural money maker.
  • Ted Turner (CNN) is the contrast case: vision, recruiting, product sense, but not a natural money maker.

Signature markers:

  • High hit rate: succeed, succeed, fail, succeed, instead of the usual fail, fail, fail, finally win.
  • Instinct over explanation: they can't articulate why, they just sniff out where money is made or lost ("a great nose").
  • Multi-category wins: no single super-skill explains it. The skill is making money.

Trait 1: Unemotional about business

  • They feel anxiety like everyone else but use emotion as fuel, not a steering wheel.
  • Passion is how founders recruit. Money-makers lean on numbers instead.

Profile 1: Carl Icahn (1980s tycoon, hedge fund/PE)

  • Bought a shipping company, spent about 2 weeks at the office, and saw 12 floors of people whose jobs he couldn't identify.
  • Fired all of them the same day.
  • Months later he asked the team whether they'd noticed the loss. They hadn't, and the business wasn't hurt.
  • Money move: cut cost without sentiment, because the numbers said to.

Profile 2: Nick Kakonus (Sam's guest, episode not yet out)

  • Studied philosophy but liked math. Turned down bank offers (e.g. JP Morgan) because he wanted to be a trader, running his own book.
  • Swings of roughly $250K-$300K per day, win or lose, on a small capital base (about $600K-$1M).
  • Made tens of millions as a trader.
  • Best year: his company made $100M profit off a single arbitrage he found.
  • The arbitrage involved ETFs getting popular. The transcript ends before the mechanism is explained, so it's unknown.

Profile 3: "Sulie" (Sam's friend)

  • Cited as the archetype: high hit rate, instinct-driven, can't explain it. No business details given.
