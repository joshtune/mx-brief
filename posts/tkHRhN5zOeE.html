A crossing guard is making $14,000 a month mailing out a physical newsletter

My First Million · 2026-07-16 · 7,249 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Crossing guard earns $14K/month from a physical newsletter.

A one-person, near-zero-tech business proving that a quirky paid product beats a polished one — pure signal for anyone tempted to over-engineer a side project.

What it is: Christine, a crossing guard, mails out a monthly physical (paper) newsletter of her on-the-job musings and pulls in ~$14,000/month.

How it works:

  • Product: A monthly printed newsletter — she writes about things she experiences as a crossing guard. Pure entertainment, silly, low-stakes.
  • Format is the moat: It's physical mail, not email. Deliberately janky — looks like it's printed on paper stapled together in a manila folder. The low production value is the appeal, not a flaw.
  • Revenue: Tens of thousands of readers/dollars a month paying for a mailed physical product. $14K/month headline figure from the WSJ piece.
  • Why it sells: Novelty + tangibility. People are drawn to quirky, physical, human artifacts in an all-digital world — the opposite of another Substack in your inbox.
  • Shaan's thesis: "The jankier, the better." He called this 2 years ago — hand-made, low-fi physical products can be genuinely great businesses.

Tools & links:

  • Source: WSJ — "The Crossing Guard Making $14,000 a Month Mailing Out Her Musings From Her Job" — original article that sparked the segment (no specific business/site named in transcript).

Why it matters for you: The lesson is a builder's counter-intuition — polish and tech aren't the product; a differentiated, tangible format with personality is. Ship the janky thing.
