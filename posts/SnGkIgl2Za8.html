Sometimes People Turn You Into The Villain

Codie Sanchez · 2026-07-14 · 17,187 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Why people cast you as the villain, and not defending yourself.

Every deal, partnership, or client that goes sideways spawns a version of the story where you're the bad guy — and how you handle that reputation hit determines whether people keep doing business with you.

What it is: A short thought-leadership riff on reputation, boundaries, and refusing to litigate your own character when someone smears you.

How it works:

  • People turn you into the villain because if you're the problem, they don't have to reflect or feel guilty — it's cheaper for their conscience than owning their part.
  • Watch how they *modify the story*: your boundary becomes "arrogance," your quiet becomes "hostility," your distance becomes "betrayal."
  • Codie's example: she posted that boundaries have gone too far and sometimes you show up for people even when it's inconvenient — by morning she was the villain to people who'd never met her.
  • The move: don't write the defense thread. Don't correct the record.
  • The deeper cut for operators — "every deal that goes sideways has two stories, and the person who behaved worst usually tells theirs the loudest."
  • Be the person who knows their own character and doesn't need to explain it; the people who actually know you respect you *more* for the silence.
