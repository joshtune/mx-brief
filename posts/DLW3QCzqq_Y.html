I Hate QuickBooks So Much I Built My Own (Week One Results)

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-11-26 · 7,568 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Chris launches a QuickBooks rival; shares launch-week numbers

It's a full autopsy of a non-technical founder testing and launching a software side hustle with no audience and about $100, including the numbers and the mistakes.

What it is: Chris Koerner's launch-week teardown of Lazybooks (lazybooks.com), a QuickBooks alternative built for people who run many small businesses. The point of the episode is a repeatable playbook for testing an idea cheaply.

The idea:

  • Pain point: Chris runs a pile of businesses, and QuickBooks bills each entity separately. Each one gets its own subscription, login, bank connection and monthly bill.
  • Thesis: "Every entity you have on QuickBooks is a separate bill." That single fact is the whole business.
  • Pricing gripes: On Aug 1 QuickBooks raised prices again. The middle plan went from $115 to $140 a month, the top plan from $275 to $340, and the starter plan is now $38 per company.
  • The math: Three small side-hustle LLCs on the cheapest plan cost 3 × $38 = $114 a month, or about $1,400 a year.
  • Other complaints: Bank connections break every couple of weeks, the reports are bloated when he only wants a P&L, and the AI features are "total garbage".
  • Target user: Someone who doesn't speak accountant (no chart of accounts, debits or credits) and just wants to know whether they made or lost money this month.

How it was carried out:

  • Domain bet: About 15 months ago he paid $800 for the domain on a hunch, then recorded the validation process. That episode got about 95K views and listens.
  • AI as devil's advocate: In Aug 2025 he gave four AI tools the same job. They read hundreds of real reviews and complaints and were supposed to tell him he was wrong. The transcript cuts off here, so the rest of the validation steps aren't covered.
  • Team split: Chris is non-technical and has never written code, and he got C's in accounting. His partner Justin, who also knows nothing about accounting software, wrote the product by hand ("not vibe coded").
  • Offer: 60-day free trial with a card up front.

Week-one numbers:

  • Signups: 663 in the first 8 days.
  • Cards on file: About 414 entered a credit card.
  • Revenue: $0 so far, because everyone is in the free trial. He says he's thrilled about that.
  • Day-one breakage and wasted spend: He promises to cover both later in the episode.

Promised in the rest of the video (not in the excerpt):

  • The cheapest test: A test that costs nothing and "sits in your phone right now".
  • No-audience launch: How to test and launch an idea with no audience and about $100.
  • Finding your fact: How to find the one "separate bill" style fact for your own idea.

Tools & links:

  • Lazybooks — Chris's QuickBooks alternative, free for 60 days
  • TKO Pod newsletter — Chris's newsletter

Caveats:

  • Bias: It's his own product, so treat the framing as marketing, though he says he's trying to keep it from being an ad.
  • Not proven yet: $0 revenue means no conversion or churn data. The 414 cards are trial signups, not paying customers.
  • Not a side hustle for most: He has a developer partner and a large existing audience, so "no audience" is a claim to test.

Why it matters for you: The pattern to steal is to find a product that bills per entity, per seat or per company, pair a non-technical idea person with a real builder, validate with AI-mined complaints before building, and launch with a free-trial-plus-card funnel.
