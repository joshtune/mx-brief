Howard Marks: The S&P 500 isn’t a good bet right now

Howard Marks says S&P valuations predict weak returns

It's not a business teardown, so you won't get a 'how they made money' list, but it's a quick valuation sanity check from a veteran investor before you pour money into index funds.

What it is: A short Howard Marks clip arguing that buying the S&P 500 at today's valuation has historically meant weak 10-year returns. The excerpt has no business-building content.

How it works (the argument):

  • JP Morgan published a scatter chart around the end of 2024. It plots the S&P 500 P/E at purchase against the annualized return over the next 10 years.
  • The correlation is negative: the higher the P/E you pay, the lower the return you should expect.
  • Historically, buying at a P/E of about 23 gave an annualized 10-year return between -2% and +2%, with no exceptions.
  • Marks says the S&P is now at about 24-25 because prices have risen, so it's at or above that danger zone.
  • Side point: the S&P has averaged about 10% a year for 100 years, but the annual return is almost never between 8% and 12%. The average is a statistical artifact, not what a typical year delivers.
