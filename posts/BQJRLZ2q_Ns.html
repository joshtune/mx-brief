What it is: Nate walks through client systems built on Claude by his community members, with prices and sales pitches. The title says 3 and the transcript says 4, and the transcript is truncated after system two, so systems 3 and 4 (including the biggest seller) aren't covered here.

How it works:

  • System 1: Voice note to document (built by Jodie for a construction company, £4,500, about $6,000)
  • A foreman sends a WhatsApp voice note instead of typing up site registers, risk assessments and plant registers.
  • The note is transcribed and matched to the job record for that project.
  • It becomes a branded PDF, saved automatically to the company's SharePoint and sent to whoever needs it.
  • A human still reviews and signs off. Everything is a draft first.
  • Pitch: lead with time and risk. Ask how many hours a week foremen spend on paperwork. 5 foremen × 5 hours = 25 paid hours a week. Paperwork sitting days behind the work is also a safety and compliance risk.
  • Why it sells: no change management, since everyone already knows how to send a voice note.
  • Where to sell it: trades where people aren't at a desk (construction, plumbing, electrical). Ask which document the team hates most, automate just that one, then expand.
  • System 2: Intake and follow-up funnel (built by Luis for a semi-retired doctor, $2,200, later a $6,000 repeat job)
  • Part 1 writes weekly social posts across platforms in the owner's voice.
  • Part 2 starts when someone submits an interest form from a post. The lead goes into the CRM and gets a text and email with a payment link.
  • After payment, intake forms and waivers go out with reminders until they're signed.
  • Then the lead gets a booking link with Zoom confirmation and follow-up reminders.
  • Result: saved the client about 15 hours a week, and the client hired Luis again.
  • Pitch: tie it to revenue. Ask how many people filled out the form last month and how many paid. If 40 filled it out and 10 paid, the system chases the other 30, and a few extra clients cover the fee.
  • Ideal buyer: a business that runs on bookings, with leads arriving via form or phone call (the third criterion is cut off in the transcript).
  • Selling framing: count hidden costs for the owner, not just the price. That means time cost, risk and change management.
  • Pricing: this video is about positioning, not pricing. Nate points to a separate value-based pricing video.
