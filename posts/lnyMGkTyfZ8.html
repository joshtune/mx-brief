The $40K/month business built for influencers

My First Million · 2026-11-23 · 37,766 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Fake private jet set rented to influencers hourly

It's a 2-minute teardown of a low-tech, high-margin rental business, but the clip gives only rough numbers and no founder details.

What it is: LA Private Jet Studio, a fake private jet interior built like a movie set in Los Angeles. It's rented by the hour to influencers who want photos that look like they're on a private jet.

How it works:

  • The asset: A fake private jet cabin in LA, built like a movie set. It never flies.
  • The customer: Influencers and content creators who want 'flying private' photos without the flight.
  • Pricing: About $70/hour.
  • Utilization: The hosts say it's booked about 12 hours a day. This is an estimate, not a verified number.
  • Revenue: The hosts guess $20K–$40K/month. $70 × 12 hrs × 30 days is about $25K, so the guess is plausible.
  • Margins: The hosts guess 80% or higher. They don't have the real costs, and rent, cleaning, insurance and platform fees aren't covered.
  • Build cost and speed: One host says you could hire people from the movie business to build it over a weekend. No cost figure is given. They say you then own it for years.
  • Distribution: It's listed on a studio-rental marketplace, so there's no custom customer acquisition. The transcript says 'Pure Space', which is almost certainly Peerspace.

What the clip doesn't tell you:

  • Who the founder is.
  • The build cost.
  • The lease or rent.
  • The marketplace's take rate.
  • Any figures verified by the owner.
  • Everything beyond the numbers above is a casual guess from the hosts.

Tools & links:

  • Peerspace — hourly marketplace for photo/film studio rentals, where the jet is listed. The transcript says 'Pure Space', which is likely a transcription error.

Why it matters for you: The playbook is to build one physical set that influencers will pay to shoot in, list it on an existing marketplace, and rent it by the hour. The clip gives no way to check the numbers, so treat them as rough and validate demand before building.
