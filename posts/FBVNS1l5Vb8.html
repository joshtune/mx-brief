How Anthropic ACTUALLY Prompts Fable 5.1

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-09-02 · 59,280 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Anthropic's own docs, distilled into Fable 5.1 prompting tactics.

Nate pulls the concrete prompting changes straight out of Anthropic's Fable 5.1 docs — outcome-first prompts, de-prescriptive skills, and effort-slider discipline — so you stop burning weekly session limits on prompts written for the last model.

What it is: A doc-sourced walkthrough of four Anthropic-recommended tactics for prompting Fable 5.1 more efficiently, with the actual doc quotes shown on screen as evidence.

Tip 1 — Tell it what "done" looks like:

  • Stop handing Fable a task list; hand it the finish line and let it derive the tasks.
  • Four things to state: the outcome, why it matters, what "done" means, and any *real* constraints.
  • Live rewrite in the video: a long multi-task prompt collapsed to one goal — *"create an appealing landing page offering voice agent solutions tailored to our target audience."*
  • Doc quote he cites: Fable 5 "tends to perform better when it understands the intent behind a request. Context lets it connect the task to relevant information rather than inferring intent on its own" — especially for long-running agents spanning multiple work streams.
  • Backed by Boris Cherny (Claude Code's creator), who Nate paraphrases as: give the model an ambitious goal and get out of its way.
  • Also cited: "Claude Fable 5.1 can execute very long tasks without much guidance on methodology, especially when the goal is clear" — clarity of goal is what unlocks the long autonomous runs.

Tip 2 — Refactor your existing prompts and skills:

  • Doc quote: "skills developed for prior models are often too prescriptive for Claude Fable 5 and Fable 5.1."
  • Translation: the do-this-then-this-then-this skill style we were all taught is now a guardrail that slows the model down and costs you tokens.
  • Peter Yang's tweet, shown on screen: run `/claude-api prompt-audit` against your skills — it surfaces redundancies and rules worth deleting for newer models.
  • Important disclaimer he flags: "Your existing Claude Fable 5 prompts should perform well on Claude Fable 5.1 without changes, but a handful of behavioral differences are worth knowing about." The 5.1 doc is an *addendum* to the Fable 5 prompting doc, not a replacement.

Tip 3 — Match effort to the task:

  • Most people pick a model and never touch the effort slider, parking permanently on high.
  • Effort levels available in the UI: low, medium, high, extra, max (plus ultra-level coding modes).
  • This is the direct lever on session/weekly usage limits — the complaint Nate opens the video with.
