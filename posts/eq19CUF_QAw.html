He paid $21M for the Magna Carta

Carlyle co-founder David Rubenstein's wealth and collecting.

A quick look at how one PE founder turned 1980s buyout returns into a fortune big enough to buy original copies of America's founding documents.

What it is: A short segment profiling David Rubenstein, co-founder of private equity giant The Carlyle Group, and the historical artifacts his PE fortune bought.

Who he is:

  • Former lawyer/aide in the Jimmy Carter administration before pivoting to finance
  • Self-described as average IQ, but noticed less-smart people out-earning him — concluded the game was *where* you play, not raw intelligence
  • Started a private equity firm (The Carlyle Group) in the 1980s when leveraged buyouts were "just killing it"

How he made the money:

  • Built Carlyle into one of the largest PE firms in the world — fee-and-carry model on huge buyout funds
  • 1980s PE timing: cheap leverage + outsized returns compounded a personal fortune
  • Now functions as a "white knight of Washington D.C." — philanthropy and patriotic acquisitions

What the fortune bought:

  • One of the last privately owned copies of the Magna Carta — $21M
  • A piece of an original Declaration of Independence
  • A Lincoln-signed Emancipation Proclamation

Why it matters for you: The real lesson isn't the artifacts — it's Rubenstein's insight that picking the right high-leverage arena (PE in the '80s) beats being the smartest person in a low-leverage one.
