Test #1 — McKinsey-level 'State of SMB' deck for a fake brand (Hercules Consulting):

  • Identical prompt to both models; needed branding, target-audience fit, and sourced research
  • Both got the facts and sources right, so the judging was on formatting and storytelling, not accuracy
  • GPT-6 Astra: 35 slides, consistent footer and logo, columns and structure, very branded, but extremely wordy. Great as a send-and-read deliverable, poor as a present-over-it deck
  • Fable 5.1: fewer words, better for presenting in a room, includes images and clickable sources, but lowercase titles, no consistent footer/logo, more 'Canva/Google Slides' than McKinsey
  • Winner: Fable 5.1 on output quality for this one (time and cost breakdown follows in the video)

Behavioral difference Nate flags up front:

  • GPT-6 Astra / Codex asks clarifying questions before starting
  • Fable 5.1 takes the prompt and just runs
  • His take: both are now so smart that agent fan-out and research quality are table stakes; the differentiator is how well they package messy data into a presentable, readable deliverable

How he ran it:

  • Same prompt pasted into both tools per task
  • Judged each on output, wall-clock time, and dollar cost
  • Burned through ~3 Codex subscriptions, ~4 Claude subscriptions, and thousands in usage credits to produce the numbers
