Every time I wanna quit it, I play this..

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-07 · 34,324 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Use a villain, not motivation, as fuel.

The side-hustle killer isn't strategy, it's the month you stop caring — and Codie's fix is a 30-second ritual you can set up tonight.

What it is: A 60-second riff on Codie's anti-quitting hack — replace motivation with spite, aimed at a specific named person.

How it works:

  • When the urge to quit hits, don't hunt for motivation (it's unreliable and externally supplied). Hunt for a villain.
  • Pick someone concrete: the person who said you couldn't, the one blocking you, the one talking behind your back. Specificity is the whole mechanic — "the haters" doesn't work, a face does.
  • Make it physical. She literally printed the person's photo and kept it in her wallet — a tangible trigger she'd bump into during the day, not a vague feeling.
  • Pair it with an anchor song. Hers: the "keep my foot on these necks" New Year's resolution track. Same track, every time — it becomes a conditioned state switch.
  • Then visualize the outcome: them watching your build on Instagram. The goal isn't revenge, it's building something "so big they can't ignore it." Spite is the ignition; the build is the point.
  • Her claim: it outperforms any energy drink or motivational content because it's self-generated and doesn't run out.

Tools & links:

  • No tools, repos, or products mentioned. Zero-cost, zero-setup — a photo, a song, a wallet.

Why it matters for you: Side hustles die in the boring middle where nobody's watching, and a durable internal trigger is worth more than any productivity stack you'll bolt on instead.
