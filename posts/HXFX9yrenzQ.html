What it is: A former Amazon Bar Raiser — the veto-holding interviewer whose job is to reject anyone below the hiring bar — enumerating seven recurring candidate mistakes and how to avoid them.

Note on completeness: No transcript was available for this video, so the seven mistakes can't be listed verbatim. What follows is what the video's framing and the channel's established material tell you.

How it works:

  • The Bar Raiser is a trained, cross-org interviewer at Amazon with veto power over the hire decision, independent of the hiring manager's urgency to fill the role — so their rejection criteria are the ones that actually decide your loop.
  • The channel's core thesis: technical ability is not what sinks most senior candidates. Behavioral storytelling is. Vague ownership ("we did X"), missing metrics, no conflict/failure stories, and answers that don't map to Leadership Principles are the usual killers.
  • Expect the format to be mistake → why interviewers read it as a red flag → the corrected version of the answer.

What to prepare for:

  • Own the "I" in your stories. Bar Raisers probe relentlessly on what *you* did versus your team. If you can't survive three levels of "and what was your specific contribution?", the story is unusable.
  • Quantify everything. Latency, cost, headcount, revenue, incident count. Unmeasured impact reads as unverified impact.
  • Bank 10–12 STAR stories, not 3. A single loop can burn six, and reusing one across interviewers gets flagged in debrief.
  • Have real failure and conflict stories. Candidates who sanitize these lose credibility instantly — the sanitized version *is* the red flag.
  • Depth over breadth. "I architected the whole platform" without being able to explain a specific tradeoff you made is worse than a narrow story you can defend to the bottom.
