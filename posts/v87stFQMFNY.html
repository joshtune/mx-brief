How To Negotiate Top Salaries Without Competing Offers

A Life Engineered · 2026-08-17 · 19,841 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Negotiation expert debunks the competing-offer myth.

Your comp is set by leverage and process, not performance — and you can pull that lever without walking into an interview loop somewhere else.

What it is: An interview with Colin Lernell, a tech salary negotiation specialist who has coached engineers through executives on seven-figure packages, focused on getting paid more *without* a competing offer in hand.

The core claim:

  • Pay has nothing to do with performance. Colin's origin story: at Udacity he built a team and found the *worst* performer earning ~$120K/year more than the best one.
  • The gap wasn't merit — it was negotiation at entry. What you accept on day one compounds for years.
  • "If you don't ask, it's always going to be no." Most engineers simply never ask.

The competing-offer myth:

  • You do *not* need competing offers to negotiate — "even at places like Google, you just have to push back on it."
  • Recruiters optimize for the highest probability of a great outcome with the least time and effort. That incentive is the lever, not a rival offer letter.
  • 90% of candidates fail on questions that are "super simple" — the loss happens in the recruiter screen, not the final round.

The cautionary tale (do not copy this):

  • Colin cold-emailed a CFO he'd met once, said he came in at the wrong level, and asked for $100K/year more in equity. It worked.
  • He explicitly says: don't do that. It worked because he'd already demonstrated value above his level and his VP backed him. It's survivorship bias.
  • "My friend sent an email and got a big increase" is exactly the kind of anecdote that gets people fired or blacklisted. Ask — but ask in a safe way.
  • Those compounding lessons later became a $600K/year increase on a $2M offer.

Stay vs. leave — the diagnostic question:

  • "Do you really trust that your manager values you and knows how to get you to the next level?"
  • If no, internal negotiation has a ceiling and the answer is an external move.

Why it matters for you: The message to engineers is that comp is a separate skill from engineering — prepare for negotiation as its own discipline (know your level, know market data, rehearse the recruiter screen), and stop waiting for an outside offer to give you permission to ask.
