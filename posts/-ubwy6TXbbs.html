The Simplest $10K/M Business You've Never Heard Of

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-22 · 33,557 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Selling ad space on restaurant whiteboards for $10K/month.

It's a genuine $0-startup side hustle with recurring revenue and near-zero ongoing time — the closest thing to passive income you can bootstrap on weekends.

What it is: Place a whiteboard (or ad board) inside a local restaurant or high-traffic business, sell the ad slots on it to other local businesses, and collect recurring monthly payments. The guest interviewed is doing $10K–$15K/month after six months.

How it works:

  • Get the placement. Approach a restaurant or local venue and ask to hang a board. The venue costs you nothing — you're bringing them free wall decor and, typically, a free slot or cross-promotion.
  • Sell the slots. Each board is subdivided into ad slots sold to local businesses. One board reportedly generates ~$3,000/month in slot revenue.
  • Design the creative yourself. Ads are made in Canva or ChatGPT's image generation (the transcript's "Chad GBT" / "GPT image 2"). No designer needed — the guest notes image gen quality is now good enough to ship as-is.
  • Print and install. Send the design straight to a local printer, print as-is, mount it on the board. That's the whole fulfillment loop.
  • Startup cost: $0. Printing is paid for out of the ad revenue you've already sold. Time investment is front-loaded — once a board is placed and slots are sold, it "just does its thing."
  • Scale by adding boards. Revenue is roughly linear in board count: ~$3K/board × N locations. Four boards ≈ $12K/month.
  • Failure mode is cheap. Worst case on a bad placement: you don't sell slots and you move the board to a different restaurant. No inventory, no lease, no sunk cost.

Tools & links:

  • Canva — design the ad creative for each slot
  • ChatGPT — image generation for ad art ("GPT image 2"); good enough to send to the printer unedited
  • Any local print shop — prints the design as-is; no special vendor mentioned

The actual insight: Whiteboards have existed forever. The business isn't the board — it's being the person who packages *local attention* into a recurring monthly product. Chris's framing: "We're still early for just about everything. If you try to convince yourself that we're not, you're probably just thinking up excuses to not start."

Why it matters for you: If you want a side hustle that doesn't compete for the same brain you use to write code — no infra, no on-call, no shipping deadlines — this is a $0-downside experiment where one afternoon of sales calls buys you a $3K/month recurring line.
