Founders pitch live; Hot Plate's $100M food marketplace unpacked.

Rishi Talati built Hot Plate into a profitable, $100M-GMV storefront layer for Instagram food sellers on just $4M raised — a clean case study in monetizing a niche Shopify refused to serve.

What it is: Episode 854 of MFM's "Shoot Your Shot" segment — three founders sit down, drop their juiciest revenue number, and get live advice from Sam Parr and Shaan Puri. The transcript covers the first segment in full: Rishi Talati, co-founder of Hot Plate.

The featured business — Hot Plate:

  • E-commerce platform that lets anyone sell food online — pop-up bakers, ice cream makers, cottage-food operators, drop-based chefs.
  • Positioned as "Airbnb for food" (Shaan's framing); Rishi's own framing is the link-in-bio storefront for food creators.
  • Powered $100M+ in sales for independent food entrepreneurs.
  • Most of that $100M came in the last two years — the growth is recent and steep, not a decade of accumulation.

The numbers (what he actually said):

  • $100M+ GMV processed for sellers.
  • Hundreds of thousands of dollars in revenue per month (i.e. low-to-mid eight figures annualized run rate is *not* the claim — this is a few million ARR).
  • $4M+ total raised.
  • Profitable for ~the last year — running the business profitably on venture money still in the bank.
  • Sellers on the platform range from tiny pop-ups to six- and seven-figure businesses doing drops.

How they make money / how the model works:

  • Sellers with an existing Instagram audience put a Hot Plate link in bio.
  • Hot Plate handles pre-orders and drops — the exact use case Square and Shopify are not built for. That gap is the whole wedge.
  • Replaces the incumbent "hodgepodge": spreadsheets + Venmo + Instagram DMs.
  • Functions as a ticketing system for food — limited inventory, timed release, sells out in seconds.
  • Order management + customer notifications are Hot Plate's job; pickup logistics are coordinated by the seller, not the platform. Asset-light.
  • Distribution is free: the seller brings their own audience and trust. Hot Plate never pays for demand.

Proof points named on air:

  • Pop-Up Bagels — started in Connecticut as an appointment-only, by-the-dozen backyard drop. Hot Plate powered them early; they've since raised significant VC and do hundreds of millions. Rishi: "almost a billion dollar empire."
  • Butter & Crumble (SF) — ~83K Instagram followers, three-hour physical line daily, ~20,000-person waitlist on Hot Plate drops for people who want to skip it.
  • An unnamed bodybuilder selling "cheat meal" cookies — sells out in seconds purely off Instagram trust.
  • Ice cream maker in Minnesota; pop-up bakers selling out of their homes.

The strategic arc Rishi is playing:

  • Today: supply-side SaaS/tooling — the storefront a chef uses.
  • End state: two-sided marketplace where consumers come to Hot Plate to *discover and buy*, not just to check out. Classic tools-to-marketplace play (Shaan probes exactly this).
  • COVID was the unlock — normalized buying food from a stranger's window on an appointment.
