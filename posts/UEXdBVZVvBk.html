The Simplest $300/Hour Side Hustle Anyone Can Start

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-29 · 43,465 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Couch flipping as a high-hourly, low-capital side hustle.

It's the rare side hustle with near-zero startup cost, no audience, and no code — you buy underpriced used furniture locally and resell it, and the whole loop can be run on weekends.

Heads up: no transcript was available for this video, so the breakdown below is reconstructed from the title, tags, and Chris's usual format. Specifics (his exact numbers, the buyers he uses) are in the video itself.

What it is: Buy used couches and furniture cheap off local marketplaces, clean/stage/photograph them properly, resell at a markup. Chris frames it as ~$300/hour of *actual working time* — not $300/hour of sitting around waiting for messages.

How it works:

  • Source the arbitrage. Sellers on Facebook Marketplace / OfferUp / Craigslist price to *get rid of* furniture — moving, divorce, upgrade, estate. "Free" and "must go today" listings are the top of the funnel.
  • Filter hard. Solid-frame, name-brand, neutral-fabric pieces (Pottery Barn, West Elm, Restoration Hardware, sectionals) resell. Sagging IKEA and floral hand-me-downs don't. Recognizing brands *is* the skill.
  • The margin is labor, not luck. Deep clean, steam, fabric shampoo, tighten the frame. A $0–150 couch that looks new lists at $400–800.
  • Photos are the product. Bad phone pics in a dark garage vs. staged shot in decent light is most of the price delta. Same couch, 2–3x the ask.
  • Solve logistics first. You need a truck or van, straps, dollies, and ideally a second person. This is the real barrier to entry, which is also why competition stays thin.
  • Speed beats greed. Storage space is the constraint. Turn inventory fast; don't hold out for the last $100.
  • The scale path: hire the pickup/delivery labor, then move upmarket into full-house/estate clearouts, or niche into a specific high-margin category.

Tools & links:

  • Facebook Marketplace — primary sourcing *and* selling channel; set saved searches with alerts
  • OfferUp — secondary sourcing, often less picked-over
  • Craigslist — free/curb-alert section, still underrated for pickups
  • beehiiv — video sponsor, Chris's newsletter platform (30% off first 30 days)

Why it matters for you: If you want a side hustle that pays real money by month one instead of month twelve, this is the version with no build phase — the entire business is pattern recognition on listings plus a truck, and you can validate it with a single flip this weekend.
