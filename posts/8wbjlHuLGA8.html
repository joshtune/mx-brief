Fix your sales mindset before you fix your pitch.

The instructional secret is that side-hustlers lose deals in their own head by pricing from their wallet instead of the customer's problem, and Codie gives you the math and the exact phrases to stop doing it.

What it is: Codie's four-mistake sales framework from Contrarian Thinking (nine-figure revenue), with the transcript covering the first two: wrong mindset and selling from need instead of confidence.

The instructional secret: You don't lose sales on the phone. You lose them beforehand by deciding the price is too high for *you*, then leaking that doubt into every sentence. Sales is a learnable skill, not a gift.

How it works:

  • Mistake 1: Selling from your wallet. If a $10k service feels expensive to you, you'll never say the number with a straight face. Reframe: price the solution against what the problem costs the buyer. A $10k fix for a $100k problem is cheap.
  • The 1% price math (McKinsey): raising price 1% lifts *profit* roughly 8%. That beats a 1% cost cut or a 1% volume bump combined. Underpricing isn't safe; it's the most expensive mistake on the table.
  • Mistake 2: Selling from need. Buyers smell thirst. Your job is to sit on their side of the table, solve their problem, and never beg because you're certain the purchase is a good decision.
  • Phrase swaps (needy vs confident):
  • "Just checking in" → "Thought of you, this would be valuable for you."
  • "Let me know if you're interested" → "Does option A or B make more sense for you?"
  • "We can discount it" → "We could always make it more expensive. Have you seen your ROI on this?"
  • "I just want to be considered" → "Based on what you described, here's exactly why I'm the right person."
  • Rule of thumb: if you don't believe in what you sell at the price you sell it, quit. Belief is the prerequisite; scripts are downstream.
