Is Linux the perfect operating system? - Omarchy interview with DHH

NetworkChuck · 2026-08-25 · 109,744 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
DHH explains Omarchy and AI's effect on calm work

Chuck admits AI wrecked his own calm-workplace experiment, and DHH lays out the counter-argument — that agents working overnight are the path *back* to a calm workday, not away from it.

What it is: A sit-down interview with David Heinemeier Hansson (Rails, 37signals) about Omarchy — his opinionated Arch/Hyprland desktop distro — plus a candid detour into how AI broke the "calm work" thesis from *It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work*.

The AI / work-life thread (the actual signal here):

  • Chuck read *It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work* in 2023, restructured his team around it, and says flatly: "AI ruined it." He's been sprinting for a year straight.
  • DHH's read: AI created a sense of urgency and opportunity cost that didn't exist before — some of it energizing, but the current pace "is not sustainable" and he doesn't think it stays this way.
  • His bull case for calm: agent autonomy is pro-calm, not anti-calm. Agents absorb the toil, then bundle the decisions that need you into fixed buckets of time — and they run while you're not working.
  • He's living it mid-interview: a bell goes off on his end — that's Herder, his agent tool, doing work while he sits and talks. Chuck started using it the day before the shoot.
  • DHH admits he didn't predict the "attention urgency mania" and is feeling it himself right now. So: sprint now, slow down later — the calm workplace is deferred, not dead.

The Omarchy thread (the home lab envy part):

  • DHH ran macOS from ~2001 for 20+ years, ran Linux on servers the whole time, then "drifted apart" from Apple ~2.5 years ago — they wanted different things out of a computer.
  • Started on Ubuntu like everyone does. Called it "perfectly fine for what it's trying to do" — but it wasn't trying to do what he wanted.
  • Omarchy (he pronounces it "oh-MAR-key," riffing on *omakase* — chef's-choice, you don't pick the menu) is a preconfigured, opinionated Arch distribution: every app, keybind, and theme decided for you, installed and beautiful in ~a minute.
  • The pitch is anti-setup-toil: "I don't want to assemble a box of Legos every single time I set up a computer." Contrast — a fresh Windows box took him 90 minutes of updates and driver installs before it was usable.
  • Cost of that convenience: ~3,000 hours over the past year DHH personally invested tuning the system.
