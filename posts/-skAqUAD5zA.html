Chris turned a $600K Pokémon bet into nine figures

A clean case study of buying a minority stake in an operator with domain obsession and riding a collectibles trade show from one hotel ballroom to a nine-figure exit in four years.

What it is: A short case study on how Chris (the guest) accidentally backed into a nine-figure Pokémon convention business after losing $150K on a party.

The person / business:

  • Guest: Chris — investor who got Pokémon-pilled after watching Logan Paul buy a ~$350,000 sealed Pokémon box and set a world record
  • Business: the largest Pokémon trade show / convention in the world (transcript doesn't name it — almost certainly Pokémon-focused card conventions in the Texas orbit; treat the name as unstated)
  • Founder: a convention-industry operator with 20 years running shows, plus his two cousins — all three deeply passionate Pokémon collectors

How the money actually got made:

  • Step 1 — buy into the scene, at a loss. Chris threw "the world's biggest Pokémon party" in Vegas. Cost: ~$150,000. Revenue: $0. Entire thing written off as a loss + charity donation. This was the acquisition cost for a network, not a business.
  • Step 2 — the network pays out. He met Steve Aoki (celebrity Pokémon collector) and "Gary King Pokémon" (the #1 Pokémon guy) building the party. A month later Gary called with the deal — a party attendee wanted to start a Pokémon convention and had no capital.
  • Step 3 — back the operator, not the idea. One lunch. Chris said yes on the spot. The pitch was strong on the only two axes that matter: 20 years of literal convention operating experience + genuine collector obsession.
  • Step 4 — write the check, take real ownership. $600,000 invested for a *minority but massive* stake. Not a token angel check — enough equity to matter on exit, without taking control from the operator.
  • Step 5 — start embarrassingly small. Show #1 was in a small hotel convention room in Frisco, Texas.
  • Step 6 — compound the event count. 4 years later: 20 shows/year, 700,000 attendees, biggest Pokémon trade show on earth.
  • Step 7 — exit. Nine figures. On a $600K basis.

The playbook underneath it:

  • Events are a working-capital-light business — ticket revenue lands before most costs clear, so growth partly self-funds ("we didn't have much money")
  • Repeatable format: one show that works × 20 cities is a spreadsheet, not a new invention each time
  • Fandom + physical goods = a captive, high-intent audience that vendors will pay to reach. The attendees are the product sold to exhibitors.
  • The $150K "waste" was actually deal flow. He bought his way into a scene, and the scene handed him the deal.

Tools & links:

Why it matters for you: The whole arc is $600K → nine figures in four years, and the mechanism is legible: minority stake, obsessed operator with 20 years of the exact right skill, one small show replicated 20×, sold to a 700K-person fandom.
