The $2B brand built on nostalgia

Poppi's founder on selling nostalgia, not health ingredients

A clip-length teardown of the exact positioning trick that took Poppi from farmers-market soda to a $1.95B Pepsi acquisition — flavor first, functional benefits as the afterthought.

What it is: A short MFM clip where a Poppi operator explains the brand's core marketing thesis — sell the soda memory, whisper the prebiotics.

The business: Poppi — prebiotic soda brand (apple cider vinegar + prebiotic fiber, ~5g sugar/can). Founded by Allison and Stephen Ellsworth, launched at Dallas farmers markets, went on *Shark Tank* (Rohan Oza deal), scaled through CAVU Consumer Partners, and sold to PepsiCo for ~$1.95B in 2025. The transcript here is the marketing playbook behind that number.

How they did it — the money moves:

  • Anchored on nostalgia, not novelty. Soda already owns a memory — movies, late nights, burger-and-a-soda. They rented that emotion instead of building a new one from scratch.
  • Moved the purchase decision "from the head to the heart." Explicit framing in the clip: emotional attachment beats spec-sheet comparison, and it's what creates obsessive repeat buyers.
  • Refused to lead with the ingredient deck. Direct jab at competitors: "we also have ashwagandha and lion's mane and 38 other ingredients nobody actually cares about." Poppi led with 89% less sugar + the flavors you grew up with.
  • Buried the functional claim on purpose. "Sure we have prebiotics. We're not going to talk about it that much." Prebiotics were the *reveal*, not the pitch.
  • Engineered a two-beat product experience: (1) taste it → "holy crap, this tastes amazing," (2) flip the can → "wait, this is good for me too." Delight first, permission second.
  • Chose the wide door over the narrow one. Targeting mainstream soda drinkers instead of the "niche health consumer" made the on-ramp shorter and the TAM enormous — that's the whole $2B thesis in one sentence.
  • Made something familiar "just a little bit better." Not a new category — an upgrade to one everyone already buys weekly.

The transferable pattern:

  • Find a product with pre-loaded emotional memory.
  • Fix its single most-hated attribute (here: sugar).
  • Keep everything else — flavor, format, ritual — recognizable.
  • Let the improvement be discovered, not announced.
