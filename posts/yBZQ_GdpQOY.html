Elon's whitespace-fingerprint trick to catch a leaker

It's a 60-second clip with no business model and no money-making playbook, so it won't help your focus, but the trick is a neat bit of cheap insider-threat tooling.

What it is: A short clip where Elon Musk explains how he found an employee leaking internal company info to news outlets. It's a canary trap, not a business case study.

How it works:

  • Someone asked Elon on X how he found a leaker who kept feeding internal data to the press.
  • He sent everyone in the company the same email, with one tiny difference per recipient.
  • The difference was spacing: one or two spaces after a period, at different spots in the email.
  • Because the email was long, those choices added up to a unique binary fingerprint for each employee.
  • When the email reached the press, the exact spacing pointed to the one inbox it came from.
  • Elon calls it the fastest path to catching the leaker.
  • The leaker was asked to pursue a career at another company, meaning they were fired.

Money-making breakdown (per your focus):

  • None. The clip has no revenue, pricing, or growth details about any person or business.
  • The only business angle is risk control: leaks hurt the company, so catching them fast protects it.

Tools & links:

  • None named in the transcript.

Why it matters for you: There's nothing here on how anyone makes money. The only takeaway is the trick: make each recipient's copy unique, so a leak identifies its source.

Caveats:

  • This is Elon's own account, told secondhand in a tweet reply. There's no corroboration in the clip.
  • Whitespace fingerprints are easy to destroy. Copy-paste, screenshots, or retyping all strip them.
