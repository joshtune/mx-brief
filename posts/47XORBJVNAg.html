Six customer red flags, each with a specific fix

Side hustles live on scarce hours, and this gives you a 60-second filter for which clients to price up, restrict, automate, or drop before they eat your margin.

What it is: A tier list of six bad-customer types, from mildly annoying to fire-on-sight, each paired with the specific move to make.

How it works:

  • The secret: You can't spot your best customers until you've named your worst ones. Sort clients by the pain they cause, then apply a fixed response to each type.
  • F tier, one-time buyer: Buys once and vanishes. Proceed with caution. The transcript cuts off before the fix, so Codie's rule likely means don't build your business on them (my read, not stated).
  • D tier, discount seeker (profit killer): Their first question is price, not value. Fix: higher prices.
  • C tier, trade offer: Wants to barter or 'collaborate' instead of paying. They want your margin, not a deal. Fix: strict offers, meaning a fixed menu with no custom swaps.
  • B tier, boundary pusher: Midnight texts, demands instant replies, treats every problem as an emergency. Fix: tech, not people. Use automation, FAQs, and scheduled support instead of your own attention.
  • Payment problem (the 'fire right now' tier): Late invoices, disputed charges, endless excuses. Fix: fire them.
  • The mapping rule: Each tier gets its own response. Boundaries, higher prices, strict offers, and tech each have a job, and the worst tier gets cut.
  • Caveat: The transcript is truncated and the tier-to-fix mapping is garbled in places, so the F-tier fix is inferred.
