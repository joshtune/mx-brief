The Most Overlooked $3K/Day Side Hustle Anyone Can Start (with $0)

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-07 · 9,955 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Selling pumpkin porch-decorating packages as a seasonal business.

It's a fully-templated, cash-upfront, ~50% margin side hustle that runs on a 6-week seasonal sprint — the rare business you can actually operate around a day job.

What it is: "Porch Pumpkins" — you sell fixed-price packages where you deliver and arrange a large seasonal pumpkin display on a customer's front porch. Chris profiles Heather, a Dallas mom of three who scaled it to $100K+ in monthly sales during the fall season.

The economics:

  • ~50% of every dollar is pure profit (per Chris's framing)
  • Customers pay 100% upfront — near-zero capital risk, no receivables, no float
  • Heather reported hitting $100K in sales in a season month
  • Seasonal: the whole business compresses into roughly Sept–Oct

The package ladder (the actual core of the business):

  • Package 1 — $1,350 — the flagship; includes design/install, fills an entire porch
  • Package 2 — $800 — steps down from package 1; anchored by "grand prize" pumpkins sold individually at $185 each (two of those alone ≈ $370, which is most of the gap to package 1)
  • Package 3 — $525 — drop-off only, customer arranges it themselves; +$75 add-on for design
  • Package 4 — the other drop-off/DIY tier
  • Custom — $3,500 floor — she'll do anything bespoke, but only above this price, because custom work eats unbudgeted quoting time
  • Add-on: end-of-season pickup and haul-away (pumpkins go to a pig farm)

How it's actually run:

  • Packages, not custom quotes. Her restaurant-industry analogy: a restaurant with no menu can't run a kitchen. Open-ended choice = confusion = friction = "never mind." Every custom request costs you unpaid costing time.
  • Two-option close. When a customer asks "which one?", she names exactly two, then adds a soft upsell: "and if you want to go really big, package 1 will fit your porch."
  • The DIY tier is a deliberate segment, not a discount. Some customers *want* to decorate — sell them logistics (sourcing + delivery) instead of labor, at near-zero install cost to you.
  • Distribution is organic. The install itself is the billboard: she decorated her own porch, neighbors asked, then social media took over. "My phone just kept going, ding ding ding." Every job is a physical ad in a high-income neighborhood for 6 weeks.
  • Origin: Dallas Arboretum's "Pumpkin Village" display was the inspiration — she saw what a porch *could* look like beyond two orange jack-o'-lanterns.

Playbook if you ran this:

  • Build a one-page flyer with 3–4 fixed packages and hard prices. That's the whole product.
  • Source pumpkins wholesale from local farms/patches — the video promises a "cheap pumpkins" segment; volume buying is where the 50% margin lives
  • Collect payment upfront at booking, buy inventory after. You're never out of pocket.
  • Target dense, affluent neighborhoods where displays are visible to walkers/drivers
  • Sell the haul-away add-on — pure margin, and it's the pain nobody wants

Why it matters for you: This is a side hustle whose entire operating complexity has been engineered away into a price list — no software, no ongoing support, no recurring obligations, and it starts and ends inside a two-month window you can run without quitting anything.
