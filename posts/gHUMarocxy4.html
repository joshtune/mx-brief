Danny Yeung's IM8: $200M run rate in 18 months

A serial founder walks through the exact playbook that took a supplement brand from a collapsed COVID-testing company to a $200M annual run rate in 18 months, faster than AG1 or Grüns.

What it is: Shaan Puri interviews Danny Yeung, founder of Prenetics (NASDAQ-listed) and IM8, the David Beckham-backed daily supplement brand that hit a $100M run rate at 11 months and $200M at 18 months.

Who Danny Yeung is (career arc):

  • Age 15: telemarketer. Age 24: opened a restaurant, ran it 3-4 years.
  • Hotel furniture import/export from China to the US, including the MGM CityCenter project in Vegas.
  • 2010: moved to Hong Kong to launch a Groupon-style group-buying e-commerce company when nobody in HK used e-commerce. Groupon bought 51%; it became the largest e-commerce company in the region at ~$200M annual revenue. Exited 2014, first real payday.
  • 2014: founded Prenetics, a DNA-testing/life-sciences company, years before consumer genomics was mainstream.
  • COVID: pivoted Prenetics to PCR testing in two weeks. Became Hong Kong's and the UK's #1 PCR provider. Peak 40,000 tests/day, 28M tests total, $700M+ revenue over three years, $272M in 2022 alone. Listed on NASDAQ in 2022 at a $1B+ valuation.
  • Post-COVID crash: revenue fell ~95%, market cap fell to ~$50M. Options were shut down and return capital, or build something new inside the public shell.

How IM8 made money (the playbook):

  • Pick the category before the celebrity. Yeung was already targeting supplements when he met David Beckham; the meeting wasn't a pitch, it turned into a partnership because Beckham was genuinely into the space.
  • Celebrity as co-founder, not endorser. Beckham is a partner in the brand, which gives global distribution reach and press attention that a paid ad budget can't buy.
  • One hero SKU. IM8 Daily Ultimate Essentials is an all-in-one greens/vitamin powder aimed straight at AG1's customer, priced and positioned as the premium alternative.
  • Subscription-first DTC. Recurring revenue compounds fast; that is what drives a run rate to $100M in 11 months.
  • Speed over perfection. Same two-week-pivot muscle from COVID applied to launch: ship, then iterate on formulation and marketing.
  • Use the public-company shell. Prenetics' NASDAQ listing gave IM8 capital access and shareholder accountability, which Yeung frames as the reason he didn't just walk away.
  • Contrarian timing. Every business he built was 'not the obvious choice' at the time (HK e-commerce 2010, DNA testing 2014, supplements in 2024) and looked obvious in hindsight.

Benchmarks he cites:

  • IM8: $100M run rate in 11 months, $200M in 18 months.
  • Grüns: $100M in 22 months, acquired for $1.2B.
  • AG1: 10 years to $100M.
