You are not in enough pain to change

Codie Sanchez · 2026-09-04 · 12,978 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Comfort, not failure, is what kills your side hustle.

A 60-second gut-check that names the real reason most side hustles never launch: your day job is good enough to make you stall.

What it is: A short motivational clip arguing that "fine" is the most dangerous state, because mediocre comfort removes the pressure that forces action.

How it works:

  • Opens with a claimed Harvard study of 2,000 high performers over 10 years. Those who underperformed shared one trait: they got comfortable too early. No citation given, treat as anecdote.
  • Core thesis: people don't change because they aren't in enough pain. Not failing, not thriving, just fine.
  • Leans on Jim Collins' line from *Good to Great*: "Good is the enemy of great." Bad situations force a move. Good-enough situations let you stall.
  • Closes on the mechanism for breakthroughs: friction, exposure, difficulty, challenge. You don't level up solo.

The instructional secret:

  • Manufacture the pain you're missing. If your salary is comfortable, nothing external will push you to start. You have to create the forcing function yourself: a public deadline, money on the line, a cohort or accountability partner.
  • Audit your "fine." If you can't name what's actually broken in your life, that's the warning sign, not the reassurance.
  • Seek friction on purpose. Talk to customers, ship something ugly, join a room of people further along. Isolation is where side hustles quietly die.

Tools & links:

  • Good to Great by Jim Collins — source of the "good is the enemy of great" framing
  • Contrarian Thinking — Codie's community, the implied "don't play solo" answer

Why it matters for you: If you have a stable engineering job, your biggest obstacle to a side hustle isn't skill or time. It's that nothing hurts enough to make you start, so you have to build the pressure yourself.
