If you want the actual SpaceX business model and revenue engines decoded without the Wall Street jargon before deciding whether to buy the IPO, this is it.

What it is: Shaan & Sam's "two idiots in an F1" walkthrough of what SpaceX actually does and how it makes money, framed around whether the IPO is worth buying.

The SpaceX business (4 revenue engines + 1 emerging):

  • Launches — Builds rapidly reusable rockets; reusability is the core cost lever (drives down $/launch), which is the whole moat. This is the original business and still the cash engine.
  • Starlink — Satellite internet constellation; the recurring-revenue consumer/enterprise play riding on cheap launches.
  • X (formerly Twitter) — Folded into the SpaceX/Musk empire as a distribution + data asset.
  • xAI — Musk's AI company, bundled into the same orbit; consumes massive compute.
  • Terra Fab (emerging) — Plan to build "the largest chip factory in the world" to self-supply chips for xAI and the rockets, hedging against a 5–6 year chip-supply bottleneck by reshoring production to the US.

The origin / how Musk got here:

  • After PayPal sold, Musk netted ~$200M.
  • Checked NASA's site for a Mars mission, found nothing planned post-moon.
  • First idea: fund a stunt — send a plant/succulent to Mars to "take life to Mars" and generate public buzz for space.
  • Flew to Russia twice to buy refurbished ICBMs as cheap rockets; got laughed off.
  • Took it personally → decided to build rockets himself → reusability became the cost-killer thesis.

Why the model holds together: Cheap reusable launch → makes Starlink economical → throws off cash + data → feeds xAI compute → Terra Fab secures the chip supply. Each piece subsidizes the next.
