Why Big Tech is gutting headcount despite record profits

It's a short clip on why Meta-style mass layoffs may be a deliberate bet rather than a blip, which is worth knowing before your own org tries it. The title's "AI pushes out top performers" claim isn't argued in the portion available.

What it is: A clip from a longer interview. The host asks a guest engineer about Meta's latest layoffs and whether this is over-trimming or a real paradigm shift in tech workforces.

The message to engineers:

  • Layoffs are happening at record profits, so they are a strategy and not distress.
  • Some execs may be betting they can cut very deep, even 80%, and absorb a year or two of bad PR.
  • Twitter/X is the precedent they point to. It was cut by roughly 80% or more, didn't collapse, and now serves more traffic and live video. Whether it prospers is debated, but it survived.
  • The guest does NOT endorse the theory. He's explaining why leadership believes it.
  • Second driver: it feels like 2007-08, when lines going up fast made risky moves feel affordable, and big layoffs followed. The guest flags this as speculation outside his expertise.
  • Meta is reportedly trying to rehire managers who "knew where the bodies were buried" and to lure ICs back into management. That suggests the cuts removed institutional knowledge.
  • The host's counterpoint: Musk's bet worked partly because of a hidden back end (GraphQL aggregation) he misjudged. Outsiders misread how much is holding a system up.

What to prepare for:

  • Assume deep cuts can hit profitable companies, so don't read "record profits" as job security.
  • Know where your institutional knowledge lives. It's both your leverage and your risk. Companies cut it and then scramble to buy it back.
  • Keep your visible value legible. If leadership thinks "a small core runs everything," be demonstrably in the core.
  • Keep an exit plan and runway in case a restructuring lands on your group.
