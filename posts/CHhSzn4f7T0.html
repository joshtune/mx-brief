When To Cut People Off

Codie Sanchez · 2026-09-01 · 16,617 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Cut people whose limiting beliefs cap your ambition.

Your side hustle dies faster from the people asking "isn't that enough?" than from any bad unit economic.

What it is: A short, blunt argument for auditing your inner circle by one filter — do they cap your ceiling or raise it?

How it works:

  • The trigger: at lunch, someone asks Codie "Why do you keep buying businesses? At what point is it enough?" She reframes it instantly instead of defending herself.
  • The reframe: "How come when I do something I want to do, you assume that means I don't have enough?" Wanting more gets read as lack — that's the listener's projection, not your motive.
  • The core rule: the person telling you enough is enough is telling you what *they* couldn't have. Their ceiling is being described as your limit.
  • Two-part credential test: don't take business advice from people who've never built anything; don't take ambition advice from people who quit on theirs. Advice-giver competence is domain-specific — ambition is its own domain.
  • The action: cut, don't debate. "I literally will not let you around me if your beliefs are holding me back." She treats proximity as the variable, not persuasion.
  • The closing frame: "People who say it can't be done should not interrupt those who are doing it."

Why it matters for you: Side hustles fail quietly in the months where you stop shipping because someone you respect implied you were being greedy — run the credential test on your feedback loop before you run it on your P&L.
