The Most Profitable Solo Business Anyone Can Start

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-11-07 · 48,594 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Local marketing agency for landscapers: websites, reviews, tools

A zero-revenue dad of five hit $18k/month in 16 months selling basic websites and review systems to local trades, and it's a side hustle you can copy with no special skills.

What it is: James runs a solo local-marketing agency for small service businesses (starting with landscapers). He sells them a website, a review system, a Google Business Profile and basic tools. He's at about 60 clients and $18k in his best month.

How it works:

  • Start with zero: he quit a nonprofit job with no clients and no revenue. He had job offers and chose to build instead.
  • Find prospects in person: he photographed landscaping trucks. Two months later he spotted the same truck in his neighborhood, chased it down on his bike and pulled the driver over.
  • Be the neighbor, not the vendor: he invited the owner (a 22-year-old Hispanic orphan with a toddler, and James spoke Spanish with him) to his house to talk about his business. The conversation was about helping him, not pitching.
  • First sale, $300: the offer was to register the LLC, put a brand on the truck and build a website. That first client is still a client.
  • Learn by doing: the client had a suspended Google Business Profile. James spent 4 months and many internet rabbit holes (X threads, talking to people) fixing it. It turned out to be an outlier, and he calls it "earning his stripes." The skill became a sellable service.
  • Generate wins locally: he got that client about 15 customers by posting in his local neighborhood Facebook group.
  • Scale the same motion: the intro promises a call opener and a 30-second checklist. It also promises five calls a day sourced from Facebook groups. The transcript is cut off before these are shown.
  • Claimed math: half a workday, 6 days a week would make about $30k/month plus about $170k in upfront fees. That is the host's framing, so treat it as a best case.
  • Honest caveat: the host asks whether this episode alone is enough to start. The answer is "yes and no": you learn the approach and the offer, but you still have to learn the delivery yourself.

Tools & links:

  • High Level (GoHighLevel) — the sponsor's CRM and marketing platform with a 30-day free trial. It is an affiliate link, and the transcript doesn't confirm James uses it.
  • TKOPOD newsletter — Chris's newsletter
  • Google Business Profile — the key service for getting local businesses found, and fixing suspensions is a skill you can charge for
  • Local Facebook groups — the lead and customer source

Why it matters for you: It's a low-capital side hustle where the work is learning to deliver websites, reviews and Google profiles, then repeating a friendly local pitch until you have clients. The part about the opener, checklist and daily call math is in the later, uncaptured part of the video.
