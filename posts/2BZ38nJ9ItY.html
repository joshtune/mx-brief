Pack-opening app's buyback loop drives billion-dollar revenue

The buyback mechanic is the whole money engine, and it's a clean, copyable psychological trick, though the transcript is only a short teaser clip.

What it is: A teaser clip about an unnamed app doing ~$1B, built by a former quant (Citadel, Bridgewater) around a pack-opening loop with a buyback.

Caveat: The transcript is a short excerpt. The app and founder are never named, so no company-level detail, revenue breakdown, or timeline is available here.

How it works (the loop):

  • Open the app and see a digital pack on screen.
  • Tap to open it and reveal the contents, which are often junk.
  • Pay about $50 per pack.
  • The app immediately offers to buy the pack back for about $43.
  • Users take the credit and buy another pack, since it's only about $7 more.

How they make money:

  • Pack sales: Each pack costs about $50, and the user pays it up front.
  • Buyback spread: The company pays out about $43 in store credit, so about $7 of every $50 is free margin.
  • Inventory recycling: The returned card goes back into the deck and is resold to someone else. The company never has to actually source the item.
  • Repeat purchase: Buyback credit stays in the app, so each loss turns into the next purchase.

Why the psychology works:

  • Spending $50 and getting nothing feels like a loss.
  • Getting $43 back reframes it as store credit and a small cost.
  • The reframe makes users feel the treasure hunt is cheap to continue.
