How MrBeast sold a record number of burgers in 24 hours

MrBeast paid staff $30K extra to serve burger crowds

A short, concrete look at how MrBeast's team solved a launch-day capacity crisis by paying whatever it took, though it covers only one moment and not the business model.

What it is: A short clip from a guest who spent 24 hours with the MrBeast Burger team around the mall opening, covering one decision the night before.

How it works:

  • The crowd: 15,000 to 20,000 people were already there the night before, and some slept in the mall. Mall security was on site.
  • The problem: The team doubted they could serve everyone, and supplies couldn't be brought in fast enough.
  • The decision: Jimmy asked whether they could pay someone to make it work. He offered every worker an extra $1,000 for the day.
  • The cost: About 30 staff × $1,000 = $30,000 extra.
  • The result: The impossible turned out to be possible, and Jimmy kept pushing.

What the transcript does NOT give you:

  • No revenue figures, burger counts, pricing, margins, or deal structure.
  • No breakdown of how the business makes money (licensing, franchise, virtual brand, etc.).
  • No names beyond Jimmy (MrBeast) and 'Colin', and no business or company details beyond the opening.

Money lessons from the clip:

  • Treat a one-off cost as cheap when it protects a huge launch moment.
  • Spend to remove a bottleneck instead of accepting the stated limit.
  • Incentives work: $1K per worker bought extra effort and capacity.
