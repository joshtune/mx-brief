Claude Artifacts Just Got a Huge Upgrade

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-11-25 · 113,735 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Claude Artifacts can now install as native desktop apps

Nate walks through exactly how he turns Claude-built dashboards into installable desktop apps and shared team pages, and he flags the lock-in risk before you commit.

What it is: Anthropic added "Install as app" to Claude Artifacts, so a Claude-built dashboard or tool runs as a native Mac/Windows app. Nate also covers the wider artifact upgrades that make them a team workspace.

How it works:

  • Build normally. Make an artifact as usual, then open it in the Claude desktop app.
  • Install. Click Share, or click the artifact's name, then hit Install as app. It gets its own icon and its own window.
  • Nate's example: His "content OS" dashboard pulls in his video pitches every morning. It now opens as a real app instead of a browser tab.
  • What installed apps can do:
  • Still call Claude and use all your connectors.
  • Send notifications.
  • Sit in the menu bar or system tray.
  • Auto-update whenever you update the artifact.
  • Need no API key or other setup.
  • Limits:
  • It's in beta and just dropped, so it may still be rolling out to you.
  • Artifacts made from templates (docs, slides, sheets) can't be installed.
  • If an app asks for local access (running programs, changing files), only allow it for apps you built or trust. Anthropic doesn't auto-review them.
  • What an artifact is: Anything Claude makes that you'd put in front of someone (design, deck, doc, dashboard, small interactive tool). It's a page at a live link.
  • Seven templates: docs, slides, design, data, sheets, whiteboards, and motion (short animated explainers).
  • Plan availability (may already be outdated):
  • Docs, slides and design are on every plan, including free.
  • Sheets, whiteboards and data need a paid plan.
  • Motion is Team/Enterprise only.
  • Claude Code: Anything a session builds (a report, a small tool) can be published as a page or artifact. Each update becomes a new version of the same link, so no more re-sending HTML files.
  • Sharing: Artifacts are private by default. You can share them with your team or make them public.
  • What it replaces:
  • Pasting into Google Docs.
  • Screenshotting into Slack.
  • Deploying to Vercel.
  • Telling a non-technical person to go look at GitHub.
  • Nate's own use: He had Claude build the deck for his company's October all-hands, pushed it to an artifact, and screen-shared it on the call.
  • Artifacts can also:
  • Store data, so everyone on a tracker or signup sheet sees the same thing.
  • Read from and write to connected apps (Asana, Google Calendar, Slack).
  • Call Claude themselves, so the page can answer questions or give feedback.
  • Scale: Per the video, people have made 45M+ docs, decks and designs with artifacts.
