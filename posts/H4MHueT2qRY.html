The Problem With Shrinking an AI Workstation

Alex Ziskind · 2026-07-04 · 57,660 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Cramming a real local-AI PC into Mac Studio size.

It's a running parts-and-price tally of a tiny x86 AI box built to rival Apple Silicon — exactly the machine-and-cost comparison you care about.

What it is: A build-along at Micro Center squeezing a local-AI workstation into a case barely bigger than a Mac Studio, racing it against an M3 Ultra.

The machine (parts + prices):

  • GPU: RTX Pro 4000 SFF — 24GB VRAM, 70W (bus-powered, no extra power cable), same CUDA cores/VRAM as the full Pro 4000 but slower. Billed as the smallest 24GB GPU you can buy.
  • Motherboard: ITX, AM5 socket. MSI B850 ($289) chosen over ASUS B850 ($359).
  • RAM: Crucial 32GB DDR5-6400 CL32, $369.99. Bumping to 64GB (2×32) jumps to $879 — he stuck with 32GB. (Models load via system RAM, then spill into GPU VRAM.)
  • CPU: Ryzen (AM5), not yet specified.
  • PSU: reused from a previous build — $0.
  • Case: starting in a Silverstone ML09 (13×8×4 in), final target is the Velka 3 (near Mac Studio footprint), not yet arrived.

The catch: The SFF GPU trades speed for size/power, and single-channel-ish 32GB RAM caps how big a model fits before it leans on VRAM — the "shrinking" compromises start before assembly.
