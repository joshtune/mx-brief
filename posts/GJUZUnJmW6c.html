Robert Greene on Sublime and living less numbly

Robert Greene is a bestselling author, not a business operator, so this is a worldview episode and a poor fit for your 'how they made money' lens, unless you want his pitch for Sublime.

What it is: Shaan and Sam interview Robert Greene (48 Laws of Power, Seduction, Mastery) about his new book *Sublime*. The transcript I had covers only the first few minutes, so everything below comes from that opening.

How it works:

  • Who he is: Author known for 48 Laws of Power, The Art of Seduction and Mastery, which the hosts describe as books about control.
  • The pivot: *Sublime* is the opposite angle: letting go, feeling small, and reconnecting instead of dominating.
  • Origin story: Greene nearly died 8 years ago. He says the world looked and felt different afterward, and he wants readers to get that shift without a near-death experience.
  • The thesis: People suffer from the narrowness of their experience, and technology is a big cause. He says they feel disconnected from other people, their environment and themselves.
  • Practical claim: He calls it 'a very practical book' about bringing awe-like experiences into day-to-day life.
  • Is this time different? Shaan raises the 'every generation thinks it's the worst' idea. He mentions an ancient tablet complaining about the younger generation, and Greene doesn't dispute it.
  • Greene's answer: You can't really quantify it. People in past eras leaned on alcohol, cigarettes and ale, which he compares to today's antidepressants.
  • What he says is new: Social media and algorithms program what you see, feel and hear. He argues people want agency, and a feed leaves them feeling powerless.
  • Money angle: Nothing in the transcript covers revenue, business moves or monetization. The only commercial link is the host-promoted list of Sam's top 9 books for entrepreneurs.
