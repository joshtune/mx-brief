He built a $15B+ business without raising money

My First Million · 2026-08-16 · 17,158 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Edwin Chen's Surge AI: $1B revenue, zero investors.

A solo founder owns 100% of a company doing ~$1B/year by selling the one thing every AI lab is starved for — high-quality human-labeled data.

Who: Edwin Chen, 37, founder of Surge AI. Ex-Facebook data scientist. Owns 100% of the company — no VC, no outside funding, reportedly worth ~$15–30B on paper.

The origin problem:

  • At Facebook, Chen had a list of ~50,000 vendors and needed to classify each one (restaurant vs. grocery store vs. other).
  • He outsourced it to an external firm. It came back four months later — four months of sitting and waiting on a task that was conceptually trivial.
  • Takeaway he internalized: human data labeling was slow, manual, and structurally broken — and it was about to become the bottleneck for every ML company on earth.

What Surge AI is: A marketplace of human data labelers that supplies training and evaluation data (good/bad response labeling, RLHF-style preference data) to frontier AI labs.

How it works / what he actually did to make money:

  • Founded 2020 — right before the LLM boom made human preference data the scarcest input in AI.
  • Built a two-sided marketplace: recruit and manage a workforce, sell their labeled output to labs.
  • Scaled to ~100,000 people working in the marketplace as data labelers.
  • Sells to the labs directly — OpenAI and peers reportedly pay Surge millions and millions per contract.
  • Leaked figure: ~$1B in revenue over the trailing 12 months.
  • Zero outside funding. No dilution, so ~$1B of revenue accrues to one owner instead of a cap table.
  • Marketing footprint: surge.ai is famously a landing page with one paragraph. No content engine, no sales-led growth theater. The product sells because the buyers are ~20 companies and they all know who you are.

Why it worked (the replicable pattern):

  • Sell picks and shovels, not gold. He didn't build a model — he sold the input every model needs.
  • Services business disguised as a marketplace. High-touch, labor-heavy, unsexy — exactly why VCs undervalued it and why he never needed them.
  • Cash-flow first. Labeling contracts pay on delivery; the business funded its own scaling from day one.
  • Concentrated, high-ACV buyers. A handful of frontier labs with effectively unlimited budgets beats a million SMB customers.

Tools & links:

Why it matters for you: The most durable AI business in this story isn't a model — it's the boring human-in-the-loop infrastructure underneath it, built by one engineer who spotted a bottleneck at his day job and refused to give away equity to fix it.
