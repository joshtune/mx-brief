The $200K AI Job That Didn't Exist Last Year

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-07-14 · 42,398 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
The rise of the in-house AI builder role.

It maps where the AI-automation money is shifting — away from agencies and toward in-house builders — so you can position yourself before the crowd does.

What it is: A thought-leadership pitch arguing the next big AI career is the *in-house* AI builder/consultant embedded inside a company, not the external agency.

The thesis (how it works):

  • Pattern behind it: AI didn't just delete jobs — it let one AI-literate person do the work of 3–5. Layoffs are a symptom; the AI-users get ahead of the non-users.
  • Chegg as the canonical example: Sold homework help to millions → ChatGPT (late 2022) made it free/instant → stock crashed ~50% in a day (2023). Chegg admitted ChatGPT was killing the business.
  • Phase 1 (last ~2 yrs): Companies knew their problems but not the solutions. That gap let AI agencies/consultants charge premium prices. The AI-automation market ballooned to ~$130B.
  • Phase 2 (now): AI got so accessible that even busy CEOs use ChatGPT/Claude. The knowing-problem-vs-knowing-solution gap collapsed. Companies now want to solve it in-house instead of paying an agency — the same accessibility that made agencies rich is now replacing them.
  • The opportunity: Be the in-house AI person. Nate stresses that being the *best builder* is only a small piece — what matters more is diagnosing problems and knowing what to build (the rest of the '4-step roadmap' is teased but cut off in this transcript).
