The Most Profitable Online Business No One Talks About

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-07-30 · 24,761 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Government grants fund your online trade school

It's a side hustle where the government pays you $5K–$15K per student for an online course you don't even have to build — 80–90% margins, near-zero startup cost, and no sales pitch beyond "this is free for you."

What it is: An online vocational trade school funded by state workforce-development grants. Chris interviews Hector, who runs a forklift technician trade school — has never driven a forklift — and did $2.4M in year one with over $1M profit.

The core arbitrage:

  • State workforce grants reimburse $5,000–$15,000 per student for approved trade training
  • You license/buy the course content from someone else for "a fraction of the price"
  • Student pays $0. State pays you. You keep the spread.
  • Hector's courses sold at $12,000–$15,000 each, delivered online

How it works:

  • Incorporate — LLC first, then apply to become a state-approved training provider
  • Pick an in-demand trade — forklift repair, EV technician, HVAC, plumbing, pool cleaning, dog grooming. Criteria: real job demand on Indeed (25+ openings), decent wages ($30/hr), and AI-proof (hands-on physical work)
  • Don't build the course — buy or license existing curriculum. This is the unlock. Hector calls it "almost like a digital reseller."
  • Go online-only — no classroom means no capacity cap. Hector services all of Texas and "sells courses by the masses." In-person doesn't let you charge more, so don't bother.
  • Sales is trivial — "it's not really much of a sales pitch, they're getting a free grant." Tuition is 100% covered; no partial reimbursement, no out-of-pocket, as long as you price within the state's cap
  • Stay under the cap — every state has a per-student reimbursement ceiling. Price at the cap, never above it.
  • Operations are near-passive — automations handle delivery, the course runs itself. Human touch only at onboarding and at graduation.
  • Job placement is a non-issue — grads interview 2–3 times and get an offer. Hector cherry-picks the best graduates as employees for his own repair business and lets the rest place themselves.

Why the margins hold:

  • No financial reserves required (course is digital, delivered on demand)
  • No classroom, no instructors at scale, no inventory
  • 80–90% profit margins, very low startup cost
  • Hector kept quiet about it for a while — "I thought it was too good to be true, who's going to believe me anyway?"

Tools & links:

  • Bizee — LLC formation, sponsor of the video; Hector uses it, charges only state fees
  • Your state's workforce commission — the actual gatekeeper. In Texas that's the Texas Workforce Commission's approved-provider list. This is the real research step and the video glosses over the approval process.
  • Indeed — free demand validation tool. Search a trade + your metro, count the openings.

The catch the video underplays: Becoming a state-approved training provider is a compliance process — applications, curriculum approval, outcome reporting, audits. The grant money is real, but the moat is bureaucratic paperwork, not code. If you're comfortable reading spec docs and satisfying a validator, that's a familiar skill set.

Why it matters for you: This is the rare side hustle with a built-in payer and no consumer acquisition cost — you're selling a free thing to motivated buyers and billing the state, which means the whole business collapses to a licensing deal plus an automated delivery pipeline you could stand up in a weekend.
