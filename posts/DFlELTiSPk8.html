18 Codex concepts, explained for non-coders

Nate walks through his real Codex setup (the 'Herk 2' AI operating system) on screen, so you can copy the project and agents.md structure he uses.

What it is: A four-part walkthrough of 18 core OpenAI Codex concepts, building from foundations to more advanced features. The transcript I had cuts off during concept 2, so everything below covers only parts 1–2.

How it works:

  • Structure: 18 concepts in four parts, ordered easiest to coolest. Part 1 is Foundations.
  • Concept 1, Projects:
  • A project is just a folder of files that you open in Codex. Nothing more exotic than that.
  • It fixes the problem of re-explaining your business at the start of every web chat.
  • His sidebar has Herk 2, Hyperframes, Trading Challenge and AIS demo. He mostly works in Herk 2, which he calls his AI operating system.
  • The Herk 2 folder holds rules about him, credentials, all his projects and his YouTube videos.
  • Demo: he asked for a rundown of the past month. Codex read the project files and his earlier Codex chats. It reported work on Herk 2/AISOS, YouTube content and strategy, model testing and demos, app experiments, websites, branding and business ops.
  • His point: it stops being a chatbot and becomes a co-founder or personal assistant.
  • Concept 2, agents.md:
  • A plain markdown file of ground rules, one per project. Hyperframes would have its own.
  • Codex reads it before it reads your message, so you never re-explain context.
  • His Herk 2 file has three parts:
  • A role statement: you are Nate Herk's AI operating system, and you cut his ops time so he can make videos.
  • Core operating rules and desktop safety rules.
  • A routing map, which is most of the file. It points to where things live: the wiki for business info, a corporate-structure section, a voice section, course knowledge, and active projects.
  • Routing keeps a project with thousands of files navigable without stuffing everything into context.
  • Concepts 3–18: Not in the transcript I received. He says they get more advanced.
