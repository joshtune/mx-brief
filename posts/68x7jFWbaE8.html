The Simple One-Man Side Hustle That Runs Itself

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-09-06 · 106,112 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Trailer rental side hustle: $6K/month, 3 hours/week

Nathan bought 10 used trailers off Facebook Marketplace for $35K and now nets thousands a month with a full-time job and two kids, and the playbook is almost entirely buy-assets-then-automate.

What it is: A trailer rental business run by Nathan, 31, from Lancaster, PA (county pop. ~500K), pulling $5,500 to $7,500/month revenue on 2 to 4 hours of work per week, 15 months in, while keeping his full-time auction job.

The numbers:

  • Revenue: $5,500 to $7,500/month
  • Time: 2 to 4 hours/week, which Chris pegs at $200+/hour
  • Startup: $35,000 for 10 used trailers (5 enclosed, several open, utility, equipment, no dump trailer)
  • Financing: Available on trailers, so the $35K doesn't have to be cash

How he got in:

  • Scrolling Facebook Marketplace, found a local guy selling a 10-trailer rental business plus his "book of business" because he was moving two hours west
  • Nathan had rented a boat from the seller years earlier, so cold outreach wasn't cold
  • Seller wanted $35K for trailers plus $20K for the customer list. Nathan paid the $35K for assets only
  • Customer list was worthless as an asset: home addresses and landlines, no emails, no CRM, nothing automatable
  • Instead of $20K for the list, Nathan negotiated a referral fee for any past renter the seller sent his way. No non-compete, seller keeps operating two hours away at smaller scale

What came with the deal:

  • A dated WordPress site with keyword-stuffed copy and backlinks that still ranked locally for "trailer rentals"
  • A Google Business Profile with ~10 years of reviews, around 4.2 stars (seller split trailers from his boat/small-equipment rentals, which rated lower)
  • Zero automation, so the upside was in systematizing what was already producing demand

Why the model works (per Chris):

  • Near 100% automated after setup
  • Low risk, low cost, not much can go wrong with a trailer
  • You can validate demand before launching by watching local search and Marketplace rental listings
  • Asset-backed: if it flops, you sell the trailers

Tools & links:

  • Facebook Marketplace — where Nathan found the business for sale, and where seller-financed asset deals surface
  • Google Business Profile — the inherited review base that drives inbound rental leads
  • beehiiv — sponsor, Chris's newsletter platform

Caveats:

  • Transcript cuts off partway through the interview, so the automation stack (booking, payments, pickup/dropoff) isn't captured here
  • Revenue figure is gross, not profit. Chris says "thousands in profit" but no net number is given in this portion

Why it matters for you: This is a buy-then-automate play, not build-from-scratch. Look for unsophisticated local rental operators with rankings and reviews but no systems, buy the assets, negotiate the goodwill down to a referral fee, and layer software on top.
