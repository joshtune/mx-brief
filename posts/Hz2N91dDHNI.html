How Sam Newhouse built an $8 billion media business

Sam Newhouse's newspaper-buying path to a media empire

It's a clean case study of a 5'2" dropout who turned one turnaround into a 60-year buy-a-paper-every-three-years machine, and it shows the playbook behind one of America's biggest private companies.

What it is: A case study of Samuel Newhouse (b. ~1895, New Jersey), who started with nothing and built one of the largest privately owned companies in America, now roughly $8B a year in revenue.

Who he was:

  • Born ~1895 in New Jersey, about 5'2".
  • Dropped out of school at 13-14 because his mom needed him to earn money.
  • Took a mail-in order course, then talked a local businessperson into hiring him.

How he made money (in order):

  • Got hired on hustle: He convinced a local business owner to give him a job at 13-14.
  • Turnaround operator at 16: His boss owned a newspaper that barely made money and asked him to keep an eye on it. In 6-7 months he turned it profitable.
  • Raised capital from family: After about 3 years, he convinced a bunch of relatives to pool loans so he could buy his own paper.
  • First acquisition: The Staten Island Advance, which became the base of the empire.
  • Repeatable acquisition cadence: For the next ~60 years he bought a new newspaper about every 3 years.
  • Compounding result: By ~1980 it was one of the largest privately owned companies in America.
  • Today's footprint (per the hosts): ~$8B/yr in revenue, with holdings they list as Reddit, Charter Communications, Warner Bros. Discovery, Vanity Fair, GQ and Vogue. The "Iron Man" in the transcript looks like an auto-caption error.

The playbook, distilled:

  • Prove you can fix a money-losing asset before asking anyone for money.
  • Use family and friends as the first capital, backed by a track record.
  • Buy slowly and consistently instead of swinging for one giant deal.
  • Stay private, so you keep control and never answer to public markets.

Caveat: The transcript we have is only the cold open. It has no detail on financing terms, purchase prices, how he later moved into magazines and cable, or the later stakes in Reddit, Charter and WBD. Watch the full episode for those.
