He Turns Free Stuff Into $10K+/Month

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-09-08 · 27,446 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Flipping free curbside appliances into $10K/month

This is a near-zero-capital side hustle with a real repair walkthrough on camera, so you can judge in 15 minutes whether appliance flipping is a hustle you'd actually do.

What it is: Chris flies to Utah to shadow Kyler, a 24-year-old with no technical training who pulls free or near-free washers and dryers off Facebook Marketplace and curbs, fixes them with cheap Amazon parts, and resells them for $200 to $1,000+, clearing over $10K/month.

The idea:

  • Tens of thousands of appliances hit the curb daily, many nicer than new units at Lowe's.
  • Most are discarded over a $5 to $20 part failure. Kyler calls it the "infinite money glitch."
  • Supply is free, parts are cheap, and the buyer pool (used appliances) is huge and local.

How it's carried out:

  • Space: Rent a cheap garage off Facebook Marketplace. Kyler pays $300/month.
  • Sourcing: Free listings on Facebook Marketplace plus curb pickups.
  • Training: YouTube University. Kyler learned everything from repair videos, no school.
  • Parts supplier: Amazon. A power cord is about $5; a full dryer repair kit (heating element, thermal fuse, thermostats) is about $20.
  • Tools: Screwdriver, a multimeter, and that's basically the shop.
  • Diagnosis flow shown on camera for a dead dryer:
  • Add a power cord (three bolts, middle wire to middle terminal).
  • Plug in, set to timed dry, test. No power.
  • Pull the back panel (nine screws) to expose components.
  • Test the thermal fuse with a multimeter continuity check. Beep means good, no beep means replace.
  • Swap the fuse from the $20 kit. Pinch, pull, plug in the new one.
  • Retest. In this case it still didn't spin, so more diagnosis follows (transcript cuts off here).
  • Economics: Target in the video is turning one free dryer into $1,080 by end of day.

Tools & links:

  • Facebook Marketplace — sourcing free appliances and cheap garage rentals, plus reselling.
  • Amazon — repair kits and parts, roughly $5 to $20 per fix.
  • GoHighLevel — sponsor link, not part of the business model.
  • TKOYES.COM — pitch your own money-making story to the show.

Why it matters for you: If you want a side hustle with almost no upfront capital and a skill you can learn from YouTube in a weekend, this is one of the more concrete, replicable playbooks Chris has shown.
