How LeBron turned his Nike contract into $300M

My First Million · 2026-09-11 · 15,860 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
LeBron securitized his Nike deal for $300M tax-free cash.

A two-minute masterclass in turning a future income stream into present-day, tax-free capital without selling the asset — the exact kind of money move the reader wants dissected.

What it is: LeBron James, while still on the Cavaliers, issued a 30-year bond secured by his lifetime Nike endorsement contract, pulling ~$300M in cash forward without selling the contract or paying income tax on the proceeds.

Who did it:

  • LeBron James, his business team, and his money manager engineered the structure
  • Collateral: LeBron's lifetime Nike contract (off-court endorsement income only, not NBA salary)

How it works:

  • Created a bond offering backed by the future cash flows of the Nike contract
  • 30-year term with a sub-5% coupon — LeBron pays roughly 5% interest per year
  • Received ~$300M up front instead of waiting decades for the lifetime deal to pay out
  • Structured as debt, not a sale — so the $300M is loan proceeds, not taxable income
  • Retains 100% of the Nike contract's upside; the contract is expected to pay out far more than $300M over its life
  • Only endorsement income is pledged; on-court basketball contracts are untouched

The money moves, itemized:

  • Securitize a long-dated, contractually guaranteed income stream (endorsement deal) instead of waiting on it
  • Borrow against the asset rather than selling it, so you keep the upside
  • Take proceeds as debt to avoid an income-tax event on the lump sum
  • Lock in cheap, long-term fixed-rate capital (30 years, under 5%) that the asset's own cash flow services
  • Deploy $300M today into investments compounding faster than the ~5% cost of capital
