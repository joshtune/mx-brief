She Makes $2K Every Saturday From Her Parents' Front Porch

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-15 · 316,811 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
20-year-old's porch micro bakery clears $2K in an hour

It's a near-zero-overhead side hustle blueprint: bake at home, sell one morning a week, sell out by 9am.

What it is: A home micro bakery (Little Bit Bakery) run by Angela, 20, from her parents' front porch. She sells bread, cookies, cakes and sourdough from a self-built cart every Saturday morning.

How it works:

  • Launch: Started in September, about 7 months before recording. She wasn't an influencer. She published what she was doing online and locals showed up.
  • Revenue: $2,000–$2,100 in sales per Saturday. She's sold out by 9am, with a line that can reach roughly 100 people.
  • Cadence: One sales day a week. She bakes all week and stocks a backstock line behind the front door. On Saturday she runs back and forth restocking.
  • Honor system: Customers add up their own totals at the cart. Her only theft so far was one fake pre-order pickup. She sometimes earns extra from tips.
  • Pre-orders: Customers order and pay online ahead of time. Pre-order bags sit behind the cart and she checks a QR code to verify them. She admits she skipped that check once, which is how the theft happened.
  • Payments in person: Venmo is the most popular, then Zelle, then cash. Standard transfers take 1–3 days and carry no fees. Instant transfer is the only paid option.
  • Cart build: She bought a ~$15 build plan on Etsy. Her parents and she built it with Home Depot materials and tools already in the garage.
  • Display: Decor came from Hobby Lobby, bought at 50% off. Chris jokes it looks like Hobby Lobby and a farmers market had a baby.
  • Transcript caveat: The transcript cuts off early. Marketing details, pricing, cottage-food licensing and product mix may be covered later in the episode, so treat anything beyond the points above as unverified.

Tools & links:

  • Hot Plate — pre-order and checkout platform many home bakers use. Customers pay by card and funds transfer to the baker.
  • Etsy — where she bought the ~$15 cart build plan.
  • Venmo / Zelle — walk-up payments via QR code, free on standard transfer.
  • beehiiv — Chris's sponsor, code CHRIS30. Unrelated to the idea.

Why it matters for you: This is a cheap, low-risk side hustle. You need a kitchen, a ~$15 plan and a weekend, and the whole business is one tight sales window with pre-orders smoothing demand. Check your state's cottage-food rules before copying it.
