/roast-room: How I Use Claude to Think Like the Top 1%

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-10-22 · 22,886 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Claude Code sub-agent council roasts your business decisions

Nate shows how a 'Roast Room' of sub-agents and per-persona knowledge files turns Claude Code into a debating advisory board, and the pattern is easy to copy.

What it is: A Claude Code setup where famous-founder personas (Bezos, Graham, Hormozi, Gary V, Martell, Wickman, Buffett) debate a business question, then deliver a final verdict. A localhost UI visualizes the debate and exports a PDF.

How it works:

  • Input: You pose a business question with options (A/B/C). The council roasts it and picks a winner. The demo ended on option C.
  • Seats: Each advisor has one fixed lens question. Buffett asks 'What does it do to cash, risk, and long-term value?' Hormozi asks 'Will people pay? How much? How do we close?' Wickman asks 'Can the business run this without the founder holding it together?'
  • Rounds: Advisors don't just opine. They argue with each other (Bezos vs Gary V, Graham vs Gary V, Hormozi vs Gary V, Buffett vs Hormozi), and then a final verdict is produced after three rounds.
  • Output: A downloadable PDF you can read in depth or share with your team.
  • The UI is optional: The localhost app is a visual layer only. The real engine is Claude Code running sub-agents, so you don't need the UI open.
  • Layer 1, agents (`.claude/agents/`): One short markdown file per council member (Bezos, Buffett, Gary V, Graham, etc.). Each covers how they debate, how they talk, and their lens, plus how they think, which sources they reference, and how they make decisions.
  • Layer 2, brains (`skills/roaster-room/brains/<member>/`): The deeper knowledge. Each member gets their own folder. Leila Hormozi's holds `hot` (her pillar: 'Can your team and your leadership carry this? Who has to change?'), `books`, `frameworks`, `positions`, `principles`, `sources`, `stories` and `voice`. The structure is the same for everyone, but the content differs.
  • Roster: 11 seats total. Seven defaults, plus four extras: Naval, Cody Sanchez, Sara Blakely and Leila Hormozi. It's fully swappable, so you can drop in your own personas.
  • Source material: Personas pull from public talks, lectures, books, principles and articles.
  • Orchestration demo: Nate asked Claude 'what are the other council members?' through Glaido, which now orchestrates chats between coding agents. Claude Code answered with the 11 seats.
