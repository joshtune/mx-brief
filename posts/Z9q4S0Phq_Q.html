Replace Google Photos with self-hosted Immich

Chuck's home lab now runs local AI (face and object detection, smart search) on his family's photos, and he wired it into his own family portal through Immich's API.

What it is: A tutorial on self-hosting Immich, a free open-source Google Photos replacement, on a spare laptop running Ubuntu Server 26.04.

How it works:

  • Motivation: Google holds every family photo and video, and Chuck's wife asked whether any of it was backed up locally. It wasn't, so the project moved up his list.
  • Storage: Photos and videos live on an external drive (or a few). A NAS is the next step up, but he skips it in this video. He also stresses a 3-2-1 backup, to be covered later. The tags suggest Backblaze B2 as the offsite copy.
  • Compute: The Immich server is a spare laptop, a VM, or an app on a NAS. Minimum specs are low and CPU-only works fine.
  • GPU note: His laptop has a GPU, which speeds up the machine-learning features. He says that opens up "amazing possibilities" in part two.
  • Remote access: Twingate, the video's sponsor, gives free access to Immich and the rest of his home lab from anywhere. That lets phones auto-backup and browse memories away from home.
  • Setup start: Flash Ubuntu Server, enable OpenSSH, find the IP, then SSH in from another machine. The docker compose install is in the tags, but the transcript cuts off before it.

The AI angle (what he's doing for his workflow):

  • Immich runs local AI: face recognition, object detection, smart photo search, memories and moments. No cloud API is involved.
  • He used Immich's API to build it into his custom family portal. He calls it "amazing" but doesn't show how in the part I could read.
  • A GPU-accelerated local AI follow-up is teased for part two.
  • Gap: The transcript ends right after the SSH login. The deployment steps and the portal integration aren't covered here.
