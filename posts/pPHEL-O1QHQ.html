AI companion app hits 100K+ paying iOS subscribers

A concrete breakdown of Tolan — an AI companion iOS app doing seven figures monthly off 100,000+ paying subscribers — including exact price tiers and the exact demographic paying for it.

Featured business: Tolan — an iOS app where you meet and customize a friendly AI companion/guide that helps you talk through whatever you're going through in life.

The numbers (as stated in the clip):

  • 100,000+ paying subscribers on the iOS app
  • ~$25/month for the top tier, "Tolan Plus" — the most popular tier
  • A lower-priced tier also exists, so blended ARPU sits under $25
  • Hosts push him on "100,000 × $25" — he says it's "not quite there," but confirms seven figures a month
  • Naive ceiling: 100K × $25 = $2.5M/mo; actual is lower but still 7 figures

How the product works:

  • Download the app, then talk to a character called the Oracle — a magical, god-like onboarding persona
  • The Oracle interviews you: hopes and dreams, what you're into, what you care about, what you're stressed about
  • That interview drives the customization of *your* Tolan — a personalized AI companion
  • Onboarding-as-character is the trick: the data collection that would feel like a boring signup form becomes part of the fiction

Who actually pays:

  • Core demo is 35–45 year olds
  • Skews heavily female — "mostly moms"
  • Not the tech-early-adopter crowd you'd assume for an AI app. That's the whole insight: the paying market for AI companions is emotional support, not productivity

The monetization playbook:

  • Consumer subscription, not ads — recurring revenue from day one
  • Multiple price tiers — a cheap entry point to convert, a $25 tier to capture the committed users
  • iOS-only distribution — App Store subscriptions, which means Apple's cut but also Apple's payment rails and discovery
  • Character-led onboarding doubles as the personalization engine and the retention hook

Tools & links:

Why it matters for you: This is the clearest recent case of an AI wrapper app that actually prints money — the moat isn't the model, it's the character design and the fact they found a non-obvious paying demographic (middle-aged moms) that nobody in tech was building for.
