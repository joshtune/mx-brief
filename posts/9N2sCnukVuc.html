Ranking social platforms by how much money they actually make

If you're building a side hustle, the real lesson isn't which platform pays best, it's that you should funnel attention into an audience you own (email) instead of renting it.

What it is: Codie ranks six platforms by monetization potential, then reveals the actual point: own your audience, don't rent it.

How it works:

  • X — F tier: Content dies fastest. Worst place to put effort if money is the goal.
  • TikTok — D tier: Big views, low pay, hard to sell. Her 2M followers moved only about a dozen tickets to her last event. Only worth it with a visual product.
  • LinkedIn — B tier: Sponsored posts run roughly $1,500-$8,000 each, plus the clients and hires it brings in.
  • Instagram — A tier: Sponsorships run $2,500-$7,500 per post. The real edge is buyers: she sold 100x more tickets there than on TikTok.
  • YouTube — A tier: Viewers sit with you for 30 minutes, so trust builds fast. Lower-tier sponsorships run $5,000-$15,000.
  • Newsletter — the winner: Subscribers hand you their inbox. It's the highest-intent audience you have, and the only one you own.

The instructional secret:

  • Sponsorships aren't the goal. The goal is working out where your audience is and converting that attention into something you own.
  • Rented attention (algorithm platforms) can vanish overnight. Owned attention (email list) compounds.
  • Follower count is a vanity metric. Conversion to buyers is the real one: 2M TikTok followers sold about a dozen tickets, while Instagram sold 100x more.

Why it matters for you: For a side hustle, pick one platform where your buyers already hang out, use it to feed an email list, and judge it by sales rather than views. Note that the sponsorship figures are her numbers at scale, so treat them as ceilings, not starting points.
