The Best Books for Entrepreneurs

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-29 · 87,764 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Three books that fix procrastination, selling, and standing out.

It's a 90-second reading list targeting the three things that actually kill side hustles — not shipping, not selling, and not being memorable.

What it is: A rapid-fire three-book recommendation (plus a plug for Codie's own new book) aimed at people trying to make money, not people trying to look smart.

How it works:

  • The War of Art — Steven Pressfield. Framed as the anti-procrastination book. Pressfield's core idea is "Resistance" — the internal force that shows up strongest right before work that matters. The fix isn't motivation, it's treating the work as a professional obligation you show up for regardless of mood.
  • Influence / Persuasion — Robert Cialdini. Codie's pitch: "most people do not know how to sell." The value is the pre-work — figuring out what to say in the pitch *before* you open your mouth. Cialdini's principles (reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity) are the checklist.
  • Unreasonable Hospitality — Will Guidara. The differentiation play. Guidara ran Eleven Madison Park; the thesis is that giving people unexpected, disproportionate service is a moat in a crowded market. Money follows hospitality, not the other way around.
  • Bonus: Owner Be Owned — Codie Sanchez. Her new book, positioned as the systems she used to run multiple nine-figure companies without losing her life to them.
  • The implicit structure: book one gets you *shipping*, book two gets you *closing*, book three gets you *remembered*. That's the whole funnel for a small operator.

Tools & links:

  • The War of Art — Steven Pressfield — beating Resistance and shipping consistently
  • Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion — Robert Cialdini — the six principles behind why people say yes
  • Unreasonable Hospitality — Will Guidara — service as competitive differentiation
  • Contrarian Thinking — Codie's site, where *Owner Be Owned* is promoted

Why it matters for you: The instructional secret is the sequencing — a side hustle dies at one of three checkpoints (you don't start, you can't close, nobody remembers you), and each book is a targeted fix for exactly one of them.
