Why this billionaire didn't invest in Facebook

My First Million · 2026-09-16 · 7,188 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
A billionaire passed on Facebook, mistaking it for a dorm dating app.

A rare first-person account of turning down Facebook at the ground floor, and why the reasoning made sense at the time.

What it is: A billionaire investor recounts declining to invest in early-stage Facebook because his son-in-law was a Harvard classmate of Mark Zuckerberg's.

How it works:

  • His son-in-law was in Zuckerberg's Harvard class and pitched him on investing.
  • At that stage, the product wasn't a social network — it was framed as a dating tool: photos of students you could browse and message.
  • The only stated growth plan at the time was expanding from Harvard to Yale and Princeton — nothing about a mass-market, non-student platform.
  • He passed, reasoning it was a niche campus dating app, not a scalable business.
  • He admits even Zuckerberg likely didn't foresee the platform Facebook would become.
