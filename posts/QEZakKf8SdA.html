M5 MacBook Air Against Every Generation for Dev Work

Alex Ziskind · 2026-08-14 · 40,273 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Five MacBook Air generations benchmarked on dev workloads.

If you're eyeing an Air for local dev or AI work, the M1→M5 gains are real but incremental — nowhere near enough to justify jumping generations, and a Mac mini still buys more compute per dollar.

What it is: A head-to-head benchmark of the M1, M2, M3, M4, and M5 MacBook Airs on developer workloads, framing what Apple actually fixed with the M5.

How it works:

  • Primary test shown: single-core C++ quicksort over 10 million integers — deliberately single-threaded, so it isolates per-core performance rather than core count.
  • M1: 3:38
  • M2: 3:42 — *slower than the M1*. The M2 is the outlier loser of the group; the efficiency/thermal tradeoffs of that generation show up under sustained single-core load in a fanless chassis.
  • M3: 2:57 — the first genuinely meaningful jump (~19% faster than M1).
  • M4: 2:31 — biggest generational gain in the set.
  • M5: 2:19 — better, but incremental (~8% over M4).
  • Net: M1 → M5 is roughly a 36% single-core improvement across five generations. Real, but not a per-year upgrade case.
  • The rest of the video covers where older models still hold up for dev work — the M1 in particular punching well above its age.

On the AI/hardware angle (what's NOT here):

  • This is a CPU single-core test, not an LLM inference test — no tokens/sec, no GPU or Neural Engine numbers, no memory-bandwidth comparison.
  • For local AI, the Air is the wrong axis anyway: it's fanless, so sustained loads throttle, and it caps at lower unified memory than the mini. Memory bandwidth and RAM ceiling — not single-core speed — are what gate model size and inference throughput.
  • Rough price framing: an M4 Mac mini starts around $599 and gives you an actively cooled chassis with the same class of silicon. A MacBook Air starts around $999 for less thermal headroom. If the machine isn't leaving your desk, the mini wins on dollars-per-token every time.
