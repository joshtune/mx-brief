Weekly AI roundup, mostly new video generation models.

Almost nothing here touches web app development — it's two video generation models and industry drama, so skip unless you're shipping video features.

What it is: Matt's weekly AI news roundup. The transcript covers industry "drama and shake-ups" plus two new text-to-video models — ByteDance's SeaDance 2.5 and Black Forest Labs' Flux 3 Video.

How it works:

  • SeaDance 2.5 (ByteDance) — up to 30 seconds of video per generation; accepts up to 30 images, 10 video clips, and 10 audio clips as reference material in a single pass. Has timestamp-level control for targeted audio/video edits.
  • Generates audio alongside video, and stamps an AI watermark on output.
  • Coherence still breaks down over long clips — Matt's scuba demo turns a jellyfish into crystals mid-shot on an angle change.
  • Flux 3 Video (Black Forest Labs) — up to 20-second clips, pitched as "designed to model reality as accurately as possible" (read: better physics). Already live across the usual platforms.
  • Flux 3 Video auto-generated multiple camera angles from a single prompt rather than one static shot — notable if you care about shot composition without stitching.
  • Cost reality check: Matt paid $18/month on Dreamina and got roughly three generations out of it. These models are expensive per call, which matters if you were considering wrapping one in a product.
  • Ambient theme: AI slop and the collapsing gap between real and generated footage. No technical takeaway, just context.

Tools & links:

  • dreamina.capcut.com — currently the place to use SeaDance 2.5; ByteDance's own front end
  • Runway — has Flux 3 Video now; Matt tested the same prompts here
  • Leonardo — also carrying Flux 3 Video
  • futuretools.io — Matt's AI tool directory
  • Genspark SecondBrain Note — sponsor, a physical notebook product, not a dev tool

Why it matters for you: Video generation is a vertical that doesn't intersect with web app work — no APIs, SDKs, or integration patterns discussed, and the per-generation cost makes it impractical to build on right now. The only transferable signal is that these models are now cheap enough to reach consumer platforms, so if a client ever asks for generated video, Runway and Leonardo are the aggregation layer to hit rather than a raw model provider.
