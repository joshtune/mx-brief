NetworkChuck · 2026-11-11 · 96,406 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Float switch + Raspberry Pi GPIO = toilet telemetry

It's a real homelab debugging move (timestamp the physical event, then correlate it with NAS and network drops), and Chuck says AI is what made a dumb project like this doable.

What it is: Chuck gives his toilet an IP address by putting a float switch in the tank and reading it over GPIO on a Raspberry Pi. The goal is millisecond-accurate flush timestamps.

How it works:

  • The problem: When his editors flush, everyone's machines drop their NAS connection and the internet sometimes hiccups. He doesn't know why, and he's tired of asking "did y'all just flush?"
  • The approach: Log flushes with precise timestamps so he can line them up against NAS and network anomalies. His line: "that's just good IT work."
  • Hardware: An old Raspberry Pi 3 Model B+ (he only needed the GPIO pins, so any SBC with GPIO works), plus a float switch from an Amazon multi-pack, mounted inside the toilet tank.
  • Sensor choice: He considered a handle sensor, a motion sensor and a camera, and settled on the in-tank float switch as the best option.
  • AI angle: He says "We have AI now, we can just do things, even dumb things." The transcript cuts off before the build, so how he used AI (code generation, wiring help, etc.) isn't shown.
  • Sponsor segment: Wiz AIDR, AI detection and response. It's pitched via a prompt-injection demo where an AI agent runs attacker code, steals cloud creds and opens a reverse shell, and Wiz stitches CloudTrail, DNS and model logs into an attack graph.
