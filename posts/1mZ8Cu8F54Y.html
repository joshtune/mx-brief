How to find and exploit the "glitch" that can make any business take off

My First Million · 2026-10-07 · 25,277 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Angel Studios founder Jeff Harmon on exploiting marketing glitches

Jeff Harmon went from selling a tongue scraper out of a dentist's garage to running Angel Studios (~$500M revenue, per Shaan), and this episode walks through the plays that got him there.

What it is: Shaan Puri interviews Jeff Harmon (Angel Studios founder, author of *Poop to Gold*) about finding your "glitch", meaning places where attention is cheap and nobody else is looking. Note: the transcript I was given cuts off early, so only the Orabrush origin story is covered below. The Angel Studios half isn't in it.

Who: Jeff Harmon, a marketer with no film background who ended up running Angel Studios. Shaan pitches it as "maybe one of the top 10 TV movie studios in the world" at "almost 500 million in revenue". Those are the host's claims, not verified.

How he made money (Orabrush, from the transcript):

  • Spotted the real market — On his last day of class, a dentist (Dr. Bob) asked the class how to sell his Orabrush tongue scraper, with boxes of inventory in his garage. The class said only ~8% of people would buy online and he should partner with P&G or J&J. Jeff objected that 8% is ~30M people in the US alone, and Dr. Bob hired him.
  • Bought an audience instead of building one — Dr. Bob paid $5,000 for a Facebook page called "Kisses" (~1.3M followers). Jeff renamed it Orabrush, which gave the brand a huge following overnight. Bad breath tied to kissing was the hook.
  • Sold the benefit, not the product — The pitch was the happy ending, a version of you that can kiss without bad-breath shame, with the scraper as the tool.
  • First attempt flopped — Spamming the bought audience sold "hardly anything". Google ads ran at roughly $3–$5 spent per $1 of sales, which is a loss on a cheap product.
  • Found the glitch: YouTube — Late-night watching of early vlogger iJustine gave him the idea. She used jump cuts against a plain white background. He figured they could make something at that level cheaply.
  • Assembled a team from roommates — Evan Graham (film major who worked free on sets) and Joel Ari (screenwriter and comedian). The transcript ends as Jeff pitches them.

The core lesson:

  • Every business has a glitch: a place where attention is underpriced and competitors aren't looking.
  • Paid ads on a low-ticket product lose money. Underpriced attention (YouTube, at the time) is where it works.
  • Ignore the "only 8% will buy" framing and ask how big 8% actually is.
