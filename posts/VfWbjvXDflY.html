How to Handle "10x More Efficient" Expectations From Your Manager

A Life Engineered · 2026-08-01 · 4,193 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Scripting the 'I'm at capacity' talk in the AI era

Your manager is about to assume AI makes you 10x, and this gives you the exact framing to push back without sounding like you're dodging work.

What it is: A short conversational script for responding when your manager loads more work on you because "AI writes the code now."

The dynamic he's naming:

  • AI tooling arrives → leadership assumes a productivity multiplier
  • The multiplier gets banked before it's proven, and shows up as extra scope on your plate
  • Saying plain "no" reads as low performance; saying nothing reads as consent

The script (three moves):

  • Establish shared value first — "I'm doing a lot of things for you, for the team, for our shared goals. I'm being very productive." Frame it as *for you*, not *to you*.
  • Assert the constraint — "There's a priority and a value to the things I do. I'm now at capacity." State it as fact, not complaint.
  • Bring the proposal, not the problem — "Can we move some of the lower priority things off my plate so I have room for higher value? Here are my ideas. What can we do together?"

Three ways to free capacity (offer these, don't wait to be asked):

  • Stop doing it entirely — kill the low-value work
  • Automate it — invest once, reclaim the recurring cost
  • Delegate down — hand it to someone more junior who grows from it

Why it lands as win-win: It's simple math. The manager already agrees your output is valuable. You're not reducing output — you're reallocating it toward higher value. Nothing for them to argue with.

What to prepare for:

  • Have your actual work inventory ranked by value *before* the conversation — the proposal only works if you can name the low-priority items on sight
  • Expect this conversation to recur as AI expectations ratchet up; treat it as a recurring negotiation, not a one-time fix
