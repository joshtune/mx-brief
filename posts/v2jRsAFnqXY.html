How I Would Get A Small Business To $84k/Month

Codie Sanchez · 2026-10-25 · 33,262 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Live coaching: fire bad clients, raise prices, scale a styling agency

The secret is the anti-client: define who you refuse to serve, then price for your best customer, and a side hustle gets more profitable without more work.

What it is: Codie coaches Tannya, owner of a $350k/yr Seattle styling agency, toward $1M using the "12 profit levers" framework. Only three levers are covered: persona, pricing and product.

How it works:

  • The problem: Tannya's messaging is scattered across multiple avatars and tries to speak to everyone. Codie's fix is to pick one customer and exclude the rest.
  • Step 1, name the red-flag avatar (worst client): Under $150k income, a $1-2k budget per shopping trip. They don't trust the process, take over decisions, and then get a worse result.
  • Step 2, name the best client: C-level execs and lawyers who travel and earn $500k+. They hand it over, ask few questions and spend $7-20k per trip. They come back 3-4 times a year.
  • Step 3, compare the two on income and spend: The gap is 5-10x per transaction for roughly the same effort. The best client is the more profitable one by a wide margin.
  • Step 4, check pricing: Codie says $4k is "pretty cheap" for the best client. Tannya can't remember her last price increase, which is the tell that she's underpriced.
  • The anti-sell (the instructional secret): Write your "commandments in reverse", meaning the rules that make someone not welcome. For Tannya:
  • Must have income of a couple hundred thousand or more.
  • Must be in Seattle for in-person work.
  • Must be an executive.
  • Must want to hand it over, not pick their own clothes.
  • Why it works: Nailing your main people and excluding everyone else makes you more money. Ads and social posts then speak to one person ("you hate shopping") instead of a crowd.
  • Note: The transcript cuts off mid-ad-copy. The product lever and the 30-day check-in results aren't in the available text. The title's "$84k/month" is roughly the $1M/yr target.
