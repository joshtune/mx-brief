This Might Be the Most Ridiculous PC I’ve Ever Built

Alex Ziskind · 2026-08-27 · 18,636 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Threadripper build with dual RTX Pro 6000 GPUs.

This is the x86 counterweight to a Mac Studio for local LLM work — dual RTX Pro 6000s give you ~192GB of real VRAM, but at roughly 4–5x the price of a maxed Mac Studio and a 2500W power bill to match.

What it is: A first-time Threadripper workstation build aimed at running local AI models on two NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 GPUs.

The parts list (from the transcript):

  • Motherboard: ASUS Pro WS TRX50-SAGE WIFI — Threadripper 7000/TRX50 workstation board, ~$900
  • CPU: Threadripper 7000-series ("1900" tier mentioned) — Alex calls it "the most expensive box" in the pile
  • Memory: ECC RDIMM — required by Threadripper, not optional; adds meaningful cost over consumer DDR5
  • Cooler: SilverStone Threadripper cooler (sTR5 socket, huge contact plate)
  • Storage: Crucial 1TB SSD
  • PSU: be quiet!/Hella-class 2500W unit — this is the tell for how much power two Pro 6000s pull
  • GPUs: 2× NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 (Blackwell workstation card, 96GB GDDR7 each)

The number that actually matters — VRAM and price:

  • 2× RTX Pro 6000 = ~192GB VRAM, ~$8,500–10,000 *per card* street
  • GPU cost alone: ~$17,000–20,000
  • Rest of the build (Threadripper + TRX50 + ECC + 2500W PSU + case/cooler): ~$4,000–5,000
  • Total: roughly $21,000–25,000

Compared to what you'd actually consider:

  • Mac mini M4 Pro, 64GB: ~$2,000 — ~48GB usable for models, runs 70B quantized slowly
  • Mac Studio M3 Ultra, 512GB unified: ~$9,500 — *more* addressable memory than the dual-GPU box, but far lower memory bandwidth and no CUDA
  • This Threadripper box: ~$22,000 — less total memory than a maxed Studio, but real CUDA, ~1.8TB/s per card, and token generation several times faster on models that fit in 96GB
  • Power: the Mac Studio idles under 20W and peaks around 250W. This build needs a 2500W supply and a dedicated circuit if you push both cards.

Tools & links:

Why it matters for you: If your question is "what do I buy to run AI locally," this video draws the ceiling — ~$22K and 2500W for CUDA-class inference — against the Mac mini/Studio floor at $2K–9.5K, and for most local-model work the Mac side wins on dollars per GB of usable memory while this box wins on raw speed.
