Codie Sanchez on why wash-and-fold beats walk-in laundromats

It's a quick look at where the real money in laundromats is: pickup-and-delivery wash-and-fold, not the coin-op floor.

What it is: A short clip of Codie Sanchez on the economics of laundromats, including one $3M/yr location and a career owning 25-30 of them. The transcript we have is only a fragment, so this covers only what was said in it.

How it works (what she did to make money):

  • Volume of ownership: She owned roughly 25-30 laundromats over her career, so this was a repeatable playbook, not a one-off.
  • The big one: She invested in a single laundromat doing about $3M/year, roughly $8K/day.
  • Margins: That kind of operator runs about 15% margins, which is roughly $450K/year in profit on $3M (my math, not hers).
  • Where the margin comes from: Wash-and-fold, not walk-in customers.
  • The wash-and-fold model: A customer leaves a bag outside their door. You pick it up, wash it, and drop it back off.
  • Target customer: Higher-level customers, with the 'single mom' as her example. They pay for convenience, and that is where you make more money.
  • Why it's a moat: It needs cars, logistics and some oversight. It's more complex than a walk-in laundromat, so fewer people run it well.
  • Baseline benchmark: The average laundromat makes about $100K-$500K/year at 10-15% margins.

Not in this transcript:

  • Exit multiples
  • Per-location profit beyond the numbers above
