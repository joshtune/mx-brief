Weekly AI roundup: ChatGPT Images 2.5, Meta's Muse agent.

Only one nugget touches your web apps: the new ChatGPT Images 2.5 API ships in two price/quality tiers with much better reference-photo consistency, so if you generate or edit user images, it's worth a re-test.

What it is: A news roundup. The transcript cuts off after two stories, so this covers what's actually there: OpenAI's image model update and Meta's new personal agent.

ChatGPT Images 2.5 (the builder-relevant bit):

  • Headline improvement is consistency: reference-photo edits keep the face, pose, and composition intact (the "put this person in a suit" test actually looks like the person now).
  • Edits preserve fine structure: fingers, object shapes, and layout stay fixed while contents change (their ticket demo).
  • New Sketch feature: draw on a canvas in ChatGPT (web and mobile), then use the sketch as a reference image with a prompt. Fun, not a product feature you'd build on.
  • Available in ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work, and Codex across all tiers.
  • Two API variants: one higher-detail and pricier, one faster and cheaper. That's the lever for your apps: pick the cheap one for thumbnails or previews, the detailed one for final output.

Meta Muse (skim it):

  • "Personal agent for everyone" that runs in its own secure VM, not on your machine.
  • Connects to email, calendar, apps, plus your Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp context.
  • Browses, fills forms, negotiates, keeps working after you close the app, and pauses for approval before sending email or spending money.
  • Meta claims it can't see passwords or payment methods. Consumer product, no developer surface mentioned.
