Inside China’s $400/month fat-loss camps

My First Million · 2026-11-24 · 38,463 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
China's all-inclusive, prison-style weight-loss camps

It's a business built on one idea: sell people a place where they can't cheat, at $400 a month.

What it is: Chinese fat-loss camps where overweight people check in for 30 days to do nothing but lose weight. The hosts pitch them as "a prison just for weight loss."

How it works (the business):

  • Price: $400/month, all-inclusive. Roughly $13/day covers food, housing and programming.
  • Product: Controlled calories, controlled movement, and no way out of either. As the hosts put it, "the math is math."
  • Meals: 3 low-calorie meals a day, and nothing else.
  • Exercise: 4 required workouts a day.
  • Accountability: Weigh-ins every morning and every night, so progress is visible and public.
  • Enforcement: A giant sign outside reads "no food delivery allowed or you will also be locked up." Campers caught smuggling food in get locked up.
  • Proof of result: A YouTuber visited and dropped 5 lbs in the first 48 hours. That's the viral hook and free marketing.

What they did to make money (from this excerpt):

  • Sold willpower-as-a-service. The camp takes the decision-making away, and that's what customers pay for.
  • Charged a flat monthly fee with everything bundled in, so there's no upsell friction.
  • Used a fixed 30-day program, so every customer has a clear start, finish and before/after photo.
  • Got free distribution from the "prison" angle. It's shocking enough that YouTubers cover it.
  • Cut costs with low-cal meals, which are cheap to serve, and a standardized daily schedule.

Caveat: The transcript we have is only the intro, about 150 words. The founders, occupancy, margins, revenue and customer acquisition aren't covered here. Treat the money list above as inferred from the stated pricing and structure, and check the full episode for real numbers.

Why it matters for you: The takeaway is to sell the removal of choice, not information. Pair it with a bundled flat price and a shocking, shareable hook, and you get a business that markets itself.
