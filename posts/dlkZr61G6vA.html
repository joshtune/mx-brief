What Hormozi's team actually automates with AI

It's a commentary reel, not a build tutorial, but it gives concrete benchmarks (90% ticket resolution, 200 units/month from one text agent) and a workflow-decomposition framework you can reuse for client builds.

What it is: Nate's digest of 200+ Hormozi videos and posts on how his companies use AI, where it works, where it doesn't, and the $350K mistake. This is commentary with quotes, not a build walkthrough.

How it works:

  • Context: On Sept 30, Hormozi told his team no more AI budgets. They spend 16x more on AI per employee than the average tech-forward company, and revenue is flat against rising AI usage.
  • Support agents: 5 AI agents handled about 120,000 tickets during the book launch and resolved 90% with no human. Most tickets were people asking where to get their free stuff.
  • Media team: Output tripled in a month with no added headcount. Prompts take over 30-50% of the workload, so one person does the work of three, then five, then ten.
  • Core framework: Don't hire an "editor". List the 5 things an editor does and make each one a workflow.
  • Clip workflow (Hormozi Hotline): AI reads the transcript, finds the highest-tension moments, and starts the clip there. It then strips ums and ahs and cuts the clip.
  • Nate's parallel: He records, hands the footage to Claude, which cuts retakes and dead air, and he keeps building editing pipelines over time.
  • Writing: Tweets went from about 90 min/day to about 90 min/week (roughly 7x). AI generates ideas and a human iterates, so it is not automated.
  • Content ideas: Meetings are recorded and transcribed, then AI scans them plus his calendar for the most interesting moments. Videos are built from real events that week.
  • Sales scorecard:
  • Jan 2025: predicted an AI appointment setter was about 3 months away.
  • Apr 2025: an AI SDR matched the human team.
  • Now: humans are still hired for SDR and phone work, because after 2 years on voice AI they still beat the best AI.
  • One AI text agent sells about 200 units/month.
  • The $350K mistake: A business paid 11 VAs about $11K/month for data cleaning. It then spent $350K building an AI system to replace them, and that work wasn't even the business's constraint. The transcript cuts off mid-story.
