Five AI automations with real 2026 buyer demand.

Nate reverse-engineers what businesses are actually paying for right now and spells out the exact portfolio build for each — scope, guardrails, and the metrics to prove it works.

What it is: A demand-ranked list of five AI automation workflows, each with a buildable portfolio spec, drawn from surveys, marketplaces, job listings, case studies, and community chatter.

The filter he used: Only workflows with recent buyer demand, real deployments, a clear buyer, and a measurable result made the cut. He's explicit that it's evidence-ranked, not a market-share census.

Coverage: The five span customer acquisition, service, back-office, and employee ops — learn the patterns and you can map automation opportunities across any business. (Transcript cuts off after #2 and the sponsor read; #3–5 are in the full video.)

#1 — Lead qualification & follow-up:

  • Lead arrives → enrich → score against company rules → collect missing fields → update CRM → follow up or book a meeting
  • Evidence: Salesforce says Siemens runs this across 2,800 inbound leads/week using ~50 verification rules
  • The 50 rules ARE the product — the value is encoding how *that* company defines a good lead, not the plumbing
  • Portfolio build: pick one vertical (commercial cleaning, HVAC), capture + enrich, score, create the CRM record, route by territory or service, and hand the salesperson a short explanation of the score
  • Guardrail: low-confidence and unusual leads route to a human, not the trash, until testing dials it in
  • Metrics: response time, % of qualified leads that book
  • Anti-pattern he calls out: don't build 10 agents talking to each other. One reliable workflow + clear rules + human handoff + clean CRM record does the job

#2 — Customer support:

  • Answers only from approved sources, pulls relevant context, executes a few safe actions, escalates risky cases with a conversation summary
  • Evidence: agentic AI adoption in service orgs jumped 39% (2025) → 66% (2026); 70% of teams already running service agents saw measurable value within 60 days
  • E-commerce demo scope: order status, return eligibility, address changes — pull the real order and policy, verify identity before mutating anything
  • Always escalate: refunds, cancellations, suspicious requests, anything sensitive
  • Handoff payload must include summary, sources used, actions taken, recommended next steps — "don't make the human start from zero"
  • Metrics: correct resolution rate without a human, plus reopen rate — high resolution means nothing if customers keep coming back because the answer was wrong
