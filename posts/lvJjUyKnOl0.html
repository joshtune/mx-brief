The Most Overlooked Businesses Anyone Can Start (One Hit $800K)

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-27 · 60,318 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
11 real side-hustle stories, starting with $800K brick mailboxes

If you want a side hustle you can run around a day job, this is a menu of proven, low-startup-cost businesses with real prices, first-customer tactics, and revenue numbers from people who actually did it.

What it is: Chris rounds up 11 listener-reported side hustle stories (self-reported, not audited) and breaks down what to charge, where the first customer is, and the key tool for each. The transcript covers the intro and the first story; the rest (pickleball courts in Nebraska, etc.) isn't in the excerpt.

How it works (headline case: Andrew, brick mailbox repair, Fort Worth TX):

  • The idea: Fix and rebuild brick mailboxes. Big masonry outfits won't touch a $400 job, so small-job demand is wide open. In DFW, someone backs into one every week, and cheap builder-grade mailboxes go crooked within a couple of years thanks to Texas soil.
  • Startup: About $2,000. He kept his medical-device day job and puts in 15-20 hrs/week.
  • First customer: Knocked on doors on his own street and offered to do a new-build neighbor's mailbox.
  • Pricing: $600-$1,500 per job (patch vs. full rebuild). Materials on a rebuild run a couple hundred bucks, so one mailbox a weekend is real money.
  • The unlock: Do the first job at cost for a before/after photo. Take that photo to the HOA board, since an HOA is one buyer that owns about 200 mailboxes.
  • The expansion: Mailboxes opened the door to HOA work like retaining walls and entrances. Saying yes to the "off-brand" jobs the Twitter gurus say to refuse led to 3 crews and 2 admins in 2 years.
  • Numbers: About $800K in 2 years. Best month $80K at about 30% gross margin and 15% net.
  • Lead gen: Watch Nextdoor for "anyone know a mason?" posts (few, but highly motivated). A website is fine, but a Google Business Profile with 10+ photos is what makes the phone ring.
  • Failure worth noting: He also tried washer/dryer rentals, got 60 customers, and shut it down because it was painful. Chris says he'll revisit it later.
  • Honest caveat: Taking profit out of a young business is psychologically hard.

Tools & links:

Why it matters for you: The pattern to steal is simple: pick an unglamorous, small-ticket local service the big players ignore, land one cheap first job for the photo, then sell the proof to a repeat buyer like an HOA.
