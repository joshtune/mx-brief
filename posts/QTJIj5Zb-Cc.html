Same game prompt, four Claude budgets, $1 to $1,000

It shows what each budget tier buys with Claude (single cheap model up to an 8-agent team), but it's an experiment and not a how-to, so don't expect a build guide.

What it is: Nate gives Claude one prompt, an open-world driving game set in Miami, at four budgets ($1, $10, $100, $1,000). Each run keeps building until the money runs out.

How it works:

  • $1 tier (Haiku, the cheapest model): Buildings are ugly boxes, but it's a real game with missions, traffic lights and a radio. The transcript says it cost "about 98", which is almost certainly 98 cents.
  • $10 tier (transcript says "Sonic", almost certainly Sonnet): Adds cops that chase you, rival racers, and rain that makes roads slippery.
  • $100 tier (Opus, a team of 3 agents): This is where it feels like a product, with a title screen, a garage and car upgrades.
  • $1,000 tier (Fable 5.2, the most expensive model, a team of 8 agents): The first ~$70 was planning only, and the game was a gray road with pink boxes at that point. One build round then ran 3+ hours and cost $312. The result was a full city with a speedboat, ambulance ships, a story where characters call your phone, and buyable businesses that earn money while you drive.
  • Pattern across tiers: More budget brought a bigger model, then multi-agent teams, then a long planning phase. Each step up added game systems (traffic, cops, weather, progression, economy), not just polish.
