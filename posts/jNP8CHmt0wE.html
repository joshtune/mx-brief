Buying a Tesla should be as easy as buying a pizza

My First Million · 2026-07-22 · 17,304 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
How Tesla cut car-buying from 64 clicks to 10.

A firsthand look at how radically simplifying product options unlocked both a better checkout and a cheaper factory — a lesson in constraint-as-strategy.

What it is: A Tesla insider recounts how Elon pushed the online car-buying flow from 64 clicks down to ~10, using Domino's pizza checkout as the benchmark.

How it works:

  • Baseline: buying a Tesla online took 64 clicks; buying a Domino's pizza took 10 thumb taps. Elon set 10 as the target.
  • The insider pulled data on build-to-order combos: of 360,000 possible configurations, customers were effectively only buying two.
  • He took that to manufacturing head Greg Reichow and engineering head Doug Field, asking what collapsing to two configs would do for the factory.
  • Manufacturing's answer: it totally simplifies the factory — fewer SKUs, less complexity, cheaper production. They'd been waiting for someone to force this.
  • Elon, described as "super rational," heard both sides and greenlit it.
  • Result: today Tesla's buy flow offers just two or three configurations, not 360k.

The playbook (how the decision got made):

  • Benchmark against the best consumer UX, not your industry (Domino's, not other car sites).
  • Follow the data — 360k options existed but demand clustered on two.
  • Sell it internally by aligning incentives — the checkout fix also solved manufacturing's complexity problem, so ops became an ally, not a blocker.
