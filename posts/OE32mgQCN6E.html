Bill Perkins on dying with zero and spending life well

You get the origin story of Bill Perkins, a natural gas derivatives trader who made his fortune short-selling winter risk premium, and a preview of his 'Die With Zero' philosophy for spending it.

What it is: Episode 878 of My First Million. Shaan Puri and Sam Parr interview Bill Perkins, author of *Die With Zero*, about how he made his money and how he thinks about using it. The transcript we have covers only the opening, which is mostly the money-making half.

How he made his money (featured individual summary):

  • Start: Clerk ('assistant peon') on the New York Mercantile Exchange floor when the natural gas futures contract launched. He chased that market on purpose.
  • Brokerage: Became an over-the-counter broker. He made moderate money but hated brokering.
  • The pivot: In 1995, energy deregulation hit. Firms recruited New York people to run derivatives desks in Houston. He moved to Houston and became a trader, which is where the real money started.
  • The trade: He traded natural gas derivatives (futures, options, spreads, betting on price direction or volatility direction).
  • The edge: He describes himself as a 'glorified insurance agent', not a forecaster. He traded against a theoretical fair value and charged a premium for risk, instead of out-guessing other smart traders.
  • The core bet: He was usually short natural gas. Every winter, buyers fear price spikes and overpay for hedges. He sold them futures. The winter risk premium usually bled out and prices fell, so most of his profit came from being short over time.
  • Scale: Per the hosts, he made close to $1B in profit for the firm that employed him.
  • Personal take: He was an employee, not the owner. He says he should have put far more of his own money in the fund. His old logic was 'no job and no money is the one unacceptable state', so he kept his capital out of the fund. In hindsight, he says a young person with a re-hirable skill set should concentrate risk and bet the farm.

What to steal (money-making list):

  • Get close to a brand-new market early (he joined the exchange when natural gas futures launched).
  • Follow regulatory change to where the talent shortage is (deregulation led to the Houston desks).
  • Sell insurance-like products where fear inflates prices (winter risk premium).
  • Anchor on fair value and a distribution of outcomes, not predictions.
  • Take more personal risk early in your career, when you can get another job.
