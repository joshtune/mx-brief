The $2 billion deal behind Poppi

Why Poppi sold to PepsiCo for $2B

A short, blunt breakdown of the exact constraint that forced a $500M+ revenue beverage brand to sell — distribution contracts, not product-market fit, decide who wins in soda.

What it is: A Poppi insider explaining why the prebiotic soda brand took PepsiCo's ~$1.95B acquisition instead of staying independent.

The business: Poppi — apple-cider-vinegar prebiotic soda, founded by Allison and Stephen Ellsworth (originally "Mother Beverage," Shark Tank 2018, Rohan Oza deal). Rebranded to Poppi in 2020, went hard on TikTok/influencer marketing, became the #1 growth story in functional soda. Acquired by PepsiCo, announced March 2025.

The wall they hit (the actual story):

  • Poppi was the *official soda of the Lakers* and still couldn't be sold inside the stadium — Coke owned the pouring contract.
  • Same lockout at Madison Square Garden, Taco Bell, Subway. Every high-traffic venue in America is pre-sold to Coke or Pepsi on multi-year exclusivity.
  • Marketing can create demand; it cannot create shelf space or a fountain line. That's the ceiling.

The exit math — only three buyers exist:

  • Keurig Dr Pepper
  • PepsiCo
  • Coca-Cola
  • Poppi talked to all three. Price is a function of *timing* — which portfolio gap is hot on that buyer's roadmap this quarter.

The alternative path (Celsius model):

  • Stay independent, IPO, then sign a distribution deal where the giant takes a minority stake instead of the whole company.
  • Pepsi distributes Celsius and holds a small equity position. You keep control and the upside; you give up the premium of a full sale.

Why Pepsi won the deal:

  • Pepsi is actively prioritizing "better-for-you" — pulling artificial dyes out of chips, repositioning Gatorade against Prime/Electrolit.
  • Poppi filled a hole Pepsi couldn't build fast enough internally.
  • Stated buy criteria, in order: the vision, the data, the story, the team, the brand.

Playbook takeaways:

  • Identify the chokepoint in your category *early* — in CPG it's distribution, in software it's the app store / cloud marketplace / default integration.
  • Run a real three-way process. One bidder is a valuation; three bidders is an auction.
  • Sell into the acquirer's *current* strategic obsession, not your own metrics. Pepsi wasn't buying revenue, it was buying a health-halo answer.
  • Have the data ready. "They saw the data" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

Why it matters for you: The whole $2B outcome came down to one structural fact — three companies own the pipes — which is the same shape as every platform-dependent business you'll ever build on.
