What it is: A career-framing video arguing that the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role is a high-paying job that overlaps with running an AI automation agency. The transcript ends mid-sponsor-read, so the promised roadmap and where to apply are not covered here.

How it works:

  • The role: The FDE role started at Palantir. An AI vendor embeds an engineer at a customer to build the product into their messy production systems, so the customer gets an outcome instead of a login.
  • Anthropic's definition: Kevin Bay, ex-Palantir and now on Anthropic's applied AI team, calls an FDE "a customer-facing software engineer". The customer buys an outcome, not software or hours.
  • What Anthropic's FDE posting lists as deliverables: MCP servers, sub-agents and agent skills. Nate says this is the same work many viewers already do in Claude Code, just with real company stakes.
  • Why demand is high: Nate cites the MIT report (95% of companies get zero return from generative AI spend). He says FDE postings grew more than 10x last year.
  • Pay data: Nate pulled about 200 unique live FDE postings from 24 AI companies. The typical base range is around $180K–$275K, and median total pay with stock and bonuses is about $230K. OpenAI has a posting at up to $325K base.
  • Nate's angle: He ran an AI automation agency and later a consultancy. He says that was effectively an FDE team for small businesses such as real estate agencies and HVAC companies. The job was working out what the client actually needed, then building it so it worked for them.
  • Who fits: Nate says it's "not even that technical". He says software engineers, consultants and former founders are all landing these jobs.
  • Sponsor segment (Multiverse Computing CompactifAI API): It serves GLM 5.3 at $0.20 per million input tokens and $0.64 per million output tokens. Nate says that is the lowest blended price on Artificial Analysis. His example is 80M input and 20M output tokens a month, which costs about $200 on the model maker's API and under $30 on CompactifAI.
