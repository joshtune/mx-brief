18 Months of Pricing AI Automations in 21 Mins

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-08-01 · 20,600 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
How to price AI automation builds using client math

Zero build content — this is the number-by-number breakdown of a real $5,500 appointment-setter deal, so you can defend a price instead of guessing at one.

What it is: Nate's pricing playbook after selling 100+ AI automation systems, walked through one real appointment-setting agent deal with every number exposed.

The worked example (appointment setting agent):

  • Baseline: 20 leads/week, ~1 hour of human time each = 20 hours/week
  • Fully-loaded employee cost: $40/hour → $800/week
  • Annualized: $800 × 52 = $41,600/year of recoverable labor cost
  • Build price: ~13% of the annual number = $5,500
  • Client return: $41,600 on a $5,500 investment ≈ 7.5x

The pricing formula:

  • Find the annual cost of the manual process (headcount hours × loaded hourly rate × 52)
  • Price the build at roughly 13% of that annual number
  • Golden rule: show a 10x return over 12 months — that math makes it hard to say no
  • Close the gap between 7.5x and 10x with *baseline growth*: as the system frees up time, leads/week climb from 20 → 21 → 23 → 27, compounding the return (a flywheel: more usage → more growth → more usage)
  • Frame growth as a projection, never a guarantee — do not tie your fee to specific revenue outcomes

Sequencing the conversation:

  • Never quote before discovery. Walk the client through the full solution first — how the agent works, what testing looks like, impact on speed-to-lead and lead quality
  • Standard deflection when a client pushes for a number early: *"to give you an accurate ballpark I need to understand the complexity of the system and how it integrates into your actual business processes"*
  • The client's own numbers become your price — that's what makes it defensible when they ask "where'd you get that number?"

Maintenance retainers:

  • $400/month flat, standardized across all projects — keep it simple, don't bespoke it
  • What it covers: keeping the build doing what was scoped — breakages, API changes, new model releases, edge-case tweaks
  • What it does NOT cover: new functionality. That's a separate scope and a separate conversation
  • Sanity check: if servicing a $30k system actually costs your team more than $400/month, you're losing money — don't anchor on the standard number

Cash flow:

  • Get paid in stages, so you're never carrying more than ~30 days of unpaid work
