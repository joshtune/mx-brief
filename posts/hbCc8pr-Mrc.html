We Suffer More In Imagination Than Reality

Codie Sanchez · 2026-07-29 · 33,429 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Confidence is earned after action, not before.

If you're stalling on a side hustle waiting to feel ready, this reframes fear as an information gap you close by doing, not by thinking.

What it is: A ~45-second Short stitching Seneca and Dale Carnegie into one argument — fear is a symptom of missing preparation and action, not missing ability.

How it works:

  • Seneca's framing: "we suffer more in our imagination than reality" — the anticipated version of the task is worse than the task.
  • Carnegie's framing: "inaction breeds doubt and fear, action breeds confidence and courage" — the causal arrow points from doing to feeling, not the reverse.
  • Codie's synthesis: "fear only exists where preparation and action don't." Fear is diagnostic — it tells you which of the two you're short on.
  • The reframe that matters: "you're not scared because you're incapable, you're scared because you haven't earned certainty yet." Incapability is permanent; unearned certainty is a to-do item.
  • The punchline: "you don't get confidence before the work, you get it after." Waiting to feel ready is a logically inverted plan.

Tools & links:

  • No tools, repos, frameworks, or URLs mentioned — this is a pure mindset Short with zero tactical content.

Why it matters for you: The instructional secret is that fear is a checklist, not a verdict — when a deal or a first client call scares you, the move is one reps-and-research cycle, not more deliberation, because the confidence you're waiting on is a byproduct of shipping.
