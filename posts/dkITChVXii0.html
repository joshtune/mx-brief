128GB AI Desktops… HUGE Price difference (ACTUALLY Tested)

Alex Ziskind · 2026-11-24 · 362,722 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Four 128GB local-AI desktops, $3K–$7K, same prompt

It names the four machines he runs local AI on and shows that the cheap one can wait 26.8 minutes before it answers, which is the price-versus-speed tradeoff you want to see against a Mac mini.

What it is: Alex gave four 128GB desktops the same 133K-token prompt (about a 10,000-line codebase) to see what the extra ~$4,000 buys when you run LLMs locally.

The machines (all 128GB, roughly $3,000–$7,000):

  • Beelink SER10 Max — mini PC with a Ryzen AI 9 HX 470. This is the orange "claw" edition with Linux preinstalled. He says pricing on this one fluctuates a lot.
  • AMD Ryzen AI Halo — AMD's own Strix Halo box. It's small and has a light around it.
  • Dell Pro Max GB10 — basically an NVIDIA DGX Spark in Dell's chassis. Performance is about the same as other GB10 systems.
  • Mac Studio M5 Max — new, and 128GB is its maximum spec. It is not the M5 Ultra.

What he ran:

  • Model: Qwen "3.8 Flash Next" (name as transcribed), a 4-bit quant that is a 94GB file. It has about 125B parameters but only ~6B active per token.
  • Test: every machine got the same file and the same 133K-token prompt through llama.cpp.
  • Smaller models also tested: Qwen 3.8 27B dense (19GB, fits in 32GB) and GPT-OSS-120B (63GB).
  • Fit rule: a 64GB machine can't hold the 94GB model. Even GPT-OSS-120B at 63GB won't fit in 64GB once you leave headroom.

Results (the transcript cuts off before the full table):

  • Slowest: one machine took 26.8 minutes to finish reading the prompt before it started answering.
  • Biggest swing: switching one machine from llama.cpp to its own vendor engine and model build cut the wait from 8 minutes to 71 seconds. The transcript doesn't say which machine.
  • His takeaway: 128GB gets the model loaded, but the hardware and the software stack decide how long you wait.

Mac mini comparison: The video, at least the part we have, never mentions the Mac mini and gives no per-machine prices. Only the $3K–$7K spread is stated. The only Mac in the lineup is the M5 Max Mac Studio, so it can't tell you which box costs how much next to a mini. The 64GB point does matter: any 64GB machine can't run the 94GB model.
