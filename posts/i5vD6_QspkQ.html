The Most Overlooked Path to Wealth

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-19 · 25,242 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Low-cost, high-revenue franchises with real FDD numbers behind them

If you want a side hustle with a proven playbook instead of a blank page, this breaks down franchise concepts you can start for cheap — including financing tricks that require almost none of your own cash.

What it is: Chris flies in Alex (founder of Franzy, who claims to have read thousands of Franchise Disclosure Documents) to name low-startup-cost, high-profit franchise concepts — backed by publicly filed FDD numbers, not spreadsheet fantasy.

The core thesis:

  • Franchising is "the most overlooked path to wealth creation in America"
  • Accessible, high revenue, low cost to enter, operational in under 30 days
  • The edge is FDD data: average investment range and average franchisee revenue are publicly disclosed, so you can verify profitability before you buy
  • Filter used: growing but not saturated (explicitly not Crumbl-style "tired" concepts with no opportunity left)

How the money side works:

  • Claim: there's an "express loan" path to ~$500K in under 60 days
  • That's how people buy franchises with little to none of their own capital
  • (Transcript cuts before the loan mechanics are fully explained — likely SBA express lending)

Concepts named:

  • Freight brokerage franchise — you (from home or a small office) match shippers with carriers and pocket the spread between what the shipper pays and what the carrier charges. Pure B2B sales, no assets. Average franchisee revenue for this brand: $1M+/year. Mature UK/Europe concept entering the US for the first time, HQ'd in Dallas — so you'd be early.
  • Garage renovation — epoxy floors, custom shelving, slatwall. Average revenue $1M+. No heavy equipment. Alex's pick for "cheapest concept I'd put my own money into right now." Demand far exceeds supply.
  • Chris also queues up: shady-but-legit-looking franchises to avoid, which concepts are fads, and whether Subway is the worst franchise deal in America.

The AI-resistance angle:

  • Chris pressure-tests freight brokerage against self-driving trucks (Tesla Semi)
  • Verdict: brokerage is the sales/demand-gen layer, not the driving layer — automating trucks doesn't automate the person drumming up loads
  • Relevant if you're a software engineer thinking about where to park capital outside of code

Tools & links:

  • Franzy — Alex's business; franchise discovery/matching platform
  • GoHighLevel — 30 days free (sponsor); CRM/marketing automation for local service businesses
  • GoHighLevel AI tier — 14 days free of advanced AI features
  • FDDs (Franchise Disclosure Documents) — the actual research tool here; publicly filed, contain Item 19 financial performance representations. Free to read before you commit a dollar.

Why it matters for you: A freight brokerage franchise is a laptop-and-phone business with a proven unit economic model — the closest thing to a side hustle you can run nights and weekends without inventory, employees, or writing a single line of code.
