The butcher who built Shake Shack's burger

My First Million · 2026-06-13 · 22,524 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
How a Brooklyn butcher shop became Shake Shack's supplier.

A 100-year-old family butcher turned one restaurant relationship into a $270M+ meat-supply business — a masterclass in owning the supply chain.

What it is: The story of LaFrieda Meats (Pat LaFrieda), the Brooklyn butcher that grinds the custom blend behind Shake Shack's burger.

The person/business:

  • Anthony LaFrieda emigrated from Italy ~1909, opened a butcher shop in Brooklyn. Solid but unremarkable business.
  • The business passed down generations; Pat LaFrieda Jr. was pushed by his dad to go to college and get a "real job."
  • Pat went to a good school, became a Wall Street stock broker for 9 months, hated it, and came back to take over the family butcher shop.

How they made money (the inflection point):

  • Danny Meyer (upscale restaurateur, Union Square Hospitality) was already buying meat from LaFrieda for his fine-dining spots.
  • Meyer pitched a new fast-casual burger concept — Shake Shack — and asked Pat to develop a custom burger blend for it.
  • Pat said yes, created the blend, and now supplies meat to all Shake Shack locations exclusively.
  • The "that's our burger" moment: Pat took his dad to the first Shake Shack, saw 200 people in line — proof the wholesale-supply bet had paid off.
  • Today the company does ~$270M/year. A single anchor restaurant relationship scaled into a regional meat-supply empire (LaFrieda now supplies hundreds of top NYC restaurants).

The playbook:

  • Inherit an unglamorous, cash-flowing business instead of starting from zero.
  • Move up-market: supply premium restaurants, not commodity buyers.
  • Land one marquee, high-growth customer (Shake Shack) and grow *with* them — your volume scales as their footprint scales.
  • Own a custom, proprietary product (the blend) so you're not a swappable commodity vendor.

Tools & links:

  • Pat LaFrieda Meats — the wholesale/retail butcher business
  • Shake Shack — the anchor customer

Why it matters for you: The money wasn't in the storefront — it was in becoming the proprietary, hard-to-replace supplier to a fast-scaling brand; pick the right anchor customer and you grow on their rocket.
