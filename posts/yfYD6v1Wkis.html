I Tried The "Easiest" AI Bot To Make Money From

Codie Sanchez · 2026-11-24 · 6,579 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Testing an AI clipping bot against paid editors

The secret is to run the AI on a real, live channel and judge it by cost per clip and views, not by how impressive the demo looks. That gives a side hustler a cheap way to check whether a bot can replace a $50-250 editor.

What it is: Codie feeds long-form videos to an AI bot (transcribed as "Grockbot"/"Rockbot", so the exact name is unverified). The bot finds the best moments and cuts short clips. She posts one on her real account to see whether it performs and whether anyone spots the AI.

How it works:

  • Input: hand the bot a few long-form videos and tell it to find the best moments.
  • Output: it sifts raw footage, flags the strong moments and edits a short. She says this saves hours every week.
  • Live test: she posted one bot-edited clip to her account with millions of followers instead of a throwaway test channel. After 48 hours it sat at 4/10 on average view performance against her other posts.
  • Cost benchmark: she pays human editors $50-250 per short. If the bot matches their numbers, that line item is cut, or the same budget buys more clips.
  • Audience check: she read the comments for anyone saying "AI slop" or "weird cuts". Nobody noticed, and commenters praised the tight editing.
  • Her verdict: views are the metric, but the bank account is the real test. The bot's cost saving only counts if performance holds.

Caveats:

  • The sample is one clip, 48 hours, and a 4/10 performance score. That is thin evidence.
  • The transcript cuts off before any final numbers or a how-to.
  • The "tens of thousands per year" savings claim is not backed by a breakdown here.

Why it matters for you: If you run a content-led side hustle, the editing bill is a real cost. Test a bot on one live clip, compare its performance to your human baseline, and only then decide whether to cut the editor.
