HTTPS Doesn't Hide This From Your ISP!!

NetworkChuck · 2026-06-18 · 41,860 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
HTTPS encrypts traffic but still leaks which sites you visit.

A quick reminder that the padlock isn't privacy — your ISP still sees every site via SNI — though this short skips Chuck's lab and AI workflow entirely.

What it is: A short explainer on why HTTPS (and even encrypted DNS) doesn't hide your browsing destinations from your ISP.

How it works:

  • The padlock = HTTPS = your traffic *payload* is encrypted, but metadata isn't
  • Secure DNS (DoH/DoT) hides DNS lookups from a Wireshark capture — but doesn't solve the problem
  • The leak is SNI (Server Name Indication) in the TLS handshake, sent in cleartext, which reveals the exact hostname you're connecting to
  • A VPN fixes it by encrypting *all* traffic from the machine (not just browser) and shifting the visible endpoint to the VPN server

Tools & links:

  • Wireshark — packet capture used to demo the SNI leak
  • Full long-form video: youtu.be/axfNxZ1R6C4
  • Note: ECH (Encrypted Client Hello) is the real fix for SNI leakage, but it isn't mentioned here

Why it matters for you: Nothing here on Chuck's home lab or his AI work — it's a 60-second privacy PSA, safe to skip unless you want the SNI talking point.
