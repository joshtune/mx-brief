What happens to you when a problem shows up?

Codie Sanchez · 2026-09-04 · 5,362 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Fixers profit from problems; freeloaders hand the profit away.

A 60-second reframe that turns your side hustle's worst moments into the exact spots where the margin lives.

What it is: A short mindset clip. Codie splits people into two camps, fixers and freeloaders, and argues the split predicts who ends up owning the business and who ends up working for it.

The instructional secret:

  • Problems are not obstacles to profit. They *are* the profit. Anything that goes wrong in a business is a task someone will pay to make go away.
  • Whoever fixes the problem collects the money attached to it. If you outsource the fix, you outsource the margin.
  • She trained a reflex: when a big problem lands, replace the sinking feeling with "money, money, money." That's the whole trick. Reframe the emotional response before you act.

How it works:

  • The leaky boat: Fixers grab putty and bail, even when the hole isn't their fault. Freeloaders stand in the same boat saying "not my job" or "someone else will handle it."
  • Ownership over blame: The fixer explicitly does not care whose fault it is. Fault-finding is a freeloader activity dressed up as diligence.
  • The profit rule: "Every time you find a problem in business, that's where the profit is." Read it literally. The messiest, most annoying job in your niche is the one with the least competition and the highest willingness to pay.
  • Self-selection: She tells the viewer they probably already have the fixer mindset. The clip cuts off before she finishes the contrast with people who don't.

Tools & links:

  • None mentioned. No repos, no products, no URLs in the transcript.

Why it matters for you:

  • Side hustles die on friction. The first customer complaint, the broken integration, the vendor who ghosts. This clip says that friction is the moat, not the exit sign.
  • Practical application: list the three things that annoy you most about your current side hustle. Each one is either a service you can charge for or a competitor you can beat by simply not flinching.
  • Engineers are natural fixers. The freeloader failure mode for a technical person is the "that's a business problem, not my problem" shrug. Drop it. The business problems are where the equity is.

Honest take: This is a motivational short, not a tutorial. No numbers, no acquisition mechanics, no checklist. The value is one memorable heuristic you can apply in the next five minutes.
