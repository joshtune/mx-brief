Anthropic's New Study: You're Not Controlling AI as Much as You Think You Are

Cole Medin · 2026-10-31 · 14,007 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
You rubber-stamp your agent's recommendations way more than you think

If your mx-workflow planning skills (/mx:prd, /mx:plan, /mx:build discovery) lead with a recommended answer, this study says users mostly just accept it, and that's a design flaw you can fix.

What it is: Cole uses an Anthropic study on "AI disempowerment" to argue that agent-driven planning flows (ask-user-question, grill-me style skills) quietly steer you instead of capturing your judgment.

How it works:

  • The study setup: Each analyst's agent was secretly given a randomized lean on every decision. Its system prompt told it to argue its case but change its mind if given good enough evidence.
  • 86% followed the agent's lean across four analytical choices. With no influence the expected rate is ~50%.
  • Stated preferences collapsed: Analysts who had already given their own preference switched away 60% of the time when the agent disagreed, versus 6% when it agreed.
  • Why it happens (Cole's read): LLMs sound equally confident whether they're right or dead wrong, so persuasion works regardless of correctness.
  • Cole's own workflow as the example: He describes the feature at a high level, then the agent runs the ask-user-question tool with multiple-choice questions. The agent almost always puts its recommendation as the top option, so he mostly clicks that one.
  • The core failure mode: You feel like you're deciding, but you're approving a plan the agent wrote.
  • Matt Pocock's Grill Me skill: Cole cites it as the canonical planning-interview skill. He says Matt already documents the risk: "The failure mode is passivity, answering agreed, agreed, agreed for 40 questions, coming out with a plan the agent wrote and you nodded at." Cole says Matt has similar fixes to the ones he covers at the end.
  • Caveat on this summary: The transcript cuts off before Cole's actual solutions. He promises them at the end of the video, so this summary doesn't cover them.

Why it matters for you: mx-workflow's discovery and planning commands likely use the same recommended-first multiple-choice pattern, so candidate fixes are to ask for your own answer before showing the agent's lean, flag when you accepted every default, and have the agent argue against its own recommendation.
