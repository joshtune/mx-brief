AI News: OpenAI Made a Massive Move Against NVIDIA

Matt Wolfe · 2026-08-28 · 127,180 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Open-weight models are quietly eating closed-model token share.

The Vercel AI Gateway data shows open-weight models flipping from 28% to 62% of token volume in two months — that's a direct signal about where your app's inference costs and model routing should go.

What it is: A weekly AI news roundup whose actual substance is a shift in the inference layer — OpenAI building its own chips, NVIDIA reportedly buying Hugging Face, and hard data showing open-weight models taking real production traffic.

How it works:

  • OpenAI showed first results from its Jalapeno inference chip — claimed up to 104x performance on open-weight models (GPT-OSS, DeepSeek R1). They benchmarked on public models on purpose so the numbers are independently verifiable; they claim the lead widens further on their own frontier models, which nobody can check.
  • Jalapeno is inference-only. Training still goes to NVIDIA for now. That's the distinction that matters — inference is the cost line item your app actually pays every day.
  • Per *The Information*, NVIDIA agreed to buy Hugging Face — unconfirmed by either company at recording time. Hugging Face is the GitHub of model weights *and* a GPU rental provider.
  • The strategic read: OpenAI, Meta, and Google are all building in-house silicon, shrinking NVIDIA's biggest customers. NVIDIA's counter-bet is to own the open-weight stack — release open models, own the place people run them, and capture inference demand directly instead of through the labs.
  • The number worth caring about: Vercel's CEO posted AI Gateway data — two months ago closed models took 71.6% of tokens vs 28.4% open. This week: 62% open, 38% closed.
  • The caveat Matt actually flags: switch the Gateway leaderboard dropdown from token volume to *requests* and the story inverts — most requests still hit closed models. Open models are winning on token volume (big-context, high-throughput batch jobs), not on request count (interactive app traffic). Don't confuse the two.

Tools & links:

  • Vercel AI Gateway model leaderboard — the open-vs-closed token/request data; toggle the dropdown yourself before drawing conclusions
  • Hugging Face — open model weights + rentable GPU inference; ownership possibly changing
  • futuretools.io — Matt's tool directory
  • ChatGPT/Codex app — sponsor segment, includes the new Sites feature

Why it matters for you: If you're routing model calls in a web app, the token-vs-request split is the actionable bit — open-weight models are already good enough for your bulk/background work even if your interactive paths stay on frontier closed models, and a gateway abstraction is what lets you move that line without a rewrite.
