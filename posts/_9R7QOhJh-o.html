Glasses That Make You Telepathic

Radar glasses read silent speech, type 200 WPM

Skip it for app-building value: this is a gadget news blip, but silent-speech input is a distant hint at where voice-driven UX could go.

What it is: A ~1-minute news hit on Subvocal's smart glasses that claim to turn silently mouthed words into text at 200+ WPM.

How it works:

  • Radar signals are sent through the face; AI interprets tiny lip and jaw movements as text
  • Claimed 200+ WPM vs ~40 WPM typical typing
  • Demo: silently asking an AI to summarize Slack messages with coworkers nearby
  • Two wearers could supposedly talk privately across a room
  • Pre-orders open at $499; still a prototype with no independent speed testing
  • Context: Apple reportedly paid $1.6B earlier this year for a company with similar tech

Tools & links:

  • Subvocal — Y Combinator startup behind the glasses (no URL given in the transcript)
  • FutureTools — Matt's AI tools directory, mentioned in the channel description only

Why it matters for you: Nothing actionable for building web apps today; at most, a long-range signal that silent or non-keyboard input may eventually become an interface you'd design for. Treat the speed claims as unverified.
