Founders pitch MFM live, reveal real revenue numbers

Three unfiltered founder breakdowns — real revenue figures, funding raised, and the exact mechanics of how each business makes money — with almost no podcast filler between the numbers.

What it is: Episode 852 — a live "Shoot Your Shot" format where Sam Parr and Shaan Puri bring in founders (blind, unvetted, pulled from the listener base) who each get 15 minutes to open with their single most impressive number, pitch the business, and ask for advice. Filmed in San Francisco; a prior edition was shot in NYC.

The format mechanics:

  • Founders range from ~$1M/year to ~$100M/year businesses
  • Each opens with one "big juicy number" — the hosts' stated rule is that they are "shameless numbers guys"
  • 15 minutes per founder, then the founder gets to ask the hosts anything
  • Hosts don't know who's coming — described as "American Idol but for business"
  • Title claims three businesses at $8M, $10M, and $40M/year

Founder 1 — Raghav Gupta, Posha (the only one in the available transcript):

  • Age/background: 32, grew up in India, based in the Bay Area for 5 years. First real company; earlier attempt was a micro wind turbine project.
  • The number: ~1,000 real-world deployments — his framing is that Posha is "amongst the 1% of robotics companies that are actually shipping a real product in real homes, in real kitchens"
  • Product: A countertop cooking robot — "a private chef in every home"
  • The thesis: If Waymo, Tesla, and Zoox can use computer vision + AI to move you from point A to B, the same stack can cook a fresh meal. Attacks three costs at once: takeout price (~$25/serving), cooking time (~60 min), and food freshness.
  • Funding: $8M raised, backed by Accel
  • Timeline: Started as a project 8 years ago out of his parents' ground floor in India; formalized as a company ~5 years ago
  • Live demo: Cooked spaghetti alfredo with mushrooms on set from fresh ingredients (garlic, milk, butter, pasta, parmesan)
