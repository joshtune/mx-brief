4 wild AI predictions from a $39B tech founder

My First Million · 2026-08-14 · 54,252 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Brett Adcock on humanoid robots and AI agents

A four-time founder who went from mortgaging his house to a reported $19B net worth breaks down exactly which bets he made and why he thinks AI is 100x bigger than the internet.

What it is: Episode 851 — Sam Parr and Shaan Puri interview Brett Adcock (third appearance in three years), founder of Figure.ai, on his company portfolio and his predictions for AI over the next five years.

Who he is — the money trail:

  • Raised in rural Illinois, no tech pedigree
  • Vettery — recruiting marketplace, sold for $100M+ (his first real exit)
  • Archer Aviation — eVTOL aircraft company, took it public
  • Figure.ai — humanoid robots, currently valued ~$39–50B depending on who's counting
  • Cover — security company aimed at stopping school shootings
  • Hark — new AI lab, ~1 year old, raised at a billion-dollar+ valuation
  • Reported net worth: $19B (Fortune). His reaction: "I don't care about that at all."

The bet that made it:

  • After Vettery he had tens of millions liquid
  • He put almost all of it into Figure at founding — took a mortgage on his house, rest of the money tied up in Archer, which was underwater at the time
  • Concentrated, not diversified. That's the whole play.

His framing on Figure:

  • "There is no flatline here" — outcome is binary: robots scale or they don't
  • Believes his companies are only now hitting inflection point — thinks 100x–1000x from current valuations is the live scenario
  • Still hasn't solved the hard part: actual robot output volume

The AI predictions:

  • AI will be ~100x bigger than the internet — after 15 years in software/internet he says nothing ever moved on a trend line this fast
  • Figure is the max ceiling of AGI in the physical world — embodied intelligence as the endgame
  • Hark is the digital mirror of that thesis: every human gets a paired AI — your own weights, your own memories, possibly your own hardware
  • The bottleneck he's attacking: AI must use computers general-purpose. His line — "you would never hire an assistant that couldn't use a computer." Only 1 in 1000 websites have APIs, so agents have to drive the GUI: financial models, booking flights, ordering DoorDash, all autonomously

Tools & links:

  • Figure.ai — humanoid robotics, his main vehicle
  • Archer Aviation — eVTOL, publicly traded
  • Brett Adcock on X

  • Sam's millionaire-timeline database — HubSpot lead magnet, referenced in show notes

Why it matters for you: The computer-use bottleneck he names — agents driving GUIs because APIs don't exist — is the single most concrete engineering thesis in the episode, and it's the same problem anyone building agentic tooling is hitting right now.
