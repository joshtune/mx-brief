Once You Get Money, Buy These 10 Things Immediately

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-11 · 49,018 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Spend money to buy back time, not status.

It hands you a single arithmetic rule — your buyback rate — that tells you exactly when outsourcing a task is profitable instead of indulgent, which is the difference between a side hustle that scales and one that eats your weekends.

What it is: A spending framework for people who've started making money — 10 "upgrades" filtered through one test: does this buy back my life, or just make me look richer?

The core mechanic — your buyback rate:

  • Take net income ÷ ~2,000 working hours per year = your hourly rate
  • Divide that by 2 = your buyback rate (the 50% margin)
  • Any task you can delegate *below* that number, delegate it
  • Worked example: $100k net → $50/hr → $25/hr buyback rate. Pay someone $10/hr to do it, that's free money
  • The margin is the whole point — you're not breaking even on the trade, you're netting 50%

The upgrades she actually names:

  • Rule of One (wardrobe): She explicitly rejects "upgrade your whole wardrobe." Buy *one* expensive, consistently-worn signal piece — one purse, one nice watch. Her line: "It could be fake. Nobody needs to know." Matters if you sell real estate, raise capital, or want a promotion
  • Rule of Three (closet): Weekly, cut three worn-out items and replace them. One in, one out. She frames it as psychological — stop dressing like you don't belong at the level you're playing at
  • Proximity (the big one): Pay for shorter commutes. Her Goldman Sachs story — a 2-minute walk vs. a colleague who was "smarter than me, better pedigree, better at Excel" and lived 45 minutes out. He missed every early morning and every second trip back. She made a million before 30; he didn't
  • Rooms over things: Better gym (full classes = people who might invest or hire), co-working space instead of your kitchen table, masterminds. She joined 3–4 before the money came. Framing: "you're paying for who else paid to be in the exact same room and stealing their 10,000 hours"

The evidence she cites:

  • Raj Chetty-style Harvard research mapping billions of Facebook friendships across millions of people
  • Strongest predictor of climbing economic brackets wasn't school, neighborhood, or income — it was whether your social circle reached *up* a rung
