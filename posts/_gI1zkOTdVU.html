He Makes $16K/Month Renting Out a Space He Doesn’t Own

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-19 · 23,280 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Lease a strip-mall space, rent it for parties

It's a concrete, low-cost, part-time side hustle (rental arbitrage on an event venue) with real numbers: under $3K to start, ~$16K/month, no employees.

What it is: Event-venue rental arbitrage. Lease a cheap ~1,200 sq ft retail space (an old church in a strip mall), spruce it up, and rent it by the day for birthdays, baby showers, and anniversaries.

How it works:

  • Origin: The guest and his wife hosted lots of kid events at home. For a baby shower they rented a 1,200 sq ft strip-mall venue (~$2,200 for a Sunday brunch, plus $2–3K for decor and a chef) and thought they could do it better.
  • The math that sold them: Comparable 1,200 sq ft retail space leases for about $2–4K/month. One afternoon booking roughly covers a month's rent.
  • Slow start: About 3 years of casually touring venues and nearly signing four leases before finding the right space. The hard part is finding a space that works for you, not the money.
  • Startup cost: The guest says you can do it for under $3,000 once you have the space.
  • Revenue: Average about $16K/month, best month $22.5K. Part-time, weekends only, no employees (just him and his wife), and he still keeps his day job.
  • Anchor tenants: Three churches rent the space every Sunday. That recurring income covers the whole rent, so the party bookings are mostly profit.
  • Facebook ads: A $100 ad spend produced about $10K in bookings in one day.
  • Referral loop: A QR code on the wall turns every party's guests into leads for roughly four more parties.
  • Risk: Renters can get weird. He once came back to find a goat hanging from the ceiling with guests cooking it with torches. Plan for rules, deposits, and insurance.
  • Research tactic: Tour competing venues as a customer. Note how they sell (often dusty office, old-school paperwork) and what you'd do better.

Why it matters for you: This is a low-capital, weekend-only side hustle: find a cheap space, lock in recurring anchor tenants to cover the rent, and fill the remaining dates with Facebook-ad-driven party bookings.
