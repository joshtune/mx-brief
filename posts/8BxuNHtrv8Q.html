Aaron Levie on building Box for 20 years.

A rare founder's account of taking a file-sharing idea from dorm-room consumer app to public enterprise company — and the pivot that made it work.

What it is: Aaron Levie, co-founder/CEO of Box, tells Sam Parr and Shaan Puri the origin story of building Box over ~20 years — from a simple "access your files anywhere" idea to a public enterprise company.

The founding crew:

  • Four co-founders who've worked together ~30 years — three went to middle school together, four to high school together, tried "lots of different ideas" as kids.
  • The Box idea emerged as they scattered to different colleges, then reunited on it.
  • Dropped out of college in two waves — 2005 and 2006 — to go all-in.
  • All four are still involved 20 years later.

Where the co-founders are now:

  • Dylan — CFO, runs multiple functions; famously appeared on *Millionaire Matchmaker* (his "main claim to fame").
  • Jeff — getting into farming.
  • Sam — the (retired) CTO, now at Anthropic working on Claude / Claude Code; built much of Box's core infrastructure still running today. Note: he retired from Box ~6 years ago, not recently — despite recurring viral "Box CTO left for Anthropic" posts.

The core business move — the consumer→enterprise pivot:

  • Box started agnostic to the user: "a secure way to access your files from anywhere" — an obvious idea before cloud storage was common.
  • Hit the classic startup "wandering period" — multi-month uncertainty over pivoting, business model, and next funding round.
  • The decisive fork: co-founders debated consumer vs. enterprise and chose enterprise — the bet that turned Box into a durable, public company.

Industry aside:

  • Levie notes Anthropic appears to be recruiting a roster (~10) of ex-public-company CTOs/founders — including Instagram's Mike Krieger (now with Andrej Karpathy referenced) — as a near-"requirement."
