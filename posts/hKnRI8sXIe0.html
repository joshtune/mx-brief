Why The Best Investors Never Trust Their Gut

My First Million · 2026-07-18 · 8,701 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Great investors replace gut instinct with coded rules.

The transcript spells out a repeatable, systematizable process for turning any judgment call into a backtested, automated decision rule — directly useful if you build tools that encode logic.

What it is: A short clip (Ray Dalio–style systematic investing) arguing the top investors never act on inspiration — they run a pre-built, backtested game plan.

How it works:

  • Mistake most smart people make: They invest by inspiration/mood ("I'm in the mood") without a game plan.
  • The fix — build a decision system:
  • Before every decision, look back: "If I made this decision in these circumstances, how would it have worked in the past?"
  • Turn the answer into an explicit decision rule.
  • Program the rule into a computer so you execute the game plan automatically instead of reacting emotionally.
  • Rule quality bar: Principles/rules must be timeless and universal — they should hold across time periods and situations, not fit one moment.

Featured individual / how they made money:

  • Speaker is almost certainly Ray Dalio, founder of Bridgewater Associates, the world's largest hedge fund.
  • His edge: codifying investing principles into systematic, algorithmic rules (the basis of his book *Principles* and Bridgewater's rules-based process) rather than discretionary gut calls.
