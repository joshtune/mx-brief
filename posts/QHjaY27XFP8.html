Pokemon party led to nine-figure trade show business

A $600K check into three passionate Pokemon collectors turned into the world's biggest Pokemon convention with 700,000 attendees and a nine-figure exit — a clean case study in backing obsessives inside a niche you just stumbled into.

What it is: The origin story of the world's largest Pokemon trade show, seeded by a guy who threw a money-losing Pokemon party and turned the relationships into a nine-figure investment.

The person: An unnamed MFM guest/investor who chased a Logan Paul-inspired Pokemon obsession into a minority-but-massive stake in a convention business. The operators were a trio — one guy plus his two cousins — all deeply passionate Pokemon collectors with zero capital.

How the money got made — step by step:

  • Step 1 — Chase the trend, don't overthink it. Logan Paul bought a ~$350,000 Pokemon box and set a world record. Guest's reaction was literally "that looks really fun, I'm gonna go do the same thing."
  • Step 2 — Buy access via a party, not a pitch. Threw "the world's biggest Pokemon party" in Vegas. Cost: ~$150,000 out of pocket. Direct revenue: $0. Treat it as a customer-acquisition spend for relationships, not a P&L line.
  • Step 3 — Recruit the scene's actual kingmakers. Pulled in Steve Aoki (celebrity Pokemon collector) and "Gary King Pokemon" (the #1 Pokemon guy) to co-host. Credibility borrowed from insiders, not bought with ads.
  • Step 4 — Let deal flow come to you. ~1 month later, Gary calls: a party attendee wants to start a Pokemon convention but has no money. The $150K party generated a proprietary deal no amount of cold outreach would have.
  • Step 5 — Fund the obsessives. Lunch with the founder and his two cousins. Invested $600,000 for a minority — but "massive" — stake. Capital was the missing piece; passion and domain knowledge were already there.
  • Step 6 — Scale the format. 4 years later: 20 shows/year, 700,000 attendees, the biggest Pokemon trade show in the world. Nine-figure exit.

The playbook underneath:

  • Losing $150K on a party was the cheapest possible option on a nine-figure outcome — ~0.15% of exit value.
  • Events are a relationship-generating machine disguised as a business. The party made zero; the network it produced made everything.
  • Fandom conventions monetize on tickets × frequency × merch/vendor booths. 700,000 attendees across 20 shows ≈ 35K per show — real scale in a niche most investors dismiss as kids' stuff.
  • The founders had no money but had status inside the community. That's the asymmetry: capital is commoditized, credibility with collectors is not.
