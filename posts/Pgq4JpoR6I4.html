Kahneman's loss aversion is quietly blocking your next bet.

A 3-step reframe for sizing risk applies directly to deciding whether to take a paycheck cut, spend savings, or bet time on a side hustle.

What it is: A breakdown of Daniel Kahneman's prospect theory (loss aversion) and a 3-step framework to override it when making financial bets.

How it works:

  • Kahneman's research: people won't take a 50/50 bet unless the potential win is 2-3x the potential loss (e.g., risk $100 to win $200-300, not $100)
  • This is loss aversion — losses hurt roughly 2x more than equivalent gains feel good
  • Fix #1 — Flip the fear: stop asking "what do I lose if I fail," ask "what am I losing by staying still"
  • Fix #2 — Increase the reward: make sure the upside is substantially bigger than the downside before you bet
  • Fix #3 — Increase your odds: turn a 50/50 into a 70/30 or 90/10 by stacking the deck in your favor
  • Tactical lever for #3: surround yourself with people who've already won, and rack up reps to shift the odds

Why it matters for you: Side hustles die in the planning stage because the perceived loss (time, money, ego) outweighs the perceived gain — this gives you the exact mental math to correct for that bias before you talk yourself out of starting.
