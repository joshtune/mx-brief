Ex-Amazon VP reveals what it's really like working with Jeff Bezos

My First Million · 2026-10-28 · 40,631 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Ex-Amazon VP Ethan Evans on working with Bezos

It's a career-and-leadership interview, not a money-making playbook, so watch it for Bezos's management habits (and the Twitch acquisition backstory), not for a revenue breakdown.

What it is: Episode 877. Shaan Puri and Sam Parr interview Ethan Evans, a former Amazon VP (Prime) and now an executive coach, about Jeff Bezos's strengths and flaws. Caveat: the transcript I got cuts off a few minutes in, so everything below covers only the intro and the start of the Bezos critique.

Who the featured person is:

  • Ethan Evans spent about 15 years at Amazon and rose to VP over Prime
  • He was placed on Twitch's executive team after the acquisition, as Amazon's 'adult in the room'
  • Staff suspected he was a spy or a replacement-in-waiting for Twitch CEO Emmett Shear. He says that was never the plan
  • He is now an executive coach who has led hundreds of people

What he did / how the money worked (from the intro only):

  • Twitch sold to Amazon for about $970M, just under the headline $1B
  • Amazon stock rose roughly 5x during the earn-out period
  • Twitch engineers with a market salary of $200-250K were vesting about $2M a year
  • Amazon would not repeat the $2M payouts, so a big vest cliff followed and many of those engineers left
  • No revenue figures, side businesses or monetization tactics appear in the portion I could see

Bezos takeaways so far:

  • Flaw: He was demanding and gave only corrective feedback. You'd bring results, get a nod, then three things to fix. This was hard on people with thin skin
  • He worked on it: Evans saw Bezos use an executive coach to try to fix this
  • Taste: Evans calls it great. Bezos picked the right fights on Kindle, Prime and two-day shipping becoming one-day shipping
  • Misses: The Fire Phone cost about $170M in hardware write-offs, plus opportunity cost
  • Cold open: Evans mentions Bezos slapping his CFO in front of a room as 'a message'. The context is not in the visible transcript
  • Annoying part: Teams over-prepared and Bezos still found the gap in minutes
