A Really Annoying Rich People Secret I Learned On Wall Street

Codie Sanchez · 2026-11-07 · 432,346 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Owners get taxed after expenses; employees get taxed first.

If you're building a side hustle, this shows why the same $300k is worth far more as business profit than as salary, because owners deduct before tax and employees deduct nothing.

What it is: A 60-second pitch for owning a cash-flowing business, built on the idea that the tax code rewards owners over employees.

The instructional secret: Order of operations. Employees get taxed first and spend what's left. Owners spend on the business first and get taxed on what remains.

How it works:

  • Employee: $300k salary becomes roughly $170-190k after federal, state and payroll taxes.
  • Owner: Buy an HVAC company doing $1.5M revenue and $300k profit. Taxable income may show closer to $150-180k.
  • Deductions she lists:
  • Depreciation on trucks and equipment
  • Interest on the SBA loan
  • Software and marketing
  • Healthcare
  • Travel and vehicle expenses
  • Home office
  • Result: Same $300k of cash flow, but the owner keeps dramatically more and also builds equity in an asset.

Reality check (not in the video):

  • Depreciation is a non-cash write-off, so it shifts when you pay tax, not what you earn.
  • The $300k is likely before you pay yourself, so a real salary and the interest on the loan eat into it.
  • Deductions only count for real business expenses. Personal spending dressed up as business spending is how people get audited.
  • The headline gap is real but smaller than the pitch suggests. Talk to a CPA before you copy the numbers.
