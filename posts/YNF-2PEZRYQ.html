When To Get Rid Of Clients

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-27 · 7,965 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Six customer red flags, tiered from annoying to fireable.

Your side hustle's real bottleneck usually isn't lead gen — it's the handful of clients quietly eating your margin and your nights, and this gives you a triage system for spotting them before they do.

What it is: A tier-list ranking of six bad-customer archetypes, with a prescribed response for each instead of a blanket "fire them all."

The tier list:

  • F tier — the one-time buyer: Buys once, vanishes, never returns. Proceed with caution, not panic.
  • D tier — the discount seeker: Opens with price, not value. Classic profit killer.
  • C tier — the trade offer: Wants to barter or "collaborate" instead of paying. They don't want a deal, they want your margin.
  • B tier — the boundary pusher: Texts at midnight, demands instant replies, treats every problem as your emergency.
  • Fire-now tier — the payment problem: Late invoices, disputed charges, endless excuses. No triage needed — cut.

The prescribed response per tier:

  • F tier → set boundaries
  • D tier → raise prices (let price filter them out for you)
  • C tier → strict offers, no flexibility on terms
  • B tier → route them to tech, not people (self-serve, async, ticketing — stop paying human attention for it)
  • Payment problems → fire immediately

The actual instructional secret: The move isn't "fire bad clients." It's that most bad clients are a *pricing or systems* problem, not a people problem. Only one of the six archetypes actually warrants termination; the other five get solved by changing your price, your terms, or your tooling. Firing is the last resort, not the first.
