Laid-off founders bootstrapped a SaaS by saying yes to everything.

If you're eyeing a side hustle, this teases a non-technical playbook for starting a SaaS by reselling/outsourcing work until MRR beats your salary.

What it is: A podcast clip where founders explain how getting laid off forced them to bootstrap a service-turned-SaaS business that now out-earns their old salaries.

How it works:

  • Rock bottom as fuel — a 20% surprise layoff put their family at risk; instead of job hunting again, they chose to "make it happen myself."
  • Say yes to everything — "say yes to everything until you can afford not to." They took design work, marketing work, and the full range of requests.
  • Outsource the delivery — they sold the work, then outsourced fulfillment, pocketing the spread without doing it all themselves.
  • Grow into recurring revenue — they pushed until monthly recurring revenue (MRR) exceeded any prior salary.
  • Let AI feed your funnel — customers now arrive saying "ChatGPT sent me" or "I was chatting with Claude and they sent me your way" (i.e., being mentioned/recommended by LLMs is a real acquisition channel).
  • Pay for ads only after traction — they didn't run Google Ads until *after* crossing 10,000 (customers/users), letting organic + AI referrals prove the model first.
  • The cliffhanger — clip ends asking for "the playbook people could implement if they're not technical to start a pure SaaS business" (answer is in the full episode).
