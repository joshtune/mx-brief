How the Rich Avoid Taxes

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-15 · 31,910 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Buy, borrow, die — the billionaire tax playbook.

It reframes your side hustle from a paycheck replacement into an appreciating asset you can borrow against instead of sell — which is where the tax math actually flips in your favor.

What it is: A 90-second breakdown of the "buy, borrow, die" strategy — how Elon Musk grows billions in Tesla stock while paying near-zero income tax, and why it's the system working as designed, not a loophole.

How it works:

  • Step 1 — Get paid in equity, not salary. Musk takes no Tesla salary. He's compensated in stock. Salary is income and taxed immediately; stock is an asset and isn't.
  • Step 2 — Never sell. If $10B in stock doubles to $20B, that $10B gain is untaxed. Unrealized gains are invisible to the IRS. Tax is only triggered on sale.
  • Step 3 — Borrow against it. Need cash to live? Don't sell — take a securities-backed loan. Pledge $20B in stock, borrow $100M. Loan proceeds aren't income, so no tax event. The stock keeps compounding while you spend the bank's money.
  • Step 4 — Die. The step-up in basis resets the asset's cost basis to its date-of-death value ($20B). The $10B in embedded gains vanishes for tax purposes. The estate settles the $100M loan. The IRS collects nothing on decades of appreciation.
  • The core mechanic: taxes are triggered by *events* (earning, selling), not by *wealth*. Owners control the timing of their events. Employees don't.

Tools & links:

  • Securities-backed line of credit (SBLOC) — offered by most major brokerages (Schwab, Fidelity, Interactive Brokers). Not billionaire-exclusive; typical minimums start around $100K in pledged assets.

The side-hustle translation:

  • Your equivalent of "stock" is equity in a business or real estate — an asset that appreciates without generating a taxable event.
  • W-2 income is the worst-taxed dollar you can earn. A side hustle structured as an *owned entity* changes both the rate and the timing.
  • Cash-flowing businesses layer on top: you can deduct legitimate expenses against revenue before tax, which a paycheck never lets you do.
  • The borrow step scales down. HELOCs on appreciated property and SBLOCs on brokerage accounts are the retail version of the same move — access liquidity without triggering a sale.

Caveats worth knowing (the video skips these):

  • Borrowing against a volatile asset means margin calls. If the collateral drops, the bank forces a sale — at the worst possible time and with the tax bill attached.
  • The step-up in basis is a *policy*, not physics. It's been on the chopping block in multiple legislative proposals.
  • This is a strategy for people whose assets already appreciate. Step zero is owning something that grows — which is the actual hard part.

Why it matters for you: The instructional secret isn't a tax trick — it's that ownership gives you control over *when* you're taxed, and a side hustle is the cheapest way to move from the taxed-on-earning column to the taxed-on-your-schedule column.
