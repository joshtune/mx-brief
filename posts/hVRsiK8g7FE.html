Fake food-delivery app that never actually delivers.

A 60-second look at a novelty app concept ('Food Never Comes') and the browsing-as-dopamine insight behind it — but there's no real business or revenue model to dissect here.

What it is: A clip riffing on a joke app called "Food Never Comes" — a fake food-delivery service where you browse menus, fill a cart, and check out, but no real order is ever placed.

How it works:

  • User opens the app and browses real-looking delivery menus, reviews, and carts
  • They "check out" and pick a delivery speed — rabbit (fast) or turtle (slow)
  • They watch a tracker animate the delivery toward their house
  • The food never arrives — that's the entire bit (hence "blue balls of entrepreneurship")
  • The premise leans on a real insight: much of online shopping's pleasure is the *browsing and checkout ritual*, not the product

Tools & links:

  • No real product, repo, or company mentioned — "Food Never Comes" is presented as a gag, not a launched business
  • HubSpot free CRM — episode sponsor, unrelated to the bit

Why it matters for you: There's no featured founder, business, or money-making playbook here — just a comedic riff on the dopamine loop of e-commerce browsing, so don't expect a case study to extract.
