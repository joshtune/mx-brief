Not even close… M5 Max vs Razer Blade 18

Alex Ziskind · 2026-08-19 · 82,093 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
M5 Max MacBook Pro benchmarked against RTX 5090 Razer Blade 18.

Both machines land in the $5–6K range, so this is the direct answer to whether a maxed-out Mac or a 5090 gaming laptop is the better single-machine buy — and neither is anywhere near Mac mini money.

What it is: Head-to-head Geekbench comparison of the new M5 Max MacBook Pro vs. the 2026 Razer Blade 18 (RTX 5090, 24GB VRAM, Core Ultra 9), framed around price-for-performance rather than raw gaming wins.

The machines:

  • Razer Blade 18 — RTX 5090, 24GB VRAM (up from 16GB on last year's 4090 model), new Intel Core Ultra 9. Roughly $5,000–$6,000.
  • MacBook Pro M5 Max — same rough price bracket, ~$5,000+ configured.
  • Alex's framing: nobody buys both. Pick one, and it has to do productivity *and* games.

Benchmark numbers (Geekbench 7):

  • Razer, unplugged, balanced: 1,840 single / 15,118 multi — weak, which is why the rest of the video keeps it plugged in.
  • Razer, plugged, high performance: 2,918 single / 24,149 multi.
  • Razer, plugged, balanced: 2,908 single / 23,957 multi — essentially identical. Plugged vs. unplugged is the variable that matters, not the power plan.
  • M5 Max: 3,220 single / 22,658 multi — *and this was inside a Windows 11 VM*, which is the transcript's cliffhanger.
  • M5 Max holds full power on battery. The Razel loses ~35% multi-core the moment you unplug it.

For the AI/VRAM question:

  • The Razer's 24GB of dedicated VRAM is the hard ceiling for local model loading — bigger models simply won't fit.
  • The M5 Max uses unified memory, so the whole RAM config is addressable by the GPU — the reason Macs punch above their spec sheet for large local models.
  • Alex notes the Razer is much quieter this year than the 4090 model, which spun fans up aggressively under load.

Windows power tuning trick (if you go Razer):

  • Settings → System → Power exposes "best performance" for plugged/battery separately.
  • Control Panel → Power Options is missing the High Performance plan by default. Restore it from an admin terminal: `powercfg duplicatescheme <GUID>` (Alex duplicated the Ultimate Performance scheme), then `powercfg /setactive <new-GUID>`. Exit and re-enter to see it.
  • Setting it this way locks power mode to best performance permanently.
