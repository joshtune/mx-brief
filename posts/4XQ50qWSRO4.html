Success is Hard Until You Understand This Formula

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-21 · 20,159 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Ditch LTV math, price for profit on order one.

If your side hustle can't turn a profit on the very first sale, you're financing customers with money you don't have — and this video gives you the 5-minute ratio that tells you before it kills you.

What it is: A single ratio — AOV to CAC — that replaces the Silicon Valley LTV story with money you can actually count today.

The core argument:

  • LTV is a borrowed metric. It was built for SaaS: no sales team, no cost of goods, recurring by default. Most side hustles and Main Street businesses are none of those things.
  • The repeat-purchase data doesn't support it. Only 18.8% of e-commerce customers buy again within a year. Of those, 50% return inside 30 days and 76% inside 90. If they haven't come back by month three, they're gone. Brick-and-mortar is worse.
  • Most people can't even compute it. Codie claims that across the 15,000 businesses they teach, only 42% calculate LTV correctly. So the metric is usually wrong — and wrong even when it's right, because the repeat behavior it assumes doesn't happen.
  • The trap: "$250 first order, $2,000 lifetime value, so I'll spend $500 to acquire." You eat a $250 loss on day one and wait a year for a payback most customers never deliver. That only works with VC money to burn. Without it, the only guaranteed winner is Meta, who collects your ad spend either way.

The math, done right:

  • AOV $500, CAC $100 → healthy, profitable on first transaction.
  • Raise AOV to $1,000 → you can now afford $300–$400 CAC and still win.
  • The move is raising AOV, not raising your *estimate* of LTV. Inflating LTV to justify higher CAC is what Codie calls finance arbitrage — you end up paying for the *right* to have a customer.
  • The rule: if you're profitable on the first order, you never need a projection to save you. You win even when you mess up.
