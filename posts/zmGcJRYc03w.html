Why You're Not Getting Promoted (And What to Do About It)

A Life Engineered · 2026-08-15 · 7,962 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Promotion stalls when your superpower isn't valued locally.

It reframes a stalled promo from "I'm not good enough" to "I'm in the wrong room" — and gives you a cadence for deciding when to leave.

What it is: A 60-second Short (teaser for Monday's full episode) naming the #1 reason engineers don't get promoted, plus a self-check ritual for deciding whether to stay.

The core claim:

  • The top reason you're not promoted isn't skill — it's fit. Your superpower isn't valued by that team, that project, or that manager, at that time.
  • This is situational, not permanent. The same skill set that's invisible here is a promo case somewhere else.
  • You can grind as hard as you want against it and still lose. Effort doesn't fix a valuation mismatch.

The two exits when your superpower isn't valued:

  • Wait it out — a manager change resets the valuation. New hiring manager, new definition of what's impactful.
  • Move — go to a project or team where your skill set is the thing they're short on.

The instructional bit — the 6-month check-in:

  • Run it every 6 months at a company. Run it *immediately* when you feel worn out (burnout is the signal, not the schedule).
  • The question: "Should I stay or should I go?"
  • The real question underneath: *Am I growing at the rate I believe my career should be growing?*
  • Growth is measured across multiple axes, not just title:
  • Promotion
  • Raises / comp
  • Scope of work
  • Resume-building work (things that make you more hireable, not just more useful to this employer)
  • If several axes are flat for two consecutive check-ins, that's your answer.

What to prepare for:

  • Do the diagnosis *before* you conclude you're underperforming — separate "I'm not delivering" from "they don't value what I deliver."
  • Know what your superpower actually is. You can't audit fit if you can't name the skill.
  • Have the internal-transfer path mapped before you need it; moving teams is usually cheaper than moving companies.
  • A reorg or manager swap is an opportunity window, not just disruption.
