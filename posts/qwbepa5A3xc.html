What it is: Episode 850. Sam Parr and Shaan Puri walk through three businesses that are enormous but invisible to normal people — starting with the lab-testing monkey trade.

Business #1 — Research monkeys for drug trials (0:00–11:01)

  • The hook: a single research monkey costs ~$20,000. Sam guessed $1,000. He was off by 20x.
  • Why: nearly every drug that reaches market gets tested on non-human primates during preclinical/phase-one work. Cynomolgus macaques are the standard model.
  • The supply chain is the business — breeding facilities, import brokers, and quarantine/holding operations. Not the labs themselves.
  • Structural moat: import restrictions, breeding lead times (years to sexual maturity), and disease-free colony certification make supply nearly inelastic. Demand is set by pharma R&D budgets, which don't care about price.
  • The result is a commodity market with luxury-goods pricing and effectively zero consumer awareness.

Business #2 — The salad oil crisis (11:01+)

  • Show notes flag this as the second segment. This is almost certainly the 1963 Allied Crude Vegetable Oil scandal — Tino De Angelis collateralized loans against tanks of "salad oil" that were mostly water with a thin oil layer floating on top (oil floats; inspectors dipped from the top).
  • ~$175M in fraud, nearly took down American Express, and famously created Warren Buffett's early AmEx position.
  • The MFM angle is presumably: commodity-backed lending is only as good as your physical audit.
