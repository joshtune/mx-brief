Chocolate bar brand hits $40M via chef-led influence.

A family-run CPG brand did $40M in 12 months on a $2.49 unit price, and the founder names the exact distribution hack — court the chefs, not the celebrities — that got them free Kardashian placement.

What it is: Mid-Day Squares founder breaks down $40M in trailing-12-month revenue selling a $2.49 refrigerated chocolate bar, and the unglamorous relationship strategy behind their celebrity reach.

The business:

  • Product: Mid-Day Squares — a refrigerated functional chocolate bar, $2.49 retail
  • Team: founder + his wife + brother-in-law + two other partners (family-operated)
  • Revenue: ~$40M in the last 12 months
  • Unit math: ~60 bars consumed *every minute*, 24/7, 365 days a year to clear that number — the founder's own framing of what $40M at $2.49 actually requires

What they did to make money:

  • Picked a low-ticket, high-frequency SKU and won on volume, not margin per unit — $2.49 means the entire business is a distribution and repeat-purchase problem
  • Built the product to be habit-forming enough that the interviewer says he "had to wean myself off of them" — retention is the growth engine at this price point
  • Ran a decade-long chef-relationship play instead of paid celebrity endorsements: seeded free product to private chefs for "years and years"
  • Specifically: Chef K, who cooks for the Kardashians, got product for years — which turned into Kim Kardashian carrying the bars in her bag and posting them twice, unpaid
  • The stated principle: "Who influences the influencers?" Influencing the influencer's inner circle is cheaper and easier than buying the influencer
  • Explicit contrarian bet: "we're not looking for the direct connection to celebrities — that's what everybody's doing"

The transferable playbook:

  • Find the gatekeeper layer one step behind the audience you want (chefs → celebrities; in software: staff engineers → CTOs, DevRel → dev teams)
  • Seed free product to that layer with a multi-year time horizon and no ask attached
  • Zero CAC on the placements that matter most — the Kardashian posts were unpaid
