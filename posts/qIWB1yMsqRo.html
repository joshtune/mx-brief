Four fundamentals for a simple, effective AI second brain

Cole's 'keep it dead simple' second-brain audit (Context, Connections, Capabilities, Cadence) gives you a ready-made checklist to test mx-workflow's skills against.

What it is: A fundamentals video on building or auditing an AI second brain with the 4 C's framework, which Cole credits to Nate Herk. The four are Context, Connections, Capabilities and Cadence. The transcript I had cuts off during the tools intro, so the C-by-C detail isn't covered below.

How it works:

  • Core claim: the second brain should 'know your business off the top of its head', as an extension of you. Most people overcomplicate it.
  • Stack: Claude Code as the agent and a folder of markdown files as the memory. No sprawling toolchain.
  • Capabilities run locally: video editing, diagrams and PowerPoint decks are done by the agent running commands on his machine, with no paid service for each.
  • Payoff he claims: 25-30 hours saved per week. He says the LLM's power is what lets the system stay this simple.
  • Self-improving: once the foundation exists, the second brain is used to improve itself.
  • Harness-agnostic: the 4 C's apply whether you build on Claude Code, Grokbots, Hermes or OpenClaw. He says he has also run Codex for everything at one point, and that swapping harness or LLM is easy but shouldn't be done often.
  • Audit mode: use the four C's as a checklist against an existing second brain.
  • Use cases he names: AI news research, content prep, diagrams and presentations, and improving the agentic systems he teaches.

What's different or new vs. his earlier videos:

  • This is the first time he's stripped his second brain down to a framework instead of showing his own system.
  • His emphasis is now minimalism: few tools, markdown files, and a strong coding agent doing the work.

Tools & links:

  • Claude Code — the agent harness running his whole second brain
  • Obsidian — the vault of markdown files used as agent memory (Notion is named as an alternative)
  • Codex, Grokbots, Hermes, OpenClaw — alternative harnesses he says work equally well with the 4 C's
  • Nate Herk — originator of the 4 C's framework (Cole spoke at his virtual conference); no URL given in the transcript
  • mx-workflow — your project, the thing to audit

Why it matters for you: Run mx-workflow's skills through the 4 C's as an audit:

  • Context: do your skills and agents load project knowledge from plain markdown files, or does that knowledge live in prompts?
  • Connections: which external systems (MCPs, Linear, Trello and so on) are wired in?
  • Capabilities: are your skills doing real work through direct commands, or are they thin wrappers?
  • Cadence: do you have scheduled or recurring runs, like the daily pipeline pattern, rather than only on-demand commands?
  • Also try letting the system improve itself, e.g. a skill that audits and proposes edits to other skills.
