How I launched a marketplace with no buyers and no outside sellers

My First Million · 2026-09-21 · 58,830 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Whatnot's founder on building a $20B live-shopping marketplace from zero.

If you're building any two-sided marketplace, this is a rare founder-level breakdown of how Whatnot solved the classic cold-start problem — get sellers first, buyers follow.

What it is: An interview with Grant Lafontaine, co-founder and CEO of Whatnot, a live-shopping marketplace app now valued at $20 billion.

The founder & company:

  • Grant Lafontaine — background includes working in a deli as a kid, almost failed out of high school
  • Co-founded Whatnot with Logan (co-founder, last name not given in transcript)
  • Company started ~6.5 years ago (~2020)
  • Prior career: both founders spent their careers in consumer internet, specifically two-sided platforms/marketplaces before starting Whatnot
  • Whatnot's valuation ($20B) exceeds the combined market caps of Wendy's, Under Armour, Hertz, Harley-Davidson, and American Airlines cited in the episode

How Whatnot makes money / what it actually is:

  • Live-shopping marketplace app — sellers go live and sell items via livestream auctions/listings
  • Started niche: collectibles — Funko Pop dolls, trading card pack openings
  • Expanded far beyond collectibles — the hosts describe buying a live crab (seafood) on the platform mid-podcast for $34
  • Sellers build public track records (e.g., one seller "I Love Crabs" had sold 3,300 items and reportedly made "over 100 grand" selling crabs)
  • Revenue model implied is standard marketplace take-rate on transactions (not explicitly stated in this excerpt)

The hard part — solving the cold-start problem:

  • Founders identified marketplaces as "the most durable and also the most challenging" businesses to build
  • Key insight discussed: the priority was getting sellers first, not buyers — "How did you get your first 1,000 sellers... Sellers, not buyers"
  • The transcript excerpt cuts off right as Grant begins explaining the seller-acquisition strategy — the tactical playbook likely continues later in the full episode

Investor angle mentioned:

  • Shaan Puri tried to invest early but didn't follow up on a funding round when Grant said the round was "already full" — a missed investment he now jokes about regretting
