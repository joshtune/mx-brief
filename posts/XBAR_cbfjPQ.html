This Simple AI Setup Makes Me $8K Every Month

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-06 · 25,631 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Sell AI Spanish video dubbing to local businesses

It's a concrete, low-startup-cost side hustle: AI lip-synced Spanish dubs sold for ~$300 a video to businesses that have zero Spanish content, with a text-message pitch you can steal.

What it is: A side hustle selling AI-generated Spanish dubs (with lip-synced mouths) of existing videos to local businesses and creators.

How it works:

  • The gap: 44M US people (about 1 in 7) speak Spanish at home, yet almost no plumber, dentist, roofer, or gym has a Spanish version of their video ads or website clips.
  • Why it's open: Dubbing used to mean hiring Spanish-speaking actors and re-shooting. The translation was never the hard part; mismatched lips ("1978 kung fu movie") were. AI lip-sync now fixes that, and the business owner can't do it themselves, or doesn't know it's possible.
  • The tool: OpenArt's Arena leaderboard ranks AI models by job. Pick the top-ranked lip-sync model (he tries one called "shade 11" in the video) and check how it looks up close on the lips.
  • The workflow: Take a customer's existing video, run it through the lip-sync dubbing model, and deliver the Spanish version. Chris says it takes about 20 minutes per video.
  • The money:
  • $300 per video
  • Businesses post ~2 videos a month, so ~$500-600/month per client
  • 10 clients is ~$6K/month, and it takes about an afternoon of work
  • Chris's own channel is dubbed into 13 languages, so he runs this himself
  • Five customer types to pitch:

1. Home services (roofers, plumbers, HVAC, landscaping): already run video ads and serve Spanish-speaking homeowners, and competitors have no Spanish content.

2. Dentists and med spas: high ticket, lots of video on their sites, big Spanish-speaking patient base.

3. Real estate agents: 2M+ of them, each posting a weekly walkthrough. One video, two languages, twice the leads.

4. YouTubers with 100K+ subs: a different pitch. YouTube allows extra audio tracks, so one dub opens Mexico, Spain, Colombia, and Costa Rica.

5. Farmers market vendors, food trucks, taquerias, small restaurants: this is the one he builds live. Owners are the easiest to text, and he already has a farmers market video to use.

  • The pitch: A short text message to the owner. He says he'll send it to a real local business owner on camera and show the reply, whatever it is. The exact prompt and text are in a free doc linked in the description. The transcript cuts off before the demo, so the results aren't covered here.
