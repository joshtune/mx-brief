The Most Overlooked $1K/Hour Business Anyone Can Start

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-09-25 · 29,641 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Kids' woodworking classes turned a Facebook post into $50K.

A guy with zero marketing, zero website, and $450 built a business charging $75/hour per kid off a single unpolished Facebook post — the exact playbook is copyable this weekend.

What it is: Mike Herod runs Voskll, a trade school teaching kids ages 7-18 shop/woodworking classes — he stumbled into it by posting on his personal Facebook the week of Thanksgiving with zero prep.

How it works:

  • Posted on his own personal Facebook (no business page, no ad spend, no followers) with just: age range, price, when/where — made up on the fly as comments asked
  • First classes ran in people's backyards with a folding table and whatever tools he owned
  • Split kids into two age brackets (7-9 and 10+) after learning a 6-year-old and 12-year-old can't be in the same class
  • Scaled from Facebook comments to spring break/summer camps clearing $30K in a single stretch
  • Landed a $30,000 recurring contract (two hours every Friday) — implied to be a school or camp-style deal
  • Charges $75/hour per kid; mentions the State of Florida pays him within 24 hours (suggesting some sessions are subsidized/voucher-funded, e.g., homeschool or state programs)
  • Collects payment upfront, which is why startup capital wasn't required
  • Startup cost was under $450 (basic hand tools/jigsaws)
  • Teaser (not yet reached in this transcript excerpt): a one-sentence pitch got a tool company to send $5,000 in free gear, and a single word turned casual side-hustle bookings into real contracts

Tools & links:

  • Omnisend — sponsor, 30% off first 3 months w/ code KOERNER (email/SMS marketing, not core to the business idea)
  • TKOPOD.com — Koerner's newsletter/community, referenced but not part of the business model itself

Why it matters for you: If you want a side hustle with near-zero startup cost, this is a template — pick a skill you already have, post honestly and specifically about it locally, charge upfront, and let demand (not a business plan) define your pricing and structure.
