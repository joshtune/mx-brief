I Didn’t Expect Local AI to Go This Far on a Laptop

Alex Ziskind · 2026-07-22 · 57,294 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Two MSI Raider gaming laptops run local AI, VRAM decides everything.

It benchmarks $3K+ Windows gaming laptops as local-AI boxes against his daily MacBook Pro, so you can weigh VRAM-for-dollars against a Mac.

What it is: Head-to-head of two near-identical MSI Raider 16 Max HX gaming laptops (RTX 5080 16GB vs RTX 5090 24GB) as local AI inference machines.

The hardware (what you actually asked about):

  • Both: Intel Core Ultra 9, 24 cores, 32GB RAM (user-upgradable via quick-access panel), OLED screen, 400W power brick
  • Only difference: VRAM — 5080 = 16GB, 5090 = 24GB. Same CPU, same RAM, same everything else.
  • VRAM is the whole story: it caps model size + context you can fit before speed collapses.

How the AI runs:

  • Nvidia RTX GPUs + CUDA stack — the reason inference is fast on Windows/Nvidia
  • Qwen 3.5 4B (4-bit quant, 3.4GB): 151 tok/s on 5080, 146 tok/s on 5090 — ~30x faster than you can read
  • Gemma 4 12B (4-bit quant, 7GB): still fits under 16GB, but model size *plus* context eats the budget
  • Rule: keep model + context under your VRAM ceiling or speed tanks

Tools mentioned:

  • Ollama — `ollama pull` / `ollama run --verbose`, defaults to 4-bit quant
  • LM Studio — GUI alternative shown for the 12B model

Cost note: Transcript gives no laptop price (affiliate 'MSI Raider on sale' link only). MSI Raider 16 Max HX with 5090 typically runs ~$3,500+ vs a Mac mini M-series starting ~$599 — but the mini has far less GPU/VRAM headroom for large local models. He daily-drives a MacBook Pro, not these.

Why it matters for you: The one number that matters is VRAM (16 vs 24GB), and these are premium ($3K+) machines — a Mac mini is dramatically cheaper but won't touch this VRAM/throughput for local LLMs.
