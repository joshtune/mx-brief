M5 MacBook Air Changed More Than It Looks

Alex Ziskind · 2026-08-16 · 34,470 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Five MacBook Air generations benchmarked for sustained multicore throttling.

If you're eyeing an Air as a cheap local-AI box, this is the sustained-load reality check — the M5 wins on raw speed but throttles hardest, and no model here gets discrete-GPU or high-memory territory.

What it is: A head-to-head sustained-load test of all five Apple silicon MacBook Airs (M1 → M5), measuring both peak speed and thermal throttle drift.

How it works:

  • Test harness: a custom C++ merge sort that saturates every core, run for 5 consecutive iterations per machine — the repeat runs are the point, since they expose throttling in a fanless chassis
  • Metric that matters isn't iteration 1, it's the delta from iteration 1 → iteration 5

The numbers (last-iteration time, lower = better):

  • M5 — 129s — fastest overall, but 6% variance across iterations (worst throttler)
  • M4 — 149s
  • M3 — 195s — M3 and M4 both lost ~5% from run 1 to run 5
  • M1 — 222s
  • M2 — 226s — slowest, but 3% variance (most thermally consistent)

Read on the thermals:

  • All Airs are fanless, so every one of these throttles — the question is how much headroom the chip burns before it clamps
  • M5 is ~1.7x faster than M1 on this workload even after throttling, so the throttle penalty doesn't erase the generational win
  • M1 ran coolest to the touch; M5 ran warm but not hot

Caveats for your use case:

  • This is a CPU merge sort, not an LLM inference test — no tokens/sec, no GPU or Neural Engine numbers, no memory-bandwidth figures
  • No prices are given in the transcript, and no Mac mini or Studio comparison appears at all
  • For local AI, the Air's real ceiling is unified memory and sustained thermal headroom, not peak multicore — a fan-cooled Mac mini at similar chip tier will hold clocks that an Air can't

Why it matters for you: It's the cleanest evidence yet that the fanless Air is the wrong chassis for sustained AI workloads — but you'll still need a mini/Studio comparison elsewhere to get the price-per-performance answer you're actually after.
