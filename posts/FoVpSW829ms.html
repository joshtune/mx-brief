The $14B mistake this billionaire still regrets

My First Million · 2026-09-17 · 3,212 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Book exec recalls turning down early Amazon equity from Bezos.

A first-hand account of meeting Jeff Bezos in his one-room Amazon office and the equity decision that cost an early partner roughly $14B.

What it is: A guest (unnamed in this clip, a book-industry executive) recounts renting Amazon his company's "Books in Print" bibliography database in Amazon's earliest days, and the equity he passed on.

How it played out:

  • Jeff Bezos needed a bibliography of books in print to launch Amazon's online bookstore.
  • The guest's company owned that data. Bezos offered 20% equity in his about-to-launch company to license it.
  • The guest's business partner refused equity in an illiquid startup — insisted on cash instead.
  • They settled on a flat licensing deal: $100,000/year for 5 years.
  • The guest later visited Bezos's first office — one room, Bezos personally packing books and driving them to the post office each night.
  • Bezos was already positioning to compete directly with Barnes & Noble, telling the guest, "I understand how to do this better than they do."
  • The guest doubted Amazon would succeed and tried to renegotiate for stock — Bezos agreed to a smaller stock grant since he "didn't need them as much" by then.
  • The company sold that Amazon stock at the IPO instead of holding it.

Tools & links: None mentioned — this is a verbal anecdote, no products, sites, or repos referenced.

Why it matters for you: No repeatable playbook here — it's a cautionary story about undervaluing early equity in a founder who was self-evidently obsessive about execution, not a how-they-made-money breakdown.
