Jev: a fast AI that returns decisions, not words

Chuck is wiring a cloud AI agent (Grok Bot) into his home lab through Twingate service accounts, and he's using a fast decision model to automate yes/no calls.

What it is: Jev from TypeSafe is a "decision model". It takes a state and a question and returns a true/false probability as JSON instead of prose. It's a System 1 model: fast, narrow, and meant to be wired into automation.

How it works:

  • Input: you give it a `state` (the context, such as a YouTube comment) and a `question` (such as "Is this spam?").
  • Output: JSON with a probability, not text. Example: ~92% true that the comment is spam.
  • No agent features: it can't call tools, search the web, or chat. That's the selling point, because the output is predictable enough to automate on.
  • Speed: Chuck's spam test on Jev was near-instant. The same prompt in a GPT "instant" model took about 2 seconds, and a second Jev run in the demo took 17 seconds. The transcript cuts off before he explains the gap, and the numbers conflict, so don't treat them as a benchmark.
  • Copycats: Chuck says the Decisions API from OpenAI, Clef from Cloudflare, and Ollama are all copying the idea. The transcript doesn't give details.
  • Demos: it plays Doom quickly and orders a Domino's pizza, but the transcript doesn't show how.
  • Playground: open to everyone now, after being closed for a while because of demand.
  • Cold-open claim: Chuck says he is using Jev "right now" in his own workflow, but the transcript is cut off before he shows how.

Home lab angle (the sponsored part):

  • The problem: Chuck runs Grok Bot, a cloud-only agent, and wants it to reach a few custom apps inside his network without exposing the whole lab.
  • The fix: a Twingate service account scoped to one custom app. The agent can reach that app and nothing else on his network.
  • No open ports: a Twingate connector runs inside the network and makes outbound connections only.
  • Connector hosts: a Raspberry Pi, a NAS, or nearly anything else.
  • Cost: free for home labs.
  • Sponsor caveat: he got Jev to say "85% true" that Twingate fits, which is a gimmick. Treat Twingate as a sponsor pitch, though the least-privilege pattern is sound.

Tools & links:

  • Twingate — zero-trust remote access with no open ports, free for home labs, with service accounts for AI agents
  • Jev by TypeSafe — decision model with a free playground (no URL given in the transcript)
  • Grok Bot — cloud-hosted agent Chuck is connecting to his lab
  • Decisions API (OpenAI), Clef (Cloudflare), Ollama — mentioned as copying the decision-model idea

Why it matters for you: The takeaway for your own lab is to give each AI agent a scoped identity into your network instead of opening ports. Use a decision model like Jev for fast classification and triage steps, and keep full LLMs for the work that needs them.
