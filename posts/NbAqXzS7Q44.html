Stop Following Your Passion (Do This Instead)

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-07-12 · 27,018 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
A tactical playbook for finding your real obsession.

If you're hunting for a side hustle but don't know what it should be, Chris hands you a concrete method to mine your own data instead of waiting for a passion to strike.

What it is: A rejection of "follow your passion" in favor of a data-driven, AI-assisted process for surfacing the thing you're actually obsessed with.

The core argument:

  • "Follow your passion" assumes you (a) already know your passion and (b) only have one — both usually false.
  • Most people have *multiple* undiscovered obsessions; the advice fails because it's inspirational, not tactical.
  • Chris's own passion isn't any industry (iPhone repair, RV parks, tree trimming, snack business) — it's the *zero-to-one puzzle*: getting the first dollar into Stripe. The industry is just "the costume it shows up in."

How it works (the tactical playbook):

  • Audit your own behavioral exhaust for patterns you didn't notice:
  • YouTube watch history
  • Google search history
  • Amazon order history
  • Conversations where you "hijack the table" and talk for 20 minutes without noticing
  • Feed that data into AI to surface your obsession in ~20 minutes instead of wandering for 20 years.
  • Reframe passion vs. profit: School #1 = keep them separate (9-to-5 job, 5-to-9 passion); Chris hints the modern opportunity is making them overlap.
  • Let dopamine drive *action/execution* (first sale), not endless research and procrastination.

Tools & links:

  • Playmakers AI community — consultants learning to charge $500–$5k/mo riding the "AI tidal wave"
  • YouTube membership perk ($5) — full business plans from his videos
  • DIY toolkit: your own YouTube/Google/Amazon history + any LLM as the analysis engine

Why it matters for you: Instead of stalling on "what side hustle should I even start," you get a repeatable, AI-powered method to extract a direction from data you already generate — and permission to chase the zero-to-one build rather than a lifelong "calling."
