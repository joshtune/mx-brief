Fable 5.2 launch: benchmarks, positioning, and when to use it

Skip the hype: the useful bit for a web app builder is that Opus 5.5 stays your daily driver, and Fable 5.2 is for orchestration and advisor roles.

What it is: Matt's same-day reaction to Anthropic's Fable 5.2 (public) and Mythos 5.2 (cyber-testers only). He has no early access, and the transcript is mostly benchmark walkthrough with few hands-on demos (it cuts off before the artificial analysis section).

How it works:

  • Same model, two guardrail levels — Mythos 5.2 is the full model, limited to cybersecurity testers. Fable 5.2 is the same model with extra cyber guardrails, and it's the one you can use.
  • Anthropic's own advice — start with Opus 5.5 as the daily driver for most coding and knowledge work. Fable/Mythos are for the most ambitious, open-ended technical work.
  • Recommended architecture — use Fable 5.2 as an *advisor* that Haiku/Sonnet/Opus consult, or as an *orchestrator* that delegates simpler tasks to smaller models.
  • Coding gains are modest — Frontier Code benchmark 45.5% (Opus 5) → 49.1%. A second benchmark went 62 → 69.
  • Biggest leap is agentic scientific research — 58.7% (Opus 5.5) → 74.6% (Fable 5.2). Gemini Carbon scored 64.8% and Astra 68.1%. That isn't a web-app workload.
  • Not a sweep — Gemini Carbon beats it by about 2 points on business workflows, and Opus 5.5 edges it on one knowledge-work benchmark.
  • Cost and thinking-budget findings — on Frontier Code, Fable 5.2 at *high* roughly matches Opus 5.5 at *max* and costs less. Fable 5.2 also scores better at *extra high* than at *max*, so more thinking got more expensive and slightly dumber.
  • Strengths Anthropic lists — theoretical physics, protein folding, mathematical formalization, hardware and robotics.

Why it matters for you:

  • Don't swap your web-app coding workflow to Fable 5.2 by default; keep Opus 5.5 and test Fable 5.2 at *high* effort (not *max*) for hard debugging or architecture planning.
  • If you build agent features, the advisor/orchestrator-over-smaller-models pattern is worth trying for cost control.
  • Skim only; the rest is science and benchmark talk that doesn't apply to your projects.
