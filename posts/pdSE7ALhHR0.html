How a $10,000 start became a billion-dollar fortune

My First Million · 2026-10-29 · 9,445 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Rakesh Jhunjhunwala: India's greatest investor, built from $10K borrowed

It's a 40%-a-year compounding case study: rapid-fire trading on one side, a few stocks he never sold on the other, and the second half made the money.

What it is: A short clip naming Rakesh Jhunjhunwala as India's greatest investor and describing his two-track approach. The transcript is only a snippet, so there's no deeper business breakdown here.

Who he is:

  • Rakesh Jhunjhunwala, described as the greatest investor from India
  • Started with $10,000, and it was borrowed. He didn't have even that himself, and someone lent him the money
  • Built a billion-dollar fortune from that start, per the title

What he did to make money:

  • Active trading: Ran several Bloomberg screens, watched charts, and traded rapid-fire
  • Never-touch holdings: Held two or three stocks he never sold, and those went "through the roof"
  • Compounding: The untouched positions compounded at north of 40% a year for several decades
  • Doing both well: The speaker says he was great at both the fast trading and the patient holding, and says they know no one else like that

What the clip doesn't say:

  • The names of the stocks he never sold
  • Position sizes, entry prices or how much leverage he used
  • Who lent him the $10K
  • Any detail on the trading strategy itself
  • The speaker's closing remark is that he could have been an operator as well as an investor. The clip doesn't develop that

Tools & links:

  • None mentioned in the transcript
