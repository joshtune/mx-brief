DGX Spark cluster

Alex Ziskind · 2026-08-29 · 75,958 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Eight DGX Sparks clustered into 1TB of unified VRAM

This is the current ceiling for local LLM hardware — and the price-per-GB math is what tells you whether a stack of Sparks or a maxed Mac mini/Studio is the smarter buy.

What it is: Alex wires DGX Sparks together past NVIDIA's officially documented two-node limit — four nodes for 512GB of pooled memory, eight for a 1TB "VRAM" cluster.

The hardware:

  • Each DGX Spark contributes 128GB unified memory; 4 nodes = 512GB, 8 nodes = 1TB
  • Interconnect is 400Gbps switching — the expensive, unglamorous part nobody budgets for
  • NVIDIA only documents 2-node pairing; going to 4 and 8 is off-script

Scaling numbers (VL32 model, 66GB):

  • 1 node — 3.58 tok/s generation
  • 2 nodes — 6.14 tok/s (~1.7x)
  • 4 nodes — 11.36 tok/s (~3.2x), plus a solid jump in prompt processing
  • Scaling is close to linear, which is the actual headline — most multi-node setups fall apart here

The cost reality check:

  • Even at 4 nodes, ~3.58 → 11.36 tok/s is slow for a 66GB model. You're buying *capacity*, not speed
  • Sparks land around $3–4k each; four of them plus a 400Gbps switch is a five-figure build before you've run a single token
  • A maxed Mac Studio gets you 512GB in one box, one power cable, no switch, and no cluster orchestration — same memory ceiling, far less complexity
  • A Mac mini tops out much lower on memory but costs a fraction; it's the wrong comparison for 100GB+ models and the right one for everything under ~64GB

Why it matters for you: If you want to run 100GB+ models locally, this is the honest picture — clustering works and scales well, but the switch tax and the tok/s ceiling mean a single big-memory Mac is usually the better dollar unless you specifically need to go past 512GB.
