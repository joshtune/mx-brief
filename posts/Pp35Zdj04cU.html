Is Writing Code by Hand Still Worth Learning?

A Life Engineered · 2026-10-22 · 2,045 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Hand-coding skill vs. an AI-written future

Short, honest take on what to keep sharp as AI does 70-80% of the typing: your judgment, review, and editing skills.

What it is: A short clip where the host admits that career advice is harder to give now that AI tools blur which skills are the safe bets.

The message to engineers:

  • 10 years ago the advice was easy: invest in Go or Rust in 2016 and ride a clear growth area.
  • Now it's hard to separate any growth area from AI tools in general.
  • His current advice, which he says has worked for people he mentors: learn to write high-quality code by hand.
  • He wants to keep that skill himself, even though its role in the industry is changing.

What to prepare for:

  • Mentees who prefer hand-coding still do roughly 70-80% of their job work with AI.
  • The human share is the 20-30%: review and editing on top of AI output.
  • He expects the AI share to keep increasing.
  • Your value shifts from typing code to judging it. You can't review what you can't write.

Instructional takeaways:

  • Keep a hand-coding habit going. Side projects, katas, or a no-AI day each week all work.
  • Practice reviewing and editing AI output critically instead of accepting it.
  • Build the taste to tell high-quality code from plausible-looking code.
  • Don't bet your career on one language or framework the way you could in 2016.

Caveats:

  • The transcript is a short excerpt. There's no step-by-step guide and no data behind the 70-80% figure. It's anecdotal.

Why it matters for you: Hand-coding is becoming the foundation for reviewing AI output, not the main way you spend your day, so train both.
