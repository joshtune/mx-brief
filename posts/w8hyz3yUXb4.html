The $81K/Month Business Where Every Job Is Outsourced

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-09-11 · 1,673 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Zero-experience founder hits $81K/month by subcontracting every install

A guy who was driving DoorDash 85 days ago built an $81K/month service business with $500 in setup and zero licenses — by doing the selling and outsourcing every job to licensed contractors, which is a repeatable side-hustle template.

What it is: A home-services middleman business — the founder markets and sells an overpriced, overlooked installation service, then hands every job to licensed installers on a per-job payout while he never touches a tool.

The numbers:

  • 85 days in business, month three: $81K revenue in August
  • $54K already booked for September at the start of the month
  • $500 total setup cost, started as a one-man show
  • Founder background: no degree, no internship, no work experience, was driving DoorDash

The core thesis (why the opportunity exists):

  • Skilled trades are shrinking — far more tradespeople retiring than entering
  • Many niche service categories have prices "completely made up" by the handful of people offering them (e.g. a job priced at $10K with no real justification)
  • These services stay overlooked because outsiders assume they're harder or more complex than they actually are — that ignorance is the moat

How it works:

  • Founder is not licensed — so he owns the customer-facing side: marketing, quoting, scheduling, billing
  • Licensed contractors handle only the install
  • Installer economics: ~16 jobs each per month at ~$500 per job = ~$8K/month extra per installer, which makes it a win for them too
  • Owner's physical involvement: on-site roughly twice a month, otherwise fully hands-off
  • Result: an asset-light, license-free business where the founder captures the spread between an inflated market rate and what installers actually need to be paid

Replication playbook (implied by the episode):

  • Find a niche install/service category with high sticker prices and few providers
  • Verify whether you actually need a license, or whether you can subcontract the licensed portion
  • Spend minimal setup ($500-tier: site, phone, lead gen) and sell first
  • Recruit licensed tradespeople on a flat per-job rate that beats their W-2 math
  • Stay out of the field; own the pipeline and calendar

Why it matters for you: This is the purest side-hustle version of "sell first, outsource fulfillment" — no credentials, no capital, no field work, just arbitrage on a mispriced niche service that everyone else assumes is too hard.
