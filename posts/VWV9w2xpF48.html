Seven sales phrases that quietly kill your deals.

If you're selling a side hustle's services to strangers, these seven swaps cost nothing to adopt and directly change how prospects read your status and urgency.

What it is: A ranked list of seven common sales phrases that leak neediness or trigger distrust, each paired with a direct replacement.

How it works:

  • "Just checking in" → "Quick idea for you." Checking in signals you have nothing to offer. Leading with an idea gives the prospect a reason to open the message.
  • "Let me know if you're interested" → "Does A or B work?" Open-ended asks push the work onto them, and people avoid effort. A binary choice makes replying nearly free.
  • "To be honest" → "Here's the trade-off." The first implies you weren't honest before. The second frames you as someone who names downsides — which is what actually builds trust.
  • "Are you a decision maker?" → "Who else should join?" The first attacks status and makes them feel small. The second gets the same information without the insult.
  • "We can't discount it" → "Here's your ROI." Refusing to discount admits the first price was arbitrary. Shifting to return-on-investment moves the conversation off price entirely.
  • "Sorry to bother you" → "I'll keep this brief." Apologizing frames you as an annoyance instead of a solution. Promising brevity frames you as respectful of their time.
  • "No problem" → "Absolutely" / "Of course." "No problem" plants the idea that there could have been a problem. The alternatives leave no residue.

The underlying pattern:

  • Every bad phrase either lowers your status, creates work for the prospect, or introduces a doubt that wasn't there.
  • Every replacement does the inverse: adds value, reduces friction, or holds status neutral.
  • Nothing here requires a script rewrite — it's a find-and-replace on language you're already using.
