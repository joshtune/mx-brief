Thin clip, but it gives you Rockefeller's money-making playbook (consolidate, hide it, crush rivals at any cost) and a lens on why AI and Zuck are acting like it's war.

What it is: A short clip comparing John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil tactics to today's AI arms race and Zuckerberg's behavior. The transcript is only a snippet, so there's no full business breakdown.

How it works (what Rockefeller did to make money):

  • Consolidation: Standard Oil was pitched as the first oil company in America. It grew by buying up the small local operators (the 'wildcatters', basically oil cowboys) and rolling them into one giant.
  • Secrecy: He kept the roll-up quiet, so competitors and sellers didn't see the full scale of what he was building.
  • Enemy/friendly sorting: He and his partner Archibald talked about every player as either 'an enemy' or 'a friendly'. Friendlies got bought or worked with.
  • Scorched-earth pricing against enemies: The stated stance was roughly: we will lose unlimited amounts of money before this person profits off one more pint of oil. He would burn bridges and eat losses to crush a rival.
  • Result: Rivals were crushed or absorbed, and Standard Oil took the market.

The AI parallel:

  • The speaker says that after reading about Zuck and watching AI, it looks the same: it's war right now.
  • The implication is that the big AI players will spend almost without limit to deny rivals any profit, which is the same logic as the oil playbook.
