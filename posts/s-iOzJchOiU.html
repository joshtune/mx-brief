A $ box tricks your Mac into thinking it's a keyboard

Skippable for your hardware focus — it's a sponsored review of a physical KVM-style gadget (Violoop), not a rundown of his AI rig, though he name-drops running DeepSeek V4 Flash on his own local machine with zero specs or cost given.

What it is: A sponsored deep-dive on Violoop, a physical box that plugs into your Mac's USB-C/HDMI and presents itself as a real keyboard+mouse (a "KVM with a brain"), so an AI can click through macOS security dialogs that software-based agents (Claude, ChatGPT computer use) are blocked from touching since Mojave.

How it works:

  • Apple discards synthetic (software-generated) clicks aimed at permission/security dialogs — real hardware input isn't filtered
  • Violoop watches the screen, decides what to click, and sends genuine HID signals through the box, so it can approve dialogs like "Terminal wants access to Finder"
  • You assign it a "brain": Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, or bring-your-own-key via any OpenAI-compatible API
  • Alex points his at DeepSeek V4 Flash, which he says runs on his own local AI machine — no model, specs, or price given for that machine
  • Builds a persistent memory of what it sees on screen (people, work context) that you can inspect/delete
  • V3 unit shown in the video; V4 (with touchscreen, Dynamic Island support) ships via Kickstarter starting Sept 15

Tools & links:

  • Violoop — hardware KVM-style AI input device; Kickstarter/Discord links only in description, no URL given in transcript
