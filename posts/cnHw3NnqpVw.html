Sounds Too Simple, But One Client Pays $15K/Month

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-25 · 15,707 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
College students sell AI departments to mid-market firms

It's a concrete, low-capital side hustle (productized AI assessments leading to a retainer) that three students grew to a $15K/month client in 4 months, using nothing but their contact lists.

What it is: Certified AI Partners, a business run by three 21-23 year old college students (James, Brendan, Carter) who act as a company's outsourced AI department, like an IT department but for AI.

How it works:

  • Origin: James heard a prior episode where Corey Ganon described doing AI assessments for companies, called Brendan, and they started 4 months ago.
  • Prior experience: Each had a small AI business already. James sold voice agents to HVAC companies, Brendan did AEO (answer engine optimization), and Carter sold Open Claw. All sold to small businesses.
  • Step 1, cold walk-ins: They walked into local businesses in Provo (orthopedics, dental offices, insurance companies) and said "Can we do a free AI assessment for you? It takes 15 minutes." The assessment was the foot in the door.
  • Step 2, hit a wall: Small companies had the problems but not the budget, so they moved to mid-market companies.
  • Step 3, pivot the offer: They stopped selling one-off automations and pricing each one. They now sell an ongoing "we become your AI team" retainer.
  • Step 4, lead gen via network: Mid-market owners are harder to reach than walking in on a small shop. Their #1 lead source is texting hundreds of contacts and asking "who do you know that owns a business of this size that needs help with AI?" The intro, not the pitch, is the ask.
  • Pricing tactic: They offered three plans at $10K, $15K and $25K a month. The $25K plan was a price anchor (a Hormosi trick) and wasn't actually available. The client closed at $15K/month, a $30M construction company.
  • Result claimed: Proposals that took employees about an hour dropped to about 5 minutes. No office, no employees, no connections. One of them dropped out of BYU for it.
  • Chris's take: Start with your sphere of influence, even though it's awkward. "Ask for money and you get advice. Ask for advice and you get money."
