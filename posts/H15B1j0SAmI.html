Wrap proven businesses in a 'thing + GPT' chat interface

Shaan walks through real 'X GPT' businesses (PointsGPT, TaxGPT, PhotonGPT, FightItGPT) and the exact playbook: clone a proven product, swap buttons for chat, and ride the naming shortcut.

What it is: Shaan Puri's pitch that the next wave of businesses are proven products rebuilt as chat interfaces, named '[thing] GPT' so customers instantly get what they are buying. (Transcript provided is partial, so only the first ~9 minutes of ideas are covered.)

How it works (the money playbook):

  • Step 1: Pick a proven model. Find a business that already makes money and has an annoying UX.
  • Step 2: Rebuild it as chat. Replace buttons and forms with natural-language input. The user can be sloppy or messy, and the AI figures out intent.
  • Step 3: Add proactive value. Alerts and ongoing monitoring, such as 'ping me when business class to Europe is a good deal'.
  • Step 4: Name it '[thing] GPT'. The suffix works as a mental shortcut, like the 'i' prefix or 'I fix it'. Shaan expects it to work well in ads.

Businesses featured:

  • PointsGPT
  • What it does: chat-based flight booking with credit card and airline points. You tell it your destination, rough dates and which cards you hold (Chase, Amex), and it finds options.
  • Extras: it sets alerts for high-value redemptions.
  • How it makes money: Shaan saw it advertised on Instagram. The transcript doesn't give its revenue or pricing model.
  • What it's cloning: Points.me, which does the same thing through a normal interface.
  • The Points Guy (the proven model behind it)
  • What it was: a blog on maximizing credit card points.
  • Outcome: the founder sold about two years in, owning the whole thing. Shaan guesses roughly $20M, about 10+ years ago. This is a guess, not a confirmed figure.
  • Points.me
  • What it does: finds flights you can book with points.
  • Why it sells: points are hard to use. The rewards portal is a 'huge rip off'. You have to use the 'transfer partners' tab, move points to United or Flying Blue, then search for award seats on airlines that don't want to make that easy.
  • TaxGPT
  • What it does: described as QuickBooks with chat instead of buttons.
  • Revenue: millions of dollars a year, growing fast.
  • Caveat: some argue chat is worse than buttons because buttons are quicker. Chat wins on tolerating messy input.
  • PhotonGPT
  • What it does: a ChatGPT plugin that gets you actual prescriptions through ChatGPT.
  • Launch: the launch tweet claimed the first doctor's prescription via ChatGPT.
  • Why it's plausible: many people already use ChatGPT for health questions.
  • FightItGPT ('do not pay')
  • What it does: you upload a bill or ticket and it works out how to dispute it. It keeps fighting and updates you on progress.
  • Example: a $4,000 Quest Labs bill for a blood draw.
