Turning obsessive hobbies into full-time businesses

A perfume nerd built a full-time NYC business on curated scent tours — the episode breaks down exactly what she sells and to whom.

What it is: Sam Parr walks through Asia Grant's Scent Social Club — a paid perfume-shopping tour business in NYC — then generalizes it into a thesis about enthusiasm as a moat.

The featured business — Scent Social Club (Asia Grant):

  • Ex-consultant, obsessed with perfume, turned that obsession into a full-time job in New York City
  • Product: a guided walking tour of a cluster of NYC perfume stores
  • Positioning line in her bio: "I have a perfume for you. The best New York City perfume tour."
  • Sam paid for it as a date-night activity and bought a bottle on the spot — the enthusiasm converts

How the tour actually works (the operational detail):

  • Sends a Typeform to every attendee *in advance* — personality, tastes, what they're into
  • Attendees meet at a perfume store; she hands out a printed sheet listing 3 stores and 3 perfumes (or clones) to sample per store
  • Walks the group through all three stores, pitching specific bottles at specific people: "here's why I think *you* would like this"
  • Zero purchase pressure — no commission-hunger, which is what makes the recommendation credible
  • Bottles range from budget clones up to ~$1,200 niche fragrances

Revenue lines (three customer types):

  • Private tours — ~$500 per tour (what Sam booked)
  • Group / public tours — lower ticket, higher volume
  • Corporate bookings — companies hiring her for team events; this is where the real money is
  • Cold-start method: began by running the tour for her own friends, then let it spread

The comp — Museum Hack (Nick Gray):

  • Same playbook, different obsession: NYC museums like the Met are free and offer free tours
  • Gray's take: free tours are boring — so he ran a better one and charged $200 a head
  • The hook was a reframe, not new content: "here are five pieces of art I'd love to steal today"
  • Grew to $3M in annual revenue in roughly 3–4 years

The thesis:

  • "You don't want to be the best, you want to be the only" — Shaan Puri
  • The mechanism: keep whittling away the generic parts of what you do until only the idiosyncratic part remains; a market of one
  • Enthusiasm is the moat — it's contagious, and it can't be copied by a competitor who doesn't actually care
  • The product is *not* the tour. It's the curator's taste. Same asset (free museums, existing perfume stores), repackaged by someone who genuinely gives a damn
