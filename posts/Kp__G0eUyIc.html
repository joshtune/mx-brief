Howard Marks: how I make money while you worry about a market crash

My First Million · 2026-07-15  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Legendary investor Howard Marks on AI and investing amid fear.

A billion-dollar investor explains exactly which analytical tasks AI will automate and which human judgment still commands a premium — a map of where your edge survives.

What it is: Howard Marks (co-founder of Oaktree Capital, ~$200B distressed-debt investor famous for his memos) sits down with Shaan & Sam to explain how he reversed his AI-bubble call and how he thinks about opportunity when everyone else is scared.

Who he is / the money playbook:

  • Built Oaktree Capital Management into one of the world's largest distressed-debt / credit investors.
  • Core strategy: buy when others are paralyzed by fear — "if you wait until you have nothing to be afraid about, the opportunity has passed." Being early and uncomfortable is the edge.
  • Famous for widely-read investor memos; the last MFM appearance drew 1M+ listeners.
  • Discipline over emotion: stay rational, never get "seduced" into a thesis — that's when smart people make bad bets.

The AI reversal (his new thesis):

  • Wrote an AI-bubble-warning memo ~Dec 9; son Andrew (a VC living in AI daily) told him "so much has happened, update it." Rewrote it entirely by early February.
  • Two qualities he says are unprecedented in tech history:
  • Autonomy — give it a job without telling it how, and it figures it out (unlike railroads, computers, internet, which were just tools/speed-ups). Carries the "it may take over" concern.
  • Unpredictability — he's never seen a technology whose future shape is genuinely beyond prediction; the internet never felt that way.

How AI reshapes the investing business:

  • AI can do the Warren-Buffett-reading-Moody's-manual grind — digest 800 companies' data instantly, without arithmetic or emotional mistakes.
  • Historical parallel: indexation "defrocked" active equity managers by exposing that most underperform the averages. AI will do the same to another tier of professionals whose talent isn't as great as they claim.
  • Open question he poses: computers could only read, remember, add, subtract, compare — a limited but powerful list. Is AI's list limited or unlimited?

What may stay human:

  • Judging *people* — avoiding bad actors. "For undefinable reasons you just say, it doesn't feel right… the hair on the back of your neck goes up." The next great investor may be a human + AI, not pure AI.
