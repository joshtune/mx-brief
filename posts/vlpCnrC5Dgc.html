What it is: A short reaction video to Huawei's Mate XT2 announcement and its new Kirin chip, which Huawei says uses a technique called 'logic folding' under its 'Tao scaling law' marketing umbrella.

How it works (per Huawei's claims, not Alex's testing):

  • Transistors inside a chip are linked by tiny wires; the longest chain of them is the critical path that caps clock speed.
  • 'Logic folding' supposedly shortens wiring along that critical path to cut signal delay.
  • Kirin chips will be the first to adopt it, debuting in the Mate XT2 tri-fold phone with its U-shaped folding structure.
  • Alex's take: the bottleneck is real, but identifying it and actually fixing it are different things — he wants to run code on it before believing the marketing.

What's NOT here:

  • No hands-on device, no benchmarks, no local-LLM tokens-per-second numbers.
