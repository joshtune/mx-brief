Is it a banger?

Codie Sanchez · 2026-09-11 · 5,127 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Hire for results, not resumes. Starbucks proves it.

Side hustlers get burned by hiring and taking advice from people who explain businesses instead of running them, and this 60-second Starbucks autopsy gives you the one filter to avoid it.

What it is: A short case study on Starbucks' CEO flop, used to teach one hiring rule: never confuse someone who can explain a business with someone who can run one.

How it works:

  • Starbucks hired Laxman Narasimhan, a 22-year McKinsey consultant who had advised companies but never operated one.
  • Market cap went from $114B to $74B in 17 months. A $40B miss in under two years.
  • The board corrected by hiring Brian Niccol, the operator who took Chipotle from $7B to $70B.
  • Stock jumped $20B the day Niccol was announced. The market priced the difference between explainer and operator instantly.
  • Codie's rule from the 'People' chapter of *Owner Be Owned*: hiring on resumes instead of results costs small owners hundreds of thousands or millions to discover the mistake.
  • The one question before hiring anyone or taking advice: Have they actually done what they're telling you to do?

Tools & links:

Why it matters for you: Side hustles are cash-constrained and can't absorb a bad hire or bad advice, so filter every contractor, partner, coach, and course by operator track record, not pedigree.
