I Found the Perfect First Business for Introverts

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-17 · 32,706 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Introvert makes $15K/mo implementing AI for small businesses

It's a $20-startup-cost side hustle where your engineering brain is the product and the "sales" is just helping people you already know — no cold calling, no code required (but you can code, which is an edge).

What it is: An AI implementation/consulting practice — Ryan, a 24-year-old self-taught non-coder in Cincinnati, went from a $52K/yr dead-end job to $15K/mo profit in about 18 months. Total startup cost: $20.

The numbers:

  • $15,000/month profit at 24 years old
  • $20 total to start (essentially just tool subscriptions)
  • Project pricing: $1,000 – $20,000 per implementation, scoped to complexity
  • Client revenue range: pre-revenue to $20M, but the sweet spot is sub-$10M
  • Zero sales experience, zero door-knocking, zero boiler room

Who the customer is:

  • Ideal avatar: startup founders who already believe in AI but whose teams aren't actually implementing it
  • They've read the hype, dabbled in ChatGPT, and have no idea how to operationalize it
  • Geography is not a constraint — Cincinnati is explicitly *not* a tech hub, and that's the point. Less competition, more unmet demand
  • Age range of clients is all over the map; the gap is capability, not demographics

How the idea is carried out:

  • Step 1 — Build a list of 100 people you already know. Scroll your phone contacts. Friends, friends' parents, previous co-workers, church, neighbors, teachers. Ryan's whole pipeline started here.
  • Step 2 — Reach out to provide value, not to sell. This is the introvert unlock: "it's way easier if you're not trying to sell. I was just trying to get on the phone and provide value in any way I can." The call has no ask, so there's no rejection to fear.
  • Step 3 — Join rooms where founders already are. Local AI meetups, Cincinnati business groups, online and in-person business masterminds. He deliberately targeted founders with the *opposite* skill set — they need technical help, he needs distribution.
  • Step 4 — Solve their single biggest problem with simple tools. No custom code. Off-the-shelf AI + automation, self-taught.
  • Step 5 — Run the DASA framework. Pitched as the key to his success: the structured process he runs before *any* AI project — used to scope and de-risk the engagement so it doesn't turn into an open-ended mess. (The transcript teases it heavily; the full breakdown is in the video body.)

Why the timing argument holds:

  • You have the same AI access enterprises have — that asymmetry is gone
  • You arguably have *better* tools, because enterprises are throttled by internal policy and governance
  • Speed is the moat: you ship in days what their procurement takes quarters to approve

Tools & links:

  • beehiiv — newsletter platform Chris has used for 18 months, sponsor of the episode (30% off first 30 days). Relevant if you want an owned distribution channel alongside the consulting
  • Core "stack" is unglamorous by design: no-code/low-code automation + LLM APIs. Ryan's differentiator is problem selection, not tooling

Where the framing is thin:

  • "$20 to start" ignores the 18 months of unpaid learning and network-building that actually produced the outcome
  • One data point, selected for being exceptional — treat $15K/mo as the ceiling of a good run, not the median
  • "Never learned to sell" is doing a lot of work here; making 100 warm outreach calls *is* selling, just with the pressure removed

Why it matters for you: You already have the technical depth Ryan taught himself in 18 months — the only thing standing between you and $1K–$20K projects is a list of 100 contacts and a call that doesn't try to sell anything.
