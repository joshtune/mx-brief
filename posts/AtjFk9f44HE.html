His AI Made 20 Reels Before Breakfast

Matt Wolfe · 2026-10-28 · 69,765 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Nate Herk's Claude Code workflows for video and social

Mostly a video-editing and social-media demo with little web-app content, but the 'feed it proven examples so the skill isn't generic' approach to building Claude Code skills carries over to your own projects.

What it is: Episode 2 of Matt's "show me your workflow" series. Nate Herk demos three Claude Code builds: an AI video-editing skill, an AI expert panel called "The Roast Room", and an AI social media manager. The transcript provided only covers the intro and the start of the editing workflow.

How it works:

  • Output: Nate says he produced about 20 reels in one morning. The pipeline handles ideation, scripting, B-roll grabbing and AI-generated video clips. He reads the script off a teleprompter and records raw, mistakes included.
  • Why earlier attempts failed: Slow re-renders and constant fix-ups. Fixing that took a change in how he thought about the workflow, plus saving feedback and knowledge into reusable skills.
  • Core idea: LLMs predict the likely next word, so they default to what most people would do and produce generic, "cheap" results. Nate's fix was to give the skill proven reels as reference examples, so it copies what already worked.
  • Edit structure: Three scene types: him on the bottom with B-roll on top, full-screen B-roll, and full-screen him. Styling is consistent (font, motion graphics, shimmers, sound effects, music).
  • Weak spot: Music and sound design. Nate says a human editor still does it better. He keeps a "Herc edit kit" of saved sound effects, music and trending audio, and keeps feeding in notes on when to use each.
  • Not covered in the provided transcript: The Roast Room panel and the social media manager. Don't assume details about them.

Tools & links:

  • Claude Code — the agent harness he builds skills in
  • FutureTools — Matt's AI tools directory, from the channel description
  • "Reverse engineer anything" repo — Nate mentions it as a recent viral repo, but gives no URL in the transcript

Why it matters for you: There's nothing here on building web apps. The transferable idea is to build Claude Code skills from proven examples plus saved feedback, instead of prompting from scratch. Skip the rest unless you make short-form video.
