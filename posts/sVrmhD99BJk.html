30 real-world Grok Bot use cases, errands to autonomous companies

It's a roundup of what people actually got Grok Bots to do (bill negotiation, phone calls, device fixes), ending with Nate's own business setup, but the transcript we have cuts off at #10, so the build details aren't covered here.

What it is: A listicle of 30 real user stories showing Grok Bot agents doing real-world tasks, from one-off errands to running a whole company. Nate says the last one is the setup he runs his own business on.

Coverage note: The transcript was truncated at use case #10. Items 11-30, including Nate's own setup, are not covered below.

How it works (use cases covered, #1-#9):

  • #1 Lower your bills: Peter Yang's bot called Comcast, navigated the phone menu, and declined three upsells. It used a competitor's cheaper fiber offer as leverage, and the bill dropped from $84 to $45/mo on a faster plan in about 25 minutes. It paused for his approval before accepting the deal.
  • #2 Sit on hold for you: DJ's bot stayed on an AT&T call for the full 90 minutes, which is the max call length. The rep didn't realize it was a bot until the end.
  • #3 Book a doctor's appointment: Mencho described his daughter's injury and the bot found nearby clinics and called one when it opened. When the receptionist asked something it didn't know, it pushed the question to Mencho in chat. She was seen the same day.
  • #4 Book travel: Philip Kaplan (founder of Drokid) used it for a complex family trip. It works through the real booking flows on Delta, American, Alaska, JetBlue and Frontier, and it confirms with you before booking.
  • #5 Hunt down sold-out items: Liam was told the iPhone 18 Pro Max would take a month to arrive. His bot, given a virtual card, emailed stores, found one in stock, ordered it, and had it delivered a month ahead of Apple's date. Elon quoted this one and said it "actually works."
  • #6 Hand off the mental load: Stella Garber's bot went through back-to-school emails, found three forgotten gift cards (~$150), and booked an overdue physical by offering three time slots. Matthew Burman's bot reads school and sports emails and puts the important dates on his calendar.
  • #7 Fix Wi-Fi: Igor gave the bot his parents' router model, setup and device list, then followed its steps. Speed went from 85 Mbps to over 1,400 Mbps.
  • #8 Strip ads and tracking from a smart TV: Robert Corson's bot used his desktop to connect to a TCL TV over Wi-Fi, listed every ad and tracking app, and disabled them. It took about 10 minutes, done from his phone.
  • #9 Big boring jobs: Elon's mom (May Musk) had it sort 2,000 Dropbox photos into folders. She had blocked a week for it, and the bot finished in about 5 minutes.
  • #10: Getting your phone number off spam lists. The transcript cuts off here.
  • Teaser claims from the intro: $13,000 off a car, four years of tax mistakes caught, and skipping a $200,000 hire.

Patterns across the stories:

  • The bot works through real interfaces (phone calls, booking sites, the user's desktop) rather than APIs.
  • Human-in-the-loop checkpoints: it asks before spending money or committing, and escalates questions it can't answer.
  • It uses leverage and context (competitor offers, device specs) rather than just following scripts.
  • It can spend through virtual cards.
