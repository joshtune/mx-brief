How two guys built a $300M horse empire

My First Million · 2026-09-10 · 5,385 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Equine Network: from dying horse magazines to $300M exit.

A textbook playbook for spotting a monopoly-adjacent niche inside your own ad data, then buying and building every adjacent business until the whole vertical is yours.

What it is: Andy and Tom started Equine Network in the early 2000s as a horse-owner magazine publisher, pivoted through a mediocre website, then used a single advertiser insight to roll up the entire horse-services vertical. Sold for roughly $300M about six months before this clip.

The people:

  • Andy and Tom (surnames not given in the clip) — founders of Equine Network
  • No outside playbook; they grew by noticing who was buying ads from them and why

Timeline of what they did:

  • Early 2000s: launch Equine Network as a set of print magazines targeting horse owners
  • Print declines, so they convert to a website — only ~600K monthly uniques, unimpressive on its own
  • They notice one recurring advertiser: essentially the AAA for horse trailers (roadside assistance for people hauling horses)
  • They call the owner and ask how he grows. His answer: he doesn't, he just advertises on their site and that's where every customer comes from
  • They buy that business for next to nothing, since they own its only real acquisition channel
  • Realization: own the audience, then own every business that sells to it

How they made money (the full list from the clip):

  • Horse-owner magazines — the original, now-declining print business
  • Equine Network website — the audience/ad platform with ~600K monthly uniques that became the distribution engine for everything else
  • Horse trailer roadside assistance (the AAA for horses) — bought cheap, grown by owning its marketing channel
  • Fly-control subscription — ~45,000 customers paying ~$300/year for recurring shipments to keep flies off horses; roughly $13.5M/year in recurring revenue
  • Largest roping event in the country — live events, tickets, sponsorship, and entry fees
  • Plus an unnamed string of additional businesses launched or bought under the same thesis: start every business under the sun that a horse owner could pay for
  • Exit: sold the whole roll-up for ~$300M roughly six months ago

The playbook, distilled:

  • Build or own a niche audience, even a small one, where you are the only meaningful channel
  • Read your own ad logs: whoever keeps buying ads from you has a business that depends on you
  • Buy that business cheaply because its only growth lever is already in your hands
  • Repeat across every product a member of that audience buys: insurance-style services, consumables on subscription, events
  • Subscriptions on consumables (fly control) are the quiet cash engine that makes the whole thing valuable to an acquirer
