Llama Cpp Flags That Instantly Speed It Up

Alex Ziskind · 2026-09-18 · 12,683 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Four llama.cpp flags that 2-3x local LLM speed on NVIDIA's DGX Spark.

It's a flags tutorial, not a buying guide, but the benchmark numbers give you real DGX Spark throughput to weigh against a Mac mini.

What it is: A walkthrough of llama.cpp build/runtime flags tested on NVIDIA's DGX Spark, benchmarking two models (a smaller "Nimatron" model and OSS 120B).

The hardware (what you care about):

  • Machine: NVIDIA DGX Spark — Nvidia's compact AI desktop box, not a Mac mini competitor spec-for-spec (it's built around a GB10 Grace Blackwell superchip, not Apple silicon).
  • No price mentioned in the video; DGX Spark retails around $3,000-4,000 depending on config — several times a Mac mini's cost, so the comparison is really "local LLM throughput per dollar," not a swap-in replacement.
  • Reported numbers on DGX Spark: ~44 tokens/sec baseline decode on the smaller model; with OSS 120B, ~42 tokens/sec decode and ~1,300 tokens/sec prompt processing after tuning.

How it works (the flags, if you want them):

  • `FA=1` — flash attention, universal win, turn it on always.
  • `-ub` (batch size, e.g. 2048) — biggest lever for prompt processing (PP) speed; model/backend-specific, worth testing per model.
  • `--reasoning-format auto` — cleans up reasoning-model output formatting.
  • `--no-mmap` — significantly faster model loading specifically on Spark-class hardware.

Tools & links:

  • llama.cpp — the inference engine being tuned.

Why it matters for you: Treat this as a data point on DGX Spark's real-world LLM throughput (42-1300 tok/s depending on stage) rather than a hardware-cost comparison — you'd need to pair it with Mac mini M-series benchmarks elsewhere to get the cost/performance answer you're after.
