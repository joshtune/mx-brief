How to Know If You Should Leave Your Company

A Life Engineered · 2026-08-16 · 3,172 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Four gut-checks on whether to quit your job

It hands you a concrete diagnostic for the question you've probably been circling for months — is my ceiling here real, or is my manager just retaining me?

What it is: A four-part gut-check for deciding whether to stay or leave — two external signals, two internal ones. No frameworks, no fluff, just questions with real answers.

How it works:

*External check 1 — talk to people at the companies that intimidate you*

  • Specifically the ones triggering imposter syndrome ("I might not be as good as them, so why try?")
  • Ask three questions: What do you do day-to-day? How did you get this role? What do you actually get and make here?
  • The point is reframing. Your fear is built on a guess about their bar; talking to them replaces the guess with data — and the data is usually less scary than the fear.

*External check 2 — research competitors at your stage*

  • Same industry, same or similar company stage. Apples to apples.
  • His claim: this research alone sometimes flips your decision — no conversations required.

*Internal check 1 — audit your manager's signal honesty*

  • The tell: "you're up for promotion" three times in a row, each time blocked by "things beyond my control."
  • That leaves exactly two readings, and both are bad news for you:
  • They aren't politically savvy enough to land it, or
  • They don't actually value you that much and are dragging you along to retain you
  • The real question underneath: do you trust that your manager both values you *and* knows the mechanics of getting you promoted? Those are two separate skills, and you need both.

*Internal check 2 — price your existing skill set elsewhere*

  • Would the same skills, unchanged, be valued more (and paid more) in a different org?
  • Note the framing: this is not "do I need to level up first." It's a market mispricing question. Same you, different buyer.

What to prepare for:

  • The three-strikes promo pattern is the loudest signal here — start counting. Once you hit three, stop treating it as bad luck and treat it as information.
  • Do the external research *before* you're desperate. Both external checks require goodwill conversations and calm comparison, which are much harder to run from a burned-out position.
  • Build the network now. "Talk to people at those other companies" assumes you have people to talk to. If you don't, that's your actual first task.

Why it matters for you: The message is that "should I leave?" is answerable with legwork rather than vibes — four questions, two weeks of conversations, and a hard count of how many times your manager has promised something they didn't deliver.
