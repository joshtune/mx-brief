This Socrates Quote Will Change Your Life

Codie Sanchez · 2026-07-01 · 9,063 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Wealthy operators relentlessly audit their own mistakes.

The single habit that separates people who compound wins from people who repeat losses is a blame-free post-mortem — the exact discipline a solo side-hustler needs when there's no team to catch errors.

What it is: A 30-second riff on Socrates' "the unexamined life is not worth living," reframed as a wealth-building operating habit.

How it works:

  • Break down everything in your business and life — what's working, what's not.
  • When you make a mistake, trace the *process* to find exactly where it broke.
  • No defensiveness, no gatekeeping — a mistake isn't a character flaw, it's data.
  • The rule: never make the same mistake twice.
  • The mechanism for not repeating it: obsess over what you did the first time until you can name the failure point.

Tools & links:

  • No tools, repos, or URLs mentioned in the transcript — this is pure mindset.

Why it matters for you: Running a side hustle solo means you *are* the QA department; a ruthless, ego-free post-mortem on every mistake is the cheapest edge you have to stop bleeding time and money on repeat errors.
