A Genius Mathematician Who Wanted Nothing

Perelman refused fame and a $1M math prize.

A short philosophical aside on ambition and balance — almost no business or money-making meat for your focus.

What it is: A brief Shaan/Sam riff on Grigori Perelman, the Russian mathematician who solved the Poincaré conjecture and then walked away from every reward.

The story:

  • Perelman proved the Poincaré conjecture (one of math's hardest unsolved problems).
  • He was offered the Fields Medal (the hosts call it "the Nobel Prize for math" — note: there is no actual Nobel in mathematics) and turned it down.
  • He was also eligible for the $1M Clay Millennium Prize and declined that too.
  • Famous quote when called about the award: *"You're disturbing me from picking mushrooms"* — then he hung up and never collected.

The actual takeaway:

  • The hosts use him as a foil, not a role model.
  • Their point: people romanticize extreme rebels who "want nothing," but going *all-in* on any single identity — pure ascetic or pure striver — is the real mistake.
  • The lesson is balance, not renunciation.
