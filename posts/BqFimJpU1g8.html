Do AI models really get worse after launch?

This is not a build-along tutorial, but it explains why your Claude or GPT workflows can feel degraded a few weeks after launch and where to check, which matters if you ship anything on these models.

What it is: A commentary video, not a tutorial. Nate looks at whether frontier models get "nerfed" after launch, what the labs have admitted in writing, and what the public trackers show. The transcript is cut off mid-sentence, so the ending is missing.

How it works:

  • The launch-page gap: Anthropic's own launch page shows Opus 5.5 at 57.8% on coding, but the version people actually use scores about 52%.
  • Anthropic's admissions: It published two postmortems saying Claude got worse for weeks at a time. One covered Claude Code feeling less intelligent. The other admitted its evals "simply didn't capture the degradation that users were reporting."
  • OpenAI's admission: The day after GPT-5 launched, Sam Altman said it seemed way dumber because something broke behind the scenes. The usual remedy is a usage reset.
  • No proof the weights change: Nate found no evidence that the launch-day model differs from the one weeks later. The "dials" are around the model, not the model itself.
  • Quantization theory:
  • Weights are the billions of numbers that make up the model's brain.
  • Quantization rounds them off to make the model smaller and cheaper, like 720p instead of 4K.
  • People suspect labs do this after launch, but nobody has admitted it, leaked it, or proven it.
  • Trackers:
  • Live Nerf runs the same 78 questions through a model daily. After 50 days it saw no drop in Opus 5.5.
  • Margin Lab caught a real drop from about 65% to about 50%. The cause was an update to the Claude Code app, not the model.
  • Both were built by independent people. The labs publish no daily scores, and nothing requires them to disclose when they turn a dial down.
