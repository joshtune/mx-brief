Why seasonal Halloween pop-up stores are a sneaky good business

Short, number-heavy look at a cheap-to-launch seasonal retail model (about $600K to buy 3-4 stores, about $1M revenue), though the transcript excerpt is thin on the full playbook.

What it is: Codie Sanchez (guest) and the hosts break down the economics of Halloween pop-up retail, the Spirit Halloween and Halloween Express model, after trying to buy a Halloween Express franchise.

How it works (what the excerpt supports):

  • The deal they looked at: Halloween Express was listed at about $600,000 for 3-4 retail locations, plus whatever last year's inventory was.
  • Standardized footprint: Spirit and Halloween Express use the same model, with every store about 10,000 sq ft.
  • Cheap, fast build-out: A typical retail store costs millions and takes months to open. These cost roughly $10-50K to set up and are open in days or weeks.
  • Short operating window: Stores are open about two months a year.
  • Revenue: They said these locations would bring in about $1M, recalled as roughly $950K. The excerpt doesn't say whether that is per store or across the group.

What they did to make money / how the business earns:

  • Rent big, empty retail space for a short window.
  • Fill it with seasonal inventory.
  • Sell at high volume for about two months, then close.
  • Keep fixed costs low through the cheap, quick setup.

Not in the excerpt (don't assume): Codie's full background, whether they bought the stores, margins, lease terms, inventory costs, staffing, and profit figures. Watch the full video for those.

Why it matters for you: The model is low capex, short-duration retail with a large revenue-to-setup ratio, but the excerpt gives no profit numbers, so verify them before treating it as a playbook.
