Anthropic Engineers Just 10x'd Everyone's Claude Code

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-10-08 · 41,893 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Delete your bloated Claude prompts; less context wins

Nate audits his own CLAUDE.md and skills against Anthropic's new 'smaller context' guidance and shows the /doctor workflow, which is the practical part you'll want to copy.

What it is: A context-engineering video. Nate argues that the rules, skills and CLAUDE.md files you piled on for older models now slow Claude down, and he walks through how to prune them using Anthropic's own approach.

How it works:

  • The claim: An Anthropic engineer published an article (titled roughly 'the new rules of context engineering for Claude 5 generation models'; the transcript garbles the author's name and title). It says the team cut over 80% of Claude Code's system prompt for Opus 5 and Fable 5 / 5.1 and saw no measurable loss on their evals. Nate hasn't verified this and cites it secondhand.
  • Rule rot: Claude makes a mistake, you add a rule, the model improves, and the rule stays. Eventually you have duplicate or slightly different versions across CLAUDE.md and skills.
  • Anthropic's example: A hard cap on code comments was replaced with 'follow the style of the surrounding code'. The cap had become too restrictive for projects that needed more explanation.
  • Boris Cherny's advice: Boris is the creator of Claude Code. He says to delete all your skills about every 6 months. The transcript garbles the surname, so check it.
  • Prompt shape: Give Claude the outcome you want (what 'done' looks like), why you're doing it, and the constraints to respect. Then leave it room to work.
  • Nate's rule of thumb: The best context is the smallest amount that gets the highest-quality result at the lowest cost. Anthropic's applied AI team adds that minimal doesn't mean short. Keep any detail the model actually needs.
  • Evidence cited: A 2025 paper tested five models on math, QA and coding. Performance dropped as input got longer, by 13.9% to 85% depending on model and task, even when the models could retrieve the relevant info. Nate cautions these are old, controlled experiments and don't predict what trimming your own CLAUDE.md will do.
  • Stronger evidence: Anthropic loaded MCP tool definitions only when needed. They reported an 85% token reduction. MCP eval scores rose from 49% to 74% on Opus 4 and from 79.5% to 88.1% on Opus 4.5. Claude kept access to the tools and just didn't load every instruction up front.
  • /doctor: Nate ran it on his setup and got a workflow audit listing specific instructions and handoffs to clean up. He promises a demo.
  • Demo (not in the provided transcript): He has Claude review his path from a YouTube idea to a filmable script or outline. He asks it to find one small improvement and point to the file so he can verify it.
