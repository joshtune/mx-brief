Forget Snack Machines. This One Refills Itself

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-09-14 · 13,500 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Ice vending machines as a zero-employee cash-flow side hustle.

If you want a semi-passive side hustle with ~90% margins and no staff, CJ in Fort Worth lays out exactly how he buys, places, and runs ice vending machines.

What it is: Chris interviews CJ, a Fort Worth operator running a handful of ice-and-water vending machines about a mile apart, with zero employees, pulling $15–20K/month.

The idea:

  • Unattended ice vending (Everest-style machines) that make and bag ice on-site, so inventory refills itself.
  • Cost per bag is ~7–10 cents; all-in cost per sale is roughly a nickel-plus, priced at $2 for 10 lb and $3.50 for 20 lb. Call it ~90% gross margin.
  • Undercuts CVS and gas stations (~$4 for 7 lb) by nearly 3x on volume, so the customer base is sticky.

How CJ got in:

  • Owned DFW Pool Services with 7 trucks and 3 vans, spending a couple grand a month on ice and water for crews.
  • Turned the cost center into a profit center by buying his own machine instead of paying gas-station prices.

How to find machines cheap:

  • Network hyper-locally with individual owners of Everest machines and water stations. Most are small-business owners and happy to talk.
  • Target single-machine owners: older, burned out, and one Saturday breakdown away from quitting. That's your distressed inventory.
  • A friend of CJ's got a machine for $500 that was worth $10–15K after a few parts. He's now flipping it for $15–20K to upgrade.
  • Watch for neglected locations near you and make a lowball offer, same playbook as distressed real estate.

How to pick a location:

  • Traffic counts first. Pull daily car volume from your state DOT (.gov) site; CJ's spot sees 30–35K cars/day.
  • Easy pull-in parking that fits a truck with a trailer. Core customers are landscapers, electricians, and pool crews filling coolers Monday through Friday.
  • Visibility from the road matters more than foot traffic.

How it's run day to day:

  • No employees. CJ and a partner handle jams, dirty machines, and cracked doors themselves, with one on-call tech for the hard stuff.
  • Leasing one machine at $550/month; first-week Saturday sales hit $1,000–1,500, so the lease covers itself in a weekend.
  • Scale by clustering machines within a mile of each other so one person can service the whole route.

Startup numbers:

  • Small ice machine: $500–1,000 if you find a distressed seller.
  • Full Everest-style unit: $10–20K used, or lease around $550/month.
