Use These 5 Psychology Tricks to Make a Ton of Money

Codie Sanchez · 2026-09-27 · 47,046 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Persuasion tactics mapped to five brain regions

Every side hustle lives or dies on getting strangers to say yes, and this gives you concrete, copy-paste wording for authority, social proof, and scarcity instead of feature-dumping.

What it is: A persuasion playbook that organizes sales psychology by five brain regions; the transcript covers the frontal lobe and the start of the occipital lobe (vision) before it cuts off.

How it works:

  • The instructional secret: People don't buy on logic. They buy on a shortcut answer to "is this good for me?" Stop listing features and feed that shortcut instead.
  • The "is this good" triangle: three mental shortcuts (heuristics) that answer the question for the buyer.
  • Authority (the kingmaker):
  • Someone else has to call you the expert. You can't say it about yourself.
  • Cialdini's team tested this on a real estate agency. A receptionist added one line before transferring calls: "Let me put you through to Peter, our head of sales. He has over 20 years selling properties in this area."
  • Result: appointments up 20%, signed contracts up 15%.
  • It works best on people who are alone and unfamiliar with you.
  • Social proof:
  • Tell buyers what most people do, and attach a number.
  • Hotel towel study: "Almost 75% of guests reuse their towels" beat "save the environment."
  • Offer application: "75% of people need the middle offer." Buyers upgrade from your entry offer more often.
  • Scarcity:
  • Never write "limited spots available." Nobody believes it.
  • Write the real count instead: "I have four slots in this cohort. Two are gone."
  • Specificity implies scarcity without announcing it.
  • Bonus, no round numbers:
  • Quote $437,500, not $450k. Quote $9,850, not $10k.
  • Keep it believable. A condo listed at $454,963 reportedly got zero offers.
  • Prices should look informed, not random.
  • Occipital lobe (vision): Teased at the end of the excerpt. People judge you visually in under a second, so your cover matters. The excerpt cuts off before the details.
  • Not covered in the excerpt: the other three brain areas.

Why it matters for you: For a side hustle you'll be selling with no brand behind you, so a borrowed expert line, a real percentage, and an honest slot count are cheap tweaks you can add to your pitch today.
