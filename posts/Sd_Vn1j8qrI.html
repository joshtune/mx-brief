These AI-proof businesses can make you $10M/year

My First Million · 2026-11-04 · 62,997 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Boring peer-group communities are AI-proof money machines

You get a full teardown of NATA 20 Groups, a roughly $13-15M/yr peer-group business with ugly marketing and no ads, plus the exact model to copy in a different niche.

What it is: Sam Parr argues that non-AI people can still build durable businesses on communities, and uses NATA 20 Groups (auto dealer peer groups) as the main case study. White Coat Investor (finance community for doctors) is the second example, but the transcript cuts off before the details.

How it works (NATA 20 Groups):

  • Who: Run through NATA, which Sam believes is the National Automotive Dealer Association. He thinks it's an association or nonprofit. The program started in the 1970s.
  • Entry: You apply to join, then pay about $6-8K per year in dues.
  • Grouping: Members are placed in groups of about 20-24. The groups are non-competitive, so nobody in your group is in your region.
  • Meetings: Members fly to the same city three times (Sam's phrasing is muddled on cadence).
  • The product: Each member brings their P&L. The group goes line by line while the owner explains the numbers. Peers say either "you're killing it, I'll copy that" or "you're underperforming, try this."
  • Expansion: It started with car dealers and now admits HVAC company founders, marina owners and boat repair shop owners.
  • Revenue (Sam's guess): About $13-15M per year. He bases this on the publicly stated dues and member counts.
  • Marketing: Terrible website, no ads, and the pitch is generic ("Learn from the best"). It has run for decades anyway.

How it makes money:

  • Annual membership dues of about $6-8K per member.
  • Recurring revenue with high retention. The value is peer P&L benchmarking that members can't get elsewhere.
  • The cost base is low: the members supply the content and the org supplies the structure and logistics.
  • It grows by letting in adjacent industries such as HVAC and marinas.

The copy-it playbook Sam pitches:

  • Pick a boring, high-income niche where the average member earns about $1M a year. His examples are dentist practices, lawyers, and a "millionaire next door" owner in South Dakota.
  • Avoid ecom, which is too competitive. Sam references his own Club LTV, a community for ecom owners.
  • Copy the NATA format: a P&L-sharing peer group with a paid application.
  • Run ads, because the incumbents never do.
  • Sam's claim: it's AI-proof and could last decades.

White Coat Investor (briefly):

  • A community for doctors, and maybe dentists, focused on the finance side of their lives.
