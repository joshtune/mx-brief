This Is What Happens When You CRUSH An AI Video Model

Alex Ziskind · 2026-06-19 · 43,760 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Quantizing local AI video models reveals format beats bit count.

Heads-up for anyone running AI video locally: the quant format you pick wrecks or saves quality more than the bit count — but note this one's about software, not which machine to buy.

What it is: A hands-on test crushing two local text-to-video models down an 8-step quantization ladder to see what breaks first.

How it works:

  • Tested two models: Wan 2.2 (14B params) and LTX 2.3 (22B params), text-to-video.
  • Walked each from FP16 down through FP8, Q8, and lower across 5 prompts.
  • Measured drift from full-precision baseline using LPIPS (perceptual similarity score).
  • FP8 and Q8 are the *same disk size*, but FP8 drifts ~2x further from baseline than Q8.
  • FP8's failure was qualitative, not just blurry: on a "red car" motion prompt, the car drove backwards — only at FP8, no other quant.
  • Takeaway: "Format matters more than bit count" — where the format spends its precision beats how many bits it has.

Hardware shown (the part you care about):

  • Runs on an RTX Pro 6000 GPU — B&H link. This is a workstation card (~$8K+ class), not a Mac. No Mac mini comparison in this one; it's GPU-bound video gen, which Apple Silicon doesn't lead on.
  • Peripherals only: Thunderbolt 5 external SSD, 15" magnetic display.

Why it matters for you: No Mac-mini-vs-X cost angle here — this is an NVIDIA workstation video, and the real lesson is software (pick Q8 over FP8), so you can safely skim or skip unless the RTX Pro 6000 pricing interests you.
