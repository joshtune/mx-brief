I Thought Local AI Video Was Good

Alex Ziskind · 2026-06-21 · 48,951 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Local AI video vs frontier cloud models, compared.

If you're weighing whether your own machine can do real AI video work, this shows where local generation still loses badly to cloud — though Alex never names the rig or its cost here.

What it is: Alex pits locally-generated AI video (run free/private on his own machine) against a top frontier cloud model, then muses on whether video gen is about to get the same agentic leap code did.

How it works:

  • Local stack: WAN 2.2 (BF16, highest quality) for video and LTX for audio-synced video — both run start-to-finish on his machine, nothing sent to cloud, ~10–30 sec clips
  • Cloud comparison: Seed Dance 2.0 accessed via Higgsfield, called from Claude Code through an MCP endpoint
  • Same prompts/seeds produce wildly different results — Seed Dance wins on sharpness, hair/water motion, camera movement, and frame-to-frame consistency
  • Local results show glitches: rubbery faces, merging marbles, broken physics, facial drift across frames
  • Killer demo: he fed Seed Dance a single frame from a local clip and it rebuilt the whole video with consistent faces and realistic voices
  • Big idea: video gen today feels like code AI a few years ago — prompt-in/clip-out — and may be about to become "agentic" (understands intent, builds the next version)

Hardware note: This video does NOT specify the machine or its cost. He references an earlier video where he compared quantizations and generated local video — that's likely where the rig specs live.

Tools & links:

  • Higgsfield MCP — MCP endpoint connecting Claude Code to AI video/image/audio generation (Seed Dance 2.0)
  • WAN 2.2 — local open video model (BF16 quant for top quality)
  • LTX — local model for video with synced audio

Why it matters for you: No machine name or price tag here — the takeaway is strategic: local AI video runs free and private but still trails cloud frontier models hard, so for now the spend question is cloud credits vs. a local rig, not which GPU.
