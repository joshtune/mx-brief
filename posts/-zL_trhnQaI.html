So You Learned Claude, Now What?

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-06-22 · 32,288 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Turn Claude skills into income as an AI consultant.

It reframes 'knowing Claude' as a fast-decaying asset and argues the durable, high-paying move is diagnosing what to build — useful context if you're weighing how to monetize AI skills inside or beyond your dev job.

What it is: A thought-leadership/career roadmap (not a build tutorial) arguing that the next high-value AI role is *consultant* — the person who decides what to build and why — not the fastest builder.

The core argument:

  • Getting good at Claude *as a tool* means little long-term — tools change constantly. What lasts are the underlying skills and the ability to re-apply them to each new AI phase.
  • The AI money-making phases have rotated roughly yearly, and early movers each time caught the wave; latecomers fought a race-to-the-bottom market.

The phases he maps out:

  • ~1 year ago: set up *one automation or chatbot* for a small business → easy money
  • Next: *AI systems / AI Automation Agency* phase — productized done-for-you services
  • Then: *AI agent builder* era — agents that think and execute repetitive tasks
  • Now: *agentic* phase — AI that does the work, not just answers (Gartner projects ~$202B agentic AI spend in 2026)

The thesis — be the doctor, not the pharmacist:

  • A pharmacist hands you what you ask for; a doctor diagnoses what you actually need.
  • Builders = pharmacists, consultants = doctors. Clients don't know what they need — they only know what hurts. The diagnostician gets paid.
  • Your job isn't winning the build race; it's *naming the real problem*.

Stats he leans on:

  • McKinsey: ~88% of orgs use AI somewhere, but only ~⅓ have turned it into real projects — "almost everyone uses AI, almost nobody is good at it." That gap is the opportunity.
  • AI consulting market projected to pass $64B by 2028.
  • ~30% of company AI projects get abandoned (transcript cuts off mid-point here).
