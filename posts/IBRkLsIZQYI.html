Billion dollar business: Car vending machine

My First Million · 2026-08-08 · 11,509 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Shaan and Sam gush over Carvana's car vending machines

It's a 90-second riff, not a teardown — you get the hook (glass tower = free billboard) and the hosts' anecdotal read that Carvana overpays for used cars, but zero numbers on how the business actually makes money.

What it is: A short clip where Shaan Puri and Sam Parr riff on Carvana — the online used-car retailer known for its multi-story glass "car vending machine" towers.

The business, as described in the clip:

  • Buy a used car entirely on your phone — no salesperson, no dealership haggling
  • Sell your car to them online: they make an instant offer sight-unseen
  • Delivery theater: the car gets dispensed from a vertical glass tower stacked with inventory

What they actually did to make money (per the hosts — anecdote, not analysis):

  • Removed the salesperson. The whole transaction is app-based, which strips the highest-friction, highest-cost part of used-car retail.
  • Built the store as the ad. The towers sit on highways as "Lego towers of cars." Shaan: it has "that kind of wow factor" — the building is the marketing spend, not a line item.
  • Overpaid on acquisition to win supply. Sam shopped his car around after getting a Carvana offer; every dealer told him "they pay like crazy high for cars, sell it to them." Paying above market for inventory is how they won sourcing — and why Shaan thought they'd go bust.

The unresolved question: Both hosts are customers, both are baffled. Sam's closing line: "I don't know how on earth they make profit, but it's amazing." The clip never answers it — no unit economics, no margin structure, no discussion of the 2022 near-bankruptcy or the recovery.
