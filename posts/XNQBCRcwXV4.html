I Deleted All My Claude Skills... And Claude Got Smarter

Nate Herk | AI Automation · 2026-08-12 · 29,433 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Deleting CLAUDE.md, skills, and hooks improves Opus 5 output.

Anthropic's own Claude Code lead is telling you to delete your CLAUDE.md, skills, and hooks every six months — and Nate actually ran the experiment on a duplicated repo to see what happens.

What it is: A reaction-plus-experiment video built on an interview with Boris (Claude Code's creator), arguing that the scaffolding you built for older models is now actively degrading Opus 5.

The core claim (straight from Anthropic):

  • Anthropic deleted over 80% of the Claude Code system prompt for the Opus 5 release.
  • Every model launch, they delete/rewrite system prompt chunks, swap the tool set, and rewrite tool descriptions. Prompts do not transfer across model generations.
  • Reason given: most of the old system prompt was *correcting for behaviors the model should have known*. Opus 5 already does those things — so the corrections are now noise.
  • Boris's direct advice to non-builders: "every six months, delete your CLAUDE.md, delete your skills, delete your hooks, see what the model does, and it might surprise you." For Opus 5 specifically, he says they *really do recommend* trying this.
  • The term for it: unhobbling the model — removing constraints rather than adding them.

Nate's experiment:

  • He'd been feeling Opus 5 as *degraded* — falling back to 4.8 for some tasks and getting better results.
  • Realization: the degradation may be his own instruction stack fighting a smarter model, not the model.
  • Method: duplicated his repo, stripped out CLAUDE.md and all skills, ran it clean, compared.

The mental model he uses:

  • Instructing a 10-year-old on a slide deck → be maximally prescriptive (10 slides, exact titles, colors, text formatting). Zero freedom, because stakes are high and competence is low.
  • Instructing a 10-year veteran who's made hundreds of decks → the same instruction set *gets in their way* and blocks their subject-matter expertise.
  • Opus 5 is the veteran. Your CLAUDE.md is still talking to the 10-year-old.

Practical takeaways:

  • Treat every model release as a different personality — different flaws, strengths, and prompting style. Re-audit skills on each drop.
  • Don't assume same-model-same-results: how much scaffolding you *remove* is now a differentiator.
  • Test by duplication, not deletion — copy the repo, strip it, A/B it. Cheap and reversible.
