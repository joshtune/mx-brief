It's an interview, not a build-along: you get Pocock's case for why clear communication and engineering fundamentals beat raw prompting, but no walkthrough of what he builds.

What it is: A sit-down interview where Nate Herk talks to Matt Pocock, a self-taught dev who coded by hand for about 10 years, then went all-in on coding agents.

How it works:

  • Caveat: The transcript I got cuts off about two minutes in, so everything below is the opening only. Later segments (the war story, career advice, process design) are teasers from the cold open, not verified content.
  • Pocock's turning point: He was a skeptic until Opus 4.5 and OpenClaw landed in December 2025. After that he stopped writing code by hand.
  • His edge: He likes making colleagues and their environments better, so he applied that to making agents better.
  • Core claim: The best output from agents comes from telling them what you care about. People underestimate the communication gap between human and agent.
  • Fundamentals still matter: Software engineering principles matter more now that AI is in every dev process. Non-technical people can use them too, if they can articulate intent.
  • Cold-open teasers (unverified):
  • The skill is knowing your unknown unknowns (security, deployment). Otherwise the agent makes stuff up.
  • The big opportunity is building knowledge-work processes around agents for organizations, since every org works differently.
  • A war story about spec-driven development: code and agent output got worse over months of trying it.
