Run Your AI Software Factory Like a Real Engineering Org (Here's How)

Cole Medin · 2026-10-23 · 16,662 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Wrapping an AI software factory in Port for enterprise scale

Cole shows what a solo autonomous coding loop needs once it has to serve a team (registry, context, standards, ownership), and that's a checklist you can hold mx-workflow against.

What it is: A sponsored walkthrough of Cole plugging his AI software factory into Port, a platform that adds guardrails, context and observability around the factory without changing the harness inside it.

How it works:

  • The core loop is unchanged. You plan a release with the agent (a PRD or similar, sized to roughly 10-20 tickets). The harness then splits it into tasks and implements them in a loop. Greenfield means planning the proof of concept. Brownfield means planning the next epic.
  • The new part is a wrapper layer. His claim is that the factory alone isn't enough once you have many apps or are at enterprise scale. It was a hard requirement that adopting Port not force changes to his custom harness.
  • Four wrapper components:
  • Agent and skill registry
  • Context lake: one central layer of context shared across all factories
  • Standards applied to every factory
  • Control to bring each factory in line with those standards as they evolve
  • Questions the wrapper answers: who owns the code, what must pass before it ships, and when a human has to sign off.
  • Port is wired into pull requests. It watches them and gives a health check across the repo.
  • Component ownership is a first-class concept.
  • Advice: if you're just starting, keep it simple and stick to the core loop. Add the registry and context lake only when you scale.
  • Caveat: the transcript cuts off right as he starts the core-loop refresher. The Port demo itself (the team knowledge agent in the description) isn't covered here, so the specifics below are unverified.
  • Disclosure: he says he's partnering with Port. Treat the praise as sponsored.

Why it matters for you: Check mx-workflow against his four layers. It likely already covers the loop (`/mx:plan`, `/mx:implement`, `/mx:loop`, `/mx:review`). The gaps worth considering are a skill and agent registry with ownership metadata, shared cross-project context, and codified standards (`/mx:ratchet` and `/mx:reject` are already close). Don't add them until you're running multiple projects.
