He made $1.8 million by selling a trash franchise

My First Million · 2026-08-22 · 48,250 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Guest bought a Smash My Trash franchise, exited for $1.8M.

A concrete, unsexy franchise playbook — buy a truck-based service unit, sell customers a guaranteed cost reduction, and exit for seven figures without inventing anything.

The business: Smash My Trash — a mobile dumpster-compaction franchise. The guest bought a franchise unit and later sold it for $1.8 million.

The problem it solves:

  • Industrial customers (warehouses, factories, manufacturers) throw bulky, air-filled waste into open-top dumpsters
  • Haulers bill per pickup, not per pound — roughly $1,000 per haul
  • A high-volume site does ~10 pickups/month → ~$10,000/month in trash spend, sometimes more
  • The trash isn't heavy, it's *fluffy*. Customers are paying to haul air.

How the service works:

  • A truck with a crane-mounted, spinning spike arm backs up to the customer's dumpster
  • The arm drops in and compacts the trash down in place
  • Same dumpster now holds far more waste per haul → pickups drop from ~10/month to ~5/month
  • Mobile service — no capital equipment installed at the customer site, the truck goes to them

The money model (the part worth stealing):

  • Shared savings pricing. Customer was spending $10k/month; service cuts it to ~$5k; the $5k saved is split between operator and customer.
  • Sales pitch is a *guaranteed cost reduction*, not a new line item — the buyer's budget doesn't grow, so the objection surface is tiny
  • Recurring revenue tied to an existing, non-discretionary spend (waste hauling never stops)
  • Asset-light per customer: one truck serves a whole route of accounts, so utilization drives margin
  • Franchise model = no product invention, no brand build; buy the unit, run the route, sell the exit

The exit:

  • Bought a franchise → operated it → sold the franchise for $1.8M

Why it matters for you: The transferable pattern is arbitrage on a per-unit pricing model — find a vendor billing per *event* rather than per *value*, insert a service that reduces event count, and split the delta; it's the same shape as compressing anything billed by volume.
