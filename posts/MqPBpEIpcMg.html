How To (Legally) Steal Customers

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-12 · 52,705 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Poach competitors' unhappy customers instead of educating strangers.

The instructional secret is that your cheapest customer acquisition channel is your competitor's one-star reviews — a side hustle can skip the expensive job of creating demand and just intercept demand that already exists.

What it is: A ~$200/month customer-acquisition play that targets people already paying a competitor they hate, rather than strangers who've never bought.

The core framework — three buyer types, ranked by difficulty:

  • The curious — never spent money on the problem. Hardest sale on Earth. You're paying to educate them.
  • The burned — bought before, got wrecked. Most common sale, but you're fighting their scar tissue.
  • The predisposed — already writing checks to a worse version of you. Easiest sale on Earth. This is the target.

How it works (the trades example, but it generalizes):

  • Open Google Maps, search your trade + your zip code.
  • Filter to every competitor under four stars.
  • Read the one-star reviews: "never called back," "showed up late," "overcharged me."
  • Reframe: those aren't complaints, they're a list of qualified buyers — people with budget, intent, and an active grievance.
  • Run geo-targeted ads to that zip code with one message that answers the specific complaint. Her example: "We answer the phone."
  • Cost of the ad spend + tooling: roughly $200/month.

The second play — buy the phone number, not the trucks:

  • ~10,000 boomers retire every day; small local operators are winding down constantly.
  • When a competitor retires, don't buy the hard assets (trucks, equipment, inventory) — buy the phone number and business listing, structured as a percentage of revenue rather than a lump sum.
  • Every inbound call for the next decade is pre-qualified demand dialing you on purpose. Zero CAC, no depreciation, no capex.
  • Rev-share structure means you only pay when it works — the seller carries the downside risk.

Tools & links:

  • Google Maps — free competitor + review mining; sort by rating, read the 1-stars
  • Google Business Profile — where the acquired listing/number actually transfers
  • Google Ads / Meta Ads Manager — zip-code-level geo-targeting for the $200/mo spend

The engineering translation:

  • This is scraped-signal lead gen. Reviews are a public, structured, timestamped dataset of unmet demand — trivially harvestable via the Google Places API.
  • Obvious build: pull competitors under 4.0 in a radius, extract 1-star review text, cluster the complaints (slow response, price, no-show), and auto-generate ad copy that answers the top cluster.
  • The transferable insight for any side hustle — SaaS included: your best positioning copy is already written, verbatim, in your competitor's negative reviews. G2, Capterra, App Store, and Reddit are the same play for software.

Why it matters for you: If you're starting a side hustle with limited hours and limited budget, this reframes marketing from "create demand" (expensive, slow, uncertain) to "intercept demand" (cheap, fast, already validated) — and gives you a concrete Saturday-afternoon task to test it.
