Bidding $70 on pallets became a $15K/month resale business

A 26-year-old with no capital, no warehouse, and no car built a $15K/month side hustle from a parents' garage by reselling auction pallets, which is a low-risk model you could copy.

What it is: Pallet-and-liquidation reselling: bid cheaply on pallets of books, computers, monitors, and groceries, then flip the items via garage sales, Facebook Marketplace, and eBay.

How it works:

  • The spark: JV (26, restaurant night manager, headed to law school) watched an earlier episode where Mindy bought a pallet of books for $1 and sold it for about $5,000.
  • The first bid: He bid $70 on seven pallets of books as a joke, and he won. His reasoning: worst case you lose $70.
  • Zero overhead: No warehouse, employees, box truck, or startup capital. He started in a corner of his parents' garage and now rents a storage unit for about $140/month.
  • Sales channels:
  • Garage sales: about $700 to $1,000 every Saturday. He skips Sundays for church, and says doing both days would roughly double it.
  • Facebook Marketplace: one post sold out his entire storage unit.
  • eBay: used as another outlet alongside the other two.
  • Product mix: Books (10,000+), computers, and monitors bought for about $1 each. A pallet of groceries doubled his money in a week.
  • Growth timeline:
  • Posting books specifically made about $1,000 in the first week.
  • He quit his job 2 to 3 weeks later.
  • Income grew fast after he went live.
  • Month four was on track for $15K or more.
  • Core rule: "Buy with math, sell with emotion." Research what an item sells for before you bid, then price and pitch it so buyers want it.
  • Caveat: He says the limit is how much research you do, and that garage sales are uncomfortable at first. The $5K to $10K/month claim comes from the host and guest, not an independent source.
