Shaan's collection of extreme, frame-breaking CEO tactics

Shaan Puri walks through seven real anecdotes of legendary CEOs doing things that sound almost illegal — the kind of aggressive management and dealmaking moves that actually built the companies and fortunes behind them.

What it is: Episode 857 — Sam Parr and Shaan Puri break down seven real anecdotes of "extreme behaviors" from legendary CEOs: moves that sound wrong, borderline illegal, and work anyway.

The framing (Shaan's thesis):

  • There's no CEO school, no CEO training, no one managing the manager. Nobody gives the CEO real feedback because they're the boss.
  • Most people running companies right now are "not being hardcore enough."
  • He collects these as frame-breaking stories — the world looks like it fits inside a border until one story forces the border to expand.
  • The elephant-and-stake metaphor: tethered as a calf, grown strong enough to rip the stake out, still doesn't try. Most CEO limits are that stake — an illusion of control, not a real constraint.
  • Mix of famous companies you'll know, famous companies you won't know the story of, and one or two from friends.

Featured individual: Martin Basiri (Applyboard)

  • The story that kicked off Shaan's collecting habit — breakfast with Martin Basiri, who Shaan calls "the one that got away."
  • The setup: Shaan, ~24-25, making roughly $160K/yr in San Francisco. Not enough money to be an angel investor, but he wanted to be one.
  • The scout: a 13-year-old kid hanging around tech circles emailed him offering to be his scout — three companies a week, no obligation to invest, just feedback in exchange for learning. Shaan had never made an angel investment and now had a scout.
  • The first deal sent: Martin Basiri, based in Canada, running Applyboard.
  • What Applyboard does: closes the gap for international students who want to attend university in the United States. A kid in Malaysia with Malaysian parents has the dream but no idea what UC Davis is, where it is, or how to fill out the application. Applyboard handles the discovery and application pipeline.
  • Why Shaan passed: he was in San Francisco and "not trying to invest in a Canadian company" — he wanted Silicon Valley deals. Applyboard went on to become a massive company. That miss is why Martin is the one that got away.
