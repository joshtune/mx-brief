If you want a concrete, low-barrier path from 'I know Claude' to paid client work, this is Nate's playbook, though the transcript cuts off before the build steps you'd copy.

What it is: A four-step road map for getting paid with Claude, starting with AI-built websites for local businesses. Only step 1 and the intro appear in the available transcript.

How it works:

  • Start small. Nate's first sale was a LinkedIn content system for $1,200 that took about 2 hours to build. Small, easy-to-say-yes offers get you in the door.
  • Become 'the AI person'. He sold one-hour consulting calls early on, and most clients came back with bigger projects. You only need to know a bit more than the person hiring you.
  • Why websites. He claims 17% of small businesses have no site and many of the rest look dated. He says Opus 5.5 is now very good at design, especially with extra skills added.
  • Step 1: pick ONE client type and only build for them. You reuse one template per niche, build a portfolio, and get trusted the way a Mercedes-only mechanic does.
  • Three filters for a niche:
  • They already spend money on marketing or websites, so you replace a cost instead of adding one.
  • Each customer is worth hundreds of dollars (dentists, roofers).
  • A list of them exists somewhere, such as member directories. Community member Sav used the American Institute of Architects directory instead of big lead tools.
  • Example niche (dentists): every site needs booking, insurance info and reviews, so the second build is much faster than the first.
  • Lead sourcing: search Google Maps for local businesses in your area and build sites for them. Pitching in person builds the relationship faster. You can also ask Claude where a member list for your industry might exist.
  • Steps 2 to 4: these were cut off. Step 2 is teased as the step where people waste weeks and where AI has changed the most, probably prospecting or building a demo first. The transcript doesn't say.
