This Is What Happens When You CRUSH An AI Video Model

Alex Ziskind · 2026-06-18 · 42,239 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Quantizing local AI video models from 16-bit down to 2-bit.

He runs it all on a custom RTX Pro 6000 Linux box — not a Mac — so this is the rare Alex build that tells you exactly what hardware (and price tier) you'd need to do local AI video at home.

What it is: A quantization stress-test of two local text-to-video models, stepped down an 8-level ladder from full precision (FP16) to 2-bit to see what breaks first.

The hardware (your actual interest):

  • Custom Linux box, not a Mac — built around an RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell GPU
  • 96 GB of VRAM, enough to hold every full-precision model at once
  • FP8 is *natively* supported on this GPU, so 8-bit is essentially free performance
  • Cost context: an RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell runs roughly $8,000–$10,000 for the card alone — i.e. multiples of a maxed Mac mini (~$2k) and in a different league entirely from the M-series machines Alex usually tests. This is a workstation, not a desktop.

How it works:

  • Same prompt, same seed, same settings every run — only the quantization changes
  • Models tested: WAN 2.2 (14B params, text-to-video, FP16 weighs 27 GB on disk) and LTX 2.3 (22B params, generates video *and* audio together, so picture and sound can degrade separately)
  • Ran via ComfyUI using GGUF quantized versions (Q4 is the default most people never move off of)
  • Scored with SSIM, LPIPS, and prompt-alignment benchmarks, plus eyeball/ear checks
  • Finding tease: two totally different architectures collapse in the *same* way as bits drop; FP8 (14 GB) is the obvious sweet spot on this GPU

Tools & links:

  • ComfyUI — node-based local AI video/image generation
  • WAN 2.2 & LTX 2.3 — the two video models benchmarked (GGUF quantized builds)
