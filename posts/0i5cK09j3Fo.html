What it is: A short clip, not a full episode, in which Bill Perkins argues that ego, more than financial risk, is what keeps people from starting businesses or chasing opportunities.

How it works:

  • Core claim: To live a non-standard life and succeed, you have to constantly crush your ego.
  • High-ego exceptions: Some high-ego people make it, but only in a narrow lane, and he thinks they pay for it with a less fulfilling life.
  • Ego vs. money risk: Most people can handle the money risk, such as losing a job and becoming a waiter. What they can't handle is the status drop of ranking lower among friends on the money-making scale.
  • Motivated reasoning: People say they didn't try because it was hard or too salty. Perkins ignores the adjectives and asks one question: was it a shot at the life you wanted, and why didn't you take the risk?
  • The real blocker: They can't face the judgment of themselves, their parents, friends and society if they try and fail.
  • Crab-bucket effect: People quietly root for you to fail, because your attempt exposes their own cowardice.
