He Built This Business in One Weekend for $40

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-07-27 · 32,553 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Print-on-demand pet portraits hit 7 figures from $40

This is the cleanest side-hustle template in the genre — one weekend, $40, no code, no inventory — and Chris spends the episode reverse-engineering how to restart it now that the original operator's sales are declining.

What it is: Chris interviews Spencer, founder of Printed Pet — a custom pet gift company that puts your dog's face on Renaissance-style portraits and other SKUs. Built over a single free weekend for the price of a Shopify subscription, it did seven figures in revenue in 2020. Sales are down now, so the episode doubles as a live ideation session.

How it works:

  • Origin was accidental. Spencer was running a pet bandana/scarf company manufactured in Dallas. While buying ads for it, his Instagram explore page filled with competitor pet businesses — including one doing pet-as-Renaissance-royalty portraits. He recognized the art style from home decor sites and figured it'd transfer to custom pet products.
  • The $40 stack. $30 Shopify membership. Canva for the design work (he didn't know what Photoshop was). Fiverr/Upwork for anything he couldn't do. His own phone for product photos. Zero coding, self-taught off YouTube.
  • Print on demand = no inventory risk. This is the whole unlock. Manufacturing is outsourced per-order, so there's no capital tied up in stock and the business is cash-flow-heavy from day one. He was funding his life teaching fitness classes after quitting commercial real estate; the business never needed a runway.
  • Timing was luck, and he says so. Launched 2019, months before lockdown. Everyone got a pet or had a baby. Printed Pet hockey-sticked; the bandana company stayed flat and got shut down.
  • Etsy was the actual growth channel. He was selling direct through Shopify and doing fine. A friend mentioned the product would work on Etsy. He listed it there — that's when it blew up. Two months alone did 800k+ in revenue.
  • Scaling was the hard part. By end of 2020: 80 artists paid per-artwork commission (not salaried), plus 3 full-time remote employees. The labor model is the interesting bit — variable-cost artists means gross margin holds as volume swings.

Tools & links:

  • Shopify — the storefront, $30/mo at the time
  • Etsy — the channel that actually caused the hockey stick; built-in gift-intent demand
  • Canva — all the design work, no Photoshop
  • Fiverr / Upwork — gap-filling for photos and services
  • beehiiv — sponsor, 30% off first 30 days (Chris's newsletter platform)

The transferable pattern:

  • Pick a product category with high emotional attachment (pets, kids, weddings) where buyers don't price-compare.
  • Find an art/design style already working in an adjacent vertical, port it over.
  • Outsource fulfillment entirely so launch cost approaches zero.
  • Launch on your own store to control margin, but list on the marketplace that already has the buying intent.
  • Scale labor as commission-per-unit, not headcount.

The caveat worth naming: the reason this episode exists is that Printed Pet's sales are *down*. Print-on-demand pet portraits in 2026 is a saturated category with AI image generation collapsing the artist moat. Steal the pattern, not the niche.

Why it matters for you: If you want a side hustle you can validate over one weekend with a $40 downside and no inventory, this is the exact playbook — and the fact that it's now decaying tells you which part was the durable skill (channel selection, variable-cost ops) and which part was 2020.
