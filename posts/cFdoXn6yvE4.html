Wi-Fi GPU shares an RTX 5090 across devices

Chuck's home lab gets a shared 'family GPU' that runs a 750B-parameter DeepSeek model locally on a 32 GB box, so you can see how he's fitting big local AI into his daily workflow.

What it is: A sponsored first look at WiCi, an early-engineering-model wireless GPU box (RTX 5090 + mini PC) that any laptop, Mac, or tablet on the network can use as its own GPU.

How it works:

  • The box holds an NVIDIA RTX 5090 plus a mini PC running OpenWRT for networking, so it shows up as its own device on his network.
  • Client devices see the 5090 in Task Manager as if it were local. Chuck plays Cyberpunk 2077 and runs Blender on a small laptop through it.
  • A Mac uses the same GPU at the same time for local image and video generation.
  • The Mac also talks to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, a 750B-parameter model. It normally needs 500+ GB of memory, but he says it runs at full quality on 32 GB of VRAM.
  • The transcript cuts off before explaining how the model fits. Chuck says that's the craziest part and built a visualizer to show what the model is doing on the GPU.
  • The Wi-Fi problem: PCIe moves about 512 Gbps with sub-microsecond latency. Wi-Fi gives about 1 Gbps at about 1.5 ms round trip.
  • His analogy: if PCIe hands you a picture in 1 second, Wi-Fi takes about 25 minutes. Games and GPUs are chatty, sending instructions thousands of times per second, so latency is the real enemy, not bandwidth.
  • How WiCi hides that latency is promised in the video but isn't in the excerpt.
  • Caveat: This is a sponsored video about an early engineering unit. The claims are Chuck's own demos, not independent benchmarks.
