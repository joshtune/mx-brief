The 7 rules of sales psychology

My First Million · 2026-08-05 · 34,900 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Seven blunt heuristics for selling anything.

A 90-second, zero-fluff distillation of what actually closes deals from someone who ran sales orgs for 20 years — useful if you're selling software, consulting, or yourself.

What it is: A short-form clip where an unnamed 20-year sales leader lays out seven rules of sales psychology. No guest interview, no company teardown — pure heuristics.

The 7 rules:

  • 1. Sales is like golf. You can make it infinitely complicated, or you can walk up and hit the ball. Choose the second.
  • 2. It's people and problem solving. That's the whole job description.
  • 3. People buy exactly four things: time, money, approval, and peace of mind. Sell anything outside those four and you fail.
  • 4. Sell aspirin, not vitamins. People *always* buy painkillers. Vitamins sell sometimes, unpredictably.
  • 5. All things being equal, people buy from friends. So make everything else equal, then go make a lot of friends.
  • 6. Be valuable and useful. Help people, send them interesting things, expect nothing back. Do it consistently and authentically and money finds you.
  • 7. Nobody cares about your quota, payroll, opex, or burn rate. They care about their problem. Closing line: "There are more than a hundred trillion dollars in the economy just waiting for you to breathe it in."

The operating model behind it:

  • Rules 3 and 4 are the filter — if your pitch doesn't map to time/money/approval/peace-of-mind *and* register as pain rather than nice-to-have, you don't have a sale.
  • Rules 5 and 6 are the long game — relationship surface area compounds; it's the only durable edge once features and price commoditize.
  • Rules 1 and 7 are the anti-patterns — over-engineered process and seller-centric framing are the two most common self-inflicted wounds.

Money angle: No business teardown here. No revenue numbers, no company, no monetization playbook — the featured individual isn't even named in the clip. The value is the framework, not a case study.

Tools & links: None mentioned in the clip.

Why it matters for you: If you ever pitch a product, a contract, or an internal project, rules 3, 4, and 7 are a one-minute checklist for rewriting your pitch — but skip this if you're here for detailed business breakdowns, because there isn't one.
