Is Learning to Build Software Still Worth It with AI? (My Honest Take)

Cole Medin · 2026-10-12 · 22,207 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Why intent and harness skills beat 'meat proxy' coding

Cole argues the harness (up-front grilling, end-of-task validation) is the skill that grows in value, and that is the layer your mx-workflow skills sit on.

What it is: A thought-leadership take on what's still worth learning as agents get good. It uses the viral Reverse Engineer Anything (REA) repo as the foil. The transcript is truncated, so the second half (the harness details) is missing.

How it works:

  • The hook: REA hit #1 on GitHub Trending. You point an agent at an app and it works out how a feature is built, down to the binary, then rebuilds it. Cole calls it a prime example of what proper harness engineering gets you.
  • The pushback: People are claiming 'engineering is solved'. Cole says there's a specific reason agents excel at this task, and that it doesn't extrapolate to everything. The explanation was cut off in the transcript.
  • His core claim: LLMs can't read your mind. Communicating intent through a harness around the agent only gets more important as tasks get harder.
  • His own workflow: He hasn't written a line of code in over a year. He delegates the coding but owns the planning up front and the validation at the end.
  • What he does differently (the actionable part):
  • The harness *grills him with questions* before building, to get on the same page. This is the interview or grill step before planning.
  • The harness *validates intent at the end*, not just whether tests pass.
  • If the agent errs, he treats it as his failure to communicate intent, not the model's failure.
  • The anti-pattern, 'meat proxy': Dumping a Jira ticket or Slack question straight into the agent with no critical thinking, then pasting the output back. You add zero value, you still own the code, and the reviewer ends up reviewing raw AI output ('Claude said...').
  • Why REA works anyway: It's a task with a very clear, checkable target. That's the opposite of vague product intent, so don't generalize from it.

Tools & links:

  • Reverse Engineer Anything (REA) — MCP-based repo for reverse engineering app features. The quick start is a single request, and the repo URL isn't given in the transcript, so check the video description.
  • mx-workflow — your project, the thing to compare against.
  • Archon — Cole's open-source agent orchestration/harness tool. He's expected to show it in the later part, but it isn't in the visible transcript.

Why it matters for you: Audit mx-workflow for the two bookends he stresses. Check that /mx:prd and /mx:plan force a relentless clarifying-question pass before any code. Check that /mx:review, /mx:qa and /mx:reject validate against the *stated intent* and not only lint and tests. Also consider a guard against meat-proxy mode, such as requiring the user's own decisions to be captured in the plan before /mx:implement runs.
