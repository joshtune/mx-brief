I Tried to Make $10K in 10 Days With Costco Returns

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-12 · 140,543 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Buying Costco liquidation pallets cheap, reselling for profit.

It's a side hustle with a hard-numbers playbook — bid on B-Stock auctions at pennies on the dollar, flip on Facebook Marketplace or Whatnot — and the barrier to entry is capital and logistics, not skill.

What it is: Chris brings in Shannon (a friend who's made millions doing this) to attempt $10K profit in 10 days buying Costco customer returns off liquidation auction sites and reselling them.

How it works:

  • Costco dumps customer returns onto B-Stock, a wholesale liquidation auction marketplace. Roughly 465 live auctions on the Costco storefront at any given time.
  • Categories span food, shoes, oral care, consumer electronics, appliances — you bid per-lot, and the listing shows units, Costco's average retail price, and current average bid per unit.
  • The math that makes it work: a lot of musical instruments listed at ~$628 Costco average sold for $63/unit on a single bid. A pallet of 47 fridges was sitting at $4/unit with two bids. A $3,400 gazebo went for $200 all-in.
  • Bid discipline: "It's not gambling. You put in the number, you leave." Set a max, walk away. Target price on the appliance lot they liked was ~$200/unit *including shipping*.
  • Look for low-competition categories. Musical instruments and large appliances get few bids because they're weird, big, and heavy. Shannon explicitly avoids computers. Those barriers (space, freight, repair know-how) *are* the moat.
  • Read the photos before bidding. Items still in original boxes with factory stickers = likely new, not used. Well-protected pallets, varied colors/SKUs, and premium features (steam, app connectivity) all raise expected resale. Costco skews upmarket, so their returns aren't low-end junk.
  • Appliances need a repair skill layer — you should know how to order a replacement panel from Samsung or source a cracked shelf. "Used fair" condition grading means you're buying work, not just inventory.
  • Validate resale before you bid: check Facebook Marketplace for what the item actually sells for locally and whether there's demand. Costco's listed price is below retail, so it's not your comp.
  • Sales channels: Facebook Marketplace for big-ticket local pickup (appliances, gazebos). Whatnot live auctions for consumables — food, candy, coffee, energy drinks. One mastermind member moved ~$1,500 of Monster, gummies, and Liquid IV in a single hour just dropping items in boxes on a live stream.

Tools & links:

  • B-Stock — the liquidation auction marketplace hosting Costco's returns; separate storefronts per retailer
  • Whatnot — live-stream auction app, best channel for food/consumables and fast-moving small goods
  • Facebook Marketplace — both the comp-research tool and the sales channel for heavy local-pickup items

Why it matters for you: This is a side hustle you can start on nights and weekends with a few hundred dollars and a garage — the edge comes from spreadsheet discipline (comp research, max-bid rules, unit economics) and picking categories with logistical friction, which is exactly the kind of arbitrage an engineer can systematize.
