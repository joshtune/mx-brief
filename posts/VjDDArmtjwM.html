Butter in a deodorant stick went viral.

A single dumb form-factor swap on a 5,000-year-old product bought 2.8M likes and 27K comments of free distribution — the cheapest product-marketing lesson you'll get this week.

What it is: A short MFM clip breaking down a viral video where someone melts a stick of butter, pours it into an empty deodorant canister, and rolls butter onto toast and pans like antiperspirant.

The featured thing:

  • No named founder or company in this clip — the subject is the *viral video itself* and the product idea behind it (butter in a twist-up stick).
  • Creator: melts butter → decants into a cleaned-out empty deodorant tube → lets it re-solidify → twists up and rolls.
  • Sales pitch embedded in the clip: no mess, no knife, control your volume, spread evenly, "dispensing problem solved."
  • The claim: this is "the most American thing you've ever seen."

The numbers (the actual product here is attention):

  • 2.8M likes
  • 27,000 comments
  • Shaan's read: "They love it, they hate it, doesn't matter. It grabbed their attention."

The money mechanic — what someone would actually do with this:

  • Sell the form factor, not the food. The butter is commodity. The twist-up dispenser is the product. Existing butter brands compete on cows; this competes on delivery.
  • Rage/delight bait as the acquisition channel. Polarizing = comment volume = algorithmic reach = $0 CAC. The comment count matters more than the like count.
  • The proven pattern: take an ancient product, swap the container. Deodorant→butter is the same trade that made squeeze bottles, sticks, pods, and pouches into billion-dollar categories.
  • Path to revenue: ~$1–3 injection-molded twist-up tube, sold empty as a kitchen gadget (Amazon/DTC, no food-safety or cold-chain burden), or licensed to an existing butter brand as a premium SKU.
  • Validation is already free. 2.8M likes is your ad test. You don't need to guess demand; you need to convert it before someone else does.

Tools & links:

  • No repos, tools, or URLs mentioned in the transcript — this is a pure product-observation clip.

Why it matters for you: The transferable move is form-factor arbitrage — the thing that went viral wasn't a new invention, it was an old one in someone else's packaging, and that's a repeatable way to manufacture both a product and its distribution at once.
