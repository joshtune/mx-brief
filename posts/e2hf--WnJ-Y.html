5 Rules That Secretly Decide Who Gets Laid Off

A Life Engineered · 2026-11-20 · 74,663 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Ex-Amazon principal on surviving stack-ranking and layoffs

A 20-year Amazon principal engineer who sat in the stack-ranking meetings tells you where to work and how to perform so you're not on the cut list.

What it is: A former Amazon Principal Engineer's five-rule playbook for avoiding both yearly stack-rank cuts and big layoffs. The transcript provided cuts off partway through rule 2, so rules 3-5 aren't covered here.

The message to engineers:

  • Coasting to dodge a layoff is dead. Being slightly faster than the slowest person no longer works.
  • Post-pandemic, the ~6% annual "unregretted attrition" target at Amazon became a hard number, and the backfill hiring stopped.
  • Layoffs and stack-ranking are a game you play either way, so get good at it.

Rule 1: Follow the money

  • Two layoff types: a meat cleaver (whole divisions or sub-orgs cut) and a percentage cut (headcount reduced by a set amount across the board). Positioning helps with both.
  • Cost center: your area is seen as an expense. Avoid it.
  • Profit center: already makes lots of revenue and is heavily optimized. It's safe, but the work is less inspiring and the growth curve is lower.
  • Growth or investment area: new money and innovation. It's less reliably safe, but it's on the upswing and you can make your mark fast. His line: work on the iPhone, not GarageBand.

Rule 2: Be the top performer

  • Stack ranking traces back to Jack Welch at GE in the 80s: cut the bottom performers every year.
  • In ZIRP days the 6% target was soft, and you could sit in the middle and be fine. That's over.
  • Top performers rarely get cut. Even in a meat cleaver layoff, they often get a heads-up to find a safe landing spot.
  • In calibration, managers don't discuss all 12 reports equally. They bucket them into high, middle and low, and the transcript ends there. So the bucket you land in matters more than the details.

Instructional: what to do

  • Classify your org. Look at the open roles in your company and decide whether each team is a cost center, a profit center or a growth area.
  • Read backfill signals. Growth areas have many open roles and active hiring. Cost centers have only normal backfills, or get cut.
  • Watch for decay. A former growth team with fewer open roles and fewer backfills is sliding toward cost center.
  • Check the outside market. Use job-opening data as a rough proxy for market health. He points to Gergely Orosz's Pragmatic Engineer newsletter, which tracks this.
  • Move early. Pursue an internal transfer or a project on a growing team.
  • If your area is already in the crosshairs, his answer is to become the top performer rather than just pivot out.

Tools & links:

  • The Pragmatic Engineer newsletter — Gergely Orosz's coverage of tech jobs and market health
  • The channel also plugs a paid coaching offer (high performance review and promotion within a year). It's a pitch, not part of the instruction.

Why it matters for you: Prepare for stack-ranking as a hard annual cut with no backfill. Audit where your team sits on the money map now, and either move toward growth or get into the top performer bucket before calibration.
