The 5 Rules Every Billion-dollar CEO Lives By

Codie Sanchez · 2026-08-06 · 25,152 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Five operating disciplines that separate owners from operators.

It's a 90-second checklist for the exact failure mode that kills side hustles — you stay the operator instead of building something that runs without you.

What it is: A rapid-fire short listing five behavioral disciplines Codie attributes to elite CEOs — no framework, no case study, just the list.

How it works:

  • Rule 1 — Say no violently. Cited: Jobs cutting ~95% of projects annually. The claim is that focus itself is the unfair advantage, not talent.
  • Rule 2 — Think in decades, not days. Cited: Bezos operating Amazon on a 10-year horizon. Short-term inputs produce short-term outputs.
  • Rule 3 — Remove yourself from the work. Cited: Hastings (Netflix), Huang (NVIDIA) building systems and frameworks rather than doing the work personally.
  • Rule 4 — Build infrastructure, don't chase trends. Cited: Buffett buying railroads over apps; Huang building chips rather than LLMs. The "picks and shovels" thesis — sell to the gold rush, don't join it.
  • Rule 5 — Decide from data. Every high-output CEO runs a scoreboard or dashboard. No scoreboard, no ability to scale.
  • Closing frame: these are disciplines, not secrets. The unlock is acting like an owner instead of an operator.

The instructional secret: Rules 3 and 5 are the pair that actually does work — a scoreboard is what makes removing yourself *safe*. You can't delegate what you can't measure, so the dashboard is the prerequisite for the system, not a nice-to-have after it.

Tools & links:

  • None mentioned. No repos, tools, books, or URLs in the transcript — this is pure aphorism.

Caveats:

  • All five examples are survivorship-biased mega-cap CEOs. "Jobs cut 95% of projects" is a repeated-until-true anecdote, not a documented figure.
  • Zero mechanics: no guidance on what to put on a scoreboard, how to build the system, or what to say no to. Actionability is on you.

Why it matters for you: If you're running a side hustle while employed, rules 3 and 5 are the whole game — instrument the thing with a handful of metrics you check weekly, then systematize the work those metrics expose, or you've bought yourself a second job instead of an asset.
