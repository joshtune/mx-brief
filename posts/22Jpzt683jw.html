The $2.7K/Day AI Business That Practically Runs Itself

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-10-11 · 63,934 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Selling AI customer-support agents to local businesses

If you want a side hustle you can start with a day job and no coding, this is a founder who tried 8 failed ideas in 6 months, then landed on a $1M+/year AI agent business.

What it is: Guest Aaron Edwards (founder of Docspot) builds AI agents, mostly for customer support, and sells them to small and local businesses for a setup fee plus a monthly retainer.

How it works:

  • The offer: AI agent that handles customer support for a business. Aaron calls it a huge use case because it delivers obvious value.
  • Pricing: ~$2,000-$3,000 one-time setup fee, then $500-$1,000/month retainer.
  • Margins: Chris says the retainer is nearly free money. When he asks what the monthly service costs to run, the answer is about $50 on a $500/month retainer.
  • Built once, sold many times: the agent is already built, so you're selling it, not building from scratch each time.
  • No-code claim: both say it's now possible for non-technical people. The setup process can be automated, and a robot writes the monthly client report.
  • Team and scale: over $1M/year profit, no office, three people. Aaron's 19-year-old son just signed a client who says the service will save him over $100K/year.
  • Aaron's path (the part worth stealing):
  • Worked a full-time job the whole time and kept tinkering.
  • Spent 6 months launching 8 different things.
  • Flop 1: AI image WordPress plugin. It went viral on X and made $0 because nobody would pay.
  • Flop 2: AI product photos for businesses. He couldn't figure out how to sell it, and the tech was early.
  • Small win: AI profile pictures made ~$10K in 2 months, then the fad died.
  • Next try: a custom AI children's book (his wife's idea). The transcript cuts off here.
  • Idea #9, AI agents for business, stuck. He's been at it 3.5 years.
  • His mindset: AI is the next wave like the dot-com and mobile eras. He hacked together MVPs and checked whether people would pay before going deeper.
