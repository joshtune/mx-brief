Teaching Claude to edit videos via accumulated feedback rules

Nate shows the exact pattern he uses to turn every editing complaint into a permanent Claude skill rule, a loop you can copy for any repeatable workflow.

What it is: Nate has Claude edit his videos end to end (cuts, B-roll, motion graphics, sound). He doesn't prompt a perfect edit. He corrects each mistake once and stores the fix as a rule in a skill.

How it works:

  • Caption legibility: The first edit put white captions over a bright website, so they were unreadable. Claude now measures the pixels behind each caption and puts every caption on the same dark box.
  • B-roll must move: AI still images looked static. Nate's rule is that every shot must be dynamic, and a still image now breaks the build. Claude generates AI video or pulls real footage instead.
  • Sound design: The default sound effects were cheap lasers and zaps. The rule now calls for soft taps, clicks and paper sounds.
  • Lip-sync check: By the end of one reel, lips were slightly behind the voice. Claude now compares every cut frame against the raw footage before Nate watches it.
  • The core loop: Every piece of feedback gets written into a skill, so he never says the same thing twice. The "build breaks" framing means the rules are enforced by checks, not just suggested in a prompt.
  • Scope: This one video was edited entirely this way: cuts, B-roll, motion graphics and sound.
