How Facebook beat Twitter to Instagram

My First Million · 2026-11-04 · 2,711 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Zuck snipes Instagram from Twitter in 48 hours

A 90-second case study in how Facebook, by moving fast and overpaying, bought the company that would have been its biggest rival, and in what Kevin Systrom did to turn a young app into a $1B exit.

What it is: A short clip on how Zuckerberg bought Instagram out from under Twitter. It's a deal story, not a business breakdown.

The featured individual/business:

  • Kevin Systrom — founder of Instagram, mid-fundraise when the deal happened
  • Mark Zuckerberg / Facebook — the buyer
  • Twitter — the losing bidder

How the deal went down:

  • Twitter offered roughly $400-500M to buy Instagram. The clip also cites a $550M deal on the table.
  • Zuck texted Systrom directly: he wanted to talk about buying the company.
  • Systrom took the meeting even though he already had the Twitter offer.
  • Zuck's offer: double whatever Instagram was currently raising its round at. That put Instagram at about $1B.
  • The catch: the deal had to be done that weekend.
  • Zuck, his lawyers and Systrom closed a billion-dollar deal in 48 hours.
  • By Monday morning the competition woke up to a deal that was already done.

How the money was made (what Systrom did):

  • Built Instagram into a company that drew bids from both Twitter and Facebook.
  • Raised a funding round, which set the valuation Zuck's offer was anchored to.
  • Took the meeting despite having a $550M offer in hand.
  • Sold to Facebook for about $1B, roughly double the Twitter offer.
  • The clip does not cover Instagram's product, growth, user numbers or revenue. There's no breakdown of how Systrom built it.

What Zuck did (the playbook):

  • Went straight to the founder by text, with no bankers in between.
  • Anchored to a multiple of the round price (2x), which made the offer easy to say yes to.
  • Put a hard deadline on it, the weekend.
  • Ran the legal work fast so rivals never got to counter.
  • Treated Instagram as a competitive threat to neutralize. The claim is that without it Facebook 'might be dead'.
