I Did Something Insane. Here Are the Businesses I Found in It

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · 2026-08-27 · 25,249 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Buying a 231-acre ranch, and every business hiding in it

Chris spends millions on dirt and then reverse-engineers a dozen low-capital side hustles out of the process — the land-adjacent service businesses, not the land itself, are the actionable part for you.

What it is: Chris Koerner bought a 231-acre cattle ranch ~45 minutes outside Dallas–Fort Worth (57 miles from downtown), negotiated ~$400K off list on a non-distressed property, and used the six-month search as a scouting mission for monetizable businesses around raw land, ranching, and heavy equipment.

The thesis (why raw land):

  • Emotional first, financial second — he wanted a "hard work simulator" for his four kids. Fixing fence in July heat, hauling hay, feeding cows at 37°F. His framing: the hard parenting things and the important parenting things are correlated.
  • Path-of-growth bet — DFW adds 150–200K people/year, expanding north and east. The ranch sits in that path, held by one family for decades.
  • Asymmetric downside — worst case the family owns a ranch they love that doesn't cash flow; best case the metroplex crawls to the fence line and the land appreciates.
  • Break-even is the bar, not profit. He wants the asset to pay for itself via cash flow or appreciation, not to be a business on day one.

How the deal got done:

  • Not distressed, not ugly, not stale on market — the ~$400K discount came from the negotiation itself, not from a motivated seller. That's the tell: discount is a skill, not a property condition.
  • Comes with ~100 head of cattle already on it, plus an existing cast of local operators he inherits rather than replaces.

Side-hustle surface area he identifies (low capital unless noted):

  • Spreadsheet/database products — packaging land, parcel, or equipment data into a sellable dataset. Near-zero capital, pure information arbitrage.
  • Grass-fed beef — direct-to-consumer meat off an existing herd; the herd is someone else's operational problem under a lease arrangement.
  • Equipment-adjacent plays — skid steers, bulldozers, dirt work. Ownership is capital-heavy; the businesses *around* rented and idle equipment are not.
  • Pond and water services — a recurring rabbit hole he flags as a real niche.
  • Land-content as a business — he's launching a separate "Triple 8 Ranch" YouTube channel (named for his 08/08/08 wedding date, also the future cattle brand) to document the whole thing. The media asset monetizes the purchase independent of the dirt.

Tools & links:

  • Bizee — LLC formation, the video's sponsor and what Chris says he's used for years to spin up entities
  • Triple 8 Ranch YouTube channel — linked in the video description, the documentation arm of the project

Why it matters for you: If you're hunting a side hustle, ignore the millions-on-dirt part — the transferable pattern is that Chris found a dozen businesses by spending six months inside one unfamiliar industry, and most of them (data products, D2C beef, equipment services) need a laptop and a niche, not a ranch.
