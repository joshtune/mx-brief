What it is: A problem-framing talk on the context-switching fatigue that comes from running many coding agents in parallel. The solutions are promised but not in the transcript we have.

How it works:

  • The problem: Cole runs several agent sessions at once and bounces between conversations all day. He says he hasn't done a 4-hour, music-on flow-state coding session in ages.
  • The pressure: If you can ship 10x more, you feel you must ship 10x more every day. Waiting on an agent now comes with guilt, because something else could be started in another window.
  • Cited sources: A tweet from Tibo (the "fried brain effect": AI was supposed to make us work less, but he's never been this tired). Kyle Cook's YouTube video quotes Cal Newport: focus is the scarcest commodity of the 21st century.
  • His core claim: Cognitive bandwidth doesn't parallelize. Splitting attention into small slices across many agents prevents flow state.
  • What he does differently (partial): He leans on a "second brain" (an AI system that handles much of his business) alongside coding agents and harnesses. He splits his day across YouTube content, course planning, and recording rather than one long task.
