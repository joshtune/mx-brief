Hormozi's tactic: own one word to build a brand.

A short, memorable positioning framework you can apply to your own product, portfolio, or personal brand to cut through noise.

What it is: Alex Hormozi's brand strategy — pick a single word and relentlessly own it.

How it works:

  • Shaan asked Hormozi "What's your brand?" — Hormozi answered: "Business."
  • The goal isn't a tagline, it's word-ownership: be *the* person people think of for that one concept.
  • Reference point: Grant Cardone owns "real estate" — Hormozi wants to own "business" in the YouTube/content world.
  • Power comes from focus + simplicity — spreading across many themes dilutes everything and achieves nothing.
  • The gut-check test: if your brand were one word, (1) what would it be, (2) is it the word *others* actually associate with you or just what you say internally, and (3) are you executing on it *everywhere* or only in one corner?

Tools & links:

  • No tools, repos, or URLs mentioned — this is a pure positioning concept.

Why it matters for you: The featured person is Alex Hormozi and the money move is positioning discipline — pick one word, own it across every channel, and let the focus compound into an audience and a business.

Bottom line on the individual (Alex Hormozi):

  • What he did: Built a personal/content brand by deliberately anchoring to a single owned word ("business") rather than a broad message.
  • How it makes money: Word-ownership → top-of-mind recall → audience → funnel into his companies (Acquisition.com, courses, books like $100M Offers). The focus *is* the growth engine.
