Three service tiers that sell to rich people.

It ranks three service businesses you could start solo by how much money they actually make — and explains why the boring one wins.

What it is: A three-tier breakdown of service businesses that sell to wealthy clients, ranked C to A by revenue ceiling and recession-resistance.

The three tiers:

  • C-tier — Time-saving. High-end assistants and concierge services. $75–150/hr, usually on retainer. 10 clients at $2K/mo = $20K/mo at 60–80% margins. Real money, but it caps out — you're selling hours.
  • B-tier — Experiences. Luxury travel planning. $15K–50K per trip depending on how wild the itinerary is. 200 trips at $10–25K puts you in the millions. Good margins, but it's discretionary spend and you need access to the top dogs.
  • A-tier — Risk reduction. Cybersecurity. $200B global market growing 10%+/yr. Enterprise clients routinely pay $100K–250K per client per year. Nobody cancels security in a recession, breaches cost millions, and regulators don't care about your travel budget.

The actual play (the instructional bit):

  • Pick ONE expensive problem rich people hate dealing with
  • Land one client — don't build a business, build a relationship
  • Master their world until you know it better than they do
  • Productize what you learned into a repeatable offer
  • Raise prices

The one-line thesis: The wealthy don't pay for your effort — they pay for your certainty. Price on downside avoided, not hours worked.

Why it matters for you: If you're an engineer picking a side hustle, this is a direct argument for skipping the productivity/concierge stuff and selling risk reduction — security reviews, compliance readiness, incident response retainers — where your existing skills already sit in the A-tier and the buyer can't cancel you when the budget tightens.
