Replacing LLM decisions with a fast decision model

Cole swapped LLM-based gating calls for a cheap decision model in his second brain. You can use the same pattern to cut cost and latency in your mx-workflow skills and agents.

What it is: Cole shows four real use cases for "Jev", a decision model, inside his AI second brain. The name is as it appears in the auto-transcript and may be misspelled. Jev doesn't generate text. It answers multiple-choice questions about a given state, in parallel.

How it works:

  • The model: You give it a state plus a list of multiple-choice questions, and it answers them all in parallel. Cole claims it is 20-200x faster and 40-1,000x cheaper than an LLM making the same call, depending on which LLM.
  • Access: Cole calls it through OpenRouter's new decisions endpoint.
  • Scale: He made 38,000 calls in one week with 55.3M input tokens, for about $21 total. That covers his second brain and AI coding workflows.
  • Role: It is the filter in front of the expensive LLM. It decides what deserves attention, and the big model only runs on what passes.
  • Use case 1, prompt injection check: Every incoming email gets a Jev check for prompt injection before the main second brain sees it. He used to run a separate no-tools, read-only LLM session for this. Jev now does it.
  • Use case 2, reply gating: Jev decides whether an email is worth drafting a reply for, based on his priorities. The LLM drafts replies in his tone of voice but never sends them, so he stays in the loop. This stops tokens being burned on emails that don't need a reply.
  • Use cases 3 and 4: The transcript is cut off before these. The intro points to news and source filtering ("which news is worth my time") and dedupe ("which ideas I've already seen") as likely candidates, but that is inferred, not confirmed.
  • Cole's rule: He waited to make this video until each use case had been running in his second brain for real. He didn't want to ride the hype.
  • Previous video: He covered Jev in AI coding workflows in an earlier video, which got strong feedback.

Why it matters for you:

  • Cheap gates: Look for LLM calls in mx-workflow that are really yes/no or multiple-choice decisions. Examples are "does this task need the full review agent?", "is this diff risky?", and "which skill should route this?". Replace them with decision-model gates in front of your agents and skills.
  • Safer input handling: Put a decision-model prompt-injection check on any untrusted input to your skills, such as PR bodies, ticket text and fetched web content. It's cheaper than a sandboxed LLM reader.
  • Fewer wasted tokens: Gate expensive agents like the review team, so they only fire when the cheap model says the change warrants it.
  • Caveat: The speed and cost multipliers are Cole's and the vendor's figures. Benchmark on your own decisions before trusting them.
