Google Just Caught Up (Gemini-4 Argon)

Matt Wolfe · 2026-10-13 · 161,531 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Gemini 4 Argon ships; coding-heavy hands-on tests

Mostly a model-launch recap, but the 1M output tokens, low cost per task, and Antigravity coding modes are worth a skim if you pick a coding model for web app work.

What it is: Matt's hands-on look at Google's Gemini 4 Argon, announced about 2 weeks earlier and now live for higher-tier paid plans. The transcript cuts off before most of the demos (games, 3D-printing Gary Busey), so those are not covered here.

How it works:

  • Target use: built for long, complex tasks, not quick Q&A lookups.
  • Benchmarks (per Matt): new state-of-the-art on the Vals index (finance, coding, legal, tax), automation bench, legal work, and Deep Suite (agentic coding).
  • Artificial Analysis score: 53. That is on par with Fable but behind Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, and Muse's new Wonder model.
  • Cost: well below most competing models on cost per task.
  • Output limit: 1 million output tokens, roughly 750k words. Matt says no other model offers this. Most models spend their context window on input.
  • Coding focus: Matt says the biggest gains show up for coders. He argues labs push coding hardest because a model that codes well can write code to solve other problems.
  • Antigravity: Argon is in Google's coding platform with low, medium, and high modes. Matt got early access for his demos. He says the Antigravity coding interface is also coming to another Google surface, but the transcript cuts off before he says which.
  • Access: Google search has it, and so does a new competitor to Muse and Dots. Full access needs a higher-end paid plan.

Tools & links:

  • FutureTools — Matt's AI tools directory and news site
  • Gemini 4 Argon — Google's new frontier model, available on higher-end paid plans
  • Antigravity — Google's agentic coding platform, with Argon in low, medium, and high modes. No URL in the transcript.

Why it matters for you: For web app work, the useful bits are a cheaper per-task frontier coder, a huge output ceiling for big generations or refactors, and Antigravity as an option. Matt shows no web-app-specific demo in the available transcript, and the benchmarks still put Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 ahead.
