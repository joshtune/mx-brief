When You Start Promoting Your Business

Short on the awkward first phase of self-promotion.

If you're launching a side hustle, the bottleneck usually isn't the product — it's your unwillingness to tell anyone it exists.

What it is: A short-form clip on the cringe moment when you start publicly promoting your own business.

Heads up — thin source: No transcript, description, or timestamps were available for this video. Everything below is framed from the title, format, and this channel's consistent position, not from quotes. Treat it as context, not a summary.

How it works (Codie's standard take on this theme):

  • Distribution is the job. Most first-time operators over-build the offer and under-build the audience that hears about it.
  • The self-conscious phase is the tax. Early promotion feels like begging because your volume is low and every post feels like it's addressed to your friends. It stops feeling that way only after you're past it.
  • Nobody is watching as closely as you think. The perceived audience judging your first ten posts is mostly imaginary; the real one hasn't found you yet.
  • Promote before you feel ready. Waiting for the business to be "legitimate enough" to talk about inverts cause and effect — talking about it is what makes it legitimate.
