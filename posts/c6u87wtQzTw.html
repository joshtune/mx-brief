192GB of VRAM in One PC… The Cheap Way

Alex Ziskind · 2026-07-15 · 140,841 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Cheap Intel GPUs for local LLM VRAM vs NVIDIA.

If you want big local-LLM VRAM without NVIDIA's tax, Alex prices out Intel B60/B70 rigs against Mac and NVIDIA so you can see the real cost-per-gigabyte tradeoff.

What it is: A cost-and-performance shootout for stacking cheap VRAM to run local LLMs, centered on Intel's B60 (including a dual-GPU B60 card) vs NVIDIA and Mac.

The machine he's running AI on:

  • Custom PC in a Fractal case, ASUS Pro motherboard, Intel Xeon CPU
  • GPUs under test: Intel B60 Dual (two GPUs on one card, made only by Maxsun), single/dual B60s, and the B70

Price comparison (the part you actually want):

  • Intel B60 — 24GB VRAM, $650
  • Intel B70 — 32GB VRAM, ~$1,000
  • Two B60s = 48GB — ~$1,300 (cheaper than the equivalent Mac)
  • The Mac (referenced comparison) at 48GB tier is *more expensive* than two B60s
  • NVIDIA RTX Pro 4000 — 24GB, $2,500
  • NVIDIA RTX Pro 5000 — 48GB, $6,000
  • NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 — 96GB, in a class of its own

How it performs:

  • Qwen3 4B (FP8): B70 hits 92 tok/s; two B60s (tensor parallelism) beat a single B60
  • Qwen 27B (int4, ~30GB): two B60s beat the B70; a single B60 can't run it (not enough headroom)
  • 30B MoE (int4): B70 45 tok/s, B60 44.5 tok/s, two B60s slower
  • Takeaway: Intel's more-VRAM-per-dollar pitch is real, but "cheap VRAM" ≠ "useful VRAM" — software is the catch

The software catch:

  • Running LLM Scaler, a fork of vLLM that's ~a month behind upstream
  • Intel hardware is solid; the software stack still trails NVIDIA, which is why NVIDIA can charge premium prices

Tools & links:

  • Intel Arc B60 / B70 GPUs — cheap-VRAM path for local LLMs
  • Maxsun B60 Dual — only maker of the dual-GPU single-slot card
  • LLM Scaler (vLLM fork) — Intel's serving stack (supported-models list on its GitHub repo)

Why it matters for you: This is the whole answer to your question — the exact box Alex runs AI on (Xeon + Intel B-series GPUs) and how its VRAM-per-dollar stacks up against a comparable Mac, which comes out pricier at 48GB.
