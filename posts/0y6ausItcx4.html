Amazon’s Secret to Success Will Get You Promoted

A Life Engineered · 2026-10-28 · 5,075 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Amazon's real edge is high agency: pick a goal, work backward.

It gives you one reusable mental model (work backward from a specific career goal and pay the full price) plus his pitch for what to prepare for in a layoff and AI-heavy market.

What it is: A thought-leadership pitch arguing Amazon's leadership principles all reduce to "high agency", and that you should apply Amazon's "working backwards" method to your own career. It ends with a plug for his paid coaching program, Top Tier.

The message to engineers:

  • If you don't define what you want, you become an NPC in someone else's game: your manager, employer or family uses you for their goals.
  • Be specific. "Success" and "happiness" are too vague. Use targets like "staff engineer at a FAANG company by 35", "run my own startup" or "$1M total comp".
  • Everything has a price, usually not in dollars. He cites Kipling: if you didn't get it, you didn't seriously want it, or you tried to bargain over the price.
  • Failure looks like bargaining with reality. Examples: wanting a new job but skipping interview prep, or wanting a startup but refusing any risk of failure.
  • He claims Amazon's four traits (ownership, bias for action, customer obsession, working backwards) are all high agency. The intensity and stress come from a company of people who refuse "good enough".

Instructional: the working-backwards exercise:

  • 1. Pick one concrete goal, such as staff engineer.
  • 2. Picture what a typical Tuesday looks like in that life.
  • 3. Ask what must be true to get there. His example is leading cross-team initiatives.
  • 4. Ask how, repeatedly. Build influence beyond your team, which means writing design docs other teams use.
  • 5. Decide what to write about, which needs research into what would be impactful.
  • 6. Set a timeframe, such as this quarter.
  • 7. Name the first step. His example is to study influential docs and interview their authors about their approach.

What to prepare for:

  • Layoffs: He says layoffs are ongoing, but high performers are retained and paid more than ever. The best projects go to the best performers.
  • AI: He says tool trends go stale in about 6 months. Judgment can't be automated. AI will widen the gap between people who have durable skills and people who lose their jobs.
  • Durable skills he says to invest in: finding the right work, building influence, showing judgment in messy situations, telling the story of your impact, and surviving reorgs.
  • Interviews and reviews: He says understanding this model helps with Amazon-style behavioral interviews, performance reviews and faster promotion.

Tools & links:

  • Top Tier coaching — his paid program. He promises a promotion or better review in 12 months, or free continued coaching.
  • Newsletter — free weekly career newsletter.
  • Book: *Technical Behavioral Interview* — his interview prep book, which he says hit #1 on Amazon.

Caveats:

  • The promotion and retention claims are unsupported assertions with no data.
  • The video is partly a sales funnel.
  • The framework is a goal-setting method, not a tactical playbook.

Why it matters for you: Take the working-backwards exercise and run it on your next promotion this week. Skip the sales pitch.
