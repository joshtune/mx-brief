Gym equipment to $100M+ DTC

My First Million · 2026-08-25 · 3,813 views  ·  Watch on YouTube →
Wellness gear goes DTC; hyperbaric chambers are next

A repeatable playbook — take a 'medical device' out of the gym, make it look good on Instagram, sell it direct — that already minted a $100M/yr business with zero retail.

What it is: A short Shaan/Sam riff on the pattern where clinical wellness equipment gets rebranded as at-home consumer hardware and sold direct-to-consumer — sauna → cold plunge → red light → hyperbaric chamber next.

The featured business — Plunge ("the Cold Plunge Guys"):

  • Product: at-home cold plunge tubs
  • Revenue cited: ~$100M, per Shaan
  • Channel: 100% direct-to-consumer — zero retail presence
  • Marketing: seeded units to podcasters/influencers (they sent one to MFM directly)
  • Category context: at-home sauna brands are also cited at "hundreds of millions in revenue"

How the money gets made (the playbook):

  • Step 1 — Find the gatekept device. Something you currently have to leave home to use: a gym, a spa, a clinic, an altitude camp.
  • Step 2 — Shrink it and price it for a house. Same function, residential form factor, consumer price point.
  • Step 3 — Skip retail entirely. No Dick's, no Costco, no margin split. Own the customer, own the data, own the full ticket on a high-AOV item.
  • Step 4 — Make it a visual flex. Shaan's core thesis: cold plunges and saunas sold because they're *shareable* — a "wellness flex" or "discipline flex" on Instagram. The equipment is the ad.
  • Step 5 — Ride borrowed credibility. Real athletic/medical provenance does the education for you, so you're not explaining the category from scratch.

The hyperbaric chamber thesis:

  • Provenance: elite endurance runners used altitude/oxygen chambers from the '60s–'80s (the Flagstaff altitude-camp tradition) to improve oxygen efficiency
  • Bet: same arc as sauna/cold plunge/red light — clinical → athlete → mainstream at-home DTC
  • Shaan's differentiator for the company he liked: the chamber looks visually distinct from the standard medical tube — design is the wedge, because design is what makes it postable
