The Japanese Rule To Lose Visceral Fat in 3 Weeks

275,169 views 10,939 likes Watch on YouTube →

Doctor Mike Diamonds (a former medical doctor turned fitness coach) films in Japan and presents three Japanese cultural habits he frames as a way to lose visceral fat in three weeks. The three 'rules' are: (1) Hara Hachi Bu — eating to 80% fullness, supported by tactics like drinking green tea/matcha/sparkling water 20 minutes before meals, chewing 20-30 times, using chopsticks, and the PFC eating order (protein first, fiber second, carbs last); (2) Interval Walking Training — alternating 3 minutes fast and 3 minutes slow walking for 30 minutes; and (3) the Metabo Law — Japan's government-mandated waist-size measurement plus mandatory school nutrition education. The throughline is creating a 'natural Ozempic effect' by slowing eating and digestion so the brain registers fullness (which he says takes ~20 minutes). It is a health/lifestyle persuasion video citing some studies, not a rigorous scientific review, and it doubles as a funnel to his coaching program.

Rule 1 — Hara Hachi Bu (eat to 80% full)

  • Defines hara hachi bu as eating until 80% full, a Confucian-derived practice from Okinawa (a Blue Zone with high centenarian concentration). He pitches a separate future trip to Okinawa if viewers comment 'Dr. Mike, go to Okinawa.'
  • The math: at 2,500 kcal/day, a 20% deficit is 500 kcal × 7 days = 3,500 kcal = one pound of fat per week.
  • Explains the fullness delay: food triggers three hormones — CCK, GLP-1, and peptide YY — that travel via the bloodstream to the vagus nerve, taking ~20 minutes for the brain to register fullness. He likens this to how Ozempic signals fullness and slows gastric emptying, positioning his tactics as a 'natural Ozempic.'
  • Pre-meal drink tactic: Japanese restaurants serve water, green tea, or matcha before you order. EGCG (epigallocatechin gallate) in green tea/matcha is claimed to push the body to use visceral fat for energy ('adds more lines to your highway'). The drink also hydrates, stretches stomach mechanoreceptors, and (with caffeine) starts the 20-minute fullness clock. After midnight he swaps to caffeine-free sparkling water for the same stretching effect.
  • Pace tactics: set a 20-minute timer before meals; take 20-30 chews on the first bite and count them; put chopsticks down between bites (he observed Japanese diners doing this). Chopsticks force smaller bites than spoons, reducing glucose spikes and 'storage mode.'
  • Introduces ichiju-sansai (one soup, three sides) and notes veggies/fermented foods (miso, kimchi) are served first.

PFC system (live restaurant demo)

  • At his 'favorite restaurant,' late at night, he starts with sparkling water. Then demonstrates PFC: Protein First, Fiber second, Carbs last.
  • Protein: eat ~50% of the protein first (he eats half of four fillets) to stimulate peptide YY, build muscle, and raise metabolic rate.
  • Fiber: eats radish, cucumber, and burdock-root kimchi — fiber forms a 'gel-like substance' slowing carb absorption, and kimchi adds gut bacteria.
  • Carbs last (rice), then mix everything. The stated goal is a smaller insulin spike → less fat storage → another 'natural Ozempic' effect.

Rule 2 — Japanese walking method (Interval Walking Training)

  • Cites Shinshu University, 2007 research on Interval Walking Training, claiming it beats 10,000 steps: '29 times better at aerobic fitness,' 10x better leg strength, 3x blood-pressure improvement, and ~6 cm waist reduction.
  • Protocol: walk fast 3 minutes ('like you're trying to get to your gate before missing a flight'), then walk slowly 3 minutes; repeat for 5 sets = 30 minutes. He calls it 'HIIT light' — sustainable, less cortisol/injury than running.
  • Claims a 5-month study showed improved body composition, dropped visceral fat, better systolic BP and waist circumference. He says the method yielded ~4,500 steps in 30 minutes (vs ~3,000 normally).

Rule 3 — Metabo Law + school nutrition

  • Metabo = metabolic syndrome (he claims it accounts for ~80% of world deaths). Japan's law: for ages 40-74, waist must be under 33.5 inches (men) and 35.5 inches (women), measured yearly by the government; companies with too many over-limit employees can be fined. He frames it as accountability/consciousness rather than punishment.
  • Schools: Japan's 2005 law mandates nutrition education; every school employs a trained nutritionist (3 additional years of study) who builds same-day, unprocessed meals (no tomato or garlic paste) and teaches kids about macros via color-coded lines. He contrasts this with ~1 day of nutrition in his entire medical training.

The 3-week self-challenge / close

  • Weigh yourself every morning as a 1-second self-correcting feedback loop.

Key takeaways

  • The unifying mechanism across all tactics is slowing down eating and digestion so the brain registers fullness (~20 min) — repeatedly framed as a 'natural Ozempic' effect via CCK, GLP-1, and peptide YY.
  • Hara hachi bu (eat to 80% full) is the headline rule; the pre-meal drink, 20-30 chews, chopsticks, and 20-minute timer are practical ways to execute it.
  • The PFC order (protein → fiber → carbs) aims to blunt insulin spikes and increase satiety.
  • Interval Walking Training (3 min fast / 3 min slow × 5 = 30 min) is pitched as a sustainable, lower-stress alternative to HIIT and 10,000 steps.
  • The Metabo Law and mandatory school nutrition education are cultural/structural accountability mechanisms, not personal tactics — the takeaway is building self-accountability (e.g., daily weigh-ins, the 5-ingredient rule).

Gotchas & caveats

  • The video makes strong scientific-sounding claims (e.g., walking '29 times better at aerobic fitness,' EGCG 'directly targets' visceral fat, '80% of world deaths' from metabolic syndrome) without on-screen citations — treat the specific numbers skeptically.
  • It is fundamentally marketing: the description and close funnel viewers to a paid coaching program, and the disclaimer states transformations shown are 'exceptional results,' not typical.
  • 'Lose visceral fat in 3 weeks' is the framing hook; the only concrete arithmetic shown is the standard ~1 lb/week deficit math, not a 3-week visceral-fat guarantee.
  • 'It's illegal to be fat in Japan' is rhetorical overstatement — the actual Metabo Law fines companies, not individuals, and the creator himself notes it isn't directly enforced everywhere.
  • Some claimed mechanisms (caffeine + stomach acid creating gas to stretch mechanoreceptors and 'start the 20-minute clock') are presented as fact but are not established physiology.
