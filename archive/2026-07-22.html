The Storytelling Mistake: Going Depth-First in Interviews

2.9K

Tell interview stories top-down, not depth-first.

Your behavioral and system-design answers land better when you lead with the headline and let the interviewer pull detail—instead of rambling yourself into a corner.

details

What it is: A storytelling framework for interviews: structure answers like a pyramid (headline first, detail on demand) instead of going depth-first.

How it works:

  • Depth-first is the trap: Chase every detail down its rabbit hole and you drift like clicking the first Wikipedia link on every page—start at Linux, end at neoclassical Greek sculpture. You lose the thread and the interviewer.
  • Lead with the headline: Open with a 1–2 sentence summary of the entire story—the ending, up front.
  • Give just enough: Provide only the detail needed to understand how you reached that ending. Nothing more.
  • Then pause and offer: End with "I can go deeper here" and wait. Follow-up questions tell you which details actually matter.
  • Progressive detail beats guessing: Most details don't move the story. Front-loading them wastes time or steers the interviewer away from where they wanted to take it.
