What it is: A leveled walkthrough of Claude's capabilities, framed as five user tiers (Enthusiast → Beginner → and beyond), with the specific unlock that moves you up each rung.

Level 1 — The Enthusiast (the floor):

  • Treats Claude as a smarter search bar: one-off questions, quick emails, short scripts.
  • Missed upgrade most stay blind to: paste screenshots — Claude reads images, so stop transcribing.
  • Ceiling: ~30 min/day saved. You're leaving the rest on the table.
  • Cheat code to Level 2: create your first Project. Drop in reference docs, write a short system prompt (who you are, how you want Claude to respond). Every new chat in that project starts preloaded.

Level 2 — The Beginner (Projects + 6 stacked features):

  • Projects are the spine — persistent context, citations across chats, continuity instead of statelessness.
  • Memory & past chat search: Memory remembers role/preferences/decisions across conversations (free on every plan). Searching past chats is paid, but kills the "start from zero" problem.
  • Connectors (50+): Slack, Google Drive, Gmail, GitHub, Notion, Calendar. Click the `+`, OAuth in, then ask Claude to summarize a Slack channel, pull a spec doc, or check the week's calendar — no more pasting.
  • File creation: Real Excel (working formulas), PowerPoint, Word, PDF — actual downloadable deliverables, not just preview artifacts. Available to free users. Chat becomes a deliverable tool, not a brainstorming tool.
  • Artifacts (the newer version): Persistent storage between sessions, can call Claude's API directly so the app thinks for itself, and publishable via public link. A non-coder can build a customer feedback tracker in a chat and ship the link to their team — no Lovable, no Bolt, no dev.
  • Inline visuals: Ephemeral charts/diagrams inside the chat. Click to swap chart type, add variables live, drop in a CSV to visualize, compare options side by side. Different from artifacts: these live in the conversation and mutate as you talk.
  • (Sixth feature was cut off in the transcript snippet — likely a workflow/automation primitive that bridges into Level 3.)

How Howard Marks Predicted the $5 Trillion Dot-Com Crash

Howard Marks recounts calling the dot-com bubble.

A legendary investor breaks down the exact signals he used to spot the dot-com bubble — pattern recognition you can reuse on today's AI hype cycle.

details

What it is: Howard Marks (co-founder of Oaktree Capital, ~$200B AUM) explaining how he predicted the dot-com crash via his now-famous January 2000 memo *bubble.com*.

The person:

  • Howard Marks — billionaire investor, co-founder of Oaktree Capital Management
  • Known for his investor memos, widely read across Wall Street (Warren Buffett reads them)
  • Author of *The Most Important Thing* and *Mastering the Market Cycle*

How he spotted the bubble:

  • Fall 1999: read *Devil Take the Hindmost* by Edward Chancellor — a history of financial manias
  • Pattern-matched the 1720 South Sea Bubble to the dot-com frenzy
  • South Sea signal: people quitting day jobs to trade shares from alehouses
  • Dot-com signal: companies with no profits, no revenue, sometimes no product — just an idea — getting massive valuations
  • Wrote the *bubble.com* memo in January 2000 calling it out publicly
  • Nasdaq peaked March 2000, then crashed ~78% — wiping out ~$5T in market cap

The playbook (how he made money from it):

  • Recognize the pattern of mania (retail speculation, story stocks, no fundamentals)
  • Use historical analogues as your tell, not current narratives
  • Publish your thesis so you're forced to commit
  • Position via distressed debt — Oaktree's bread and butter is buying when others are forced sellers post-crash

Tools & links:

  • Devil Take the Hindmost — the Edward Chancellor book that triggered the call
  • bubble.com memo (Jan 2000) — Marks' original dot-com warning
  • Oaktree memos archive — every Marks memo, free

Why it matters for you: Same template applies to AI valuations in 2026 — if you can spot the South Sea pattern (no revenue, no product, pure narrative), you can either avoid the blowup or position for the cleanup.
