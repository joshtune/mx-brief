How it works:

  • Pattern 1 — The Mirror Test (copy, not design): The right visitor should land on the first screen and feel like they're looking in a mirror: their problem, their world, a clear sign they're in the right place.
  • Why AI fails here: It writes generic copy, so a good-looking site still under-converts.

  • Three questions your first screen must answer:
  • Who is this for?
  • What do I get? (outcome, not feature: 'fill your calendar without answering DMs', not 'AI-powered scheduling')
  • What do I do next? (one clear CTA, e.g. 'book your free call')

  • Pattern 2 — The Highlighter Rule (color): AI treats color as decoration, picking pleasant colors with weak contrast so nothing stands out.
  • The rule: Pick one standout color and use it only on the thing you want clicked (button, booking link). Highlight everything and nothing is highlighted.
  • Nate's example: First version of the new AI Automation Society site used blue everywhere and looked cheap. He went mostly black and white, kept blue for small key elements, and it felt more premium and clearer.
