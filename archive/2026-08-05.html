18 Months of Pricing AI Automations in 21 Mins

How to price AI automation builds using client math

details

What it is: Nate's pricing playbook after selling 100+ AI automation systems, walked through one real appointment-setting agent deal with every number exposed.

The worked example (appointment setting agent):

  • Baseline: 20 leads/week, ~1 hour of human time each = 20 hours/week
  • Fully-loaded employee cost: $40/hour → $800/week
  • Annualized: $800 × 52 = $41,600/year of recoverable labor cost
  • Build price: ~13% of the annual number = $5,500
  • Client return: $41,600 on a $5,500 investment ≈ 7.5x

The pricing formula:

  • Find the annual cost of the manual process (headcount hours × loaded hourly rate × 52)
  • Price the build at roughly 13% of that annual number
  • Golden rule: show a 10x return over 12 months — that math makes it hard to say no
  • Close the gap between 7.5x and 10x with *baseline growth*: as the system frees up time, leads/week climb from 20 → 21 → 23 → 27, compounding the return (a flywheel: more usage → more growth → more usage)
  • Frame growth as a projection, never a guarantee — do not tie your fee to specific revenue outcomes

Sequencing the conversation:

  • Never quote before discovery. Walk the client through the full solution first — how the agent works, what testing looks like, impact on speed-to-lead and lead quality
  • Standard deflection when a client pushes for a number early: *"to give you an accurate ballpark I need to understand the complexity of the system and how it integrates into your actual business processes"*
  • The client's own numbers become your price — that's what makes it defensible when they ask "where'd you get that number?"

Maintenance retainers:

  • $400/month flat, standardized across all projects — keep it simple, don't bespoke it
  • What it covers: keeping the build doing what was scoped — breakages, API changes, new model releases, edge-case tweaks
  • What it does NOT cover: new functionality. That's a separate scope and a separate conversation
  • Sanity check: if servicing a $30k system actually costs your team more than $400/month, you're losing money — don't anchor on the standard number

Cash flow:

  • Get paid in stages, so you're never carrying more than ~30 days of unpaid work

How To Use Debt Like The 1%

9.9K

Borrow against assets instead of selling them.

If your side hustle needs capital, this shows you how to fund it without liquidating the thing that's compounding — and without the tax hit.

details

What it is: A breakdown of the securities-backed line of credit (SBLOC) — the "buy, borrow, die" playbook Elon Musk and Larry Ellison use — scaled down to a normal person's brokerage account.

The instructional secret: Never sell the golden goose. Sell an asset and you eat capital gains tax *and* kill the compounding. Borrow against it and you keep 100% of the upside while the loan funds your next move. The whole strategy works on one condition: asset growth rate > interest rate.

How it works:

  • The billionaire version: Musk needed $44B for Twitter. Instead of dumping Tesla stock, he pledged $62.5B in shares as collateral for a $12.5B margin loan. Kept the shares, kept the voting rights, kept every dollar of upside.
  • The normal-person version: You have $100K in a brokerage account and need $20K to buy a business, a rental, or crypto.
  • Option 1 — sell $20K: You're left with $80K compounding at 10% → ~$128K over 5 years. Plus you owe capital gains (not even in her math).
  • Option 2 — SBLOC $20K: Full $100K keeps compounding at 10% → $161K. Subtract ~$6K/yr interest at 6%, pay back the $20K principal → ~$135K. $7K better even assuming the borrowed $20K earned literally nothing.
  • Paying the interest: Cover it from salary or business cash flow, same as a mortgage payment. It's a line item, not a crisis.
  • The tax kicker: Interest on the loan may be tax-deductible if the proceeds are used for investment — ask your CPA. You can never deduct cash you simply withdrew.
  • Collateral that qualifies: stocks, real estate, business equity, crypto, equipment.
  • "Buy, borrow, die": Buy the asset → borrow against it → keep borrowing against the growing base → never repay. At death the estate settles the loan, and heirs get a stepped-up basis. You never trigger a taxable sale in your lifetime.

The part she glosses over:

  • SBLOCs are margin loans with margin-call risk. Asset drops hard, bank demands cash or force-sells your position at the bottom. Musk's $62.5B collateral for $12.5B is a ~5x cushion — that ratio is the actual lesson, not the loan itself.
  • The 6% vs 10% spread is an assumption, not a guarantee. Rates are variable; a 10% annualized return is not.
  • Deductibility hinges on the money going into investments, not a vacation.

Tools & links:

  • SoFi — sponsor; small business funding (paid placement, treat accordingly)
  • Contrarian Thinking — her "Own or Be Owned" book launch event

Why it matters for you: Your side hustle's biggest constraint is usually starting capital — and this is the case for stopping the reflex to liquidate your index funds to fund it, provided you keep the collateral cushion fat enough to survive a drawdown.
