18 Months of Pricing AI Automations in 21 Mins

How to price AI automation builds using client math

Zero build content — this is the number-by-number breakdown of a real $5,500 appointment-setter deal, so you can defend a price instead of guessing at one.

details

What it is: Nate's pricing playbook after selling 100+ AI automation systems, walked through one real appointment-setting agent deal with every number exposed.

The worked example (appointment setting agent):

  • Baseline: 20 leads/week, ~1 hour of human time each = 20 hours/week
  • Fully-loaded employee cost: $40/hour → $800/week
  • Annualized: $800 × 52 = $41,600/year of recoverable labor cost
  • Build price: ~13% of the annual number = $5,500
  • Client return: $41,600 on a $5,500 investment ≈ 7.5x

The pricing formula:

  • Find the annual cost of the manual process (headcount hours × loaded hourly rate × 52)
  • Price the build at roughly 13% of that annual number
  • Golden rule: show a 10x return over 12 months — that math makes it hard to say no
  • Close the gap between 7.5x and 10x with *baseline growth*: as the system frees up time, leads/week climb from 20 → 21 → 23 → 27, compounding the return (a flywheel: more usage → more growth → more usage)
  • Frame growth as a projection, never a guarantee — do not tie your fee to specific revenue outcomes

Sequencing the conversation:

  • Never quote before discovery. Walk the client through the full solution first — how the agent works, what testing looks like, impact on speed-to-lead and lead quality
  • Standard deflection when a client pushes for a number early: *"to give you an accurate ballpark I need to understand the complexity of the system and how it integrates into your actual business processes"*
  • The client's own numbers become your price — that's what makes it defensible when they ask "where'd you get that number?"

Maintenance retainers:

  • $400/month flat, standardized across all projects — keep it simple, don't bespoke it
  • What it covers: keeping the build doing what was scoped — breakages, API changes, new model releases, edge-case tweaks
  • What it does NOT cover: new functionality. That's a separate scope and a separate conversation
  • Sanity check: if servicing a $30k system actually costs your team more than $400/month, you're losing money — don't anchor on the standard number

Cash flow:

  • Get paid in stages, so you're never carrying more than ~30 days of unpaid work

Credibility context:

  • 100+ AI automation systems sold; agency scaled past $100k/month with a $20k/month minimum retainer, then sold

Tools & links:

  • AI Automation Society (free Skool community) — Nate's free resource hub
  • $1M agency playbook — paid/lead-magnet funnel for the agency growth material
  • Glaido — voice-to-text tool, free month via affiliate link

Why Your Brain Is Holding You Back

20.4K

Loss aversion is quietly blocking your first bet.

It names the exact cognitive tax that keeps you researching side hustles instead of starting one — and gives three concrete levers to reprice the bet.

details

What it is: A 90-second breakdown of Kahneman's prospect theory and how loss aversion gets in the way of every wealth-building bet you don't take.

The research:

  • Daniel Kahneman's prospect theory (the Nobel work — note: Codie says "Nobel Peace Prize"; it was actually the 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences)
  • Study setup: a 50/50 gamble — 50% chance to win $100, 50% chance to lose $100
  • Result: most people won't take the bet until the upside is 2–3x the downside. They need $200-to-win vs. $100-to-lose before they'll play
  • Diagnosis: losses hurt more than equivalent wins feel good. But money is made on the other side of bets, so the wiring is actively expensive

How to fix it — three moves:

  • Flip the fear. Stop pricing what you lose if it fails. Price what you're already losing by staying put. Inaction has a cost line too; most people just never write it down
  • Increase the potential reward. Don't argue with your brain's 2–3x demand — satisfy it. Engineer the payoff so the upside genuinely is multiples of the downside, then the bet clears your own internal hurdle rate
  • Increase your odds. Turn the 50/50 into a 70/30, then a 90/10. Two accelerants: (1) surround yourself with winners, (2) take a lot of reps

The engineering translation:

  • This is asymmetric bet sizing. Capped downside, uncapped upside — the same reason a weekend side project beats quitting your job cold
  • "Increase your odds" = reduce variance through information and iteration. Reps are just sampling; proximity to people who've done it is a cheap prior
  • The 2–3x hurdle explains why you keep passing on decent opportunities that *are* positive expected value. Your brain isn't running EV, it's running a loss-weighted distortion of it

Why it matters for you: If you've been circling a side hustle for months without launching, the blocker probably isn't the idea — it's a rigged internal hurdle rate, and these three levers are how you reprice the bet so it actually clears.
