How it works:

  • The core claim: Intelligence has near-zero correlation with how much money you make. People "dumber than you" lap you because they work harder and take more shots.

Barry Ritholtz on how investors destroy their own returns.

A $8B fund manager's plain-spoken rules for not being stupid with money — the behaviors, not the stock picks, that actually move your returns.

details

What it is: Shaan Puri & Sam Parr interview Barry Ritholtz (Episode 832), founder of an ~$8B AUM advisory firm, on behavioral finance and how to stop sabotaging your own portfolio.

Who he is & how he built it:

  • Went to law school, did well, hated being a lawyer
  • A client ran a trading desk (predecessor to E*Trade); he jumped in and started on the desk
  • Got fascinated by *why* the same trader killed it one week and got shellacked the next — answer: emotions, cognitive biases, decision-making, not skill
  • That rabbit hole into behavioral finance became his edge
  • Started in the content/media game early — blogging on GeoCities, then podcasting — and built that audience into a large investment advisory shop
  • Named the firm "Ritholtz" as a temporary placeholder; never found a better name, so it stuck — now ~$8B AUM

His core investing rules:

  • #1 advice: "Put the phone down. Stop trading." Be less stupid, not more smart.
  • The index reality: In any given year <50% of active managers beat their index; over 5 years ~21%; over 10 years <10%; over 20 years it's a handful of names (Buffett, Lynch). Underperformers don't just miss alpha — they get *less* than the market gives.
  • The Christmas Tree portfolio: 50–70% "core" = broad, low-cost US market index (e.g. Vanguard's VOO, first ETF over $1T AUM). That's the tree. The lights/tinsel/garland = your optional tilts (momentum, extra tech, Japan via EWJ, India ETF, etc.).
  • The "cowboy account": his version of a diet cheat-meal — a small carve-out for fun bets (startups, hot tech stocks) that keeps you disciplined on the other 90% rather than blowing up the whole portfolio.
  • Recognize the decorations are likely to *underperform* the index — they're for psychological staying power, not edge.

Tools & links:

  • Barry Ritholtz on X — the guest
  • Shaan Puri / Sam Parr — hosts
  • VOO — Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (first ETF over $1T)
  • EWJ — iShares Japan ETF (example tilt mentioned)

Why it matters for you: The featured individual is Ritholtz; what he *did* to make money was turn early content/blogging into distribution, fuse it with behavioral-finance discipline, and compound it into an ~$8B firm — the playbook is audience + boring index core + small contained risk bucket.
